# Giving Green: public cost-effectiveness models

Every Giving Green (GG) page we could find that publishes or links a cost-effectiveness estimate,
checked page by page in October 2026, and what we did with each. Each entry has a status: `imported`
(with the model id), or `not imported:` with the reason.

- **Source:** all 337 pages in GG's sitemap (`/research/`, `/post/` and the site pages), the PDFs
  they link (report downloads, the activism CEA, nonprofit evaluations) and every linked Google Sheet,
  each tested through its public export. GG's older URLs (`/us-policy-change-research/...`) now
  return 404; the Wayback Machine was offline during the check.
- **Found:** 42 entries with a cost-effectiveness claim, a dollars-per-tonne figure or a linked
  model (rows below; a grouped row counts once). GG states its approach plainly: a top nonprofit should
  be "roughly $1 per metric ton of CO2-equivalent reduced", anything under $10 is plausibly in range,
  and its estimates are "plausibility checks", not rankings. Most of its pages are qualitative.
- **Imported:** 7 models from 7 entries, in `models/climate/`, each in dollars per tonne of CO₂e
  converted with the shared climate rate (docs/normalization.md 2.5): The Good Food Institute (2026
  page, sheet built 2022, updated 2024), Future Cleantech Architects (2024), Opportunity Green (2026
  page, sheet 2024), Evergreen Collaborative (2022 IRA estimate; the superseded 2021 estimate too,
  which doesn't count on the rankings), Carbon180 (2021) and, in the second pass, Industrious Labs
  (2022 CEA, from the figures in the 2024 deep-dive PDF; no sheet is published).
- **Not imported (35 entries):**
  - recommendations with no published figure of GG's own: Clean Air Task Force and Project InnerSpace
    (GG's evaluations say it "chose not to quantify" their cost-effectiveness; GG's "around $1 per
    ton" for CATF is its benchmark, from Founders Pledge's estimate, imported under Founders Pledge), the
    Australian list (five evaluation PDFs and pages checked: no figure) and about 100 grantee spotlights;
  - pages that repeat an imported sheet (older or parallel GFI, FCA and Opportunity Green pages) and
    a superseded copy of the GFI sheet with the same best guess;
  - strategy-level sheets for an intervention, not a charity (cement procurement, Indonesian and Indian
    power, grid planning tools, aviation, nuclear, geothermal, US climate activism);
  - offsets bought from companies (BURN stoves, Tradewater, Climeworks, Charm, Mash Makes, Recoolit
    and others): a company selling offsets, not a charity (none of them is a nonprofit);
  - methodology, fund and data pages.
- **Licence:** GG's terms say "© Giving Green Research Group 2025. All rights reserved" and forbid
  reproducing its material without permission. The models cite GG's pages and sheets and reproduce
  their numbers as facts; no text or sheet is copied.
- **How:** each sheet was read with `scripts/sheet-dump.py` and written by hand, row by row, as the
  import-cea skill says; every calculated row reproduces GG's value in the evaluator view. Industrious
  Labs has no published sheet: its model is built from the figures in GG's deep dive (campaign cost and
  GG's three costs per tonne), each quoted on its row, and reproduces GG's $0.52. In one
  model (Evergreen 2022) our view departs from the sheet: the sheet's formula omits the ten years of
  spending that its own cell note and GG's write-up use, so our view uses GG's published $4.88 a
  tonne and the evaluator view keeps the sheet's $0.49.
