## 2. How evaluators and funders value it

No evaluator publishes a charity-level cost-effectiveness figure on a common scale. What exists:

**Funders' bars, not estimates.**
- *Dollars per basis point (0.01%) of existential risk.* Linch (2021) would fund at about $100M per bp,
  hesitate at about $1B, oppose above $10B, and calls these "off-the-cuff"
  ([post](https://forum-bots.effectivealtruism.org/posts/cKPkimztzKoCkZ75r/how-many-ea-2021-usds-would-you-trade-off-against-a-0-01)).
  SoGive's survey of such answers (Ericson 2023) has medians of $100M ("highly cost-effective"),
  $1B (middling) and $5.2B (upper bound), and proposes £750M per bp as a "longtermist gold standard"
  ([Thresholds #1](https://forum-bots.effectivealtruism.org/posts/bmrr8DFufz5Yh8yRC/thresholds-1-what-does-good-look-like-for-longtermism)).
  Other answers in that survey: Cotra $20B, Habryka about $1B.
- Coefficient Giving, SFF (the S-process's marginal-value curves), Longview, Founders Pledge and the
  LTFF publish grant write-ups and theories of change, but no numbers. Founders Pledge's
  [AI guide](https://www.founderspledge.com/research/research-and-recommendations-advanced-artificial-intelligence)
  (Barnes 2024) recommends on qualitative grounds (CLTR, Institute for Law & AI, Effective
  Institutions Project). GWWC's 2023 evaluations of the
  [ECF and LTFF](https://www.givingwhatwecan.org/blog/2025-charity-recommendations-are-in-just-in-time-for-giving-tuesday)
  judge the grantmaking process, not impact per dollar.

**Generic models.**
- *Rethink Priorities' Cross-Cause Model* ([code](https://github.com/rethinkpriorities/cross-cause-cost-effectiveness-model-public),
  `xrisk_interventions.py`). Its "Small-scale AI Misalignment Project" costs $200k–1M, has a 96.4%
  chance of no effect, and otherwise cuts misalignment risk by a relative 1e-6 to 5.5e-5. The
  megaproject costs $8–28B, has a 97.3% chance of no effect, and cuts the risk by 50–80% if it works
  (anchored on a Metaculus question about OpenAI's Superalignment). Shared defaults:
  persistence 15–25 years; 70% chance the sign is good; harm at 30% of the benefit's size when it is
  bad; misalignment's catastrophe-to-extinction ratio 2:1; AI risk 90% misalignment, 10% misuse. Both
  presets imply about 1.2e-12 relative risk reduction per dollar. A user ran the megaproject at about
  154 DALYs per $1,000 (risk-adjusted −56 to 154), against a global health bar of about 20, and called
  the x-risk inputs "placeholders"
  ([thread](https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/CuLJQmq87WWkTMvB4/mo-putera-s-quick-takes/comment/tSGunGuRGANkozmrZ)).
- *Jones, "How Much Should We Spend to Reduce A.I.'s Existential Risk?"*
  ([NBER w33602](https://nber.org/papers/w33602), 2025). Lives are valued at a $10M VSL. A 1%
  mortality risk justifies about $100k per person. Spending at least 1% of GDP a year is justified in
  most scenarios even with no value on future generations, and up to about 5% if the risk falls in
  the next two decades. This is the academic anchor for the argument that AI safety beats health
  even without the future.

**Org- and programme-level BOTECs.**
- *CAIS field-building models* (2023, [repo](https://github.com/centerforaisafety/AIS-cost-effectiveness),
  [professional](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/WD8iWT6P7Y2gimJ8w/cost-effectiveness-of-professional-field-building-programs)
  and [student](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/GcpvkusNtmMhwfChS/cost-effectiveness-of-student-programs-for-ai-safety-1)
  posts). These count quality-adjusted research years (QARYs) per $1M for CAIS's own programmes. The
  figures moved a lot between revisions: Trojan Detection Challenge 390 → 1,800, NeurIPS ML Safety
  Social 29,000 → 34,000, NeurIPS workshop 3,300. Student groups and undergraduate stipends rank above
  MLSS and Atlas. The unit stops at research years and doesn't reach risk.
- *Arb's AI Safety Camp assessment*: about $12–30k per new AI safety researcher (a commenter's bounds
  are $6–60k). MATS is about $27–35k per scholar. In a 2024 survey of 72 alumni, 78% work on
  alignment.
- *AI safety video* ([Chen & Abramovitch, Sept 2025](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/SBsGCwkoAemPawfJz/how-cost-effective-are-ai-safety-youtubers)):
  quality-adjusted viewer minutes, about 150–300 per dollar for good channels. Contested.
- *ControlAI's UK outreach* ([Torchbearer, 2026](https://forum-bots.effectivealtruism.org/posts/hYJsycsFE3Rogyt7N/how-effective-is-controlai-s-parliamentary-outreach)):
  125 parliamentarians signed from 160+ meetings, about 31 per parliamentary FTE a year. It
  benchmarks outputs against other campaigns and has no dollars-to-risk chain.
- Nuño Sempere's 2021 [shallow evaluations](https://nunosempere.com/blog/2021/06/24/shallow-evaluations-of-longtermist-organizations)
  are mostly not AI organisations, and he says they are not for decisions. 80,000 Hours' problem
  profile puts existential catastrophe from AI this century at about 10%.

**Shared facts a builder could cite for the risk itself.**
- Existential Risk Persuasion Tournament (2022, published 2023): AI-caused extinction by 2100 is
  0.38% for superforecasters and 3% for domain experts. AI catastrophe (≥10% of humans dying) is
  2.13% for superforecasters; the experts' figure needs checking in FRI's report.
- Grace et al. 2024 ([arXiv:2401.02843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02843)), 2,778 AI authors: median
  5% (mean 16%) for extinction or a similarly bad outcome.
- UN population (already `@catastrophe.world_population_long_run`, 10.2 billion).

## 3. Fitting it to the one scale

### 3.1 Family: `catastrophic-risk` (new), built from parts that already exist

The two catastrophe branches in conservation (`catastrophe`, `future`) are already the right valuation.
What's missing is the step from dollars to a cut in the probability. I propose a new family with
the conservation branches word for word, plus a field-margin step that every AI model shares. Policy
wins and career changes come in through policy-research-meta's branches, and regrantors use 2.8's
money-moved method with AI destinations. The output is `daly`, with present deaths in `uov` through
`dalys_from_uov`.

```
cost         program_spend, overhead_ratio → cost_total
field        p_cat           = $ai_catastrophe_chance                       (this century; slider, see 3.3)
             ext_share       = @ai_risk.extinction_share_of_catastrophe     (extinction or permanent loss of the future; XPT 0.38/2.13 ≈ 0.18 supers)
             cut_per_doubling= @ai_risk.relative_cut_per_doubling           (share of the risk removed if all effort to date doubled)
             field_spend     = @ai_risk.field_cumulative_spend              (all resources aimed at the risk to date: philanthropy, lab safety teams, governments)
             field_cut_per_dollar = p_cat * cut_per_doubling / (field_spend * ln(2))     (log returns: the next dollar at today's margin)
reach        charity_vs_field = per-charity judgment, ranged (0.1–10), anchored per 3.4
             risk_cut        = cost_total * field_cut_per_dollar * charity_vs_field     (absolute probability, before adjustments)
catastrophe  catastrophe_deaths = @ai_risk.world_population_now * (ext_share + (1 - ext_share) * cat_death_share)
             catastrophe_value_per_death = (age-band blend as in conservation) * $life_value_multiplier
             years_until_risk = @ai_risk.years_until_risk                   (5–30; timelines surveys)
             uov_catastrophe = risk_cut * catastrophe_deaths * catastrophe_value_per_death * (1 + $discount_rate) ^ -years_until_risk
future       future_share_lost = ext_share                                  (non-extinction catastrophes: ~0, as conservation)
             future_dalys_averted = risk_cut * future_share_lost * @catastrophe.world_population_long_run / $discount_rate * (1 + $discount_rate) ^ -years_until_risk
adjustments  counterfactual_adj (the work would have happened anyway: other orgs, labs)
             × leverage_funging_adj (Coefficient Giving would fill the gap: 0.3–1)
             × missing_data_adj = $missing_data_discount (always thin, see 3.5)
             (+ unmodelled_benefits_adj: sign risk as a negative share, −(1 − p_good) × intensity_bad / p_good,
                RP's 0.7 and 0.3 give −0.13; evals and capabilities-adjacent research wider, to −0.5)
bridges      dalys_from_uov (fixed); output = (dalys_from_uov + future_dalys_averted) × total_adj / cost_total × 1000
```

Read the field step as a ratio: a charity's dollar counts as `charity_vs_field` times the field's
average next dollar. The field's next dollar comes from three shared numbers and one value judgment.
That gives one consistent way for every AI model, and the per-charity work is reduced to a single
ranged multiplier plus the charity's own branches. A check row, `dollars_per_bp = 1e-4 /
field_cut_per_dollar`, shows the implied price of a basis point next to the SoGive and Linch anchors.

### 3.2 Worked defaults (illustration, not a model)

Inputs: p_cat 5%, cut_per_doubling 1%, field_spend $3B, charity_vs_field 1, ext_share 1 (to show the
extinction case), years_until_risk 15, ~46 healthy years per death (population-weighted
`@moral_weights` ÷ `$uov_per_daly`), adjustments 0.5 (missing data) × 0.7 (funging) × 0.8 (sign) = 0.28.

| | per $1,000 |
|---|---|
| risk cut (absolute) | 2.4e-10, i.e. **$420M per basis point** (inside the $100M–$1B bar) |
| deaths averted in expectation | 1.97 |
| present deaths, discounted 15 years at 4% | 50 healthy years |
| future generations at 4% (10.2bn ÷ 0.04 × 2.4e-10 × 0.555) | 34 |
| **after adjustments, 4% discount (default)** | **~24** (GiveWell's top charities on the site: ~21) |
| same at the 0.1% floor | ~700 |
| superforecasters' risk (0.38%), 4% | ~1.8 |
| p_cat 20%, 4% | ~95 |

Two things follow. First, at the site's default 4% the future-generations term is about the same
size as the deaths of people alive now, so the defaults are not driven by the future. The ranking is
driven by p_cat × cut_per_doubling ÷ field_spend, which spans about three orders of magnitude.
Second, the discount floor multiplies the future term by 40. The tornado will show two inputs on
top, `$discount_rate` and the risk slider, and the summary must say so.

### 3.3 Shared facts, sliders, per-charity judgments

| Input | Kind | Proposed value and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `$ai_catastrophe_chance` | **slider** (a belief people disagree about) | 3% default (XPT experts' extinction figure), range 0.38% (XPT superforecasters) to 20% (80,000 Hours 10%, Grace survey mean 16%). Owner decision |
| `@ai_risk.extinction_share_of_catastrophe` | fact | XPT superforecasters 0.38/2.13 ≈ 0.18; range 0.1–0.7 (RP's 2:1 ratio ≈ 0.33) |
| `cat_death_share` | fact | share dying in a non-extinction catastrophe, 0.1–0.5 (the XPT ≥10% threshold) |
| `@ai_risk.field_cumulative_spend` | fact | $2–8B: philanthropy since 2014 (Open Phil alone $336M+ by 2024, $409M in 2025), labs' safety teams, AI security institutes. Labs counting in is an owner decision (3.6) |
| `@ai_risk.relative_cut_per_doubling` | fact (judgment, wide) | 0.3–10%, best 1%. Anchors: the implied $/bp against the funder bars; RP's CCM implies ~1.2e-12 relative per dollar, i.e. ~0.25% per doubling at $3B; 80,000 Hours' solvability framing |
| `@ai_risk.years_until_risk` | fact | 5–30, from the Grace survey (50% HLMI by 2047) and Metaculus AGI dates |
| `@ai_risk.world_population_now` | fact | UN WPP 2024, 8.2bn |
| `p_good`, `intensity_bad` | facts, shared | RP CCM 0.7 / 0.3; per charity only with reason |
| `$discount_rate`, `$life_value_multiplier`, `$missing_data_discount`, `$uov_per_daly` | existing sliders | unchanged |
| `charity_vs_field` | per charity | see 3.4 |
| `leverage_funging_adj`, `counterfactual_adj` | per charity | the funder bulletin: Coefficient Giving funds most of these orgs and is scaling to $1B, so the next small-donor dollar is partly funged (0.3–1) |

Persistence: in the field-margin form it sits inside `cut_per_doubling`, because a cut that lasts
only until the risk period begins is worth less. RP's 15–25 years is the anchor. Don't add a separate
persistence multiplier as well, or the same doubt gets counted twice (Convention 5).

### 3.4 Per-charity branches (bottom-up, feeding `charity_vs_field` or replacing it)

- **Technical research and evaluations** (METR, Apollo, Redwood, FAR, ARC, MIRI's old research):
  `charity_vs_field` anchored on adoption, i.e. evaluations that labs or AI security institutes
  actually run, cited in system cards and frontier safety frameworks. Evals and capability
  demonstrations get the wider negative share (they can inform capability work).
- **Policy and advocacy** (CAIS Action Fund, Encode, CLTR, IAPS, GovAI, MIRI's treaty work,
  ControlAI): policy-research-meta's policy branch. `p_success` × `policy_risk_cut` (the policy's
  share of `p_cat`, e.g. a frontier-model law with incident reporting and evals) ×
  `$win_years_counted` / horizon × attribution among the coalition. The result reports
  `estimate_basis: past_average` with `$marginal_vs_past_average`. Reference class for `p_success`:
  state AI bills passed or vetoed in 2024–25 (SB 1047 vetoed, SB 53 signed).
- **Field-building** (MATS, BlueDot, 80,000 Hours, CAIS courses): the careers branch.
  `careers_changed` (alumni in safety roles, minus the counterfactual from MATS's own surveys) ×
  `researcher_years` (discounted) × `value_per_researcher_year`, where `value_per_researcher_year =
  field_cut_per_dollar × @ai_risk.spend_per_fte_year` (~$300k: annual spend ÷ ~1,100 FTE).
  `attribution_share` = training cost ÷ (training cost + the employer's spending on that researcher),
  so the researcher's employer isn't credited twice.
- **Regrantors** (Transformative AI Fund, ECF, Manifund, FP GCR Fund): 2.8's money moved, with the
  destinations being live `#model` reads of the AI models weighted by the fund's grants. This needs the
  destination models first. `@meta.new_money_share` applies.

### 3.5 Where the existing machinery fits and where it doesn't

- **Discount floor**: fits, and it's the right control. At 0.1% the future is 1,000 years of world
  population; at 4% it's 25. But `$discount_rate` currently mixes time preference, growth and
  uncertainty (GiveWell's 4%) with "how much future generations count". **Owner decision**: keep one
  slider, or split off `$future_generations_rate` for the `future` branch only. Splitting would stop
  the AI ranking moving when someone tunes the deworming discount.
- **Background risk**: the endless future ÷ r ignores that later risks also cut the future short
  (Thorstad's "time of perils" argument). The 0.1% floor works as a background risk of 0.1% a year.
  Say this in the slider's explainer rather than add a fact.
- **Missing-data discount**: fits, and every AI branch is thin (no measured outcome), so they all take
  `$missing_data_discount`. The lint already demands it.
- **Next dollar vs past average**: the field-margin form is `marginal`, because log returns are
  diminishing returns already. Only won-policy branches are `past_average`.
- **Resource shares toggle**: works for co-funded orgs (most get money from Coefficient Giving and SFF).
- **Doesn't fit yet**: (a) sign uncertainty. The site has no shared "chance the work backfires", and
  the RP figures would be new shared facts. (b) Outcomes that are neither deaths nor extinction
  (permanent disempowerment, lock-in, suffering risks) aren't on the scale; I'd treat them as
  `future_share_lost` between 0 and 1. (c) Range display: joint ranges will run from ~0.1 to ~10,000
  per $1,000. The combined range should be shown in log space, and the page should say the ranking is
  belief-driven.

### 3.6 Value judgments for the owner

1. The default for `$ai_catastrophe_chance`: superforecasters (0.38%), experts (3%), AI researchers
   (5%), or a blend. This decides where AI sits against GiveWell, by roughly 10×.
2. Whether the headline counts future generations at the default discount (as conservation already
   does), or shows a "present people only" figure by default with the future as a toggle. My view is
   to keep it counted: at 4% it's about half the total.
3. Whether labs' and governments' safety spending is in `field_spend`. It should be, since the
   denominator is all effort on the risk, but it's poorly measured.
4. Whether c4 lobbying arms (CAIS Action Fund) are shown, given gifts to them aren't tax-deductible.
5. One shared `charity_vs_field` rubric, so that per-charity multipliers aren't free-hand.

## 4. Import vs build, and where to start

**Importable (import-cea), as evaluator views with a bridge, following "import everything"**:
- CAIS's QARY models of its own programmes: bridge QARY → researcher-year → `value_per_researcher_year`.
- Arb's AI Safety Camp cost per researcher (careers branch).
- The QAVM video estimates: these would need a viewer-minute → career or advocacy bridge, which is
  weak. Show them, but don't rank them until there's a bridge.

Everything else (RP CCM, Jones, the $/bp bars) is a source for shared facts, not a charity model.
**No charity has a published DALY or risk-per-dollar CEA**, so every AI charity needs our own build.

**Recommended first five**, which together cover each branch type:
1. **METR** (evaluations): the largest public budget, takes small gifts, and its outputs are traceable
   (lab system cards, AI security institutes).
2. **Center for AI Safety**: several programmes (research, compute, courses, statement) make it a
   whole-charity tree, and its own QARY models can be imported. Model the Action Fund separately.
3. **MATS** (field-building): the most measured outcome in the field (cohort sizes, cost per scholar,
   alumni surveys). It tests the careers branch.
4. **MIRI**: the best-known AI safety charity among small donors, now policy and communication. It
   tests the policy branch with a `past_average` basis.
5. **Longview Emerging Challenges Fund** (after 1–4): GWWC's recommended catastrophic-risk fund for
   small donors. It tests money moved into AI destinations.

Before any build, the shared parts go in: `facts/ai_risk.json`, the `$ai_catastrophe_chance` slider,
the `catastrophic-risk` skeleton (gen-templates.py), the required rows in templates.ts, and a
families/catastrophic-risk.md. The family is then reusable for biosecurity and nuclear charities.

## Decided (2026-11-04)

- **Chance of AI catastrophe** is a slider in Your values, `$ai_catastrophe_chance`, range 0.01%–20%,
  with the reasoning behind the marked points: 0.38% (forecasting-tournament superforecasters),
  3% (the same tournament's domain experts) and 5%. Default to be set with the template.
- **Future generations** count through the one discount rate (floor 0.1%); no separate slider.
- **Field spending** includes AI labs' and governments' safety spending.
- **Lobbying arms** that can't take tax-deductible gifts are shown, labelled as such.
- **One family, `catastrophic-risk`,** reusing the conservation template's catastrophe rows, for AI,
  biosecurity and nuclear risk.
- **No scoring against a field average.** Like every other family, each charity's figure comes from its
  own outputs × a shared effect per unit × the four standard adjustments × the missing-data marker.
  The shared effect per unit is the field's risk cut per researcher-year (or per policy win), from the
  field's total spending (labs and governments included) and staff. Outputs: researcher-years and
  their uptake (research, evaluations); researchers trained × years they stay (field-building); won
  policies by the won-reform method (policy); money moved with destinations read live (regrantors).
  Every branch is thin: the effect per unit comes from a model of the field, not a trial.
