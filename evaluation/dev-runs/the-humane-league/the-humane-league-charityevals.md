### Summary

The Humane League (THL) gets food companies to stop buying eggs from caged hens, and pushes companies that have already promised to do so to keep their word. It also coordinates and funds the Open Wing Alliance, 82 groups in 72 countries running the same campaigns.

Our model of THL's 2025 work gives **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="the-humane-league-charityevals">1.4</span> years of healthy life per $1,000** you give, at our default values. The range is very wide: it depends mostly on how much a chicken's suffering counts against a person's, how long a won reform counts, and how much of companies' compliance THL's pressure actually caused. Treat the headline as an order of magnitude.

### What the charity does

THL spent $21.6 million in 2025 (audited), $14.6 million of it on programs. Its annual report splits program spending into:

- **Global Programs (36%)**: campaigns outside the US, including a $2.15 million grant to The Humane League UK.
- **Education & Mobilization (33%)**: the volunteers ("changemakers"), investigations and communications that put pressure on companies, such as 1.48 million messages to decision-makers in 2025 and the largest-ever investigation into egg farms, filmed in 37 countries.
- **US Public Policy & Corporate Engagement (17%)**: negotiations with US companies, and legislative and legal work.
- **Open Wing Alliance and Animal Policy Alliance grants (14%)**: $2.1 million to 36 alliance groups, and $300,000 to 12 US groups working on state and local laws.

We model the corporate cage-free work: everything except the US legislative and legal work (about 7.5% of program spending) the broiler campaigns, mostly part of THL UK's grant (about 8%), and THL UK's cage-ban lobbying and fish work (about 3%). Each is under a tenth, and each is left out of cost and benefit alike.

### How the impact happens

A company commits to cage-free eggs. If it keeps the commitment, hens in its supply chain move from cages to barns or aviaries by its deadline, and live with less suffering for as long as the change counts before it would have happened anyway. We count three kinds of 2025 work:

1. **THL's own new commitments**: four companies, 1.6 million hens a year.
2. **The alliance's other new policies**: 212 more cage-free policies, mostly from smaller companies, many in Africa and Asia. No hen count is published; we assume 10,000 hens a policy (2,000–50,000).
3. **Accountability**: pressure on 131 companies that had already promised to go cage-free, covering 162.5 million hens a year by THL's own count (Subway, Ahold Delhaize, Best Western, Walmart and others).

Each branch is discounted in turn:

- **Pledges kept**: 77%, the rate every model on the site uses (Chicken Watch).
- **What would have happened anyway**: for new commitments, 90% are credited. For accountability we assume 30% of pressed pledges would never be kept without the pressure (range 10% to 60%), because most companies keep pledges without a campaign. THL presses the same companies year after year. At THL's own pace (7.5 million hens left cages in 2025, against about 119 million still to come under pressed pledges that will be kept), a pledge is pressed for about 16 years, so one year of pressure gets a sixteenth of that 30%. In effect, accountability is credited with 30% of the 7.5 million hens a year. The new-commitment branches leave out the part of later compliance owed to pressure (30% of the roughly 70% of pledged hens whose pledges get pressed), so nothing is credited twice.
- **Credit**: THL's share of the resources behind each result. 50% for its four new commitments, which were won alongside alliance members. 40% for accountability, THL's spending against its partners' on the same targets. 20% for the alliance's policies, THL's grants and staff against the member groups' 525 campaign staff.
- **Trust in THL's hen counts**: 70% for its 1.6 million hens under new commitments, and 70% (45% to 100%) for its 7.5 million hens spared in 2025, on which accountability rests: both are THL's own, unverified counts, and the second more than doubled from the year before. The alliance branch gets no such discount, because its hen count is our own guess.
- **Time**: each change counts the site's shared "Years a won reform counts before it would have come anyway", and the wait until it takes effect is discounted.

The suffering averted per hen comes from the shared figures in suffering-adjusted days (SADs): one figure for a hen moved from a battery cage, one for a hen moved from an enriched cage. We assume 20% of the hens are in enriched cages, mostly hens of European companies. We then take Ambitious Impact's species weight out of the SADs and count days of a chicken's pain. The chicken slider converts those days to healthy years.

### How the model works

Cost is THL's 2025 program spending without the policy, broiler and THL UK policy and fish work, plus its share of overhead: $17.6 million. Each branch counts hens per year × the share not yet cage-free × discounted years, then applies its own adjustments. The branches are added up, multiplied by SADs per hen-year and divided by cost. The headline is THL's past spending divided by what it achieved, so the next dollar is valued at the shared "How much the next dollar does compared with the average dollar so far" (50% by default).

### What drives the result

- **Accountability is a little over half of the result**, new commitments about a quarter, the alliance's policies a fifth. Accountability's key number is a judgment: the share of pressed pledges that would never be kept without pressure, 30% (10% to 60%; result about 1.0 to 2.0). No tracker compares companies that were pressed with companies that weren't.
- **The alliance's hens per policy** (our guess, 2,000 to 50,000) move the result from about 1.2 to 2.5.
- **The wait**: hens moved by a year of pressure leave cages on average about 7 years later (2 to 10), at THL's 2025 pace.
- **The shared values**: how much a chicken counts against a person, the years a won reform counts, and the next dollar against the average dollar each move the result more than any THL-specific number.
- **THL's hen counts**: the 162.5 million cancels out of the accountability branch (it sets both the stock and the years of pressure). What counts is THL's 7.5 million hens spared in 2025 (trust 45% to 100%: result about 1.1 to 1.7).

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether THL's accountability figure means hens whose companies changed because of THL, or every hen of every company THL contacted.
- The alliance's policies have no hen count at all.
- Credit in a coalition: THL, Mercy For Animals, Animal Equality and dozens of alliance groups often press the same company.
- Costs and results don't line up in time. 2025's compliance partly comes from campaigns paid for in earlier years, and 2025's spending partly pays for wins that come later. A single year's ratio is noisy.

### What isn't modelled

- **US legislative and legal work** (about 7.5% of program spending, under a tenth): defending California's Proposition 12 and Massachusetts' Question 3 in court, opposing the EATS Act, and $300,000 in Animal Policy Alliance grants towards 10 local and state wins. Its cost is left out along with its benefit.
- **THL UK's lobbying for a UK cage ban and its fish work**, about 3% of program spending: no outcome counts for 2025.
- **Broiler (meat chicken) campaigns**, about 8% of program spending, mostly THL UK's Better Chicken Commitment work, and the alliance's 18 broiler policies: no bird counts are published. Their cost is left out with their benefit.
- **Movement building**: training, mentoring and funding new groups in countries without cage-free campaigns, which pays off after 2025.
- **Spillovers**: investigations and media coverage that may change public attitudes or other companies' policies.

### How this model was checked

Built from the charityevals corporate-campaign template. Each audit was run by a separate reviewer who did not see the builder's notes.

- **Round 1**: 2 high, 3 medium and 4 low findings. The accountability branch credited one year's pressure with the whole cumulative effect on a stock of pledges, and its anchor compared two figures from the same data. It now credits a yearly share (a cumulative share owed to pressure ÷ the years a pledge is pressed), with a new anchor. We also lengthened the wait until pressed pledges take effect, rebuilt accountability credit from THL's and its partners' spending, took broiler work out of the cost, and stopped applying the hen-count discount to the alliance branch, whose count is our own guess. The result went from 11 to 4 years of healthy life per $1,000.
- **Round 2**: 1 high, 1 medium and 5 low findings. Four years of pressure per pledge and a three-year wait were inconsistent with THL's own pace of hens leaving cages. The years of pressure now follow from that pace (7.5 million hens in 2025), and the wait is 7 years. We redefined the accountability judgment as the share of pressed pledges that would never be kept without pressure, with a new anchor. New commitments now count only the compliance that comes without later pressure. THL UK's policy and fish work is taken out of the cost, the evidence adjustment is back at the template's 70%, and the share of hens not yet cage-free is 95%. The result went from 4 to 1.3 years of healthy life per $1,000.
- **Round 3**: no high findings; 1 medium and 4 low. Accountability now rests on THL's count of 7.5 million hens spared, so that count gets its own trust discount (70%, 45% to 100%). New pledges' later pressure now applies only to the share of pledged hens that get pressed (70%). We also corrected stale figures in the text. The result went from 1.3 to 1.4 years of healthy life per $1,000. This was the last round, and the fixes after it were not re-audited.
