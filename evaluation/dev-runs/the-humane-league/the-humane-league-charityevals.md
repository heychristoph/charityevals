## Summary

The Humane League (THL) gets food companies to stop buying eggs from caged hens, then holds them to those promises. It works in the US directly and worldwide through the Open Wing Alliance (OWA), a coalition of 82 groups that THL coordinates and funds. This model covers that cage-free work, which we estimate at about 60% of THL's $14.6M program spending in 2025.

Our best guess is **about 6.3 suffering-adjusted days (SADs) averted per dollar you give, roughly 1.6× GiveDirectly** at the site's default exchange rates between animal suffering and human wellbeing. The exchange rate itself is the biggest uncertainty. Among THL-specific inputs, it is how much of the credit belongs to THL rather than the other groups campaigning on the same companies, how many years earlier accountability pressure gets hens out of cages, and what THL's own count of "hens spared" actually measures.

## What the charity does

THL (Rockville, Maryland; 232 employees in 2025; $21.6M total expenses) runs corporate campaigns for farmed chickens: cage-free eggs and, for broiler chickens, its welfare standards for meat chickens (140+ US companies committed, by THL's count). It mobilises volunteers and online activists to pressure companies (1.48M messages to decision-makers in 2025), runs and publicises investigations (in 2025, footage from egg farms in 37 countries), and grants money to OWA member groups abroad ($2M+ to 36 groups in 2025) and to US state-policy groups in its Animal Policy Alliance ($0.3M). It also defends farmed-animal laws such as California's Proposition 12, and gave $2.15M to THL UK.

## How the impact happens

A campaign wins a cage-free commitment, or pushes a company that already has one to switch suppliers. Egg producers then house hens in barns or aviaries instead of cages, sooner than they otherwise would. Each year a hen spends out of a battery cage spares her about 17.5 suffering-adjusted days (Ambitious Impact's estimate, shared by every cage-free model on the site). Hens leaving the EU's and UK's enriched cages gain much less.

In 2025 THL reports 1.6M hens covered by four companies' new commitments, and 7.5M hens "spared from cages around the globe". The second figure isn't defined. THL reported 4.6M for 2024, so 2025 may have added anywhere from 2.9M (if the figure is a running total) to 7.5M (if it is that year's count); we use 6.0M, nearer the annual reading. About four-fifths of the hens counted therefore come from accountability work on promises already made.

## How the model works

The model has two branches with different counterfactuals, combined into the template's rows by weighting each by its hen-years:

- **New commitments** (1.6M hens, range 1.2–2.0M): brought forward 10 years (below Rethink Priorities' movement-wide mean of 15, range 4–36, because the market and state laws already push toward cage-free in 2025), 65% honoured (RP: 48–84%), 65% of hens not cage-free anyway (RP's no-campaign world: 18–26% of US eggs and 20–50% elsewhere cage-free anyway; we assume more today).
- **Accountability** (6.0M hens, range 2.9–7.5M): out of cages 3 years earlier on average (1–6) than without follow-up, counting zero for companies that would have switched on schedule anyway because of deadlines or state laws; 95% of reported switches stick.

Then for both branches:

- **Cost**: program expenses ($14.62M, audited) × the cage-free share (60%, our judgment from the annual report's split by program area) × overhead (1.48: total ÷ program expenses) = $13.0M.
- **Reach**: 90% of counted hens taken as genuinely still caged before the campaign (company volumes that were already partly cage-free, and Subway possibly counted in both branches).
- **Effect**: 15.5 SADs per hen-year, a blend of 85% battery-cage hens (17.5) and 15% enriched-cage hens (about a quarter of that gain, from Welfare Footprint's pain estimates).
- **Adjustments**: evidence 0.85 (welfare in real cage-free barns is worse than ideal aviaries) × counterfactual 0.88 (hen-year weighted) × THL's share of credit 0.4 × follow-through 0.82 (hen-year weighted) = 0.24.
- **Your dollar**: staff costs are 54% of spending; with a 35% pay gap counted as an in-kind donation, your dollar gets about 77% of the credit.

## What drives the result

- **The SAD-to-DALY exchange rate and the extra weight on animal suffering** (shared assumptions) move the result from 0.08× to 4.0× GiveDirectly, more than anything specific to THL. The joint low–high across every input (0.0001× to 209×) is therefore mostly a statement about those shared assumptions.
- **THL's share of credit** (20–65%): 0.8× to 2.6×.
- **Years accountability brings forward** (1–6): 0.8× to 2.7×.
- **What the 7.5M "hens spared" means** (2.9–7.5M new hens): 1.0× to 1.9×.
- **Cage-free share of spending** (45–80%): 2.1× to 1.2×.
- **Years a new commitment brings forward** (4–36): 1.4× to 2.2×.

## How this compares

Before THL's share of credit, the model credits about 1.5 discounted hen-years of cage-free housing per dollar of the full cost. Rethink Priorities' movement-wide estimate for 2005–2018 is 9 to 120 years of chicken life per dollar, about 6–80× more. Most of the gap is deliberate. RP averages the early, cheap years in which most of today's commitments were won, while THL's 2025 spending goes mostly on accountability for promises already made: expensive per hen (mobilisation, investigations, protests) and with smaller counterfactual gains, since cage-free is now 46% of the US flock and several US states require it by law. Our cost also includes all of THL's management and fundraising, and the hens are shared with OWA member groups (the 0.4 attribution comes after this comparison). New commitments are worth more per hen than accountability (10 vs 3 years), and THL's own new commitments in 2025 covered only 1.6M hens.

## Biggest uncertainties

- **THL's hen counts.** The 7.5M "hens spared" and 1.6M from new commitments are THL's own figures, with no published method. The 2024 figure (4.6M) is above OWA's count of hens at companies reporting progress that year (3.3M), so THL's measure is broader than reported company progress. USDA data (the US cage-free flock grew about 14M hens a year between December 2024 and October 2026) show the figures aren't impossible but don't confirm them.
- **What would have happened anyway.** 92% of commitments due by 2024 were fulfilled (OWA; whether by company or by volume isn't stated), several US states require cage-free by law, and the marginal effect of pressure on companies that are already converting is hard to pin down.
- **How the cage-free budget is split from broiler and policy work.** THL doesn't report spending by campaign.

## What isn't modelled

- Broiler chicken campaigns, including much of THL UK's work: neither their cost nor their benefits are in the model.
- Policy and legal work: defending Proposition 12, opposing the EATS Act, Animal Policy Alliance grants.
- OWA members' 234 new commitments in 2025, for which no hen count is published (their cost is partly included through OWA grants, so this understates the benefit).
- Indirect effects: movement building, norms that make future campaigns easier, and investigations' effects beyond the companies targeted.

## How this model was checked

Each round was an independent audit by a separate reviewer with no access to the builder's notes (audit-cea skill), plus the mechanical lint.

- **Round 1: 1 high, 6 medium, 4 low** (1.83× GiveDirectly). The high: the 7.5M "hens spared" was read as hens leaving cages in 2025 without support; THL's 2024 Form 990 reports 4.6M for 2024, so the figure may be a running total. Changes: that input became a 2.9–7.5M judgment; new commitments and accountability were split into two branches with their own years, follow-through and counterfactual (removing a double discount for laws and deadlines); count uncertainty moved out of the evidence adjustment; an enriched-cage blend was added for EU/UK hens; the salary-gap range was aligned with peer models; and the gap with Rethink Priorities' movement-wide estimate is now explained.
- **Round 2: 0 high, 2 medium, 10 low** (1.69×). Changes: the new-commitment branch now uses less than RP's 2005–2018 figures (10 years, 65% counterfactual) because 2025's market already leans cage-free; the hens-spared best guess moved to 6.0M; the new-commitment count got a range; sources and rounding were tidied. The remaining medium is the very wide joint range, which comes from the shared exchange rate between SADs and human wellbeing.

Result after round 2's fixes: 1.61× GiveDirectly.
