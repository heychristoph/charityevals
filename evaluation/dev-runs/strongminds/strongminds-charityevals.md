## Summary

StrongMinds treats depression in Uganda and Zambia, and through partners in Kenya, Malawi and Ethiopia, with six-week group interpersonal therapy (IPT-G) run by lay facilitators. In 2025 it reported **745,224 people treated** for **$11.4 million**, about **$15 a person**. This model, built by charityevals from public sources, gives about **0.035 WELLBYs per dollar you give, about 4.7× GiveDirectly**. The range is very wide (joint low–high corners from well below GiveDirectly to about 180 times it), because the size and the duration of the effect at this scale are uncertain and multiply; varying the inputs together, the middle 80% of outcomes is roughly 2–11× GiveDirectly.

## What the charity does

StrongMinds screens people with the PHQ-9 depression questionnaire and invites those scoring 10 or more to a group of peers that meets for six sessions, led by a trained lay facilitator. Since 2024 most groups run inside government programs: in 2024, 53% of clients were treated through government partners (government-paid volunteer facilitators), 23% by peer facilitators (former clients), 21% through NGO partners and 3% by StrongMinds staff. About a third of 2025 clients were adolescents. The cost per person fell from $40 in 2023 to $23 in 2024 and $15 in 2025, mainly because government volunteers took over delivery; StrongMinds reports $10.55 for the first half of 2026, so a dollar given now may buy treatment about 30% more cheaply than the audited 2025 figure the model uses.

## How the impact happens

Group psychotherapy reduces depressive symptoms; the reduction fades over the following months or years. Depression scores are converted to life satisfaction (WELLBYs) with the shared conversion of 2 WELLBYs per standard-deviation-year, and other household members are credited with a share of the client's gain.

## How the model works

- **Cost**: 2025 program spending ($7.32 million) times the overhead multiplier (total ÷ program expenses, 1.56), from the audited combined statements of StrongMinds, Inc., StrongMinds Uganda and StrongMinds Zambia.
- **Reach**: 745,224 people treated in 2025, StrongMinds' own count.
- **Effect**: 0.60 SD at the end of treatment, the publication-bias-corrected effect of psychotherapy for depression in trials from non-Western countries (Tong et al. 2023), fading linearly over two years (0.3 SD/year): 0.6 SD-years, 1.2 WELLBYs per person, times a household multiplier of 1.6.
- **Adjustments** (combined about 0.30): evidence 0.45, anchored on the one independent RCT of StrongMinds' model (Baird et al. 2024), which implies about 0.3, raised for reasons given in the model; counterfactual 0.97; StrongMinds' share of credit 0.8 (governments and NGO partners pay part of the cost); delivery 0.85 (attendance, a six-session course, administrative counts); funging and leverage 1.
- **Your dollar's share**: staff pay is 63% of costs; we assume a 10% pay gap.

## What drives the result

1. **The WELLBY exchange rate** (shared assumption): 1.6–12× GiveDirectly across its range. Measured in WELLBYs alone, the model is 4.6 times the repo's GiveDirectly wellbeing model.
2. **The initial effect** (0.34–0.87 SD): 1.5–10×. Because duration is effect ÷ decay, the effect enters twice.
3. **The evidence adjustment** (0.25–0.9): 2.6–9×.
4. **How fast it fades** (one to 2.4 years at the central effect): 2.3–5.6×.

## Biggest uncertainties

- **Effect at scale.** StrongMinds' own data show very large gains: PHQ-9 scores fall from 15 to about 3 and stay there at 12 months, and its 2026 pilot RCT found a 7.8-point gap with usual care at three months. But the pilot also showed that symptom reports depend on who is in the room, and the one large independent RCT (Baird et al., adolescent girls, delivered with BRAC) found a small effect: per girl who attended, 0.15 SD at the end of therapy and 0.11 SD at 12 months on depression scores (neither statistically significant), concentrated around the cutoff for minimal depression, and gone by 24 months. A larger StrongMinds RCT is registered (NCT07812311) and would settle much of this.
- **Duration.** No randomised study of the current six-session model has followed clients beyond three months.
- **Credit sharing.** Government and partner contributions are StrongMinds' own early estimates.
- **Adolescents.** A third of clients are adolescents; the independent evidence for them is the weakest.

## What isn't modelled

- Community sensitisation (1.04 million people in 2025) and policy work (Uganda's school mental-health guidelines, the East African Mental Health Summit); their costs are charged to the people treated.
- Effects on income, children's meals and schooling, beyond what is captured in the household spillover.
- Possible harms or null effects for people who did not need treatment (natural remission is already netted out by the trials' control groups).
- Government staff time is not added to the cost; it is handled by sharing credit instead.

## How this model was checked

Two independent audits (audit-cea skill, each in a fresh agent without the builder's notes).

- **Round 1** (1 high, 3 medium, 5 low; 6.1× GiveDirectly). High: the evidence adjustment misread Baird et al.: our 0.19 SD was a conversion of a cutoff indicator, while the paper's per-attender effects on depression scores were 0.15 SD at the end of therapy and 0.11 SD at 12 months. We re-anchored the evidence adjustment (0.6 → 0.45, range 0.25–0.9). Mediums: removed most of the dose discount (the meta-analysis trials averaged about 9 sessions, not 12–16; delivery 0.8 → 0.85), narrowed the decay range to controlled evidence (0.25–0.6), and cited the audited 'Partners' expense line to lower StrongMinds' share of credit (0.85 → 0.8). Lows: fixed the Bass/Bolton citation and the meta-analysis scope, explained the missing time discount, raised the counterfactual to 0.97, and noted the lower 2026 cost.
- **Round 2** (0 high, 2 medium, 4 low; 4.7×; pass). We restated how much the evidence adjustment credits StrongMinds relative to Baird (about 1.2× after the other adjustments), said that decay and evidence adjustment were set jointly, added a percentile band to the summary, updated the funging explainer with the mid-2026 funding gap, and added the missing sources.
