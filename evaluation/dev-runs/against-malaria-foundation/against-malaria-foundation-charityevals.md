### Summary

The Against Malaria Foundation (AMF) buys long-lasting insecticidal nets and funds their monitoring; governments and other partners hand them out in mass campaigns. Our own estimate of its work in the year to June 2025 comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="against-malaria-foundation-charityevals">25</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

More than half of the value is lives saved, mostly of young children: nets cut malaria deaths among the people sleeping under them by over two-fifths in the trials. Nearly two-fifths is children's later earnings, from more schooling. The result rests most on the value of a healthy year against a doubled income, on the schooling gain, on how well nets work today now that mosquitoes in many of AMF's countries resist the standard insecticide, and on how long a net lasts in the household.

### What the charity does

AMF raises money from the public and spends all of it on nets: in its financial year July 2024 to June 2025 (FY2025, the latest with audited accounts) it agreed grants of 75.3 million nets, for the DRC (39%), Nigeria (25%), South Sudan (13%), Chad (11%), Guinea (6%) and Zambia (6%). It spent $178.0 million in all: $172.0 million on nets, $5.0 million on post-distribution monitoring and $1.1 million on its own central costs. A small group of donors pays for monitoring and central costs, so public donations buy nets only.

Co-funding partners (ministries of health, the Global Fund and others) pay to ship the nets, register households and distribute them. AMF then pays local organisations to survey a sample of households every nine months for two and a half years to check that nets are present, hung and in good condition.

### How the impact happens

1. A net protects the people sleeping under it (AMF allocates one net per two people, about 1.8 people per net), from mosquito bites at night. The insecticide also kills mosquitoes, which protects neighbours too.
2. Fewer bites mean fewer malaria infections, fewer cases of severe malaria and fewer deaths, most of them children under five. Malaria also kills indirectly, through anaemia and weakened resistance to other illnesses.
3. Fewer infections in pregnancy mean fewer stillbirths. Children who are less often ill go further in school and earn more as adults.

### How the model works

The model follows the site's direct-health template.

- **Cost**: AMF's whole FY2025 spending, $178.0 million, about $2.36 per net funded. Partners' distribution costs are not part of AMF's cost. Instead they lower AMF's share of the credit (below).
- **Reach**: 75.3 million nets × 1.8 people with access = 136 million people. In AMF's six countries a net stays in the household for 1.59 years on average (survey-based model) and people with a net sleep under it 87% of nights (1.4 years of use); AMF's own monitoring of its distributions finds about 2.1 years of sleeping-space coverage before the next campaign. Weighting the two equally, each person gets about 1.7 years of protection.
- **Deaths**: WHO's 2024 estimate of malaria deaths per person in AMF's six countries, weighted by AMF's nets (0.66 per 1,000 a year), raised to the rate of people without a net (×1.31: today's average already includes the 53% who sleep under one). The rate is cut by 44%, Cochrane's pooled effect on severe malaria, and indirect deaths add the site's shared 0.75 per direct death. As a check, the same chain in the trials' setting gives about 6 child deaths averted per 1,000 a year, close to the trials' all-cause 5.6 (3.6 to 7.6). WHO's estimates of malaria deaths by age in these countries put 76% under five, 5% at 5–14, 8% at 15–49 and 10% at 50 or older; each death is valued at the site's moral weight for its age band.
- **Other benefits**: illness at the site's shared 9% of all modelled value; stillbirths (UN IGME's 25 per 1,000 births), using a 15% cut among women under nets (Gamble et al.'s Cochrane review found a third in early pregnancies); later earnings, from about 0.03 extra years of schooling per child-year protected (anchored on Uganda's 1959–60 eradication campaign) at a 9% return per year of schooling, shared within the household (the site's multiplier of 2), starting 10 years later and lasting 40.
- **Adjustments**:
  - evidence 0.76: the site's 0.95 for the trials' internal validity, times 0.8 because the trials predate insecticide resistance (the newer nets AMF buys win back part of that loss);
  - 75% of the protection would not have happened anyway: some old nets remain, though campaigns count usable nets before allocating new ones;
  - AMF's share of the cost of a delivered net is about 49%: its $2.36 against partners' $2.50;
  - no separate delivery discount: both coverage sources already count nets that never reach households;
  - leverage and funging 0.93: some campaigns would have been funded by others;
  - the waits, discounted: about 1.2 years from a gift to AMF's grant (offset in part by the interest AMF earns on waiting money, which buys more nets), a year from grant to use, and the time to the middle of the nets' use;
  - treatment costs averted add 20%, investment of income gains 2.5% and protection of non-users 5%; rebound from lost immunity takes off 4% and harms 2%.
- **Your dollar's share**: AMF's 14 staff cost under 1% of its spending, so credit by share of resources barely moves the result.

### What drives the result

The biggest swings are the shared value of a healthy year against a doubled income, the schooling gain (our widest judgment), the discount rate, the net's effect on malaria deaths (Cochrane's interval, 18% to 62%), the value of a life, WHO's range for malaria deaths, and the external-validity and counterfactual judgments. AMF's model page shows the full sensitivity chart.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **Nets' effect today.** All the mortality trials ran from 1987 to 2001. Pyrethroid resistance has since spread, and mosquitoes in some places bite earlier or outdoors. Dual-active-ingredient nets restore much of the effect where they have been trialled, but the size of today's mortality effect is not measured directly.
- **How long nets last.** Survey-based models for AMF's countries give 1.59 years on average; AMF's own surveys, collected by its partners, suggest longer. The model weights them equally.
- **Who pays for what.** AMF's share of the credit depends on partners' distribution costs, which AMF puts at $2.50 a net, and studies at $1.50 to $2.
- **Long-run earnings.** These rest on one quasi-experiment, Uganda's 1959–60 campaign, and a general return to schooling, yet make up nearly two-fifths of the value.

### What isn't modelled

- Effects of malaria on the economy beyond the households reached, such as productivity and the burden on clinics, other than treatment costs averted.
- Any change in AMF's country mix after FY2025. FY2026's accounts, still being audited, show far fewer nets agreed ($10.6 million), because grants are booked when agreed and arrive in lumps.
- Funding gaps changing after the 2025 cuts to US malaria funding, other than through the leverage range.

### How this model was checked

Each round's audit was done by a separate reviewer with no access to our notes, using the site's audit checklist. It re-fetched the key sources and ran the mechanical checks.

- **Round 1** (2 high, 4 medium, 5 low). Indirect malaria deaths and resource sharing in households now read the site's shared figures, as the other malaria models do. Malaria illness, rebound from lost immunity, investment of income gains and the trials' internal validity also read shared figures now. The coverage years blend AMF's own monitoring with the cross-country model, and the counterfactual was lowered to match. The stillbirth rate is now UN IGME's. The wait was shortened to AMF's pipeline, with the nets' life discounted too. Leverage no longer double-counts partners' money, and the people-per-net row was relabelled. The age split over five stays a judgment, because IHME's breakdown could not be downloaded. The site's consistency check now also catches indirect malaria deaths and malaria illness typed in by hand.

- **Round 2** (0 high, 3 medium, 5 low). The model passed, and every finding was fixed. Investment of income now uses the site's figure for nets (2.5%, a new shared fact). Malaria illness counts as a share of all modelled value, as in the SMC models. The age split of the deaths now comes from WHO's Global Health Estimates for AMF's six countries (a new shared mix). Retention uses those countries' own fits, converted from median to mean. The wait between a gift and AMF's grant is now discounted. Stale explainers were corrected, and the community effect has an anchor.

- **Round 3** (0 high, 0 medium, 5 low). The model passed. The interest AMF earns on waiting donations now offsets the wait, and the wait is measured over three years rather than one. The delivery discount was set to 1, because both coverage sources already measure lost nets. Stale explainers and the earnings share were corrected, the malaria age-mix facts were renamed as AMF's, and the sources were completed. The new nets investment fact still needs its GiveWell cell reference, which only a non-blind import can add.
