## Drivers

1. `$uov_per_daly`, shared: 6.68–1.67. Not the builder's.
2. `persistence_years`, a judgment: 1.72–4.01. Anchored now: Raising the Village 3 years, graduation follow-ups at 7 and 10 years, Blattman convergence by year 9.
3. `fade_rate`, a judgment: 4.59–2.49. It needs its reference class and the trial's pooled no-fade finding (#2). Persistence and fade together are the result's main uncertainty. The DREAMS second endline (about 2.5 years after the program) is what would most improve the model; when it lands, re-fit both rows.
4. `$uov_per_ln_consumption`, shared: 2.51–4.18.
5. `unmodelled_benefits_adj`, a judgment: 2.75–4.29. Each component is listed with its sign, and wastage is anchored on GiveDirectly's −2%.

After those come `evidence_adj` and `attribution_share_shared` (judgments, anchored), then `core_uganda_itt_month` (observed, verified, SE-based range).

The ranges are real uncertainty: effect ranges come from the reported SEs where they exist, and judgments are anchored. Point values (cost, reach, control means, the TOT/ITT ratios) are audited or measured figures, which is fine.

Peers: the result is 0.48× the cause median, below GiveDirectly (3.96). The drop from round 1 (4.03) comes from the per-country core effect, the refugee effect for Ethiopia and the added fade, not from scope or reach. The gap to Raising the Village (12.6) is still mainly cost per person: about $68 here against about $30 there. This model's persistence is longer, so persistence doesn't explain the gap. No action is needed beyond the findings above.
