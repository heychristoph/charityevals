## Drivers

1. **`life_value_multiplier`** (shared assumption, 1.60–3.23×). This is the visitor's to set, not the builder's.
2. **`evidence_adj`** (judgment, 1.87–2.96×). It is decomposed sensibly (0.9 × 0.9 × 0.92). The reference class ("GiveWell-style … 0.6 to 0.9") is asserted without a citation. The at-scale coverage report (full-vaccination gain about 17–22 points against the trial's 27) is a concrete anchor for the external-validity part, and the explainer should quote it.
3. **`coverage_gain_itt`** (sourced estimate, verified, 1.92–2.91×).
4. **`unvaccinated_risk_multiplier`** (judgment with worked reasoning, 2.05–2.86×). Too high as a single value. See finding 1.
5. **`counterfactual_adj`** (judgment with no reference class, 1.94–2.66×). See finding 6.

Next are `enrolled_share_of_births` (judgment anchored on survey coverage; finding 8) and `deaths_pneumo_hib` (sourced; finding 7).

What would most improve the model:
- Make the deaths-averted-per-birth chain cause-specific, with the coverage at the time each death rate was measured, age-at-death eligibility and mortality trend (findings 1–3). This is about −19% combined, and it is where most of the remaining bias sits.
- Anchor the counterfactual on NDHS catch-up data (finding 6).
- Resolve the cost basis with the Note 8 CCT commitments (finding 4).

Comparability: 2.42× sits between Helen Keller VAS (1.82×) and AMF/SMC (5.7×), well within 10× of peers. There is no other charityevals direct-health model of a vaccination incentive program to compare rows with, since the New Incentives peers are excluded for the blind test. Shared numbers are used correctly (`@moral_weights.uov_per_death_under5`, `$life_value_multiplier`, `$full_credit`, `$count_salary_sacrifice`), except for the typed-in cash valuation (finding 5). The joint range (0.40–15× with shared assumptions at their defaults) reflects many multiplied judgment ranges, each with a stated reason. It is wide but not arbitrary.
