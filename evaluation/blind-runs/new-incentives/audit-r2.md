## Drivers

1. **`life_value_multiplier`** (shared assumption, 1.26–2.47×). This is the visitor's to set.
2. **`evidence_adj`** (judgment, 1.46–2.27×). It is decomposed into three parts and now quotes the at-scale pre-post replication. The reference class should cite the repo peers (finding 9).
3. **`coverage_gain_itt`** (sourced, verified, 1.54–2.31×). The death-weighted 19 points is well reasoned. The at-scale data support it.
4. **`counterfactual_adj`** (judgment, 1.53–2.04×). This is the weakest anchored of the top drivers. Whether campaign doses count toward the trial's measles outcome decides whether 0.9 is right (finding 1).
5. **`deaths_pneumo_hib`** (sourced estimate, 1.64–2.09×). The value is sound, but the explainer's comparison with the Gambian trial needs fixing (finding 3).

Next are `enrolled_share_of_births` (judgment with a cross-check), `gov_cost_per_extra_vaccinated_child` (judgment) and `leverage_funging_adj` (finding 2).

What would most improve the model:
- Settle whether the trial's Measles 1 outcome includes campaign doses (finding 1).
- Give funging and runway timing a real range (finding 2).
- Cite the NDHS 2023–24 Table 15 coverages behind the per-cause multipliers (finding 4).

Comparability: 1.87× sits between Helen Keller VAS (1.82×) and AMF/SMC (5.7×), well within 10× of peers. VAS is the nearest peer and is within 3%. Shared numbers are used correctly: `@moral_weights.uov_per_death_under5` (116.25), `$life_value_multiplier`, `$uov_per_ln_consumption` (now including the cash branch), `$full_credit` and `$count_salary_sacrifice`. The joint range (0.31–11.2× with shared assumptions at their defaults) comes from many multiplied judgment ranges, each with a stated reason. It is wide but not arbitrary.
