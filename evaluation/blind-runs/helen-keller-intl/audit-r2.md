## Drivers

1. **mortality_reduction** (estimate, sourced: Cochrane FE 12%, 7–24%): 2.1× to 7.4×. Verified in R1 and now combined consistently with evidence_adj.
2. **full_cost_per_dose** (judgment, $0.75–2.00): 5.4× to 2.0×. Because spending, reach and the delivery adjustment cancel, this is now the model's real cost input. It rests on one Helen Keller co-authored Burkina Faso campaign costing (2022 USD), Baker's Côte d'Ivoire routine costs and Helen Keller's own "a little more than $1 a dose". It needs a price-year adjustment (finding 3), and the write-up should name it as the main cost uncertainty (finding 4).
3. **life_value_multiplier** (shared assumption, 0.6–1.4): 2.2× to 5.2×. This is the visitor's setting.
4. **full_credit** (accounting toggle): 3.7× → 7.2×.
5. **evidence_adj** (judgment, 0.55–1.0): 2.4× to 4.3×. It now has a subgroup reference class.
6. **counterfactual_adj** (judgment, 0.5–0.9): 2.6× to 4.7×. Its reference class supports a lower best guess (finding 1).

Comparability: at 3.68× the model sits between New Incentives (1.03×) and AMF/SMC (about 5.7×) in the uov reference class, well within 10× of its peers. No charityevals-built peer of the same family exists for a canonical-row comparison. The shared numbers are shared: moral weights from `@moral_weights`, `$life_value_multiplier`, `$uov_per_daly`, and mortality from the `@<country>` facts, all of which were re-verified. No inline moral weights were found.

What would most improve the model: a per-dose full cost for Helen Keller's largest VAS countries (DRC, Niger, Nigeria) in current prices, and a counterfactual grounded in coverage data from countries where external campaign support ended. With findings 1–3 applied as suggested (counterfactual 0.6, overhead in both sides of the share, full cost in FY2025 prices), the best guess would be about 3.68 × 0.86 × 1.09 × 0.92 ≈ 3.2×.
