# Audit: shrimp-welfare-project-charityevals, round 1

Result: 5.070 ced/$ = 1.851 years of healthy life per $1,000 (joint 5.6e-7 – 1.6e5; model's own ranges 0.10 – 11.1); cause median 8.719 (×0.21). Verdict: **fix needed** (one high finding, a rule breach with a small effect).

Scope: the Humane Slaughter Initiative (stunners bought and lent to producers), year to 31 March 2025, the latest year with audited accounts on the Charity Commission register (the 2025 and 2024 accounts are the only two filed). The accounts give no programme split (note 5 is by cost type only); the model takes the stunners at their price (fixed-asset additions £388,162) plus a 55% (40–70%) judgment of direct running costs, about 77% of programme spending on a cash basis (£617,148 of £804,501). Retailer engagement, the India farmer programme (SSFI) and research/communications are left out under Convention 12 and the summary says so. Reach is from the same period (the eleven agreements entered in the charity's Guesstimate model from April 2024 to March 2025). Scope is acceptable.

Plausibility: the model implies about 1,670 shrimp committed a year per dollar of `cost_total`, close to the charity's own (Shr)Impact figure of 1,400 (970–1,900) per dollar a year. Its Guesstimate also adds overheads ($2.38M) to stunner prices, a cost multiple near the model's ×1.77 on the stunner price. The result is 0.21× the animal-welfare median and 2.2× the nearest peer, Aquatic Life Institute (0.85; retailer stunning commitments, which work less directly). The gap is explained by the direct, equipment-backed commitments, not by a different counting rule. The model's own range (0.10 – 11) is a real spread, not a reflexive ±20%.
