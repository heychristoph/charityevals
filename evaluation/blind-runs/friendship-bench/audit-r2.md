## Structure and other checks

- **Template:** wellbeing is the right family. The chain cost → reach → effect → adjustments → outcome → donor is intact.
  - The new reach rows (`partner_clients`, `reach_count_adj`) feed `people_reached`.
  - `program_share_of_total` still cancels out of `cost_total`, as stated.
- **Double counting:** none found.
  - Partner clients are removed from reach, and partner training is removed from cost (`bench_share_of_program`).
  - `reach_count_adj` and the session shares don't overlap: the quarterly tables count clients seen and follow-up sessions separately, so "clients seen" are first contacts.
  - Publication bias is corrected once, through the prior.
- **Units and period:** 2024 reach is set against $4.0M, the 2024–25 average of a budget request. Reach grew 14% from 2024 to 2025. If spending grew with it, 2024 spending was below $4.0M and the cost per client is slightly overstated, which makes the model conservative. The `total_expenses` range covers this.
- **Shared numbers:** WELLBY conversion, household size and spillover are `@facts`; the units of value are the shared `uov_per_wellby`. Nothing is retyped.
- **Comparability:** 16.6× sits above against-malaria-foundation-hli (9.96×) and givedirectly-hli (1.01×) in the WELLBY reference class, within 10× of peers. With findings 1 and 2 applied, the result is about 13.6×.
- **Joint range:** 0.37–256× with shared assumptions at their defaults. Wide, but driven by stacked, explained judgments rather than reflexive ±20%. `evidence_adj` and `attribution_share_shared` share the same 0.6/0.8/1 range, each with its own rationale. The one judgment with no range is the 50/50 trial average (finding 2).

## Drivers

1. **`uov_per_wellby`** (shared assumption, 0.4–3 → 5.8–43.4×). The visitor's to set.
2. **`effect_duration_years`** (judgment anchored on Cuijpers 2026, verified; 2–8 → 6.7–26.6×). It is now consistent with its source. What remains unsupported is the reason for discounting the plateau (more relapse in poor LMIC households). A long follow-up of any LMIC task-shared psychotherapy trial would be the best improvement.
3. **`@wellby_factors.household_spillover_ratio_psychotherapy`** (shared estimate, 12.3–22.0×).
4. **`evidence_adj`** and **`attribution_share_shared`** (judgments, each 12.5–20.8×). `evidence_adj` is now anchored (finding 3 refines it). `attribution_share_shared` rests on the Kidia allowance figures, which are verified, plus an unsourced estimate that government in-kind costs are a quarter of Friendship Bench's spending.
5. **`@wellby_factors.wellbys_per_sd_year`** (shared, 12.5–20.8×) and **`total_expenses`** (judgment bracketed by verified anchors, 13.6–21.5×).

Hidden driver: the fixed 50/50 average of the two trials (finding 2) would swing about −23% to +0% if it were given a range. It belongs in the tornado.

What would most improve the model:

- **Sessions per client:** ask Friendship Bench for its session-per-client distribution and the definition of a follow-up session. Its quarterly and annual figures disagree by a factor of three or more.
- **Costs:** get the audited Zimbabwe accounts (Vista Chartered Accountants), or Friendship Bench Inc's Schedule F.
- **Trial weighting:** state an explicit, relevance-based weighting between the urban and rural trials.
