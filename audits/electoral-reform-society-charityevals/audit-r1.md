## Drivers
1. `$win_years_counted` (shared slider, judgment): 0.015–0.168.
2. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider): 0.013–0.130. Every branch is thin, which is correct: every gain share and chance is judged.
3. `@next_dollar.electoral_reform_society` (estimate built from judgments): 0.030–0.153. Its room factor is out of line with the reference class (finding 3).
4. `@democracy.democratization_gdp_gain` (sourced: Acemoglu et al. 2019, 21.24% ± 7.21%): 0.022–0.108.
5. `@democracy.pr_gain_share` (shared judgment, 1%–15%) and `pr_p_win` (judgment, 2%–25%): 0.037–0.135 and 0.038–0.140.

What would most improve the model: a sourced figure for the UK proportional-representation movement's money (finding 9), which sets the Society's credit on half the result; the room judgment set against the reserve-rich peers (finding 3); and the supplementary-vote reach after the PCC abolition (finding 2). The result is at the global-health median of hand-built models, mostly from one forward chance (proportional representation within ten years) and four modest past wins. Against the US voting-reform peers (FairVote 0.023, Common Cause 0.023), the gap comes from the PR branch: a national legislature for 69 million people, against city councils covering about 100,000 a year. The summary already says this.
