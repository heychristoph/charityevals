## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, 0.5). The model is correctly thin: reach is counted, but the consumption value rests on a judged goods share and the health effect is judged. It matches the four humanitarian-relief peers.
2. `$uov_per_daly` and `$uov_per_ln_consumption` (shared): about 97% of the value is the goods as consumption.
3. `affected_consumption_share` (judgment 0.42, the same row and anchor as the American Red Cross relief model: the poorest 40%'s mean from the World Bank's Poverty and Inequality Platform). Together with the stand-ins (finding 3), this sets the baseline. Per-country facts for the largest settings (Cameroon, Yemen, Chad, Syria) would improve the model most.
4. `counterfactual_adj` (judgment 0.7, anchored on 2025 shelter-cluster funding of 13–27% from OCHA FTS): well sourced.
5. `goods_share` (judgment 0.65, anchored on IFRC sector spending in the Red Cross model): ShelterBox publishes no cost split for its responses. A partner's budget breakdown (Bahar Relief, Benevolence Coalition) or ShelterBox's own response cost reports would turn this judgment into a measured figure.

Cost ($120 a person, all costs) is close to 2024's (£22.6m for 277,820 people). The result is 2.7× the cause median and between the American Red Cross international relief model (0.073) and the MSF relief model (0.70); no INV-15 reason is needed. Fixing findings 2 and 3 together would put it near 0.22.
