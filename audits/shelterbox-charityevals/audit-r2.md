1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, 0.5). The model is correctly thin: reach is counted, but the value per person rests on a judged goods share and the health effect is judged.
2. `$uov_per_daly` and `$uov_per_ln_consumption` (shared). About 97% of the value is the goods counted as consumption; averted deaths are most of the rest.
3. `affected_consumption_share` (judgment 0.42, anchored on the World Bank PIP bottom-40% means, the same row as the Red Cross relief model). Together with the two stand-ins (finding 2), it sets the baseline.
4. `counterfactual_adj` (judgment 0.7, anchored on 2025 shelter-cluster funding of 13–27% in OCHA FTS): well sourced.
5. `goods_share` (judgment 0.65, anchored on IFRC sector spending). A partner budget breakdown or ShelterBox's own response cost reports would turn this into a measured figure and improve the model most.
