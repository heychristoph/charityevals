## Drivers
1. `assault_rr` (estimate, the builder's own random-effects pool): the largest swing, and it alone can flip the sign. It should be the year-1 pool (finding 1), its trials cited row by row (finding 3).
2. `years_of_coverage` (judgment, 1 year, 0.5–2): anchored on the trials' follow-up windows and Sarnquist's year 2. Reasonable once Sarnquist is used for year 2 only.
3. `assault_rate` (judgment, 8%): anchored on the trial populations only. National VACS rates for the countries taught would improve it most (finding 5).
4. `internal_validity_adj` (judgment, 0.7): should carry the Sarnquist evidence on demand effects, with a range down to near zero (finding 1).
5. `ptsd_years_per_assault` (filed as estimate, really a judgment, 1.6): takes the high end of the source's 12-month prevalence from help-seeking samples (finding 2).

What would most improve the model: a controlled trial of the course as partners now deliver it, with independent, linked data collection and 12-month outcomes, which is what separates "fades" from "demand effects"; partners' delivery costs; and NMNW's split of 2025's reach by course version.

Peers: at 0.14 years per $1,000 the model sits at 2.0× the global-health median (0.069), well within the 10× band. No gap to explain.
