## Double counting with the advocacy model

There is no double counting. Costs are disjoint: $221.52m plus $48.875m equals programme cash of $270.395m, both read the same overhead ratio, and the line sums in charities/feeding-america.json match. The mechanisms are distinct too. This model counts enrolments the network's application help causes in the existing SNAP programme, and the advocacy model counts federal money won or kept (Summer EBT, the smaller SNAP cut), valued per person-year of benefits. One person-year can't be both an assisted new enrolment and a year that exists only because of a won reform, except at the margin where the reforms change eligibility, which is negligible. Research (Map the Meal Gap) is counted only in the advocacy model. The one inconsistency between the two is how they value SNAP (finding 3), not a double count.

## Drivers

1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, ×2 swing either way). Both branches are rightly thin: food has an audited reach but no measured effect, and SNAP's effect comes from another delivery of outreach.
2. `$uov_per_daly` (shared).
3. `food_counterfactual_adj` (judgment, 0.25–0.8, anchored on the Salvation Army's food). A reference class would help most here: the destinations of surplus food that is not donated (ReFED data) for the donor side.
4. `recipients_consumption_share` (judgment, 0.45–0.75): finding 1. With it at the evidence's level, this is the biggest single correction.
5. `snap_attribution_share` (judgment, 0.1–0.5): finding 7. Deriving it from cost per assisted application would make it sourced.

What would most improve the model: one shared, sourced consumption level for US food-pantry clients and SNAP households (findings 1 and 3), and SNAP credit computed from money (finding 7). Finding 1 alone roughly doubles the result. Findings 1, 4, 5 and 10 together, in scratch, give 0.0163.
