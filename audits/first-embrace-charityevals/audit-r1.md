## Drivers
1. `@newborn_care.kmc_mortality_rr`, estimate (Cochrane 2026, verified): 0.21–7.08 across its 95% CI. Its range is real; what would narrow it for this programme is the dose-specific mortality subgroup in the 2026 review's full text, and First Embrace's planned stepped-wedge trial.
2. `baseline_mortality_rate`, estimate (the charity's own 71-newborn cohort): 2.41–6.51; the best guess should move to the loss-adjusted 0.22 (finding 1). A longer registered baseline at the new hospitals, with lost newborns traced, would settle it.
3. `$daly_per_child_death`, the shared slider: 2.45–5.70.
4. `external_validity_adj`, judgment 0.9 (0.7–1.1), anchored on trial dose against First Embrace's dose and on the immediate-KMC trial: 3.17–4.98.
5. `implementation_adj`, estimate (charity monitoring, ≥2 h/day share): 3.22–4.83.

Peers (for the audit only, never site text): ×34.9 the cause median. The gap comes from the programme, not a counting rule. A direct mortality intervention at a 17% 28-day risk costs about $198 a newborn: about 0.075 deaths averted per $1,000 after adjustments, at about 54.6 healthy years each (84 units of value × 1.3 for illness and development, ÷ 2). That is the same order as other direct child-mortality programmes in the cause. The median is pulled down by large multi-sector and rich-country charities. Scope, reach and persistence are checked: one year's flow against one year's cost, nothing carried over.
