## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, every branch thin, correctly: no outcome measured anywhere).
2. `$uov_per_daly` (shared).
3. `abroad_recipient_share` (judgment, 0.6, anchored on Samaritan's Purse's shoebox recipients) together with the stand-in mean it scales (findings 1 and 3). The poverty base of the children fed is the single biggest open question.
4. `liv_consumption_gain` (judgment, 1.5%, World Vision's livelihoods anchor) and its persistence (finding 5).
5. `feed_gik_share`, `food_equivalence`, `feed_counterfactual_adj` (judgments; the food shares across branches reconcile with note J, which supports `feed_gik_share`).

Most improvement would come from three things: a country split of the children fed (from Convoy, or its Children's Feeding pages), so the base is a weighted mean of the actual countries; sourced food price levels (finding 3); and the mortality step (finding 2). Against the same template's models, the result sits within their range (0.04–0.70 healthy years per $1,000). The low credit shares (6% partner resourcing, 8% events) and the US goods' small log gains explain why the large US goods volume adds little. That is the site's counting rule, not an error.

Verdict: pass (no high findings; findings 1–5 are medium and should be fixed in the next round)
