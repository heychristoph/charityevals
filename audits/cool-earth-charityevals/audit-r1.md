## Drivers
1. `missing_data_discount`: the shared slider (0.5), applied to both branches. It is a shared assumption, and both branches being thin is correct as long as the cash is estimated rather than counted.
2. `uov_per_daly`, `uov_per_ln_consumption`: shared assumptions.
3. `liv_cash_gbp` (a point input, not in the tornado): it scales 93% of the result, and its value is wrong as cash (finding 1). This is the fix that would most improve the model. Cool Earth's own figure for cash disbursed would settle it.
4. `liv_consumption_share`: a judgment of 35% (20–60%), anchored on the poorest fifth's roughly 40% of the survey mean. A reference class is named. Papua New Guinea is folded into Peru's mean. A Papua New Guinea survey mean in facts/survey_consumption.json, weighted by note 8's split, would ground it better.
5. `liv_evidence_adj`, `liv_implementation_adj`: judgments, each 0.9 with ranges.

Plausibility: the result is 174× the climate median (3 models) and 477× The Nature Conservancy's. The gap comes from the cash branch, and the summary states that reason (INV-15 passes). After finding 1 the result is about 0.10, still far above the land-carbon peers for the same stated reason. The forest branch alone is about 0.011 per $1,000, roughly $58 per tonne of credited CO₂e against TNC's roughly $5,300. That is within reason for a cash-for-forest programme on active frontiers, given the loss-rate and counterfactual judgments.
