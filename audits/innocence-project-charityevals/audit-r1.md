## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, 0.1–1): every branch is thin. Correctly so, but at 1 the result is +40%.
2. `@next_dollar.innocence_project` (estimate, 14–72%): sourced and verified. Room 0.4 rests on 3.4 years of spending in reserve.
3. `@incarceration.life_years_lost_per_year_incarcerated` (shared estimate, 0.5–3): deaths are most of the result, so this one figure drives it.
4. `$win_years_counted` (shared slider, 1–20), on the reform branch.
5. `pol_reduction_share` and `pol_innocent_share` (judgments): their product, the wrongful prison-years the window's reforms avert, is the model's own biggest uncertainty (finding 3).

The result is about a hundredth of the global-health median and an eighth to a ninth of the other two US prison models (Prison Policy Initiative 0.060, Brennan Center 0.073). The gap comes from a cost of about $1.3M per adjusted prison-year averted, which the summary states (INV-15 reason present). What would most improve the model: the audited statements' split of the $31M of pro bono services (finding 1), and a per-reform decomposition of the reform branch from the National Registry's contributing-factor data (finding 3).

Verdict: fix needed (one high, three medium, nine low)
