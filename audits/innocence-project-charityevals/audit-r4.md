## Findings

1. [OK] ex_years_inside = min(released_years, ex_remaining_life / (1 + ex_life_years_lost)). Each year inside costs ex_life_years_lost of life, so the person dies inside after L/(1+y) years: the formula is the right survival cap. It feeds released_value (the WELLBY annuity) and ex_death_uov_per_freed, and nowhere else; ex_deaths_per_prison_year is gone from the model, writeups and src. net_wellbys and net_uov read the same rows, so the Network branch follows.
2. [OK] ex_deaths_per_freed = y x years_inside / L; with the cap it is at most y/(1+y), so below one death. Ends tested (rows overridden in a scratch run): L 21, y 1.2, R 25 gives 9.5 years inside and 0.545 of a death; L 21, y 1.2, R 5 gives 5 years (cap inactive) and 0.286; L 31, y 0.2, R 25 gives 25 years and 0.161; L 31, y 1.2, R 25 gives 14.1 years and 0.545; best case (L 26, y 0.5, R 12) gives 12 years and 0.231. Nothing exceeds the life left, the cap binds only at long terms with high loss, and the result is monotonic in each input.
2b. [OK] No double counting: the death value is the share of a death spread over years_inside and discounted with pv_annuity_due over the same years_inside (divided by it, so the average per year); the life satisfaction branch uses the same years; compensation and executions are separate rows. The capped years are used for both, so the two channels are consistent.
