## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider): every branch is thin, correctly; at 1 the result doubles.
2. `@next_dollar.innocence_project` (estimate, 14–72%): sourced and verified.
3. `@incarceration.life_years_lost_per_year_incarcerated` (shared estimate, 0.5–3). Deaths are about 95% of every branch's value, so this one figure carries the result. For the exoneration and Network branches it is applied well outside the time served it was measured on (finding 1).
4. `$win_years_counted` (shared slider), on the reform branch.
5. `pol_innocent_share` (judgment, 0.5–5%, three anchors), then `pol_coalition_share` and `pol_evidence_adj` (judgments) on the reform branch, now 57% of the result.

The result is about a hundredth of the global-health median and of the other two US prison models (Prison Policy Initiative 0.063, Brennan Center 0.070). The summary states the reason: about two million dollars of casework per exoneration, about 1% of wrongful imprisonment removed by the window's reforms, and a next dollar at a third of the average. What would most improve the model: a long-term mortality figure for people who serve decades (finding 1), and the audited statements' functional split of the pro bono and of the "other programmes" line (finding 2).

Verdict: fix needed (one high, two medium, six low)
