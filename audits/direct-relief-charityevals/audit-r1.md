## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (the shared slider, 0.5): the site's own setting, not the builder's.
2. `threshold_share_gdp` (judgment, 0.3; 0.12–0.6): anchored on Woods 2016 and Ochalek 2018, which I verified. A pooled share is reasonable, but it must be applied per country harmonically (finding 1). If the share rises with income, as in Woods, the poorer recipients' cost per DALY is lower still.
3. `need_vs_marginal` (judgment, 1; 0.5–2.5): has no reference class, and the sibling model uses 1.5 (finding 6).
4. `counterfactual_adj` (judgment, 0.6): partly counts the threshold's substitution case twice (finding 2).
5. `other_gdp_pc` (judgment, $3,500): inconsistent with the model's own beneficiary split and aggregated the wrong way (finding 1).

The source figures are all verified, and every cost and reach number reproduces from the audited accounts. What would improve the model most is fixing the GDP aggregation (finding 1). After that, give `need_vs_marginal` a reference class, such as stock-out data for the recipient facilities. The price ratio and the donated goods' value barely move the result: under share-of-resources credit, Direct Relief's cash is credited at about the recipients' marginal cost per DALY whatever the goods are worth.

Verdict: **fix needed** (1 high, 3 medium, 3 low).
