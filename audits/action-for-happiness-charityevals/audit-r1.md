## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared, 0.037–0.37): applies to every branch. Thin is right for all three: the course trial is of the eight-week in-person predecessor and course size is judged; the groups and the digital programme have no data.
2. `digital_effect_duration_years`, judgment 0.75 y (0.3–2; result 0.084–0.45). Anchored on Bolier's 3–6-month follow-up (65% of the effect left), shortened from the 1.1 y a linear fade implies. Half the follow-up trials are self-help, so the shortening is a mild extra discount. 1.07 y would give 0.253.
3. `digital_implementation_adj`, judgment 15% (5–30%; 0.072–0.35). Anchored on 25,000 10 Days starters out of 187,000. The model would improve most from the charity's own data on 10 Days completion and monthly active app users.
4. `digital_evidence_adj`, judgment 0.55 (0.3–0.85), anchored on White et al.'s small-study correction.
5. `digital_initial_effect_sd`, estimate 0.33 (Bolier self-help subgroup); and `$daly_per_wellby` (shared).

Peers: 0.183 is 2.5× the global-health median, within 10×. Among the hand-built WELLBY models it is far above the three UK and US service charities (about 0.0016–0.0026). The row that explains the gap is cost per person reached ($3.8, against thousands of dollars per therapy client), from the digital branch's mass reach. With 15% of the count judged to get a programme, that is about $13 per programme user. Persistence and the share of users were checked: both are anchored and ranged. The evidence supports the gap given those judgments, so it is not a finding (and stays out of site text).

What would most improve the model: the charity's own counts of 10 Days completers and regular app users (replacing the 15% judgment), and any follow-up of Happiness Habits participants beyond the course.
