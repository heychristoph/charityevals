## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider): every branch is thin, correctly, since nobody counts reach.
2. `$uov_per_daly` (shared).
3. `pro_doc_gain` (judgment, 1.5%). Its anchor is a different intervention (finding 2). This branch most needs a reference class.
4. `pro_doc_cost_per_person` (judgment, $25). It has no cited cost study and leaves UNHCR's support costs unclear (finding 1).
5. `baseline_consumption` (judgment, $900). It should be built from survey consumption (finding 3).

What would most improve the model: putting UNHCR's support costs into every unit cost (finding 1), and rebuilding the documents branch as a share of people who gain rights × a sourced effect of gaining them (finding 2). Together they decide a third of the result.

Other checks:
- No double counting found between relief and health deaths: different spending, and each removes a share of a different mortality rate.
- The documents branch excludes access to UNHCR's own aid.
- Resettlement is plausible. The model's UNHCR-wide 42,600 departures sit near the audited 37,237. The log gain is credited at 0.3 × 0.2. Its discounting over the shared working years is right.
- The solutions share is the audited figure.
- `estimate_basis` `unit_cost` fits (reach is spending over judged unit costs; no next-dollar slider).
- Side effects are rows tagged `side_effect`.
- The result sits at the global-health median, beside Save the Children's and World Vision's models in the same family.
