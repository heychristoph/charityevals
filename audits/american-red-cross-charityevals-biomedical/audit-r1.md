Peers. The gap below the cause median comes from rows the evidence supports, not from a different scope or counting rule. Only 15% of the outcome is counted as not happening anyway (`bld_counterfactual_adj`). The Red Cross's share of the resources is 0.24. Both branches take the missing-data step (0.5). A US blood product costs about $388 all in. Deaths are valued at old recipients' ages (`bld_value_per_death` 55 uov, then × 0.65 or 0.4 for survivors' shorter remaining life), against about 116 for a child's death. Before adjustments, 6.4M products × 0.0143 = 91,000 deaths averted a year. That implies the whole US supply (14.4M components) averts about 205,000 deaths a year, roughly 7% of US deaths. That is high-ish but within the judgments' ranges, so I note it here and don't file it as a finding. Findings 1 and 2 would together raise the result by about 70%, still far below the median.

## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, 0.0017–0.017): read as is.
2. `bld_counterfactual_adj` (judgment, 0.0028–0.022): the largest question the builder owns. It needs its parts as rows (finding 3).
3. `bld_death_risk_without` (judgment, 0.0035–0.016): anchored on Carson 2002 (verified). It would improve most with a cohort of massive-haemorrhage or trauma patients when blood ran short.
4. `bld_products_per_acute_patient` (judgment, 0.012–0.0052): no US survey of components per bleeding patient. A trauma or massive-transfusion registry mean (red cells + plasma + platelets per transfused bleeding patient) would anchor it.
5. `bld_acute_share` (judgment, 0.0055–0.011): NBCUS Table 3 supports it (finding 5).

Not in the tornado's top 5, but wrong in a way that moves the result more than 25%: `bld_abc_cost_multiple` (finding 1) and `bld_internal_validity_adj` (finding 2). Fixing both raises the result to about 0.014. What would most improve the model is a per-component cost split of the hospital side, plus a stated counterfactual arithmetic.

Verdict: fix needed (2 high: findings 1 and 2).
