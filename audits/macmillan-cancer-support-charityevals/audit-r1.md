## Double counting

- Support Line: `line_people` (nurses plus information advisers, 73,000) leaves out the welfare rights team, whose 34,000 get only the money branch. There is no overlap.
- `light_people` = 476,000 − 90,000 − 3,800 subtracts "people affected by cancer" (including family) from "people living with cancer", so it slightly overstates the remainder. That is small (the light branch is about 4% of the result).
- The taxpayers' cost is netted once, with the same `wr_total_adj` as the gain. Correct.
- The unmodelled share (+10%) multiplies the net total, including the negative taxpayer term. That is acceptable.

## Drivers

1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider): every branch moves one for one with it (0.00046–0.0046).
2. `recipients_consumption_share` (judgment, US anchor): 8.8e-4 – 0.0046 (finding 3).
3. `$uov_per_daly` (shared): 0.0014–0.0042.
4. `income_years` (judgment, no reference class beyond "while the illness lasts"): 0.0015–0.0040. Anchor it on the DWP's average award duration for PIP or Attendance Allowance claimants with cancer, or on the share of fast-track (special rules) claims.
5. `wr_counterfactual_adj` (judgment): 0.0014–0.0032. `side_effect_weight` swings 0.0023–0.0050 only because of finding 1.

What would most improve the model: value the taxpayers' cost the site's one way (finding 1), anchor the claimants' consumption and the benefit years on UK data, and pin down what Macmillan's "£112m potential" means (finding 2). Together these settle about three quarters of the result.

Verdict: fail. Findings: 1 high, 5 medium, 3 low.
