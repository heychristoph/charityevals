## Drivers
1. `bio_scale_adj` (judgment 0.08, 0.02–0.4; 0.17–3.4): the step that puts CEPI below its peers. Its anchor is CEPI's own Imperial College figure. Its other inputs are unranged literals and its best guess is off its derivation (finding 1). This is what most needs improving. A sourced estimate of the 100 Days Mission's funding (CEPI 3.0's $3.6B is the sourced part) and of its chance of being met (CEPI's or the IPPS's progress reports) would turn the top driver from a judgment into a calculation.
2. `$discount_rate` (shared slider; 10.2 at 0.1%, 0.53 at 8%).
3. `$win_years_counted` (shared slider; 0.15–2.1).
4. `@catastrophe.bio_extinction_cut_per_million` and `bio_catastrophe_cut_per_million` (shared estimates, Rethink's typical small project ×10 either way; 0.35–4.0 and 0.41–3.5). Finding 1's formula removes most of the catastrophe figure's pull.
5. `$missing_data_discount` (shared; every branch thin, correctly), then `@next_dollar.cepi` (estimate, 0.28–1.2; finding 2), `bio_counterfactual_adj` (0.7, the template's) and `bio_attribution_share` (0.7, anchored against Blueprint's 0.85).

Peers: 0.43× the cause median, below SecureBio (6.1), Blueprint Biosecurity (5.9) and the Johns Hopkins Center for Health Security (7.5). The write-up and summary explain the gap: the same typical pandemic cut, then the size step. Before that step the result would be about 8.6. Deaths of people alive now are 90% of the value and the future 10%; the endemic branch adds under 1% (every endemic input moves the result by under 0.01). After findings 1 and 2 the result would be about 0.52 (×0.33 of the median), still explained by the size step.
