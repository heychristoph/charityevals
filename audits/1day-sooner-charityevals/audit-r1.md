## Drivers
1. `$discount_rate` (shared slider): 72.5 at 0.1%, 4.5 at 8%.
2. `@catastrophe.bio_catastrophe_cut_per_million` and `bio_extinction_cut_per_million` (shared estimates, ×10 either way): 3.1–31.6 and 3.9–23.6. They drive both the pandemic branch and, through its calibration, the policy win.

4. `@next_dollar.one_day_sooner` (estimate, sourced; finding 4 is on its note): 2.4–9.9.
5. `pol_win_multiple` (judgment with no reference class, finding 3): 4.8–10.5. Next are `bio_evidence_adj` (judgment anchored on the Johns Hopkins Center and Blueprint) and `pol_coalition_spend` (judgment).

The cost shares that split the 990's one programme line (hepatitis C 27%, malaria 26%, policy 4%, with pandemic work the remainder) are judgments from the proposal. Schedule F's $77,767 of advocacy contractors in sub-Saharan Africa is a floor for malaria, not a split. What would most improve the model is 1Day's own 2024 spending or staff time by programme area, from the unredacted Appendix C budget or from the charity on request. That would turn the four cost shares from judgments into observations and settle how much of the money is valued as pandemic work. Next would be an independent account of SB 1308's withdrawal, which would anchor the policy branch's counterfactual and multiple.

Verdict: fix needed (two high: the malaria branch's displaced spending is a typed judgment in place of `@givewell_shared.cf_value_gavi`, and the policy win is calibrated on one size's cut, with its COVID-scale deaths valued at the world's age mix).
