Result: 1.156e-5 daly/$ = 0.0116 years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars (combined range 0.0010–0.127); cause median 0.1085 (×0.107). Verdict: pass.

Scope: all programme spending of the US United Way network, fiscal year to June 2025 (Forbes: charitable services $2.9B of $3.5B total expenses, verified). It is split into five branches that sum to `program_spend`: basic-needs grants $420M (15%), other grants $1.26B (43%), own programmes $1.05B (36%, half of it valued as services and half as the cost of grantmaking and convening), 211 $125M (4%) and tax help $38M (1%). The grant and staff shares are anchored on Greater Houston (FY to March 2024) and Greater Twin Cities (2024); both sets of statements were verified line by line. No programme is missing. In charities/united-way.json, `spending` ($2.9B, 2025) and its one `lines` entry match Forbes (normalization.md 2.17). The family (domestic-services), the template chain and `estimate_basis` (unit_cost, the template's) all fit.
Mechanical: lint 0 high / 0 medium / 1 low (`uww_total_expenses_2024` unused; its explainer says context only); invariants none; normalization-audit none for any model (474 checked); check.ts all valid; simulation stable (5% 0.39×, 50% 1×, 95% 3× the best guess).
Previous round: none on disk (the model's earlier audit rounds predate audit receipts).

Branch shares of the result (computed from each branch's raw value × its `total_adj`): basic needs 8.6%, other grants 56%, own services 30%, 211 2.4%, tax help 2.8%. Within the services chain (other grants and own services), value per dollar splits 80% children and youth, 14% financial stability and 6% health and other. Children's and youth programmes are therefore about 69% of the whole result.

## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, judgment): 0.0023–0.023. Every branch is correctly thin: the reach comes from spending over judged costs, the effects are the literature's, and 211's outcome is a self-reported follow-up with no comparison group.
2. `$discount_rate` (shared slider): 0.029 at 0.1%, 0.0066 at 8%. The children's earnings come 13–53 years after the programme.
3. `$uov_per_daly` (shared slider).
4. `edu_cost_per_child` (judgment, $3,000, $1,200–$8,000): 0.023–0.0066. Anchored on BBBS, Saga tutoring, WSIPP and state pre-K.
5. `edu_effect_sd` (judgment, 0.16, 0.05–0.30): 0.0061–0.018. Anchored on Kraft and others 2026 (verified), Raposa 2019, the Head Start Impact Study and Durlak 2010.

Next come the shared resource-sharing multiplier and earnings per SD, then `fin_earnings_gain`, `svc_edu_share`, `og_evidence_adj` and `og_funging_adj`, all ranged judgments that name anchors. The ranges are real disagreement, not reflexive ±20%. The model's own joint low–high is 0.00074–0.27.

What would most improve the model:
- a network-wide split of grants by impact area, which sets `bn_share_of_grants` and `svc_edu_share`, perhaps from United Way Worldwide's network survey or a sample of the largest locals' 990 Schedule I grant lists;
- volunteer hours per child in the youth programmes it funds (finding 2);
- a real cost and effect per kind of youth programme (finding 7).

Peers: 0.0116 is ×0.107 the global-health median, just above a tenth. The gap comes from the clients' US consumption ($23,000 a person on the log scale) and US service prices, which the evidence supports. Among same-template models, Boys & Girls Clubs (0.029) runs the same youth chain at $1,700 a member-year with after-school effects only, and Feeding America (0.017) gives food near cash. Neither gap comes from a different counting rule, except the volunteer attribution in finding 2, which pulls United Way further down.
