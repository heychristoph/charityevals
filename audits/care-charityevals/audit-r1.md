## Drivers

1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, 0.5): 0.054–0.544. Every branch is correctly thin. CARE USA publishes no reach for its own spending, so reach is spending over the confederation's cost per person or a judged cost.
2. `$uov_per_daly` (shared): 0.494–0.161.
3. `livelihood_consumption_gain` (judgment, 1.2%, 0–4%): anchored on CARE's own savings-group RCTs (Ksoll et al. 2016; Karlan et al. 2017, no detectable average consumption effect). It is a sound reference class; the household step around it is finding 2.
4. `affected_consumption_share` (judgment, 0.6): the stand-in mean of Kenya, Uganda and Malawi, weighted towards poorer crisis households and offset by Turkey and Ukraine. A spending-weighted harmonic mean over the Schedule F regions gives about the same baseline ($330 against $305).
5. `$uov_per_ln_consumption` (shared estimate). Then `relief_counterfactual_adj` (0.7) and `relief_goods_value_share` (0.6), both judgments with stated reasons.

What would most improve the model: split relief's goods and deaths adjustments (finding 1, +20%), and settle what CARE's "people reached" counts so that the food and savings-group branches read it the same way (finding 2, +17% to +30%, or −15%). Together they could move the result to about 0.38. That would still be 2.5× the cause median, below Doctors Without Borders relief (0.70) and close to Direct Relief (0.38), so no outlier statement is needed.

Findings by severity: high 0, medium 2, low 5.
