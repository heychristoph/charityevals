## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, 0.1–1): applied correctly. The branch is thin: the charity's own count of children recorded, not treated, and the effect from other organisations' oxygen systems.
2. `internal_validity_adj` (judgment, 0.5, 0.25–0.85). The reference class is Lam's GRADE "low" and the Nigerian trial. Against baseline, the trial found ALRI deaths halved (0.50) but no change in all child deaths (1.03, 0.72–1.47). The model's effect after this step, ≈11% of all child deaths before delivery, lies inside that interval. Anchored, but it carries the result: 1.2 to 4.1.
3. `daly_per_child_death` (shared).
4. `counterfactual_adj` and 5. `implementation_adj` / `external_validity_adj` (judgments). Findings 3–5: each partly re-asks a question the effect or the missing-data step already answers.

The sourced inputs (`child_mortality_reduction`, the two CFRs) come next. Finding 2 corrects them.

Peers: ×22 the cause's median. The gap comes from the reach: each recorded child is a hypoxaemic admission at a 17–25% risk of death, and the charity spent about $170 per recorded child ($112,374 / 660). The deaths averted rest on a reviewed effect, discounted for design, delivery and missing data. Scope, year and reach are the site's, so the gap is supported, not a finding.

What would most improve the model: the charity's weekly data on how many recorded children got oxygen and how many died, by age (newborn or older); Gombe's oximetry and oxygen coverage before April 2025; and the external impact evaluation the trustees plan.
