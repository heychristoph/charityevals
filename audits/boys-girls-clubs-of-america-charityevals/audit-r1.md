## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, 0.006–0.061): correctly applied to both branches. Reach is a judged cost per member and there is no controlled evaluation of Clubs, so the model is thin, not over-applied.
2. `edu_effect_sd` (a judgment in substance, 0.009–0.064): anchored on Durlak 2010, 21st CCLC and Kremer 2015, and the same after-school component as United Way's blend. A controlled or quasi-experimental study of Club membership (even a matched comparison on the NYOI data) would improve the model most.
3. `$discount_rate` (shared): the earnings arrive 11–51 years later.
4. `$uov_per_daly` (shared).
5. `cost_per_member_year` (judgment, $900–$2,800): the anchors are sound but need BGCA's services added (#5). A roll-up of Clubs' Form 990 expenses against members (BGCA's affiliates file 990s; ProPublica's bulk data) would replace the judgment with data.

Peers: 0.0306 is the highest of the domestic-services models (United Way 0.0116, Habitat 0.0054, Salvation Army 0.0052, Red Cross 0.0045). The gap comes from the children's earnings branch: the effect per dollar here (0.063 SD after attendance ÷ $1,600 ≈ 3.9e-5 SD/$) is close to United Way's (0.16 ÷ $3,000 ≈ 5.3e-5), and United Way's other branches are weaker. The result is under a tenth of the global-health median, and the summary's reason (the US log-income scale and the cost per member-year) matches the rows. No other double counting was found. The parents' and children's branches are different changes for different people. Attendance is counted once (in implementation_adj for the children, in cc_value_per_household for the parents). Government money to Clubs is credited through the cost per member, and volunteers and donated space through attribution.
