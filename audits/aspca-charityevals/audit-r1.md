# Audit: aspca-charityevals, round 1

Result: 0.6533 ced/$ = 7.19e-5 years of healthy life per $1,000 (combined range 7.6e-6 – 6.8e-4); cause median 4.87e-4 (×0.15). Verdict: fix needed.
Model-sha: 9a41977934e4479c36a10b8b0d6c311141166bcdcf8b0651f48511102b56588b

Scope: every companion-animal programme in the 2025 audited accounts (year to 31 Dec 2025): shelter and veterinary services $158.3m, public education $90.0m less 3% for farm animals, policy, response and engagement $67.5m less 8% for farm animals (the shared split in facts/aspca.json; the farm-animal model takes the rest). program_spend $307,715,599 matches charities/aspca.json's companion-animals line, and the two lines add up to total programme expenses of $315,816,482. Overhead ($85.7m fundraising, $20.2m management) is spread by cost. Ten branches: placements, veterinary care, poison control, neutering, disaster response and pet food, cruelty response, grants, training, policy and education. Owners' life satisfaction is in WELLBYs through the fixed bridge. Nothing is left out.

Mechanical: lint 0 high / 0 medium / 0 low; invariants none; normalization-audit none for this model (generated/ restored afterwards); check.ts all valid; simulation-check stable (5% 0.154, 50% 1.0, 95% 4.68 × best guess). Joint spread of the model's own ranges: 2.7e-6 – 9.4e-4.

Where the result comes from: the owners' WELLBYs (`ced_from_wellby` 0.6518 of 0.6533 ced/$) make up **99.8% of the result**. All animal branches together are 0.2%: 408,000 pain days a year against $411m, of which neutering is 40% and placements 23%. So every row on the chain `wellbys_people` = `vet_animals_helped` × `own_share_eased` × `own_ls_gain` × vet counterfactual × credit × delivery × missing data is a top driver, and findings on the animal side can only be low.

## Double counting

- Neutering against grants: spay/neuter grants are inside the 129,200 surgeries and left out of `grt_live_share`. Correct.
- Policy: `pol_counterfactual_adj` 1 with `$win_years_counted`, so one question (Convention 5). Correct.
- Neutering's breeding years against its counterfactual: the explainers split "neutered later anyway" between `neu_breeding_years` (death or later neutering) and `neu_counterfactual_adj` (the share neutered soon). Acceptable as written.
- The owners' WELLBYs carry the veterinary branch's counterfactual, credit and delivery, not its evidence adjustment (the gain is its own judgment, discounted by `people_missing_data_adj`). This matches the Dogs Trust model.
- Overlapping reach counts: finding 6.

## Drivers

1. `own_ls_gain` (judgment, 0.05 points over a year): the top swing. Its reference class is right, but it has no conversion to points (finding 4).
2. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider): the owners' chain is thin, which is correct.
3. `$daly_per_wellby` (shared): the WELLBY bridge carries 99.8% of the result.
4. `own_share_eased` (judgment 0.5) and the reach it multiplies: the reach counts unowned animals (finding 1), and owners in other branches are left out (finding 2).
5. `vet_counterfactual_adj` (judgment 0.5) and the veterinary donor share (finding 3).

Findings 1 and 2–3 pull in opposite directions (−25% against +33% and +22%), so the defaults should be refit by the rules, not netted.

What would most improve the model is a count of community-clinic patients (owned animals) and of pet-food households from the ASPCA, plus a quantified anchor for the owners' gain. The animal-side judgments do not matter at the site's defaults.
