## Double counting

- Owners' chain: the counterfactual is now asked once per branch (`*_counterfactual_adj`). The gain is a gap times a duration "without the help", and the share eased is conditional on the help being counterfactual. No overlap.
- Fees: netted from the cost and the donor shares kept, which is correct (net cost × donor share = outcome over full cost). Only the overhead base is inconsistent (finding 3).
- Pet food and disaster: disaster meals now come off both food terms, and the disaster animals' own days include their hunger. No overlap.
- Scale check of the food term against the site's income route: a household's 30 days of food for 1.6 pets is about $50–100. As consumption for a low-income US household of 2.5 (about $37,500 a year), through `$uov_per_ln_consumption` (1.44), `$uov_per_daly` (2) and `$daly_per_wellby` (0.065), that is about 0.04–0.07 WELLBYs. The model gives 0.033 per counterfactual household (0.066 after finding 1). The two routes agree, so there is no finding on the food term's scale.

## Drivers

1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared): every branch is thin, which is correct; 2.2e-5 – 2.2e-4.
2. `own_years_burdened` (judgment, 0.1 year, 0.03–0.3): 3.4e-5 – 3.4e-4.
3. `own_burden_gap_sd` (judgment, 0.33 SD, anchored on Spitznagel's p<0.001 at 119 against 119): 3.4e-5 – 2.0e-4.
4. `$daly_per_wellby` (shared): the bridge carries 99.8% of the result.
5. `own_share_eased` (judgment, 0.5, the case mix) and `vet_counterfactual_adj` (judgment, 0.5, the AVCC cost barrier): each about 5.6e-5 – 1.7e-4.

Finding 1 raises the result by about 16% and finding 3 by about 4%; apply them by the rules. The result stays below the cause's median (×0.23). The gap comes from care for one animal at a time, as the summary says, not from a counting rule. A published split of the community clinics' visits into sick or injured against wellness and neutering would most improve the model, as would a count of the households given pet food.

Verdict: pass. Findings: 0 high, 2 medium, 3 low.
