## Is the 15.6× right?

Yes. It applies the site's rules correctly and is not a modelling error. Rule 2.20 values domestic public money won as recipients' gain less payers' cost. Feeding America's advocacy model, Wounded Warrior Project's and Habitat's advocacy model do the same. The rebates go to households at about 0.6 of mean spending and are paid for by federal taxpayers. On the shared figures that makes them worth about 2.5× their payer cost. At this model's share of the law, the money is about 40× the tonnes' value. Without the money, the rest of the model is slightly negative (−0.0004, from the subsidised households' −$120). The summary states the INV-15 reason ("more than ten times its peers because …") and names the peers' gap. Two inputs move the multiple: findings 1 and 2. Together they leave it near ×16.

A note for the owner, not a finding against this model, which reads the shared rows correctly: half of each transfer's value comes from the scale mix that normalization.md 2.14 decided. Recipients are on the CE survey scale ($32,723); payers are on the national-accounts scale ($58,517), which runs 1.79× higher. So a dollar moved from an average taxpayer to an average person is valued at 2.1× its cost even before progressivity (1.79 × 1.4 ÷ 1.2). With payers on the survey scale too (1.2 ÷ (1.4 × 32,723)), this model gives 0.0117 (×0.39; 6× the climate median). The credits then turn net negative (−36,553 uov), and the domestic-transfer models across the site would move the same way. That is a shared-rule question for docs/normalization.md 2.14/2.20, not for this model.
