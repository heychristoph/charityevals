## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, 0.5): the whole programme is thin, correctly.
2. `$discount_rate` (shared slider): the 12-year wait and 40 working years make the result very rate-sensitive (9.7–83).
3. `implementation_adj` (judgment, 0.25): its cited dose-response support is a misread (#1). Re-anchoring it on the home-viewing survey estimate is the single change that would most improve the model.
4. `evidence_adj` (judgment, 0.3): the internal half (0.6) is anchored (Evans & Yuan, another charityevals education model); the external half (0.5) has no reference class (#2).
5. `repeat_year_ratio` (judgment, 0.6, 0.3–1): reasoned but unanchored. A Sesame Street or Ubongo study with second-year effects would narrow it.

Sourced inputs that set reach and cost (Form 990, the 42 million count, World Bank shares, Mares & Pan's pooled effect) all verify. The far-above result comes from a cost of about $0.26 per child-year, not from a counting error. The judgment stack (dose, external validity, repeat years, credit to broadcasters) decides where in the 4–150 range the result sits.
