## The modelling choice: a typical nuclear dollar's cut, applied to the famine

The model values ALLFED's work like this:

- **The cut.** It takes Rethink Priorities' typical small nuclear project, as a relative cut per $1 million.
- **What it applies to.** The cut goes on the famine deaths in a war the world survives. Those are 94% of a nuclear catastrophe's deaths. Before it is used, the extinction chance is multiplied by a 30% food-supply share.
- **Everything else stays the same.** The chances, the deaths, the years and the discounting are the peers' shared rows.

The choice is sound, for these reasons:

- **The arithmetic holds.** Expected deaths are the chance of a war times the deaths if it comes. A 1% cut in either one averts the same number of deaths. A typical dollar that cuts the deaths given a war is therefore valued exactly like one that cuts the chance.
- **Rethink's preset already covers severity.** The preset ALLFED reads is described in the CCM source as "A small project aimed at reducing the threat or severity of a nuclear war" (xrisk_interventions.py, line 204). The typical nuclear dollar includes work on how bad a war is, not only on whether it happens. Carrying it over to resilience needs less of a stretch than the model's wording says (finding 3).
- **The parts ALLFED can't touch are taken out.** Blasts, fire and fallout are removed (×0.94). So is the part of extinction risk that doesn't run through food (×0.3).
  - Per typical $1M, nuclear deaths averted a year come to 1,205, against 1,361 for the Arms Control Association (ACA) and the Nuclear Threat Initiative (NTI). The cut is the same; the deaths it acts on are about 11% fewer.
  - Future healthy years kept come to 365, against the peers' 1,215.
- **Comparable with the peers.** The adjustments use the same questions as ACA and NTI:
  - evidence 0.8, the same figure with a wider range;
  - counterfactual 0.75, against their 0.7;
  - attribution 0.65, against 0.9 and 0.85, because of volunteers and co-authors.
  - The adjustments combine to 0.195, against ACA's 0.252 and NTI's 0.238.
- **Why the result sits between the peers (30 against NTI's 23 and ACA's 41).** Three things decide it: the nuclear share (82%, against ACA's 91% and NTI's 66%), the lower attribution, and the next dollar (63%, against 56% and 42%). The summary's reason for the result (the same typical dollar, applied to the deaths) is correct.
- **Outside estimates.** ALLFED's November 2025 appeal quotes two outside estimates of resilient-food work:
  - Vasco Grilo: within an order of magnitude of GiveWell's top charities;
  - CEARCH: "around 30x" a typical GiveWell charity, for policy advocacy.
  This site's Against Malaria Foundation model gives 24.2. At 30.4 the model sits at the low end of that bracket, not outside it. The comparison is a check only; nothing is tuned to it.

What the choice can't settle is the size of the cut for resilience work. Rethink calibrated its preset on a field that is mostly prevention, and the model's evidence range (0.25–2) carries that doubt.

The template allows another route: ALLFED's own chain, with the share of famine deaths that prepared food plans would avert, times the chance governments use them, times ALLFED's part in that readiness. It would mean three new judgments, with no reference class better than Rethink's. It would also break comparability with the nuclear peers, which read the same shared cut. The current choice is the family's own rule: a typical dollar per area, then the charity through its evidence multiplier. Keep it.
