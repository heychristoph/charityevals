The rows that set how many hens leave cages (`eu_conversion_share`) move only the small animal side, which is why they are absent from the tornado.

## Drivers
1. `$mobiliser_share` (shared slider, judgment): 0.05–1 → -0.0016 to -0.032.
2. `$win_years_counted` (shared slider, judgment): 1–20 → -0.0020 to -0.029.
3. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, judgment): 0.1–1 → -0.0019 to -0.019.
4. `@next_dollar.animal_policy_international` (new fact, judgment-built estimate, 57%, 15–95%): -0.0025 to -0.0158. It is sourced for room (no gap published), but its "third of the budget" support is wrong (#4, #11).
5. `eu_attribution_share_shared` (judgment, 2%, 0.5–6%): -0.0046 to -0.0226. Its reference class is a list of co-advocates with no spending figures. Eurogroup for Animals' and CIWF's EU budgets from the Transparency Register would anchor it.

Next come `eu_period_share` and `eu_p_adopted` (judgments) and the EU cost (estimate, verified as quoted, but see #1–#3). The site's own default chicken weight sets the sign. A SAD is worth about half a US cent of an EU shopper's consumption, while a cage-free hen-year costs several euros. So any counted cost of a cage-free rule outweighs the hens unless chickens are weighed a few hundred times higher. The magnitude here (×-76 against the cause's median) comes from the EU compliance row. #1–#3 bring it to about -55 ced/$ (-36%) without changing the sign.

What would most improve the model: (a) an EU cost that matches the hens the rule actually moves (#1–#3), and later the Commission's own impact assessment of the laying-hen proposal (due by December 2026), which 2.21 would then require in facts/compliance_costs.json; (b) API's real 2025 spending from the Estonian register (#4); (c) co-advocates' EU spending from the Transparency Register to anchor `eu_attribution_share_shared`.
