## Structure

Carbon is the only countable channel here, so the conservation chain is the right one for a litigation win:
- Restored hectares × the expected share logged a year × CO₂ per logged hectare.
- × the shared `$win_years_counted` after a wait, discounted.
- The period's one win is spread over `period_years` and divided by a typical year's cost. The win is counted once against the period's four years of spending, as Convention 11 requires and as in the climate sister model.

Rows that follow the rules:
- `estimate_basis: past_average` with `@next_dollar.earthjustice` (dated 2026-11-14 and sourced).
- `advocacy_share` reads `@climate.advocacy_share_of_policy_win`, as the climate model's `p_success` does.
- The bridge rows and the zero branches, each with its reason.

The weak points are the judged logging volume and how the adjustments split the questions between them (findings 1 to 5).

## Drivers
1. `$discount_rate` and `$climate_heat_death_delay`: shared figures; forest CO₂ is valued only through heat deaths decades later.
2. `$win_years_counted`: shared.
3. `$deaths_per_tco2e`: a shared estimate.
4. `logged_share_per_year`: a judgment and the model's own largest driver (10× range). It needs re-anchoring on the Forest Service's own projection (finding 1); this is the change that would most improve the model.
5. `$missing_data_discount`, `@next_dollar.earthjustice`, `@climate.advocacy_share_of_policy_win`, `implementation_adj`, `counterfactual_adj`, `tongass_ej_share`: shared figures or ranged judgments. `counterfactual_adj` needs the SASS question settled (finding 3).

The next best improvement is the timber volume per acre and old-growth harvest projections from the 2016 Tongass Land Management Plan amendment and the 2020 FEIS. They would turn the logged share into acres per year directly.
