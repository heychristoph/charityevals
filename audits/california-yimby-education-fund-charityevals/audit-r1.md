## Drivers

1. `$win_years_counted` (shared slider), then `$missing_data_discount` and `$uov_per_daly` (shared): the site's.
2. `adu_coalition_share` (judgment, 10%, 3-25%): the largest own driver, with no member-by-member reference class (finding 6).
3. `rent_fall_per_stock` (judgment, 1.5, 0.5-3): anchored only on a national Australian model (finding 5). The fall in rents carries about four fifths of the value through the renter-landlord consumption gap, so findings 2 and 7 bear on it too.
4. `@next_dollar.california_yimby_education_fund` (estimate, 42%, 20-75%): verified against both filings.
5. `catch_up_rate` (judgment, 5%, 2-15%) and its timing (finding 4), then `adu_followon_share` (judgment, 45%, 25-65%), which is reasonable against the APR series (2019 15,051 → 2024 38,907; the 2016 framework alone would need 42% growth over five years at the best guess).

What would most improve the model: a sourced coalition share from lobbying disclosures, a US regional estimate of rents against housing stock, and the climate and agglomeration side effects rebuilt as above. Together with the catch-up timing, findings 1-4 net to roughly +0% to −5% at the best guesses. The verdict rests on the two rule breaches (findings 1 and 2), not on the size of the result.
