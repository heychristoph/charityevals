## Drivers
1. Shared value judgments: `$chicken_vs_person` (it sets how many SADs a dollar of compliance cost is worth, so it sets the sign's margin), `$daly_per_sad`, `$uov_per_daly`, `$win_years_counted`, `$missing_data_discount`. These are the site's, not the builder's.
2. def_opposition_risk_cut (judgment, anchored on Bergan and Cole 2015 and Baumgartner et al. 2009) and def_p_override_without (judgment, single event). Both scale costs and gains alike, so they set the size of the net harm, not its sign.
3. def_coalition_spend (judgment): above about $20M of other groups' spending, THL's credit is small enough that the result is about zero.
4. The compliance-cost fact (estimate, $150M–$1,195M) and def_cost_egg_share (judgment, finding 1). The result is negative across the fact's whole range.
5. def_revert_share (judgment, 0.1–0.6).

The result is a net harm because the site values a dollar lost by an average American at about 170 chicken-equivalent pain days (about 80 hen SADs). The defence keeps about $0.20 of yearly egg cost per hen SAD, and pork costs more per SAD. This holds at any plausible cost figure, so the sign is robust. What would most improve the model: the SRIA itself (DF-131, archived) to set the egg share and the net from its tables (findings 1 and 6), then one set of shared policy judgments across THL's two models and ASPCA's (finding 2). The simulation's median draw is positive (the override doesn't happen in 70% of draws) and its expected value negative. That is the single-event rule working as designed, not instability.
