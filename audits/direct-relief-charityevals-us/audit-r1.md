# Audit: direct-relief-charityevals-us, round 1

Result: 2.803e-6 uov/$ = 0.00140 years of healthy life per $1,000 (combined range 0.00028–0.0070); global-health median 0.338 (×0.0041). Verdict: **fix needed** (2 high).

Scope: the "usa" line of charities/direct-relief.json: US programme cash spending for the year to 30 June 2025, $52,636k. That is the audited USA column, $402,371k, less donated goods $296,794k, expired donated stock $52,595k and donated freight $346k (audited accounts p. 7, checked). Overhead comes from the consolidated cash totals ($138,516k ÷ $122,819k = 1.128, checked against pp. 6–8). The model has two branches: donated and procured medicines (credit share against the goods at the clinics' price) and grants ($37,482k, counted in full). Together they cover the whole US column, including the Santa Barbara local programmes. The international line goes to the sister model, and the two lines add up to `spending.programme` ($122,819k). No programme is missing.

Mechanical: audit-model gives lint 0 high / 0 medium / 1 low (far-below, with the reason in the summary). check.ts: all models valid. normalization-audit: 0 violations for every invariant. simulation-check: 5% 0.325, 50% 1.00, 95% 3.13 × best guess, so the simulation is stable.

Previous round: none (round 1).

How the result is built: the medicines branch's health is the goods' value ÷ the US cost per DALY at the margin × the need ratio, times Direct Relief's cash share of the resources. The goods' value appears in both the numerator and the credit share's denominator, so this reduces to about Direct Relief's handling cash × need ÷ threshold. The result therefore barely moves with `us_price_ratio` or the dose count. Grants give 62% of the bridged DALYs (51.6 of 83.2), and the medicines give 31.

## Drivers

1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, 0.5): both branches are thin, which is correct (Convention 12).
2. `us_threshold_2019` (estimate, Vanness 2021, verified): it should be split into deaths and illness (finding 1).
3. `counterfactual_adj` (judgment 0.5): double-counted channel (finding 2).
4. `grt_need_vs_marginal` (judgment 1) and `need_vs_marginal` (judgment 1.5): no reference class (finding 5).
5. `internal_validity_adj` (judgment 0.85): overlaps the missing-data marker (finding 4).

The credit-share construction is sound (Convention 2) and makes the result nearly independent of how the donated goods are priced. What would improve the model most: value the threshold's deaths by age (finding 1), then anchor the two need ratios on cost-per-QALY figures for the actual product mix and grant mix. After findings 1–3 the result would be about 0.003 years of healthy life per $1,000. That is still far below the cause median, for the reason the summary gives (US prices for health at the margin), and in line with the other US care builds (St Jude 0.0034, British Heart Foundation 0.0047).
