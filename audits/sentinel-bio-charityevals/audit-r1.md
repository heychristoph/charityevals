## Double counting checked (no finding)

- **Grants inside the recipients' own models.** CSR's ($307,258) and Epoch's ($80,000) grants are each counted once, here, at the recipient's value per dollar (#charityevals). Each recipient's model divides its own outcome by its own spending, so reading a per-dollar figure doesn't count the grant's outcome twice. The same holds for the advised gift at JHU CHS's per-dollar figure, taken for Sentinel's 30% share.
- **Recipients' next-dollar factors × Sentinel's.** For grants read live, Sentinel's 49% multiplies a figure that already carries CSR's 49%. The two answer different questions. Sentinel's asks whether its next dollar makes a grant as good as its past average one. CSR's asks what a marginal dollar to CSR does, and a grant is a marginal dollar to CSR. Money movers apply both in the same way (2.8, 2.10). The typical grants' analogue of the recipient's factor is `bio_cf_other_funders`, which is not applied to the live grants. No overlap.
- **Sentinel's own costs against the grants.** Own costs ($428,699, 28%) stay in `cost_total` and earn nothing (`own_cost_share` sits outside every branch weight). The typical-project cut is applied to grant dollars only (`bio_cost_share` = typical_grants / cost_total), so overhead is never valued as work. This matches Ploughshares' `own_grant_admin_share` treatment. The 990 shows no own research output in 2024 beyond grantmaking (Part III lists only grants). The $1,800 qualitative study is internal strategy.
- **Side effects.** These apply only to the typical-project branch (outcome_total brackets them before `+ dalys_from_live`). The live recipients carry their own.
- **Counterfactual split.** `bio_counterfactual_adj` = other organisations (0.7) × other funders (0.8). This is the same pair of questions, with the same figures, as Ploughshares' grants (`gr_cf_other_orgs` × `gr_cf_other_funders`). Each question is asked once (Convention 5).
