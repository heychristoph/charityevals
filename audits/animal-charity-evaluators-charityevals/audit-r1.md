The sign of the result rests on one modelling choice, finding 1. If ACE's shifted money is priced with the shared `prior_dollar_value` (GiveDirectly, 0.00792 units of value), every shifted dollar loses about 4 healthy years per $1,000, and the headline becomes **−2.30** years per $1,000 (computed by pointing `moved_value` at the shared `moved_dollar_net_value`). That cannot be right for ACE. Its donors' shifted money was going to animal charities anyway, not to GiveDirectly. So the builder is right that the counterfactual has to be an animal charity. The way it is built is not right: it is a parallel conversion local to this model, with the wrong reference class. The fix is a change to the shared rule (below), not a waiver.

## Points asked

1. **Shifted money, priced against an animal charity.** The counterfactual is justified: ACE's shifted money comes from animal giving, and the shared GiveDirectly baseline gives −2.30 years per $1,000 for a choice its donors never faced. The implementation breaches one consistent way: a local conversion, the template's row replaced, the shared rows left dead and off the path, and the ratio applied to the wrong reference class. The fix is finding 1: write the cause rule into the shared method and INV-12, and this model reads it.
2. **ACE as an evaluator.** All eight destinations read `#ace:<charity>`. For each one, ACE is the charity's primary evaluator on the rankings (`rankingPlan[0]`): ACE models for The Humane League, Wild Animal Initiative, Shrimp Welfare Project, Dansk Vegetarisk Forening, Good Food Fund and Aquatic Life Institute; ACE-2026 models for Sinergia Animal and Çiftlik Hayvanlarını Koruma Derneği. That is 2.8's "figure the rankings show". The Humane League also has charityevals' own three-programme build at 9.07e-7 against ACE's 1.57e-5. The rankings use ACE's, so the rule is followed, and the write-up says the model is not an independent check. No other ACE evaluation is read. The `ace_*` facts are ACE's influence metrics, which is the organisation's own survey as 2.8 allows. Only the Movement Grants valuation strays from the rule (finding 2).
3. **Own effect.** ACE's 54% × the shared non-response step (0.4 + 0.6 × 0.5 = 0.7) = 37.5%. This is the same pattern and formula as the site's GiveWell and Effektiv Spenden models. The 40% response share is a ranged judgment with anchors (Effektiv Spenden about a fifth, GiveWell 42%), and ACE's report is not public, so it cannot be checked. Only the grantmaker part is mis-stepped (finding 6). The $12.3M covers Recommended Charities only. ACE's cumulative figures confirm this ("$59 million in donations to our Recommended Charities", Movement Grants reported apart), so adding the $1.2M of Movement Grants double counts nothing.
4. **Costs and influenced giving.** Every cost figure is verified in the audited PDF: total expenses $6,582,746, programme services $5,883,860, grants $4,624,785 + $24,000, in-kind advertising $161,448 + $80,724 = $242,172 (Note 4: "valued by Google at $ 242,172"), salaries $1,376,279, benefits $31,002, supported-charity contributions $5,039,347, Note 1's "narrowed its focus to charity evaluations and grantmaking", and Note 8's balances ($625,554 for Recommended Charities, $1,390,698 + $55,510 for Movement Grants). The influenced-giving figures are verified on ACE's post: $12.3M, $6.4M, $1.2M to 32 groups, $0.84M and $6.05. Same fiscal year as the accounts.
5. **Waivers.** Finding 7.

## Drivers
1. `$chicken_vs_person` and `$daly_per_sad`: shared judgments. The result is about proportional to them through the destinations, and exactly so after findings 1 and 2.
2. `@next_dollar.animal_charity_evaluators` 36% (15–70%): an estimate from the room × use × run steps, partly unverified (finding 9). 0.00072–0.00337.
3. The destination weights: equal weights now, the published fund split after finding 3. The Humane League's ACE figure is three quarters of the Recommended Charities' value, so its share is the largest structural swing (+24%).
4. The shifted-money baseline: `@meta.prior_vs_cash` (shared, 0–3×) and above all how it is carried into the cause (finding 1: −2.30 against 0.0020 years per $1,000 between two constructions).
5. `@meta.survey_nonrespondent_influence` (shared) and `survey_response_share` (judgment, 20–70%), about ±25% between them.

What would most improve the model: the shared cause rule for shifted money (finding 1), and the published fund split as weights (finding 3). After that, ACE's influenced-giving report, for its response coverage and whether the $12.3M already extrapolates direct gifts (findings 5 and 6).
