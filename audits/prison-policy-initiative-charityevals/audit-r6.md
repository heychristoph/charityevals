## Checks asked for
1. **Source (verified).** Patterson (2013), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3673515/, abstract: "Each additional year in prison produced a 15.6% increase in the odds of death for parolees, which translated to a 2-year decline in life expectancy for each year served in prison. The risk was highest upon release from prison and declined over time. The time to recovery ... was approximately two thirds of the time served in prison." The fact's quote matches word for word, and the delay judgment (3 years, 1 to 8) rests on the recovery finding. The discussion gives about 10 years lost at age 30 for 5 years served, the same 2 per year. Ranges: 2 (0.5 to 3) is reasonable. The low end is a quarter of the point, for confounding by sentence length and for jail and short stays, which the cohort (prison terms, parolees) does not cover; the tornado shows the range moves the result 0.036 to 0.076. 40 remaining years (33 to 47) and 3 years (1 to 8) are judgments with named anchors. Both facts are shared and read the same way by Brennan.
2. **Double counting with the life-satisfaction term: the explainer holds.** The life-satisfaction fact is the wellbeing gap of a year inside against a year outside, for the years served. The deaths channel is 2 years of life lost per year served, as a share of a death (2/40 = 0.05) at the value of a death at ages 15 to 49; those years fall mostly after release (the 3-year delay, discounted) and no life-satisfaction year of the other term lies in them. The one possible overlap is a stay's health harm short of death sitting in the life-satisfaction gap, which the write-up now says is left in that term. That term is only about 2% of the result (the write-up's own split), so even if it were wholly duplicative, removing it moves the result by 2%, far under the 10% threshold.
3. **Same adjustments as the wellbeing term: yes.** dec_death_value_per_year uses dec_people, dec_discounted_years (the wait and win_years_counted) and campaign_years exactly as dec_value_per_year does, adding only the death-specific delay; dec_death_uov applies dec_total_adj (evidence, counterfactual, attribution, implementation, missing data) and then (1 + ue_dec_family + ue_dec_crime), as dec_wellbys does in wellbys_bridged. This matches Brennan's jus_uov (jus_death_delay_discount, the same shared facts and moral-weight row, `$life_value_multiplier`) and jus_uov_adj, which applies its side effects to the deaths too. Differences are judgments, not breaches: PPI's family side effect is 15% (Brennan has 10% families plus 10% earnings), the crime side effect is the same -10% (-30% to 0), and the evidence factor is 0.75 against 0.6 (finding 1). The death value is in UoV through uov_bridged, so INV-17 is not engaged (the model is not a WELLBY model).
4. **unmodelled_benefits_adj is right.** It is (ue_family_contact + ue_service_cuts) x dalys_from_uov x (1 - dec_death_uov / uov_bridged) / (dalys_direct + dalys_from_uov + dalys_from_wellby). The two phone-branch side effects are applied to the phone branches' share of uov_bridged only (the income term is zero, so uov_bridged is fed + st + dec_death), and outcome_total multiplies by (1 + adj), so the deaths carry only their own ue_dec_* terms once; nothing is applied twice and nothing is left out. dalys_direct is 0, so it does not distort the denominator.
