## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider): all three branches are thin. That is right: KIND counts relief, but no controlled evaluation of its representation exists.
2. `$uov_per_daly`, `$discount_rate` (shared).
3. `@kind.p_removed_without_status` (judgment, 30%): the removal-averted path carries about 86% of a relief's value. It is the weakest anchor in the model (F3).
4. `other_counsel_share` (judgment, 35%) and `relief_rate_unrepresented_vs_represented` (judgment, 0.25, anchored on TRAC's 0.21): together the US counterfactual, 0.49.
5. `@kind.client_consumption_vs_mean` (judgment, 0.6, anchored on the bottom-40% ratios) and `@irap.destination_consumption_share` (0.45): they set the log gain of a life in the US.

The checks of the reasoning turned up no double counting:
- Donated services are out of the cost and in the credit, at the lawyers' own wage.
- Relief is a yearly flow, scaled to 2024's spending by the same-period rule, with the wait discounted once.
- The status gain excludes the relief from fear of removal, which `ue_wellbeing_status` carries.

The result sits at 0.15× the cause median. The gap is explained in the summary: about $100,000 of KIND's own spending per permanent relief, and most children without status are never removed. IRAP's legal services (0.074) is the nearest peer: it values resettlement from poorer origins with a cheaper case. What would most improve the model: a cohort removal rate for unaccompanied children (F3), KIND's count of relief by year of intake (it would settle F2), and a 2024 reintegration count (F1).
