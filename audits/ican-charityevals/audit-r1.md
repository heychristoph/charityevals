# Audit: ican-charityevals, round 1
Result: 0.01002 daly/$ = 10.0 years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars (combined range 0.35–289; model's own ranges 0.17–483); cause median 1.58 (×6.3). Verdict: pass.

Scope: every programme of ICAN in 2024, the latest year with audited accounts. The 2025 report's figures are preliminary, and the audited 2025 statement it promised for April 2026 is not on icanw.org/ican_annual_reports. The 2024 report p. 29 gives total operating expenses CHF 2,211,011 by nature: personnel 1,142,156, consultancy 571,617, campaigning 212,192, EIF/universalisation 69,155, communication 62,191, rent 68,704, G&A 34,541, audit and legal 50,455. These sum exactly. All of it is in cost_total ($2.51M at the IRS 2024 rate of 0.881 CHF/$). It is split into nuclear field work 70%, treaty universalisation (policy branch) 25% and nuclear justice 5% (valued in unmodelled_benefits_adj). AI, bio and field-building are 0, each with a reason. charities/ican.json `spending` ($2,335,200 = (2,211,011 − 153,700) × 1.135074) and its one `lines` entry match.
Mechanical: audit-model lint 1 low (the spread, which the summary states); invariants none; normalization-audit 0 violations in every invariant (generated/ restored); check.ts all models valid; simulation stable (5% 0.077×, median 1.0×, 95% 15.9× of the best guess).
Previous round: none (round 1).

Policy branch, as calibrated. pol_relative_cut = 150 × 3.559e-4 × 0.1 / 73 = 7.31e-5 of the yearly catastrophe risk per new party. pol_deaths_per_year divides the typical $1M's deaths by its catastrophe cut, so the extinction size is cut in Rethink's proportion. Each new party is therefore exactly R × m / N = $0.205M of typical nuclear work, and the four are $0.82M. That covers both sizes, as families/catastrophic-risk.md asks. The arithmetic in the explainers (about 0.5% of the yearly risk for the whole treaty, 0.007% per party) checks out.

After pol_counterfactual_adj 0.5 and pol_attribution_share 0.25, ICAN's universalisation dollar ($0.63M) is worth 0.16 of a typical dollar before the missing-data step. Its field dollar is worth 0.13 (0.3 × 0.7 × 0.6).

The calibration's frame is sound: a ranged R, a named multiple whose best guess is the geometric middle of its 0.01–1 range, and credit for the year's flow of four parties only. Under it, each year's ratifications are credited to that year's resources, and the treaty-building years are credited through the parties that joined then (50 by entry into force). The totals add up to the whole cut, and no stock is credited to 2024.

Three things are not right:
- the channels and part of the spending are shared with the field branch (finding 1);
- each party is valued at the average party's share (finding 2);
- the next-dollar use factor is reasoned from this branch alone (finding 3).

What would most improve the model is finding 1: one home for each channel, treaty implementation at the treaty's multiple, accession pledges in universalisation. That makes the field-work multiple and the treaty's multiple two separate questions, as Convention 5 requires. Fixing the three mediums together moves the result to about 8 (−20%). That stays within the evidence's range and keeps ICAN below the nuclear peers, for the reason the summary gives.
