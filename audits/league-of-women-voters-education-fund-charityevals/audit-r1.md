## Drivers
1. `$win_years_counted` (shared slider, judgment): 0.0030–0.031.
2. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider): 0.0025–0.025. All four branches are correctly thin. Nothing measures the Education Fund's effect on a slide or on reforms, and the VOTE411 and registration counts aren't outcomes.
3. `@democracy.field_risk_cut_per_year` (shared judgment, reference class Boese et al. 2021 and the catastrophic-risk family): 0.0055–0.041.
4. `@democracy.democratization_gdp_gain` (shared estimate, Acemoglu et al. 2019) and `@next_dollar.league_of_women_voters_education_fund` (estimate, sourced to the audited statements), each about ×4.7.
5. Own judgments: `bs_direct_share` (8–30%) and the voter-information share it leaves. Each of the slide and voter-information branches carries about a third of the result, and their weights are 1 and 0.2 per dollar. So a shift of programme between them moves the result by about 0.8 slide-branch units per point.

What would most improve the model: an issue-level split of the Education Fund's spending (the Form 990 Schedule C lobbying total, or the League's own budget by campaign). That would replace the three judged spending shares, which are the model's own main uncertainty. Beyond that, fix finding 1 so the shared indirect weight's whole range can be read.
