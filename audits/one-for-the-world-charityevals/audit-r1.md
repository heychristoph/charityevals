## Drivers
1. `$discount_rate` (shared; 14.5 – 4.2): pledgers give for decades. After finding 1 it matters more, since more of the value is future pledge giving.
2. `@next_dollar.one_for_the_world` (estimate, sourced composite, 3.2 – 10.6): above its peers' 42% and 32%, justified by the funding gap (Open Philanthropy's operating support ended, about five months of reserves) and held down by mixed early results.
3. `own_effect` (its own count, 4.3 – 8.8): the best guess is its floor (finding 2).
4. `daly_per_child_death` (shared, 4.4 – 8.3): through its destinations.
5. `cohort_trend_adj` and `recurring_share` (judgments): the steps that turn the year's flow into this year's pledges. Both need anchors (findings 6, 7), and the whole conversion needs finding 1.

What would most improve the model: the age-correct stock-to-flow conversion (finding 1), or better, One for the World's own count of active pledgers and their average yearly gift, which would let the template's recipe (new pledges × yearly giving × discounted years) replace three judgments (`recurring_share`, `cohort_trend_adj`, the steady-state ratio). Its retention by pledge age (finding 5) would settle how far its 1% pledgers follow the shared curve.

Verdict: fix needed. 4 high (1 the stock-to-flow conversion, +148%; 2 own effect at its floor; 3 the undiscounted wait; 4 volunteers' time missing from the shared resources), 4 medium, 4 low.
