## Drivers
1. `$discount_rate` (shared; 27.5 – 7.4): pledgers give for decades.
2. `@next_dollar.one_for_the_world` (estimate, sourced composite; 5.9 – 19.3).
3. `own_effect` (its own count, a sourced midpoint; 6.9 – 14.1).
4. `daly_per_child_death` and `resource_shares` (shared).
5. `own_retention_adj` (judgment anchored on its own retention; 9.4 – 15.4, the low end overstated, finding 5).

Not in the tornado, because its inputs are points: the yearly gift per active pledger (findings 1–3). It is the derived ratio that most needs a range.

What would most improve the model: One for the World's own count of active recurring donors and their run rate in 2023-24, split into university and workplace donors, as it published for June 2020 (489 donors, $282,871). That would replace the curve-derived active count, `recurring_share` and `new_pledge_size_adj` with observed figures, and give the corporate branch its own yearly gift. Its retention by pledge age would then settle `own_retention_adj`.

Verdict: pass. 0 high, 3 medium (1 the size adjustment's unsupported anchor, −6%; 2 equal cohorts and a point-valued stock behind the yearly gift, +6.5% at best, about −20% at the far end; 3 the corporate channel mixed into the university pledge recipe, +4 to +14%), 4 low.
