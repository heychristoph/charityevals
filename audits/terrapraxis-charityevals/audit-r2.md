## Drivers
1. `premium_usd_per_mwh` (judgment, unanchored): the top swing, and it flips the sign (findings 1-2).
2. `$deaths_per_tco2e` (shared).
3. `coal_smoke_factor` (judgment, anchored on M&W, GBD MAPS and the shared US factor).
4. `$win_years_counted` and `$discount_rate` (shared).
5. `p_success` (judgment, 1.5%, CATF reference class) and `$missing_data_discount` (shared).

Checked and sound: the coal-site share as a share of the build, with no double count between the lines. The siting savings counted only as brought forward (1 - 1.04^-5), after the wait; a levelised alternative differs by under 5% of the result. The 2022-to-2024 CPI step on both money rows, and GW to kW (1e6). The extra MWh credited the same way as the tonnes: the coal line's extra share, all of the other line, p_success and credit. The evidence adjustment on the money rows: its questions (does the pathway get built, does enabling work move it) govern the money as much as the tonnes, unlike 2.21's rule-specific case. coal_share_of_displaced_tonnes 0.6 consistent with rep_displaced_vs_coal 0.5 and nuc_counterfactual_adj 0.35 (a coal/gas/clean mix gives 0.57-0.6). The summary's and write-up's figures match the live model (0.0055 t, 0.0036 years; the two lines 46/54% of the tonnes; money about two fifths; negative at $40). No peer comparison in site text. The model would gain most from a regional split of the money branch with sourced cost gaps (findings 1-2): those rows now decide whether the result is a gain or a net harm.
