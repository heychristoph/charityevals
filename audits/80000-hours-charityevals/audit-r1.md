## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, 0.37–7.2): the career count is self-reported with no comparison group, so the branch is rightly thin.
2. `@meta.career_forgone_pay_default` ($5k–30k; judgment, the shared placeholder): 80,000 Hours publishes no salary data. A survey of its plan changers' pay before and after (or EA-survey salary data by role) would improve the model most.
3. `@next_dollar.eighty_thousand_hours` (estimate, 10–60%): room needs dated 2026 evidence (finding 4).
4. `@meta.career_counterfactual_share_default` (judgment, 10–60%): should be 80,000 Hours' own, anchored on its CBPC criterion (finding 3).
5. `plan_changes_2012_2022` (estimate, 604–3,000, sourced to 80,000 Hours' own extrapolation).

Not in the tornado because the shares are fixed in the formula, but larger than any of these: the cause mix the next dollar buys (finding 1, ×0.27). The site's AI models are worth about 1/56 of a global-health dollar, so the result depends mostly on how many careers still go outside AI.
