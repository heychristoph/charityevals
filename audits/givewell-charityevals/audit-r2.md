## Drivers
1. `next_dollar_freed_for_grants` (judgment 0.95, 0.8–1.0; 65.2–25.7). Of the result, 69% comes from the freed share and 31% from the remaining 5%. Each dollar of that remaining operations spending is worth about twelve granted dollars, so the result is very sensitive to this share. The policy is mechanical, and unrestricted assets are about four times its threshold, so the anchor is sound. The judgment would be firmer with a statement from GiveWell on whether its operating budget responds to unrestricted revenue.
2. `daly_per_child_death`, `discount_rate`, `uov_per_daly` (shared, through the live destinations). These are the site's values.
3. `@next_dollar.givewell` (estimate 0.70; 30.8–38.0). Sourced, dated 2026-11-14.
4. `survey_response_adj` / `donor_own_effect` (33.5–37.9). Finding 1. A current GiveWell donor survey, with its response rate by dollars, would replace a survey from 2015.
5. `moved_side_effects` (judgment 10%, −5% to +30%; 34.1–37.6). Anchored on Giving What We Can's figure; acceptable.

At 35.6 this model sits between them, which is plausible for an organisation whose next dollar mostly becomes a grant. The result is 516 times the cause median, and the summary gives the reason (its grantees), as INV-15 requires. With findings 1–4 applied the result is about 36.
