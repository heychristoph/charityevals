## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, 0.1–1). Correctly applied: the reach is counted, but the pilot had no control group and the effect comes from other delivery.
2. `mortality_reduction`: a judgment (finding 1), and the deaths branch is discounted twice (finding 2). It needs a stated reference class. A trial of rehabilitation's effect on deaths in stable, milder COPD, or a cohort study of it, would improve the model most.
3. `external_validity_adj` (judgment, 0.35–1): split it into its delivery part and its milder-participants part (finding 2).
4. `years_of_coverage` (judgment, anchored on the 2024 UK analysis).
5. `baseline_mortality_rate` (judgment, anchored on a post-hospitalisation cohort; finding 11).

Peers: the result is about a fortieth of the global-health median. The row that explains it is `cost_per_person` (about $3,300). It is a setting-up year: the spending built the chatbot and content, scoped India and ran a 51-person pilot. That is the site's counting rule (the latest complete year's cost against that year's reach; Convention 3, Convention 9), not a different programme or year. Scope and reach were checked, and no participant in the year is missing. Persistence is a sourced judgment. The gap is supported by the evidence and is noted here only, not in site text. Once the second year's count and spending are known (the charity expects about 1,000 participants), the model should move to that year.
