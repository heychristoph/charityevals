## Drivers
1. pol_share_removed (judgment, 0.3%; 0.0087–0.455 across its range). Anchored on Olken, but the step from one sector's 8-point cut to 0.3% of a country's whole corruption cost isn't spelled out. The model would improve most from a stated derivation: the sector's share of the corruption cost × the effect × the rule's coverage.
2. pol_people_per_change (judgment, 30M; 0.013–0.34). Fix the explainer to the sum it implies (finding 1), which also exposes the EU majority (finding 2).
3. `$win_years_counted` (shared slider; 0.017–0.18).
4. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider; all three branches thin, correctly).
5. pol_cost_per_bribe (judgment, 1; 0.022–0.21), with pol_movement_share (judgment, 30%; finding 4) close behind.

Everything that moves the result is a judgment. The only sourced inputs (cost, counts, the bribery share) are verified. The surest improvements are findings 2–4, which together move the result by about −60% to +10% depending on how they are resolved. None is a breach of a rule that sets the number, so the verdict is pass.
