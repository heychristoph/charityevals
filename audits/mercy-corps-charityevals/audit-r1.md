# Audit: mercy-corps-charityevals, round 1

Result: 1.449e-4 daly/$ = 0.145 years of healthy life per $1,000 (combined range 0.029–0.717); global-health median 0.207 (×0.70). Verdict: pass (0 high, 5 medium, 7 low).

Scope: the model covers all five programme lines of the consolidated statement of activities of Mercy Corps and Affiliates for the year to 30 June 2025 (p. 7, $ thousands). These are relief 138,091, recovery 30,723, livelihood/economic development 206,573, civil society and education 90,381 and health 42,945, which sum to program services of 508,713 against total operating expenses of 620,331. Every figure matches the audited statements. Kompanion Bank is a discontinued operation outside these lines, so leaving it out is right. charities/mercy-corps.json `spending` (508,713,000, 2025) and its single `programmes` line match the accounts. FY2025 is the latest audited year (the statements were issued 19 December 2025). The model has one branch per line: relief, recovery, livelihoods, peace and education (a judged split of the civil-society line) and health. Each branch is on a death or ln-consumption chain, as Convention 4 allows for a cross-family portfolio and as the World Vision and Save the Children builds do. Branch shares of the result at the defaults: livelihoods 46.8%, relief 39.0%, recovery 4.9%, health 4.3%, education 2.7%, peace 2.3%.

## Drivers

1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, 0.1–1): every branch is thin, correctly, so it scales the whole result.
2. `$uov_per_daly` and `$uov_per_ln_consumption` (shared): every outcome goes through units of value.
3. `liv_direct_gain` (judgment, 3%, 0.5–8%): anchored on graduation, savings-group and farming trials. A cost cross-check supports it: at $54 of livelihood spending per direct participant, it gives about 0.08 person-percent of consumption per dollar, close to Save the Children's livelihood branch and about two-thirds of graduation's per-dollar gain.
4. `liv_years` (judgment, 2 years, 1–4) and `liv_direct_share` (judgment, 20%, 10–40%).
5. Reach for livelihoods (`liv_people`, observed but overlapping, finding 1).

What would most improve the model: Mercy Corps' OOM reach by programme line, which would settle findings 1, 2 and 11. Also any controlled evaluation of one of its market-systems programmes, which would pin `liv_direct_gain` and `liv_indirect_gain_ratio` and could make the livelihood branch measured instead of thin.
