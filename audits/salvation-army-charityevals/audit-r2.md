## Peers: the far-below lint (0.044× the global-health median of 127 models)

Re-judged: the gap is right. The model sits mid-family among the domestic-services models (0.0012–0.029; the Red Cross 0.0045, Habitat US 0.0054 and United Way 0.0094 are its nearest neighbours). Round 1's three reasons still hold at the new figures:

1. **The log scale at US consumption.** $30 a person against $10,075 is a 0.30% gain (ln_gain_per_person 0.0030).
2. **Help valued per dollar spent.** About $850M of help is valued against $3.01B of cost plus $1.12B of donated goods and volunteer time.
3. **Standard adjustments.** Half of the help is judged additional, every branch is thin (0.5), and 73% of the credit is the charity's.

The shelter branch's large log gain (0.77 a person-year: $7,769 of roof against $6,717 of consumption) is the main reason it does better per dollar than the aid branch. It follows the shared shelter-night value and the CASPEH-anchored consumption, not an error. No programme, year or counting rule differs from the site's.

## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, −80% / +100%): every branch is thin. That is correct: no Salvation Army service has a controlled evaluation.
2. `$uov_per_daly` (shared slider): almost all of the result is income and deaths in units of value.
3. `@united_states.food_aid_recipients_consumption_share` (shared, 0.3; −25% / +32%).
4. `aid_counterfactual_adj` (judgment, 0.5 (0.3–0.8), −22% / +33%): anchored on Hunger in America's turn-away rates and the logic that a pantry's food is limited.
5. `implementation_adj` (judgment, 0.9 (0.7–1), −22% / +11%): anchored on the meals-versus-recorded-food cross-check. It still holds on the national figures: $599M × 0.668 food share × 1.25 retail ÷ $2.72 ≈ 184M meals, against 184M reported.

What would most improve the model: a published split of the rehabilitation line between stores and centres, and of the territories' in-kind goods between programmes and stores; a national figure for cash assistance; any follow-up of clients' food security or housing against a comparison group.
