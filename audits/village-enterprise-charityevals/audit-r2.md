## Drivers

1. `$uov_per_daly`, `$uov_per_ln_consumption` and `$discount_rate`: shared sliders, the same for every income model.
2. `persistence_years` (judgment, 3–12; result 1.74–4.84): anchored on the program's 2.5-year follow-up, on BRAC-type graduation studies (7–10 years) and on Blattman et al.'s fade by year 9. The second DREAMS endline (IDinsight, due 2026) would improve it most.
3. `fade_rate` (judgment, 0–20%; result 4.43–2.40): anchored on the core trial's cohorts and the same outside studies.
4. `spillover_ratio` (judgment, 0–40%; result 3.42–4.79): neither trial measured spillovers. The DREAMS second endline plans to.
5. `core_uganda_itt_month` and `core_kenya_itt_month`: observed, with 95% intervals.

Then come `leverage_funging_adj`, `core_evidence_adj`, `counterfactual_adj` and `implementation_adj`, all ranged judgments with named anchors. The partner-resource rows together move the result by under ±5%.

## Plausibility

The result is 54.8× the global-health median of 112 models (lint far-above, medium). That median is dominated by service and relief models in rich countries. Among models of the same kind, log-scale consumption gains for the extreme poor, it sits beside GiveWell's GiveDirectly (3.96). The row that explains the gap is still `ln_gain_per_person` (0.389 per person, present value): two RCTs of this program measure 4–18% consumption gains at about $442 per household, and the gain is counted for about 6 discounted full-gain years. The evidence supports the gap, so no fix follows.

The summary and write-up don't compare the charity with other charities, medians or peers. They name the discounted branches (Tanzania's DREAMS, SPRINT, government adoption), as Convention 12 asks. The beneficiary shares (Uganda 32%, Kenya 28%, Ethiopia 25%, Rwanda 12%, Tanzania 3%) reproduce from the rows. The write-up's tornado figures match audit-model's.
