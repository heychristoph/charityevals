Where the result comes from at the defaults: people's WELLBYs give 0.2307 of the 0.2339 ced/$ (98.6%). Any channel to people's health or wellbeing is therefore worth more than all the dog branches together.

## Double counting with the rabies model
- Costs: both models read `@dogs_trust.rabies_share_of_international` (20%). Their lines sum to programme spending ($154,069,768). No overlap.
- Outcomes: the rabies model counts human rabies deaths from 2.2 million vaccinations and dogs and livestock spared rabies as 1% (`other_unmodelled_share`). It counts no treatment, neutering or training. This model counts no vaccination. No overlap. Finding 4's bite row must leave rabies deaths out to stay so.
- The fact puts Soi Dog's and Hope & Animal Trust's catch-neuter-vaccinate work here and only dose-buying grants in the rabies sum. That closes round 1 9(c).
- `salary_gap` still differs (`gap_uk_charity` here, `gap_local_delivery` in the rabies model) for the same group payroll. No effect at the defaults (`donor_cost_share` 1). It is the rabies audit's to settle.

## Drivers
1. `prev_owner_ls_gain` (judgment, no anchor, hidden duration): 8.1e-6 – 9.6e-5. Findings 1 and 2.
2. `$missing_data_discount` and `$daly_per_wellby` (shared sliders), through the people rows.
3. `prev_counterfactual_adj` (judgment): 1.5e-5 – 3.6e-5.
4. `freedom_credit` and `freedom_years_gain` (judgments, overlapping): 2.1e-5 – 3.9e-5 each. Findings 3 and 6.
5. `freedom_ls_gain` (judgment anchored on Pawłowska-Muc et al. 2023): 2.0e-5 – 3.3e-5.

What would most improve the model: the people rows are the whole result. Get them on observed counts (9,500 owners), give them stated durations, discounting and one counterfactual question, and give each anchored judgment a published reference class (findings 1–3, 6). Then add the human side of population management (dog bites and PEP, finding 4), which on the model's own neutering rows is likely worth as much as everything now counted. The dog branches matter little at the default weight on animal suffering.

Findings: 4 high, 5 medium, 4 low.
