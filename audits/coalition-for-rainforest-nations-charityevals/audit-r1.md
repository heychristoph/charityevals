Template choice (checked; no finding). The conservation template is right. The climate family sends forest protection to conservation's climate branch, and the site's advocacy models of land wins use it too: Earthjustice biodiversity, EDF health-nature and Sierra Club lands-health are all conservation with past_average. Forest kept carries the shared pandemic-risk rows in every forest model (Cool Earth, Rainforest Foundation US, The Nature Conservancy). A policy-research-meta-climate model in tco2e would have left that channel out, against one consistent way. The pandemic rows match Cool Earth's and RFUS's word for word: risk per hectare of the tropics' yearly loss, × lasting hectares kept (tonnes after permanence and leakage ÷ 650 t/ha), × the discounted year. `estimate_basis: past_average` with `@next_dollar.coalition_for_rainforest_nations` in both credit rows passes INV-18.

fuel_share (checked; exact). The bridge applies `air_pollution_weight` to `tco2e_bridged`. `fuel_share × tco2e_bridged` = 5.5326 × (−754.44) = −4,174.0 t. That equals the buyers' credited extra tonnes, 5,000,000 × 0.92456 (protection_years) × 9.0292e-4 (itmo_credit) = 4,174.0 t, with the sign that makes their smoke a harm. So smoke counts on the buyers' tonnes alone, at `fuel_smoke_factor`. The construction holds only because both branches share `protection_years`, `$missing_data_discount` and the next-dollar factor, which the explainer says. It reads no shared assumption, and INV-2b passes. If either branch's missing-data marker ever becomes 1, the weighting must take those factors in. The ratio is undefined only when the net credited tonnes are exactly 0, which a draw never hits.

## Drivers
1. `rbp_evidence_adj` (judgment, 0.2–1): how far Guyana's forward-looking contract holds for payments for past results. The anchor is The Nature Conservancy's 0.4–0.75 for rules judged from other places. A second evaluation of a jurisdictional programme (for example, an evaluation of a Carbon Fund ERPA country) would improve it most.
2. `@catastrophe.deforestation_share_of_land_use_risk` (shared judgment, 0.1–1).
3. `permanence_share` (judgment, 0.2–0.9): same as the other forest models.
4. `fuel_smoke_factor` (judgment, 0.01–0.14), together with `itmo_substitution_share` (judgment, 0.2–0.9): who buys the credits and what they would otherwise do (finding 4). A sourced split of Article 6 forest buyers would improve both.
5. `cost_per_tco2_avoided` (estimate, Roopsind et al.): in the wrong price year (finding 2).

Once finding 1 is built, the climate-finance branch's credit share and the finance the goal adds would become the leading drivers. Both need named anchors.

Verdict: fail
