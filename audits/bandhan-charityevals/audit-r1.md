## Drivers
1. `uov_per_daly` (shared judgment, 1–4): 10.99 to 2.76 across its range; the site's slider, not the builder's.
2. `discount_rate` (shared judgment): 7.65 to 4.14.
3. `thp_evidence_adj` (judgment 0.75, 0.5–0.95; its anchor is three named factors: take-up 0.9, transfer to today's programme 0.95, internal validity 0.9): 3.76 to 6.90. The Bihar trial's 27–30% effect is a good reference class for the transfer factor.
4. `uov_per_ln_consumption` (shared estimate): 4.14 to 6.88.
5. `thp_itt_10y` (observed, 5.7–22.4): 4.24 to 6.60; then `tail_years` (judgment 10 years, 0–20) and `fade_rate`, which together set about a third of THP's value after year 10, where nothing is measured.
Best improvements: the migration-inclusive column (finding 1), the cost of the bank-training service (finding 4), a sourced anchor for the tail (a follow-up of the 2007 trial beyond year 10, or the Bihar trial's later waves).

Plausibility: THP is 95% of the outcome on 57% of the spending; the other six branches (43% of spending) add about 5%, each discounted for missing data. THP's cost is about ₹47,000 per enrolled household (₹59,000 with overhead, about $709) against the trial's US$2,163 at 2018 PPP (about ₹43,000 in 2018 prices): consistent. The trial's own benefit-cost ratio is 379% by year 10; recomputing from the participants' effects (×1/0.5175, 3.9 people) gives about 4.9, and the model's 5.5 years of healthy life per $1,000 sits on this. The result is about 1.4 times the site's other cash-based income models in the audit's peer list (3.96 and 3.83 per $1,000), far above the global-health median only because that median is made of advocacy and research models; no row is wrong for that reason, the gap is the programme's (ln gain 2.66 per person over a 10-year trial plus a tail, on a cost of about $180 per person). No site text compares: the write-up is clean (no peers, medians or multiples); sources match the rows; the summary figure is live (`data-figure`).
