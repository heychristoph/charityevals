**Far above the cause median (lint medium): the result is right.** The global-health median (0.0686) is set by the many broad charities the site models. This model is a money mover whose money goes mostly to GiveWell-type charities. Its value per dollar moved is 0.0417 uov (0.0378 net of shifted money), within the range of its main recipients' ranked figures (Against Malaria Foundation 0.045, Helen Keller 0.082, Development Media International 0.068, GiveDirectly 0.0099). The chain multiplies that by an effective factor of about 1.04: 2.79 dollars caused per dollar of cost × 1.05 side effects × 0.91 credit × 0.996 wait × 0.39 next dollar. So the headline is roughly "a dollar to The Life You Can Save does about what a dollar to its recipients does". That follows from its 9.2× gross leverage after the shared 30% own effect and the 39% next dollar. The gap comes from the destinations, read live, not from scope, reach or persistence. The money moved is the charity's own reconciled channels, one year against one year's cost. Among the site's other movers in uov it sits at 19.6, between One for the World (12.8) and Effektiv Spenden (23.7). For this audit only, never for site text.

## Drivers
1. `@next_dollar.the_life_you_can_save`, 39% (14–80%): a TLYCS-specific estimate (room × use × run). Use 0.55 is anchored on the 2024→2025 increment (4.8× against 9.2×). Room is the weak part (finding 2): reading the Australian entity's balance sheet would improve it most.
2. `@meta.own_effect_share`, 30% (15–45%): the shared estimate from Giving What We Can's donor surveys. The charity publishes no survey of its own, so its own donor survey ("helps fill some, but not all, of the gaps", annual report) with a counterfactual question would replace it.
3. `$resource_shares` (slider): with credit by share of resources, the donors' new money ($3.6M) and staff's forgone pay share the outcome.
4. Destinations' shared sliders (`daly_per_child_death`, `discount_rate`, `uov_per_daly`, `@meta.prior_vs_cash`, `estimate_age_discount`): these work through the 13 live recipients. 53% of the money (20 charities and the fund) has no model and takes their average, so a model of Fistula Foundation, Educate Girls, Living Goods, Sanku or Village Enterprise (the largest unmodelled ones) would sharpen the value of a dollar moved.
5. `reported_influence_completeness_adj` 1.25 (0.9–2) and `moved_side_effects` 5% (−5% to +20%): judgments with stated anchors, each moving the result by under 15%.

The model's own ranges give 15.9–26.3. That is narrow because most of the uncertainty sits in the shared figures and sliders, which is as the site intends.
