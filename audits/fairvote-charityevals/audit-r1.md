Result: 3.253e-5 daly/$ = 0.0325 years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars (combined range 0.0019–0.54); cause median 0.0686 (×0.47). Verdict: fix needed.

Scope: FairVote (the 501(c)(3)), calendar 2024. The Form 990 lists one programme (Part III), $4,334,842 of $6,405,004 total expenses. The model covers all of it as two branches: single-winner ranked-choice voting (75%) and proportional representation (25%). charities/fairvote.json `spending` and its one line match the return. FairVote Action, the 501(c)(4) on Schedule R, files separately and is correctly left out of the cost; it is counted in the RCV movement's denominator.
Mechanical: lint 0/0/0; invariants none; normalization-audit none (generated/ restored); check.ts all models valid; simulation stable (5% 0.154×, median 1.00×, 95% 7.18× the best guess).
Previous round: none (first round).

Credit for proportional representation (PR) is divided consistently between the two models. Both divide by the same three-part denominator: FairVote's 2024 total expenses × fairvote_pr_share, Protect Democracy's 2024 total expenses × protect_democracy_pr_share, and us_pr_other_resources_per_year. Each model's own `cost_total` equals the shared expenses figure it puts in the other model's denominator ($6,405,004; $34,368,875). The shares are about 20.8% (FairVote) and 31.2% (Protect Democracy), with 48% left for the other groups, so they sum to 100%. Both models read the same reach, gain share, wait, evidence and repeal figures. Two things are not consistent. First, the side effects each model puts on the same PR win (finding 2). Second, the reach figure both read is a forward expectation, not the past record (finding 1).
