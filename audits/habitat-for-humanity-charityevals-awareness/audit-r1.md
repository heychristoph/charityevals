## Drivers
1. he_recipient_share (judgment, 0.5; the anchor is the national bottom 40%'s share of the survey mean, 0.28–0.59, though informal-settlement residents are urban): the result runs +0.013 to -0.009 across its range.
2. he_delivery_share (judgment, 0.6, no anchor; finding 4).
3. he_payer_multiple (judgment, 1.13, Bachas et al. 2024; finding 9).
4. @public_funds.mcpf and @aid_value.inkind_housing_vs_cash (shared).
5. unmodelled_benefits_adj (judgment, +3%; finding 3).

The households' gain and the payers' cost nearly cancel, so the sign rests on the channels counted. What would most improve the model: findings 1, 2 and 4, then a sourced reference class for he_recipient_share, for example urban consumption from the countries' own household surveys (Kenya's KIHBS for Nairobi, the Philippines' FIES for urban areas) for the bottom half of urban households.

Peers: the result is under a tenth of the global-health median (0.100) because the money is netted against what it costs those who pay. That is the site's rule (2.20), applied correctly. Within the site's rules, findings 1 and 4 are what separate this model from its siblings.
