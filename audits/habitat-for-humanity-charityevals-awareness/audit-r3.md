**he_recipient_share's narrowed range (0.25–0.75).** It is right on its evidence. The share is in real terms, because ln gains need real consumption, and it is one average over 13 countries, so its 90% range should be narrower than any one country's. The anchor is national bottom-40% shares from 0.28 to 0.59 (facts/survey_consumption.json notes; about 0.43 on average). That puts 0.5 at the bottom 40% plus a modest urban premium. Slum households in African cities spend more in money terms than the bottom 40% does. In Nairobi slums, mean household spending is KES 13,957 a month (PMC3888389), and 73% fall below an urban poverty line. But urban prices take most of that premium: Kenya's KIHBS 2015/16 poverty lines are KSh 5,995 per adult equivalent a month in core-urban areas against KSh 3,252 in rural and peri-urban areas, 1.84 times as high. Latin America's and Romania's and North Macedonia's informal and Roma settlements sit nearer the bottom of their distributions. So a real share above 0.75 on average over the 13 countries is unlikely, and 0.9 was too high. The low end, 0.25, is at the poorest country's bottom 40%, also defensible for an average. The explainer's "urban premium of about a quarter" is unsourced, so it should name the price-level reason (finding 5).
