**Peers (the `far-above` lint).** At 1.036 the result is ×11.05 the global-health median (0.0938). The fix for #1 would raise it to about 1.24 (×13). I followed the lint's instructions and checked scope, reach and the shared per-death figures. The result is right, for the reasons below. This note belongs in the audit only and stays out of the summary and the write-up.
- **Scope and reach.** The cost and the reach come from the same year, FY2025. The cost is the audited International line × the Schedule F measles share ($4.295m with overhead). The reach is 3 million households from the annual report (p. 8), verified in round 1. That is $1.43 a household and $34 per extra child vaccinated (125,770 extra children, with `counterfactual_adj` 0.031 and `implementation_adj` 0.9). The cost isn't another programme's and the reach isn't cumulative.
- **Risk.** The measles death rate is 0.00132 per unvaccinated child-year. It reconciles with the African region alone: 75,942 deaths (MMWR 73(45) Table 2) × 0.826 under five × 0.7 unvaccinated ÷ (11 million unvaccinated a year × 3 cohorts) = 0.00133. So `msl_setting_risk` 1.4 is the region's average and not inflated.
- **Shared figures.** `value_per_death` 118.05 = 0.899 × `@moral_weights.uov_per_death_under5` 116.25 + 0.101 × `uov_per_death_age_5_14` 134, at `$life_value_multiplier` 1. The indirect-death ratio (0.75), herd immunity (0.125), fewer infections passed on (0.06), illness (0.06), treatment costs and income investment are all the site's shared vaccine figures. new-incentives reads the same set.
- **Why the gap.** The model implies $26.6k per death averted before evidence, credit, waits and the missing-data step, and $86k after them. The steep cuts are all applied: 3% of children counted as extra, 0.5 for missing data, 0.80 credit, 0.85 evidence. The global-health median is set mostly by care in high-income countries and by advocacy and research models. Models of child vaccination in low-income settings on the same chain run from 0.69 (Doctors Without Borders, charityevals) to 3.98 (New Incentives, GiveWell). The gap is the setting's measles mortality, valued at the site's one value of a child's death, for a cheap visit. No row is wrong beyond #1–#3, and those raise the result.
