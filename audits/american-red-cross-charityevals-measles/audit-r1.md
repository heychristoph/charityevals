## Drivers
1. `$missing_data_discount` (shared slider, 0.07–0.72). The thin marking is right: the reach is the Red Cross's own count, but the visit's effect is a self-reported survey without a comparison group.
2. `msl_report_to_behaviour` (judgment 0.12, 0.04–0.38; 0.12–1.03). It is anchored on Benin's refusal conversions as a floor and on coverage cross-checks (Yitbarek RR 1.19; 92–95% campaign coverage). Its reference class is adequate.
3. `msl_setting_risk` (judgment 1.4, 0.8–2.5), derived from MMWR Table 2 (verified).
4. `@american_red_cross.msl_spend_share` (estimate 0.08, 0.03–0.15), from Form 990 Schedule F (verified).
5. `daly_per_child_death` (shared value of a young child's life).
Then `msl_years_unvaccinated` (judgment without an anchor, #4) and `msl_zero_dose_share`.

The result is ×3.8 the global-health median, within 10×. No peer gap needs explaining. Cost per household ($1.43 with overhead, $0.55 on the grants alone) is plausible against the Kenyan operation's scale (1,952 volunteers for 164,643 households).

What would most improve the model:
- The Red Cross's (or the partnership's) post-campaign coverage surveys comparing visited and unvisited households, which would make the counterfactual measured rather than a discounted self-report.
- The FY2025 list of campaign countries and their target age bands, which would settle `beneficiaries`, `msl_setting_risk` and the older-children share in #2.

Verdict: fix needed (1 high: #1).
