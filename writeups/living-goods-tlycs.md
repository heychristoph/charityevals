### Summary

Living Goods supports community health workers in Kenya, Uganda and Burkina Faso who treat sick children at home and follow pregnant women. The Life You Can Save estimates the children's and mothers' deaths averted by the rise in treatments and pregnancy registrations since an earlier year, at $52 per DALY across the three countries; counting the whole programme's cost, valuing the deaths the site's way and adding family planning, that is **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**.

The model reproduces every cell of The Life You Can Save's sheet. The Life You Can Save calls its figures highly approximate, and Living Goods says the 2024 figures come from an earlier version of its impact tool.

### What the charity does

Living Goods trains, equips and supervises the governments' community health workers, with a smartphone app that guides diagnosis and follow-up. In 2024 its 9,996 health workers assessed and treated 1.54 million children and supported 111,000 pregnant women, for 21.3 million dollars of programme spending (its Form 990).

### How the impact happens

1. **Treatment at home:** children with malaria, pneumonia or diarrhoea are treated the same day rather than late or not at all. The Life You Can Save credits Living Goods with the rise in treatments since 2015 (2023 in Burkina Faso), split between the three illnesses.
2. **Fewer child deaths:** each illness's expected deaths among those children, cut by 23% to 32% (studies of community treatment): 833 deaths averted.
3. **Fewer maternal deaths:** pregnant women registered by health workers are taken to cut maternal deaths by 30%: 52 deaths averted.
4. **Family planning (our step):** the health workers in Kenya and Uganda provided 242,000 more couple-years of contraception than in 2019.

### How the model works

The Life You Can Save's sheet comes first, one section per country. Our steps follow:

- **Pooled by money.** The sheet gives three country figures and no total; we divide all the cost by all the DALYs.
- **The whole programme's cost.** The sheet's costs are only the learning sites' devices and supplies, 1.3 million dollars, while the treatments are those of all the health workers: the 1.54 million children in the sheet are exactly the programme's total in Living Goods' Form 990. We count the programme's spending in the share of the treatments credited, with management and fundraising on top: about 13 times the sheet's cost.
- **Deaths valued the site's way**, children's and mothers' alike.
- **Family planning** valued with the site's shared figure for a year of contraception, half of it counted as what wouldn't have happened anyway (our judgment).
- **Side effect:** illness shortened and hospital stays avoided, 5% (our judgment).

### What drives the result

- The cost: the sheet's devices and supplies against the whole programme's spending.
- The deaths averted per treatment, from case-fatality rates and studies of community treatment rather than a trial of Living Goods.
- How much of the family planning would have happened anyway.

### Where we differ from The Life You Can Save

- **Cost:** the programme's spending from Living Goods' accounts (about 16.9 million dollars for the treatments credited) rather than the learning sites' 1.3 million.
- **Deaths:** valued by the site's weights for a death under five and at 15 to 49, not 28.5 and 24 to 24.6 discounted years of life.
- **Family planning**, **pooling**, the **side effect** and, with credit by share of resources on, the governments' share and staff's pay below market: steps the sheet leaves out.

### Biggest uncertainties

- The rise in treatments since 2015 is the programme's growth, not a comparison with what would have happened without it.
- Uganda's pregnancy registrations fell since 2015; the sheet counts all of 2024's as added.
- Living Goods' costs by country aren't public; the share of spending behind the credited treatments is our estimate.

### What isn't modelled

- Newborn care visits and immunisation follow-up by the health workers.
- Living Goods' work on government financing of community health.
