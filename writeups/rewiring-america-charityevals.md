### Summary

Rewiring America works to get American homes off gas, oil and propane and onto heat pumps and other electric appliances. It designed the Inflation Reduction Act's home-electrification rebates and runs free tools that show households what upgrades cost after incentives. Our model counts its share of the law's home-energy provisions and the households its tools and coaches bring to switch sooner, against a typical year's spending in 2021-2024. It values the tonnes those switches cut, the public money the law pays households, the households' own net gain or loss and cleaner indoor air. That comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per $1,000**, because the law's $8.8 billion of rebates, mostly for low- and moderate-income homes, and three years of larger home credits count as public money won for households: nearly all of the result. The tonnes give about 3% of it. The result is very uncertain: nothing is measured for its work, and its part in the law is our judgment.

### What the charity does

Rewiring America was a project of the Windward Fund from 2020 until 1 July 2024. Its 2024 Form 990, its first of its own, shows $22.7 million spent, $17.1 million of it on its one programme. That covers half a year of its own operations plus $7.4 million paid to the Windward Fund to settle earlier costs. Its affiliate, the Rewiring Community Investment Fund, repaid it $4.6 million of shared programme costs, so the affiliate's own work is not in this cost. The return describes:

- **Consumer tools and education.** An incentives calculator, a personal planner and a savings tool, used by over 200,000 households in 2024; "Why Electrify" courses and workshops.
- **Coaching and community work.** 990 electrification coaches trained in a four-week course, and demonstration projects in five communities.
- **States, cities and utilities.** Free technical analyses for governments applying for funds and for utility programmes and rates.
- **Research and industry.** Reports on health and jobs, a free database of 3,500 incentives, and deals with manufacturers that it says cut heat-pump prices for its programmes by 40%.

In May 2025 it laid off 28% of its staff after the federal government froze the $2 billion Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund award it shared with four other groups.

### How the impact happens

Rewiring America installs almost nothing itself. It changes what households and governments do. The model has two branches:

- **The law.** The law's $4.5 billion electrification rebates follow the appliance rebate plan Rewiring America published in June 2021. Together with the $4.3 billion efficiency rebates and the larger home credits, we judge that these provisions are about 2% of the law's projected 2030 cut, about 18 million tonnes a year. Rewiring America gets 35% of the advocacy behind the rebates, which follow its plan, and 10% behind the credits, mainly the solar industry's ask: about 14% of the provisions' tonnes, 0.3% of the whole law. The Union of Concerned Scientists gets 1% and the Environmental Defense Fund 2%.
- **Tools and coaching.** Of 200,000 tool users a year, we judge that 1.5% switch to a heat pump, or appliances worth as much, sooner than they would have, and so does one in four coaches. Each switch cuts 3.2 tonnes a year (NREL's simulation of the US housing stock, refrigerant leaks taken off) for 7 years, until the household would have switched anyway.

The law also pays households, and each switch changes the household's own money and, where a gas stove goes, its indoor air:

- **Public money won.** The rebates ($8.8 billion, of which we judge 55% will reach households) go to homes under 150% of the local median income. The larger credits would not have existed without the law after 2023: about $3.8 billion more than before in 2023 and $8.4 billion a year in 2024-2025. Like every model of public money won, a dollar counts as the recipients' gain less what it costs the taxpayers. A dollar to a rebate household, at 0.6 of the average American's spending, is worth about twice what it costs them; a dollar of the larger credits, claimed mostly by better-off households, slightly less than it costs.
- **Households' net gain.** Bills saved less the heat pump's extra cost, spread over its life, at prices before incentives. A household the law's subsidies tipped into switching would not have done it unsubsidised, so we put its net at −$120 a year (−$350 to +$50). For those the tools brought to switch sooner, $50 (−$150 to +$300). Only the heat-pump switches count, 60% of the law's tonnes.
- **Indoor air.** About 15% of the heat-pump switches also replace a gas stove. Gas stoves are linked to 12.7% of US children's asthma (Gruenwald et al. 2022), which we count at half, since the evidence is observational and disputed.

### How the model works

This is a past average: the period's wins against the period's spending. The law counts the shared years a won reform counts, after the shared three-year wait for rules and credits. Its tonnes take the shared steps every model counting the law uses: the share of a won policy due to advocacy at all (30%), the share that wouldn't have happened anyway (80%) and the share delivered after the 2025 budget law (35%). That law ended both home credits after 2025, and DOE's May 2026 guidance ended the electrification rebates for switching from gas, so we take 0.7 of the law's delivered share for these provisions. The rebates count once, discounted to the middle of their payouts (about 2027-28); the credits count the shared years a won reform counts, at most the three years they paid. Both take the same advocacy, counterfactual, credit and missing-data steps. The law's other provisions that follow Rewiring America's asks are left out: the Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund's awards were terminated, and the heat-pump factory money is small.

The tools' switches take 80% for overlap with the law's projected uptake of its own credits, and 50% for the contractors, rebate programmes and volunteers who also brought them about, of which Rewiring America's own share of the cost it ran with its affiliate is {{row:tools_own_cost_share}}. Both branches take the evidence step (80%) and the shared missing-data step. The rest of the work, mainly manufacturer deals, the incentives database and advice to states, adds 20% to the tonnes. The households' money and the asthma averted go into tonnes only through the site's fixed bridges, so they move with their own sliders.

The total is divided by a typical year's spending in 2021-2024. Those years are inside the Windward Fund's accounts, so we judge the average year at 0.9 of 2024's reported cost. The tonnes carry a little more air pollution than US power's, since gas is no longer burned at ground level, and 15% of them are upstream methane. The next dollar is valued at {{row:marginal_vs_past_average}} of the average: the once-only win is in, its home credits are gone, and new money goes to state work on electricity costs.

### What drives the result

The public money won carries nearly all of the result, so the steps it takes drive it: the shared missing-data step, advocacy's share of a won policy, the next-dollar figure, Rewiring America's share of the advocacy behind the rebates, how much of the rebate money is paid, the shared value of a healthy year against income, and how poor the rebate households are. The tonnes give about 3% of the result. The net loss of the households the subsidies tipped into switching would, on its own, put the tonnes' part below zero: a dollar a year to an American household counts for more than these tonnes' share of it.

The public money counts at Rewiring America's large share of the advocacy behind it.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How much of the law is its doing.** Its plan is in the law, but the home provisions passed as part of a much larger deal that unions, industry and other groups pushed. Its share is our judgment: 35% (15% to 60%) of the advocacy behind the rebates, 10% (3% to 25%) behind the credits.
- **Who gets the money.** The rebates are for low- and moderate-income homes, but the states' programmes are slow to open, keep part for administration and had to be reworked after DOE's 2026 guidance. Two-thirds of the residential clean energy credit went to incomes of $100,000 or more.
- **Whether switching pays.** Bills fall in most homes, but heat pumps cost more to install, and gas-heated homes save least. No study gives the net for the homes these programmes moved.
- **Whether the tools change decisions.** No one has measured how many users switch because of them.

### What isn't modelled

The affiliate's Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund work is its own organisation's and was frozen in 2025. Solar and insulation households' own net gain is left out, since no figure gives it before the credit.

### How this model was checked

Independent audits are in audits/rewiring-america-charityevals/. Round 1 found the households' net gain left out on a wrong reason: on the site's own value of consumption, a household's dollars count for more than these tonnes. It is now valued in its own unit and bridged in. Round 1 also moved the indoor-air benefit from a share of the tonnes' value to healthy years from asthma averted, bridged in the same way. The home provisions' share of the law fell from 2.5% to 2%, the tools now count only Rewiring America's share of the cost it ran with its affiliate, and the period's start rests on the June 2021 rebate plan. Those changes lowered the result from 0.0009 to 0.00075. Round 2 found the public money the law pays households valued at zero, against the site's rule for public money won. Counting it as the recipients' gain less the taxpayers' cost raised the result fortyfold, to 0.030. It also moved the subsidised households' net gain to a loss, so that the subsidy isn't counted twice, and counted only heat-pump tonnes for it. The methane share rose to 15%, since NREL's tonnes include gas leaks. Round 3 found the credits' increase half counted (before the law both credits were due to lapse after 2023, so all of 2024-2025's credits are the law's), the rebates' delivery out of date after DOE's May 2026 guidance, and the credits not counting the shared years a won reform counts. It now gives the rebates and the credits their own shares of the advocacy (35% and 10%). Together these lowered the result to 0.028. Later the law's public-money figures became shared facts, and the credits are now counted year by year for any number of years counted.
