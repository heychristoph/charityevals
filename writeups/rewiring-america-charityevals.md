### Summary

Rewiring America works to get American homes off gas, oil and propane and onto heat pumps and other electric appliances. It designed the Inflation Reduction Act's home-electrification rebates and runs free tools that show households what upgrades cost after incentives. Our model counts its share of the law's home-energy provisions and the households its tools and coaches bring to switch sooner, against a typical year's spending in 2021-2024. That comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="rewiring-america-charityevals">0.0009</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**, about 0.004 tonnes of CO₂e averted per dollar. About 95% of it comes from the law. The result is very uncertain: nobody has measured the tonnes its work saved, so the tonnes are projections and its part in them our judgment.

### What the charity does

Rewiring America was a project of the Windward Fund from 2020 until 1 July 2024. Its 2024 Form 990, its first of its own, shows $22.7 million spent, $17.1 million of it on its one programme. That covers half a year of its own operations plus $7.4 million paid to the Windward Fund to settle earlier costs. Its affiliate, the Rewiring Community Investment Fund, repaid it $4.6 million of shared costs, so the affiliate's own work is not in this cost. The return describes:

- **Consumer tools and education.** An incentives calculator, a personal planner and a savings tool, used by over 200,000 households in 2024; "Why Electrify" courses and workshops.
- **Coaching and community work.** 990 electrification coaches trained in a four-week course, and demonstration projects in five communities.
- **States, cities and utilities.** Free technical analyses for governments applying for funds and for utility programmes and rates.
- **Research and industry.** Reports on health and jobs, a free database of 3,500 incentives, and deals with manufacturers that it says cut heat-pump prices for its programmes by 40%.

In May 2025 it laid off 28% of its staff after the federal government froze the $2 billion Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund award it shared with four other groups.

### How the impact happens

Rewiring America installs almost nothing itself. It changes what households and governments do. The model has two branches:

- **The law.** The law's $4.5 billion electrification rebates follow Rewiring America's own plan. Together with the $4.3 billion efficiency rebates and the larger home credits, we judge that these provisions are about 2.5% of the law's projected 2030 cut, about 22 million tonnes a year. Rewiring America gets 25% of the advocacy behind them, which is about 0.6% of the whole law. The Union of Concerned Scientists gets 1% and the Environmental Defense Fund 2%.
- **Tools and coaching.** Of 200,000 tool users a year, we judge that 1.5% switch to a heat pump, or appliances worth as much, sooner than they would have, and so does one in four coaches. Each switch cuts 3.2 tonnes a year (NREL's simulation of the US housing stock, refrigerant leaks taken off) for 7 years, until the household would have switched anyway.

### How the model works

This is a past average: the period's wins against the period's spending. The law counts the shared years a won reform counts, after the shared three-year wait for rules and credits. Its tonnes take the shared steps every model counting the law uses: the share of a won policy due to advocacy at all (30%), the share that wouldn't have happened anyway (80%) and the share delivered after the 2025 budget law (35%). That law ended both home credits after 2025 and left the rebates funded, so we take 0.8 of the law's delivered share for these provisions.

The tools' switches take 80% for overlap with the law's projected uptake of its own credits, and 50% for the contractors, rebate programmes and volunteers who also brought them about. Both branches take the evidence step (80%) and the shared missing-data step. Then come two side effects: the rest of the work, mainly manufacturer deals, the incentives database and advice to states (+20%), and cleaner indoor air where a gas stove goes (+5%).

The total is divided by a typical year's spending in 2021-2024. Those years are inside the Windward Fund's accounts, so we judge the average year at 0.9 of 2024's reported cost. The tonnes carry a little more air pollution than US power's, since gas is no longer burned at ground level, and 10% of them are upstream methane. The next dollar is valued at 40% of the average: the once-only win is in, its home credits are gone, and new money goes to state work on electricity costs.

### What drives the result

The law carries about 95% of the result. The biggest swings are the shared discount rate and years a won reform counts, the deaths per tonne, the next-dollar figure, the home provisions' share of the law and Rewiring America's share of the advocacy behind them. The result is about half the climate median: these provisions are a small part of a law whose credit is spread over many groups, and much of the law has since been cut back.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How much of the law is its doing.** Its plan is in the law, but the home provisions passed as part of a much larger deal that unions, industry and other groups pushed. Its share is our judgment, 10% to 50%.
- **How many tonnes the home provisions bring.** No model splits the law's projected cut by provision, and the states' rebate programmes are slow to open. We use 1% to 6% of the law's cut.
- **Whether the tools change decisions.** No one has measured how many users switch because of them.

### What isn't modelled

Lower bills for the households that switch (most save money, NREL finds) are left out: a few hundred dollars a year for a US household is very little on the site's scale. The affiliate's Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund work is its own organisation's and was frozen in 2025. Climate damage other than deaths is left at the shared zero.
