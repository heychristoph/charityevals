### Summary

Founders Pledge is a community of entrepreneurs who pledge part of their exit proceeds to charity. It researches where to give, advises its members and a few ultra-wealthy Philanthropy Partners, and runs donor-advised funds and public funds. Our model of all its work in 2025 comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="founders-pledge-charityevals">8.7</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**. For each dollar it spends, about sixteen dollars go to the charities it recommends. The share of that money it caused is valued at what the site's models of its grantees and causes achieve. Most of the value comes from its health and education giving and from members' other giving it caused; its climate giving adds little, because the site's climate charities rank far below its global-health charities in healthy years.

### What the charity does

Founders Pledge works through three entities: Founders Pledge Ltd, a UK charity, its US 501(c)(3) Founders Pledge Inc, and Founders Pledge gGmbH in Germany. The UK charity's audited accounts for 2025 consolidate all three. The group spent £250 million in 2025, of which £241 million were grants from its funds. Those grants are donors' money and count as the recipients' cost. Its own costs were £9.1 million, about $12.1 million at the year's average rate, mostly the pay of its 65 staff.

Its work is one programme of directing members' and donors' money:

- **The pledge and member community.** 275 new members pledged $1.54 billion in 2025; members give through its donor-advised funds in the UK and US. It held 26 events.
- **Research and advice.** It had 65 live recommendations at the end of 2025, its "high-impact funding opportunities". Advisers help members build giving portfolios.
- **Philanthropy Partnerships.** It researches questions for ultra-wealthy donors, most of them not members, and recommends charities. Five new partnerships gave $88 million to its recommendations in 2025.
- **Public funds.** The Climate Fund granted $38.8 million in 2025, the Rapid Response Fund (launched with The Life You Can Save after US foreign aid stopped) over $13 million, the Global Catastrophic Risks Fund $1.7 million and the Catalytic Impact Fund $1.3 million.

### How the impact happens

Its community gave $359 million in 2025. Of that, $196 million went to the charities it recommends, from three kinds of giver:

- **Its funds** ($54.8 million of grants). Founders Pledge publishes no survey of its donors, so the share it caused is the site's shared figure for a meta charity without one: 30%, from 15% to 45%. Half of that counts as new money. The other half would have been given anyway, so it counts only for the gain over where it would have gone.
- **Members' own grants** (about $62 million: the rest of the $196 million). These are valued the same way, as pledgers' giving is for Giving What We Can.
- **Philanthropy Partners' advised giving** ($88 million, less a judged 10% that may have gone through its funds and is counted there). The same shared 30% is read as the share whose destination its advice changed. This money would have been given anyway, so it counts only for the gain over an ordinary charity of the same causes. It also takes the site's evidence and missing-data steps, because nobody counts what its advice changed. This is the one branch discounted for missing data.

A dollar it directs is worth what its grantees achieve, read live under your values. Its impact report splits the $196 million by cause: climate 31.5%, education 31.1%, health and economic empowerment 27.4%, system change 4.6%, global catastrophic risks 2.8% and global security 1.0%. Within each cause, its audited list of 2025 grants of £1 million or more names the grantees:

- **Health and economic empowerment** is valued at its grantees with a figure on the rankings (Evidence Action, Clinton Health Access Initiative, Fika, Precision Development and Helen Keller Intl), weighted by their 2025 grants. Its other grantees there, such as Raising the Village, take their average.
- **Climate** is valued at Clean Air Task Force, and **catastrophic risks** at SecureBio, each its cause's one grantee with a model here.
- **Education** has no grantee with a published model here. Education charities are filed under global health, so education takes the global-health charities on the rankings, with equal weights.
- **System change, global security** and the small unlabelled slices have no model here, so they take the average of the rest.

Its members also gave $163 million to charities it doesn't recommend. The share it caused that is new money is valued at an ordinary charity's worth, as Giving What We Can's model values its donors' giving below its bar, after taking out transfers to other donor-advised funds.

Shifted money would otherwise have gone to an ordinary charity of the same causes, valued in the same mix. That is the site's shared rule for an organisation moving money across causes.

### How the model works

The cost is the group's direct and support costs, without the grants. The money is the year's flow, set against the year's costs. A pledge fulfilled in 2025 counts in 2025, and the year's new pledges aren't counted on top, so nothing is counted twice.

A 3% side effect is added, with a range of −5% to +20% (our judgment). On the plus side, its public research and funds guide other platforms' donors, and its pledge spreads a norm of giving among founders. On the minus side, money can sit in donor-advised funds for years, and a few advisers steering much private money narrow a field's views. The recipients' own risks are already in their figures. The result is then multiplied by the next dollar's figure, 33% of the 2025 average. Credit by share of resources, off by default, would share the result with staff's forgone pay and with new money its fund donors and members gave.

### What drives the result

- **The next dollar** (33%, from 14% to 71%). Founders Pledge holds 20 months of operating costs in reserve, above its own 15-month minimum, and plans to grow through donors further from its members.
- **The share of giving it caused** (30%, from 15% to 45%): the site's shared figure, since it publishes no donor survey.
- **The discount rate and the value of a life**, through the charities it funds.
- **How much weight side effects get**, since members' other giving is a side effect.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How much of the giving it causes.** No survey of its members or partners is published.
- **The value of its education giving.** None of its education grantees has a published model here, so they take the global-health charities' average.
- **How the $196 million splits between funds, members and partners.** The accounts give the partners' $88 million in one sentence, and part of it may be fund money.

### What isn't modelled

- The future giving of the year's new pledges, beyond the year's flow of fulfilled pledges.
- Interest on money held in its funds before granting.

### How this model was checked

Audit rounds are in audits/founders-pledge-charityevals/. Round 1 checked every cost and reach figure against the scanned accounts and the impact report, and found two high points, both fixed:

- Its grantees with a model here are now read at their own figures within each cause, from the accounts' list of 2025 grants, in place of the cause averages (health, climate and catastrophic risks).
- Members' giving to charities it doesn't recommend is now a row of its own, at an ordinary charity's value, in place of a share folded into the side effects; the side-effect share fell from 8% to 3%.

It also lowered the judged overlap between partners' giving and fund money (25% to 10%: the large Climate Fund donor had already given in 2024, so isn't a new partner), named The Life You Can Save as the Rapid Response Fund's co-founder, removed an unused row and corrected a quote.
