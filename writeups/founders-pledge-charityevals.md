### Summary

Founders Pledge is a community of entrepreneurs who pledge part of their exit proceeds to charity. It researches where to give, advises its members and a few ultra-wealthy Philanthropy Partners, and runs donor-advised funds and public funds. Our model of all its work in 2025 comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="founders-pledge-charityevals">3.6</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**. For each dollar it spends, about sixteen dollars go to the charities it recommends. The share of that money it caused is valued at what the site's charities in its causes achieve. Most of the value comes from its education and health giving; its climate giving adds little, because the site's climate charities rank far below its global-health charities in healthy years.

### What the charity does

Founders Pledge works through three entities: Founders Pledge Ltd, a UK charity, its US 501(c)(3) Founders Pledge Inc, and Founders Pledge gGmbH in Germany. The UK charity's audited accounts for 2025 consolidate all three. The group spent £250 million in 2025, of which £241 million were grants from its funds. Those grants are donors' money and count as the recipients' cost. Its own costs were £9.1 million, about $12.1 million at the year's average rate, mostly the pay of its 65 staff.

Its work is one programme of directing members' and donors' money:

- **The pledge and member community.** 275 new members pledged $1.54 billion in 2025; members give through its donor-advised funds in the UK and US. It held 26 events.
- **Research and advice.** It had 65 live recommendations at the end of 2025, its "high-impact funding opportunities". Advisers help members build giving portfolios.
- **Philanthropy Partnerships.** It researches questions for ultra-wealthy donors, most of them not members, and recommends charities. Five new partnerships gave $88 million to its recommendations in 2025.
- **Public funds.** The Climate Fund granted $38.8 million in 2025, the Rapid Response Fund over $13 million after US foreign aid stopped, the Global Catastrophic Risks Fund $1.7 million and the Catalytic Impact Fund $1.3 million.

### How the impact happens

Its community gave $359 million in 2025. Of that, $196 million went to the charities it recommends, from three kinds of giver:

- **Its funds** ($54.8 million of grants). Founders Pledge publishes no survey of its donors, so the share it caused is the site's shared figure for a meta charity without one: 30%, from 15% to 45%. Half of that counts as new money. The other half would have been given anyway, so it counts only for the gain over where it would have gone.
- **Members' own grants** (about $75 million: the rest of the $196 million). These are valued the same way, as pledgers' giving is for Giving What We Can.
- **Philanthropy Partners' advised giving** ($88 million, less a judged 25% that may have gone through its funds and is counted there). The same shared 30% is read as the share whose destination its advice changed. This money would have been given anyway, so it counts only for the gain over an ordinary charity of the same causes. It also takes the site's evidence and missing-data steps, because nobody counts what its advice changed. This is the one branch discounted for missing data.

A dollar it directs is worth what its causes' charities achieve, read live under your values. Its impact report splits the $196 million by cause: climate 31.5%, education 31.1%, health and economic empowerment 27.4%, system change 4.6%, global catastrophic risks 2.8% and global security 1.0%. Each cause is valued at the site's ranked charities in it, with equal weights. No education charity has a published model here, and education charities are filed under global health, so education takes the global-health charities' average. System change, global security and the small unlabelled slices have no model here, so they take the average of the rest.

Shifted money would otherwise have gone to an ordinary charity of the same causes, valued in the same mix. That is the site's shared rule for an organisation moving money across causes.

### How the model works

The cost is the group's expenditure less its grants. The money is the year's flow to recommended charities, set against the year's costs. A pledge fulfilled in 2025 counts in 2025, and the year's new pledges aren't counted on top, so nothing is counted twice.

An 8% side effect is added, with a range of −5% to +25% (our judgment). On the plus side, it causes some of its members' other giving: $163 million went to charities it doesn't recommend, and the share it caused, valued as an ordinary charity's, adds about 5%. Its public research and funds also guide other platforms' donors. On the minus side, money can sit in donor-advised funds for years, and a few advisers steering much private money narrow a field's views. The recipients' own risks are already in their figures. The result is then multiplied by the next dollar's figure, 33% of the 2025 average. Credit by share of resources, off by default, would share the result with staff's forgone pay and with new money its fund donors and members gave.

### What drives the result

- **Where shifted money would otherwise have gone** (an ordinary charity of the same causes, from nothing to three times that). Here an ordinary charity is worth nearly two-thirds of the global-health charities' average, so shifted money gains little; at the high end it counts as a loss.
- **The next dollar** (33%, from 14% to 71%). Founders Pledge holds 20 months of operating costs in reserve, above its own 15-month minimum, and plans to grow through donors further from its members.
- **The share of giving it caused** (30%, from 15% to 45%): the site's shared figure, since it publishes no donor survey.
- **The discount rate and the value of a life**, through the charities it funds.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How much of the giving it causes.** No survey of its members or partners is published.
- **The value of its education and health giving.** Most of its recipients in these causes have no published model here, so they take the global-health charities' average. Its own recipients may do better or worse than that average.
- **How the $196 million splits between funds, members and partners.** The accounts give the partners' $88 million in one sentence, and part of it may be fund money.

### What isn't modelled

- The future giving of the year's new pledges, beyond the year's flow of fulfilled pledges.
- Interest on money held in its funds before granting.
