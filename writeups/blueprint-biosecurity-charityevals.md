### Summary

Blueprint Biosecurity works on defences that would protect people against any airborne pandemic: far-UVC light and other ways to clean indoor air, and better respirators. Our model of all its work in 2024 comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per $1,000**. That figure rests on forecasts of how likely catastrophic pandemics are and on an estimate of what a typical dollar of pandemic work does to that risk. Both are deeply uncertain, so the plausible range runs from {{low}} to {{high}}.

### What the charity does

Blueprint was founded in 2023 in Washington, DC, by Jake Swett. It spent $3.1 million in 2024 with ten employees (Form 990; its statements are audited). Its programmes that year:

- **Far-UVC** ($1.14M). Far-UVC is ultraviolet light that kills airborne germs and is thought to be safe for people in the room. Blueprint funded studies on what stands in the way of using it: eye safety (Shimane University, Columbia University), the pollution it can create indoors (MIT, Penn State), how well it kills airborne viruses, and a model of how much it would cut spread (Imperial College London). These grants were awarded in 2024 and paid over 2024 and 2025. It also wrote the Blueprint for Far-UVC, published in 2025.
- **Biosecurity and government affairs** ($0.57M). Strategy, partnerships and briefings across the programmes, including early advocacy for better respirators for critical workers. A government affairs team followed in 2025.
- **Other ways to clean indoor air** ($0.37M). Desk research on glycol vapours, which led to seven research grants in 2026.
- **Protective equipment** ($0.32M). The Next-Gen PPE Blueprint (June 2024, with Gryphon Scientific) argues for stockpiling reusable respirators, which protect better and take up far less space than disposable N95s.

Most of its money comes from Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy).

### How the impact happens

Pandemics carry a yearly chance of catastrophe, spread evenly to 2100, from the experts in a forecasting tournament: about 1% that one ends humanity's future and about 4% that one kills more than a tenth of people. A pandemic as deadly as COVID-19 is also counted, with a 2% chance each year.

Blueprint's spending cuts those risks by the share a typical pandemic project does per dollar, from Rethink Priorities' cross-cause model, its chance of backfiring included. The cut counts for the years such progress would otherwise have taken to come (5 by default), and each year is discounted. Deaths are valued like every death on the site; deaths in a COVID-scale pandemic are valued at COVID's ages, mostly older people. The future lost counts only through the discount rate.

Blueprint's work matters if buildings, hospitals and governments have tools that cut airborne spread from the first day of an outbreak: lights and air cleaning already installed, and respirators already stockpiled for essential workers.

### How the model works

All of Blueprint's spending is pandemic work. The model's AI, nuclear, policy-win and training branches are zero: no measure was won in 2024, and it trains no one.

|---|---|---|
| Uptake of the far-UVC work against a typical project | 0.8 | Studies aimed at the known barriers, but nothing yet deployed because of them |
| Uptake of the respirator work | 0.8 | A costed case for stockpiles; its own PPE lead carried it into a policy memo, and the mentions in Congress's bills came from 2025 work |
| Uptake of the glycol work | 0.6 | Early research on a technology with open safety questions |
| Uptake of the strategy and government work | 0.7 | Typical: no output of its own to judge in 2024 |
| Work no one else would have done now | 0.7 | Coefficient Giving funds some far-UVC studies directly; 1Day Sooner and Johns Hopkins also work on far-UVC |
| Blueprint's share of the resources behind its work | 0.85 | Universities add their own time and overheads to its grants |
| Funging by its main funder | 1 | Counted once, in the next-dollar figure |
| Missing data | slider | Nothing about Blueprint's effect on the risk is measured |

Together the uptake figures come to about 0.75 of a typical project.

Side effects: other benefits add 15%. These tools also fight ordinary infections between pandemics, such as tuberculosis, flu and COVID, net of a small risk that far-UVC adds indoor pollution if used too soon. Speeding up dangerous biology and information hazards subtract nothing: Blueprint studies defences and publishes no threat analyses.

At the end of 2024 Blueprint had under three months of spending in hand. Since then, by its grant records, Coefficient Giving has given it $7.1 million to pass on as research grants, $3.2 million for policy advocacy (shared with its advocacy arm) and $6.4 million of general support over two years, and Blueprint says it raised over $17.5 million in 2025. Money is less of a constraint now. Founders Pledge, which recommends it, rates its team highly. The next dollar counts at {{row:marginal_vs_past_average}} of the past average.

### What drives the result

The result rests on Rethink's typical pandemic project, which averts many more deaths per dollar than a typical AI project. Blueprint's research comes a step before use, and its next dollar counts for less than the past average. Deaths of people alive now are 90% of the value, the future lost 10%.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **The discount rate.** At 0.1% the result is about 25; at 8% it is about 1.4.
- **The years a cut counts.** At one year the result is 0.38; at twenty it is 5.4.
- **How much a typical dollar cuts the risk.** Rethink's figures run ten times either way; the extinction figure alone moves the result from 0.90 to 10.
- **How much the next dollar does.** From 13.5% to 79% of the past average, the result moves from 0.64 to 3.8.
- **Whether far-UVC works and is safe in real buildings.** This is what Blueprint is funding studies to find out; the far-UVC uptake runs from 0.4 to 1.2.

### What isn't modelled

- Blueprint's 2025 and 2026 work, including its larger research grants and its work with Congress, is not valued here. The model counts 2024, the latest year with a tax return.
- A separate advocacy arm that Coefficient Giving has funded is not in Blueprint's accounts and not modelled. Neither is the Mirror Biology Dialogues Fund, a related charity listed on its tax return.
- Losses beyond deaths, such as survivors' lost income and health, are a shared figure set to 0.
- The forecasts are spread evenly over the century.

### How this model was checked

The model was built from the catastrophic-risk template. Round 1 of the independent audit passed, with two medium findings and three low ones, all fixed. A policy memo citing the respirator work turned out to be written by Blueprint's own staff, so that work's uptake fell from 1 to 0.8. The next-dollar figure now cites the right grant records and Founders Pledge's assessment, and rose from 35% to 37%. Two verification rounds checked the changes and passed. Independent audits are in audits/blueprint-biosecurity-charityevals/.
