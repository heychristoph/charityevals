### Summary

The Refugee Council is the UK's national refugee charity. Our three models of its year to March 2025 cover every programme in its audited accounts (£16.9m of charitable spending). The four service lines (£12.25m) come to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="refugee-council-charityevals">0.003</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**; [therapeutic services](/refugee-council/refugee-council-charityevals-therapy) (£1.74m) to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="refugee-council-charityevals-therapy">0.002</span>**; and [advocacy and awareness raising](/refugee-council/refugee-council-charityevals-advocacy) (£2.94m) to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="refugee-council-charityevals-advocacy">0.008</span>**.

All three are far below the site's global-health median, like our other models of social services in rich countries: the people helped live in the UK, where the same help is a smaller gain on the site's one income and wellbeing scales than overseas, and councils, the Home Office or the new government would have provided much of it anyway.

### What the charity does

- **Resettlement** (£5.3m): casework for refugees resettled under government schemes (Afghan programmes, the UK Resettlement Scheme, Homes for Ukraine) under council contracts in Yorkshire, Hertfordshire and Lewisham.
- **Children's Services** (£4.1m): the Home Office-funded reception service at Dover for children arriving alone, advice and youth work for 7,022 children and young people, and challenges to wrong adult age assessments.
- **Integration and Destitution** (£2.8m): help for 3,927 people seeking asylum (asylum support, health care, legal referrals) and newly recognised refugees (housing before asylum support ends, benefits, work).
- **Therapeutic Services** (£1.7m): therapy for 303 refugees, plus MyView for children who arrived alone.
- **Advocacy and Awareness Raising** (£2.9m): research, policy, parliamentary work, campaigns, media and a new brand.

### How the models work

**Services** (domestic-services template), three branches, each on its own scale and adjustments. Resettlement: benefits and earnings brought forward, on the site's log-income scale at resettled refugees' consumption (a quarter of the UK average). Integration and destitution: support secured, plus weeks of homelessness averted for a few clients (a roof at the cheapest room's rent, and the excess deaths of sleeping rough), at the shared consumption of people in the asylum system (18% of the average). Children's services: a small life-satisfaction gain for months for every child, and a larger one for children moved from adult hotels into care after an age-dispute challenge.

**Therapy** (wellbeing template): the review of psychosocial interventions for refugees (Turrini et al. 2019, 1.02 SD on depression), fading over 1.5 years, adjusted for trust in small, mixed trials (0.45), what clients would have got anyway, the Refugee Council's share and incomplete courses, plus suicides averted.

**Advocacy** (rights-advocacy template, a past average): both years' spending against what they won. The move-on pilot added a median 15 days of support for about 31,600 refugees a year for most of a year, and the 42-day period that replaced it about 7 days for good. Restarting asylum processing spares about 29,000 arrivals a year a year and a half of limbo. Campaigning caused 30% and 5% of these, and the Refugee Council's share is 25% and 12%. Its next dollar does 42% of the past average.

### What drives the result

Services: the value of support secured for integration clients, their consumption, and the counterfactuals. Therapy: trust in the trials and how long the effect lasts. Advocacy: how much of each win campaigning caused. In every model, the missing-data discount and the site's value of a WELLBY or of income.

### Biggest uncertainties

- What the services are worth to each client. The Refugee Council's surveys of about 800 clients show improvements, but with no comparison group.
- How the 4,320 refugee clients split between resettlement and integration, and how many children MyView treats: neither is published.
- How much of the 2024 policy changes the sector's campaigns caused, given that the new government had most of them in its manifesto.

### What isn't modelled

Help to family members of a client, beyond the client. Volunteers' own wellbeing and informal support the services replace are sized as side effects. The year's smaller policy wins (the Bibby Stockholm barge closed, Afghan family reunion extended) and public-attitudes work are sized in the advocacy model's side effects.

### How this model was checked

Before the independent audit rounds in audits/refugee-council-charityevals/, audits/refugee-council-charityevals-therapy/ and audits/refugee-council-charityevals-advocacy/, the models passed the site's mechanical checks (audit-model, check, simulation-check, normalization-audit).
