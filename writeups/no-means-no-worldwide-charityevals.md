### Summary

No Means No Worldwide writes a school course that teaches girls to recognise, avoid and resist sexual assault, and boys to respect consent and step in. It trains local partners and teachers to give the course. In 2025 it does about **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="no-means-no-worldwide-charityevals">0.14</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**.

Three cluster-randomised trials tested the course for girls against a standard life-skills class. Two found a third to a half fewer rapes in the following year. The largest, the only one with independent data collectors, found none. Pooled, they suggest about 31% fewer rapes, with a range that runs from a cut of more than half to a little harm. Most of the value is the post-traumatic stress and depression that each rape averted spares.

### What the charity does

All figures are from NMNW's audited accounts for calendar 2025:

- **The No Means No course** ($1.06 million of $1.51 million total spending). NMNW adapts the curriculum, trains and certifies trainers and instructors, and funds and supports implementing partners. In 2025 that included $266,421 of grants to five partners in sub-Saharan Africa. The standard course is six two-hour sessions. Girls learn awareness, boundary-setting, verbal assertiveness and simple physical self-defence; boys learn about consent, gender equity and stepping in. A new 90-minute version, "Worth Defending", keeps only the mental and verbal skills.
- **Reach.** Partners taught 128,000 young people in six countries: Nigeria, Zimbabwe, Uganda, South Africa and others. Many partners run PEPFAR-funded HIV programmes for girls. That is down from 480,213 in 2024, after US aid cuts closed many partners' programmes.

### How the impact happens

1. Girls taught the course recognise danger sooner, refuse, shout, and fight back to escape when they need to. Fewer are raped in the following months.
2. A rape averted spares months to years of post-traumatic stress and depression, and a small risk of suicide later.
3. It also spares some pregnancies, infections, injuries and lost schooling. Girls already abused disclose more often and are linked to care.

### How the model works

- **Cost**: all of NMNW's 2025 spending. Partners' and governments' own delivery costs stay out of the cost. Instead they lower NMNW's share of the credit, judged at about $4 a young person.
- **Reach**: the 128,000, split by judgment into the standard course (55%) and the 90-minute course. Girls are 72% of the standard course, from NMNW's pre/post questionnaires (14,860 girls and 5,825 boys).
- **Effect**: the trials' outcome, forced sex in the past year. We judge 8% of the girls taught would be raped in a year without the course, anchored on the trials' control arms (6.6% to 13.8%). The three trials are pooled by random effects: a risk ratio of 0.69 (95% CI 0.43 to 1.11). The protection counts for one year.
- **Health per rape**: post-traumatic stress from the meta-analysis of prospective studies (75% of survivors meet the criteria a month after, 41% a year after). We count about 1.6 years beyond what a girl would have anyway, at GBD's anxiety-disorder weights. On top come judged extra depression and a small risk of death (mostly suicide, about 0.0006 per rape). All are discounted to when they come: about half a healthy year in all.
- **Adjustments**: internal validity 0.7 (in the two positive trials the course's own instructors gave out the surveys), external validity 0.8 (other countries and partners), counterfactual 0.8, delivery 0.85, NMNW's share of the resources about 75%.
- **The 90-minute course** has only pre/post questionnaires. It counts at a fifth of the full course's effect, by judgment, and is discounted for missing data.

### What drives the result

- **Whether the course works**: at the pooled trials' upper bound the result is below zero; at their lower bound it nearly doubles.
- **How long it protects**: one year, from half a year to two.
- **How often the girls would be raped without it**, and how much of the trials' effect is real (the internal validity adjustment).
- **The health lost per rape**: the years of post-traumatic stress and depression.

### Biggest uncertainties

- The trials disagree. The two that found large effects used the course's own instructors to give out surveys, and could not link girls' answers over time. The one that avoided both problems found no effect two years on. Its authors suggest the earlier designs may have overstated the effect.
- NMNW doesn't publish how many girls took each version of the course, nor its partners' costs.
- The health lost per rape rests on studies of mostly adult women in the US.

### What isn't modelled

Beyond the share in unmodelled benefits (pregnancy, infections, injuries, schooling, boys' own safety, disclosure and care, with backlash and false confidence against them): NMNW's research and new curricula (digital safety, athletes, disability), and the future courses its certified trainers and government partners will give.

### How this model was checked

The mechanical audit (`scripts/audit-model.ts`) runs on the model. Independent auditors, a fresh agent each round using the audit-cea skill, checked it; the rounds are in `audits/no-means-no-worldwide-charityevals/`.
