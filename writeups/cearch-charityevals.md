### Summary

CEARCH (the Centre for Exploratory Altruism Research) looks for neglected causes, mostly in global health, then makes and advises grants to the best projects it finds. Our model counts its running costs from its launch in October 2022 to May 2025 against the grants it made with the donors it advises and the funding its commissioned research changed. It comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**. CEARCH publishes no accounts, so its running costs are our judgment from what it had raised, and so is how much money its research informed.

### What the charity does

CEARCH started in 2022 from Ambitious Impact's incubation programme. It is a small team, fiscally sponsored by the Players Philanthropy Fund: its founder full time, part-time research fellows and an advisor. It has published shallow, intermediate and deep reports on some 20 causes, among them high blood pressure, diabetes, nuclear winter and giving in Asia. It names salt reduction policy and taxes on sugary drinks as its top global health causes.

It funds projects in these causes through grant rounds and a fund, with the donors it advises: 16 projects evaluated in five rounds from 2023 to 2026, four funded. It also does research commissioned by funders, such as the Flourishing Minds Fund on mental health and Meta Charity Funders on the funding of its field.

### How the impact happens

CEARCH's research points donors and funders to work it judges more cost-effective. Its grants and advice move their money there, and its commissioned reports change some of what funders decide. The good done is what that money does where it goes, less what it would have done where it was going anyway.

### What drives the result

The mobiliser's share moves the result most, then the funding a commissioned project informs, the next dollar's value and the missing-data discount. Next come the shared share of advised money changed, CEARCH's running costs, the number of commissions, the value of a life and the discount rate.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **Its running costs:** CEARCH publishes none; ours rest on what it had raised by January 2025.
- **What its research changes:** no client has said how much money a report informed or how it changed its grants.
- **Where the grants went:** its grantees have no model here, so the grants are valued at the cause's charities rather than at the policy projects themselves.

### What isn't modelled

Grants made after May 2025 count only through the next dollar, as no amounts are published. Ambitious Impact's attempt to start salt-reduction and soda-tax charities in 2026 is only in the side effects.

### How this model was checked

Audit rounds are listed in audits/cearch-charityevals/. Round 1 found two high and three medium issues. The share of the grants CEARCH caused and the share of funders' money its research changed were typed judgments where the site has a shared figure for meta charities without a donor survey; both now read it. Running costs are now anchored on what CEARCH had raised, advice to other grantmakers became a branch, the size of a commission got a reference class, and the meta funding report left the count. Together these took the result from 3.9 to 3.3. Round 2 found the charity record's spending out of step with the model, fixed; round 3 passed.
