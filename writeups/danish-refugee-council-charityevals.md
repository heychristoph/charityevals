### Summary

The Danish Refugee Council (DRC, Dansk Flygtningehjælp) spent DKK 3.5 billion ($524 million) in 2025. Almost all of it was government, EU and UN grants. It works with refugees, displaced people and their hosts in 28 countries, the largest operations in Ukraine, Yemen, Sudan, Burkina Faso and Afghanistan, and with asylum seekers and refugees in Denmark. We model all of its DKK 3.2 billion of programmes: cash and vouchers, food security and livelihoods, shelter, water and sanitation, rebuilt public facilities, protection and legal aid, mine action, peacebuilding, site management, the Standby Roster's expert deployments, the Mixed Migration Centre's research, and the work in Denmark. We estimate **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="danish-refugee-council-charityevals">0.07</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**, near the site's global-health median. DRC counts the people each sector reached (7.5 million different people) but publishes no spending by sector, no total of the cash it handed out and no controlled evaluation of its own. So every part is built from reach, a judged share of spending and the research on its kind of work, with wide ranges, and counted at half by the site's shared missing-data slider.

### How the model works

**Cash and vouchers** (799,000 people; about 60% of the result). The accounts don't split spending by sector. We price each sector's people at the Norwegian Refugee Council's 2025 cost per person in the same work, take out the sectors NRC has no line for (mine action, site management, the Standby Roster, peacebuilding, the Mixed Migration Centre), and share the rest in proportion: 31% of the international programmes for cash. Half of that is handed to people, about $92 a person, valued as consumption over the half year it covers: a judged 43% in Ukraine and other middle-income countries at displaced Ukrainians' consumption, the rest at about $700 a year (2017 PPP). In the low-income crises the cash also averts a small share of the excess deaths, as food security does, and both branches add a judged 10% for spending in local markets.

**Food security and livelihoods** (249,000 people; about 15%). Food, seeds, tools, livestock, training and business grants, about $58 a person delivered. Over half a year of crisis, mortality averages 0.02 a year against a normal 0.007, and the support removes 2% of the extra deaths. The rest of its value is consumption.

**Shelter, water and sanitation, infrastructure** (1.6 million people; about 20%). Shelter (18% of the sector's people) is valued at its cost to households over two years, with a little exposure and illness averted. WASH (74%) averts diarrhoeal deaths and illness at 15% of a crisis's diarrhoea, as household trials suggest, and saves a little time. Rebuilt hospitals, schools and water and energy systems, mostly in Ukraine (11%), are valued at their cost over five years.

**Protection** (1.3 million people; about 4%). A quarter is taken to be legal aid, valued like NRC's: a resolved document or legal-stay case adds 5% to consumption for three years. The rest gets community-based protection, child protection and psychosocial support. The 22% of people in conflict settings with a mental disorder gain 0.2 standard deviations for a year.

**Mine action and peacebuilding** (under 1%). Over 14 square kilometres released, 5,928 explosive items removed and 1.5 million people given risk education avert about 38 casualties over the years the work brings safety forward. A third of them are deaths, many of them children; the rest are lasting injuries. The released land adds to its users' harvests. Victims' assistance and 45,300 mediation and dialogue participants add small wellbeing gains.

**Site management, the Standby Roster and the Mixed Migration Centre** (about 1%). These improve aid that others deliver: the 1.7 million residents of managed sites get a judged $60 a year of aid, made 3% more useful; the 154 expert deployments, at about $51,000 each, shape a judged $0.9 million of a host's budget each, made 5% better (NRC's NORCAP: $2 million for an assignment twice the cost). Better-used aid is valued at a quarter of cash's value per dollar, as in charityevals' other relief models.

**Denmark** (under 0.1%). 8,796 asylum seekers and refugees got legal services; we judge 2% of those counselled changed an asylum, transfer or return outcome, worth 1.5 points of life satisfaction a year for ten years. 26,579 people took part in integration work. Volunteers' unpaid work (over EUR 5 million) lowers DRC's share of the credit.

Every branch then takes judged adjustments for evidence, what would have happened anyway (only 35% of 2025's humanitarian appeals were funded), DRC's share of the resources and delivery (85%).

### What drives the result

The value of a healthy year in units of value and the missing-data slider come first, then the consumption of the people reached, the share of spending that went to cash and the share of it handed out, and the cash branch's counterfactual.

### What isn't modelled

The 3.25 million people reached by mass information, protection's gains for people without a disorder or a resolved case, violence averted by mediation and site management, livelihoods' later income, anticipatory action and displacement forecasts, and advocacy (+). Tension with host communities and funging inside the response (-). Together these are a judged +5%.

### How this model was checked

Built with charityevals' humanitarian-relief template on 14 November 2026, as a portfolio of branches over DRC's audited 2025 accounts (Convention 4), reusing the judgments of charityevals' NRC model where the work is the same; the mechanical audit passes.
