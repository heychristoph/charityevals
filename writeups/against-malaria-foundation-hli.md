### Summary

The Happier Lives Institute (HLI) values AMF's bed nets by their effect on wellbeing, measured in wellbeing-adjusted life years (WELLBYs). HLI's own figure, with a neutral point of 2 (see below), is 74 WELLBYs per $1,000. In our view, which counts deaths the way every model on the site does and takes the middle of HLI's estimate of the health and income benefits, it comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="against-malaria-foundation-hli">11</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**. GiveWell's model of the same charity gives about <span data-figure="headline" data-model="against-malaria-foundation">24</span>; the rankings show both.

The evaluator view reproduces HLI's published figures exactly, for every neutral point HLI reports (0 to 5).

### How HLI values a life saved

1. **Deaths averted per $1,000**, taken from GiveWell's 2022 AMF model.
2. **Years of life saved**: life expectancy minus the average age of those saved (about 20, a mix of young children and adults).
3. **How good those years are**: average life satisfaction in AMF's countries (about 4.5 out of 10) minus the **neutral point**, the level at which life is neither good nor bad for the person living it.
4. Plus **rising life satisfaction** over the years saved, and **grief averted** for the family.
5. Plus **wellbeing gains from better health and income**: HLI's sheet adds its upper-bound estimate.

### The choice that decides it

The neutral point is a philosophical judgment, not a measurement. At 0, AMF reaches about 100 WELLBYs per $1,000; at 5, about 35, and the value of saving a life itself turns slightly negative because average life satisfaction is below 5. HLI shows the whole range and picks none; our default of 2 is a middle choice, adjustable on this page. Other accounts of why death is bad (time-relative interests, Epicureanism) give different results again; HLI's spreadsheet has them, but this model follows deprivationism.

### Where we differ from HLI

- **Deaths (changed for consistency):** our view counts the deaths in GiveWell's current AMF model (0.23 per $1,000 before, 0.20 after GiveWell's adjustment for leverage and funging), not HLI's copy of GiveWell's 2022 figure (0.34 per $1,000 per dollar AMF spends, before that adjustment). Both AMF models on the site now count the same deaths.
- **Value of a death (changed for consistency):** each death is valued as in every model, GiveWell's moral weight for the age of the person saved (116 doubled-income years for a child under five, 73 for older people; 81% of the deaths are of children) times "A child's life vs doubled-income year", not by HLI's WELLBYs of the years saved above a neutral point (133 WELLBYs a death at a neutral point of 2, grief included). The deaths count exactly what they count in GiveWell's model at any WELLBY rate: they are valued in doubled-income years and enter the WELLBYs only through a row the WELLBY conversion cancels, so the WELLBY slider moves only the WELLBYs HLI measured. HLI's neutral point stays in the evaluator view only.
- **Health and income benefits (changed for consistency):** our best guess is the middle of HLI's two cases, 15 WELLBYs per $1,000, with HLI's 'just life saving' version (0) and its upper bound (30) as the range. HLI's lower-bound tab is broken in its published sheet, and its sheet adds the upper bound (kept in the evaluator view).
- **Discounting (ours):** the health and income benefits come over years (less malaria while the nets last, higher incomes when the children grow up) and are discounted at the rate under Your values, spread over 20 years from year 3 (our judgment, with ranges). HLI doesn't discount.
- **Exchange rate (changed for consistency):** WELLBYs are converted at the depression-calibrated rate shared by every HLI model (0.065 healthy years per WELLBY); the evaluator view converts at 0.575 healthy years per WELLBY, the rate that matches HLI's view of cash transfers.
