### Summary

The Happier Lives Institute (HLI) values AMF's bed nets by their effect on wellbeing, measured in wellbeing-adjusted life years (WELLBYs). With a neutral point of 2 (see below) it comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="against-malaria-foundation-hli">43</span> years of healthy life per $1,000** at our default exchange rate. GiveWell's model of the same charity gives about <span data-figure="headline" data-model="against-malaria-foundation">24</span>; the rankings show both.

The model reproduces HLI's published figures exactly, for every neutral point HLI reports (0 to 5).

### How HLI values a life saved

1. **Deaths averted per $1,000**, taken from GiveWell's 2022 AMF model.
2. **Years of life saved**: life expectancy minus the average age of those saved (about 20, a mix of young children and adults).
3. **How good those years are**: average life satisfaction in AMF's countries (about 4.5 out of 10) minus the **neutral point**, the level at which life is neither good nor bad for the person living it.
4. Plus **rising life satisfaction** over the years saved, and **grief averted** for the family.
5. Plus **wellbeing gains from better health and income**, HLI's upper-bound estimate.

### The choice that decides it

The neutral point is a philosophical judgment, not a measurement. At 0, AMF reaches about 100 WELLBYs per $1,000; at 5, about 35, and the value of saving a life itself turns slightly negative because average life satisfaction is below 5. HLI shows the whole range and picks none; our default of 2 is a middle choice, adjustable on this page. Other accounts of why death is bad (time-relative interests, Epicureanism) give different results again; HLI's spreadsheet has them, but this model follows deprivationism.

### Where we differ from HLI

Nothing beyond choosing the default neutral point. HLI's lower-bound estimate of the health and income benefits is broken in its published sheet, so the range for that input runs from zero (HLI's "just life saving" version) to its upper bound.
