### Summary

GiveWell researches global health and poverty programmes and directs donors' money to the most cost-effective ones it finds. This model is of a gift for GiveWell's own work, its research, grantmaking and outreach, not of the money that passes through it to the charities it funds. It comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="givewell-charityevals">60</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**. Each dollar GiveWell spends on itself leads to about eleven dollars of giving it caused, valued at what the charities that got the money achieve on this site. The next dollar is counted at a fifth of that average, because GiveWell's operations are not short of money.

### What the charity does

GiveWell (legally The Clear Fund) spent $183.9 million in 2024, by its audited accounts. Of that, $163.1 million was grants: donors' money passed on to the charities it funds. Those grants are the charities' cost and are counted in their own models, so they are taken out here. What is left, $20.7 million, is GiveWell's own spending: staff and contractors, research, outreach and administration.

In its 2024 metrics year (February 2024 to January 2025) GiveWell raised $415 million and directed $397 million. Three quarters went to its four Top Charities: the Against Malaria Foundation, Malaria Consortium's seasonal malaria chemoprevention, Helen Keller Intl's vitamin A supplementation and New Incentives. The rest went to about 35 other organisations, from PATH's malaria vaccines to tuberculosis contact tracing and malnutrition treatment. Its research influenced another $45 million that other funders gave on their own.

### How the impact happens

GiveWell's research decides where hundreds of millions of dollars go. Without it, some of that money would still have reached good charities and some would not.

The model counts only the giving GiveWell caused:

- **Open Philanthropy** (now Coefficient Giving) gave $108 million. It would have given this money to global health anyway, through its own grantmakers. GiveWell is credited with a quarter of it (10% to 50%), our judgment, for steering it better than Open Philanthropy would alone.
- **Other donors** gave $307 million, less the $16 million of their unrestricted gifts that paid for GiveWell's operations. GiveWell is credited with 60% (35% to 84%), our judgment. In its last published donor survey, from 2016, every respondent said GiveWell had changed where or how much they gave, apart from 16% who were just starting to give. The site's shared figure for fundraisers, from Giving What We Can's surveys, is 30%. GiveWell sits above that because the other effective-giving organisations mostly recommend its charities.
- **The $45 million its research influenced** is credited at the same 60%.

A dollar moved is worth what the charities that get it achieve. Each recipient is read live at GiveWell's figure for it on this site, under your values, weighted by what GiveWell directed to it in 2024. The 15 small recipients with no model here ($5.8 million) are valued at the average of the rest. The influenced money is valued at the charities it went to, with equal weights.

Half the giving GiveWell caused is counted as new money. The other half would have gone to other charities, so it counts only for the gain over where it would have gone. These are the site's shared figures for every organisation that moves money.

### How the model works

Money moved = (funds raised − what paid for operations) × the share GiveWell caused, by source, plus the influenced funding at the same share. That is about $227 million against $20.7 million of spending. Each dollar moved is valued at its recipients' live results, net of where shifted money would have gone. A 10% side effect is added, our judgment: research used by funders and governments who don't report it, less donor attention drawn to what can be measured. The result is then multiplied by how much the next dollar does compared with the past average: 21%. Money moved is counted, not estimated, so no branch is discounted for missing data.

### What drives the result

- **The next dollar** (21%, from 10% to 50%). Unrestricted gifts were $111 million in 2024, five times GiveWell's operating costs. Under its excess-assets policy, money it can't use productively on itself is granted out. So a further dollar for operations mostly frees another donor's dollar for grants.
- **The share of other donors' giving it caused** (60%, from 35% to 84%). This is a judgment; GiveWell has published no donor survey since 2016.
- **The value of a life** and **the discount rate**, through the charities GiveWell funds.
- **Where shifted money would otherwise have gone** (one times GiveDirectly, from 0 to 3 times).

### Where we differ from GiveWell

GiveWell publishes no estimate of the cost-effectiveness of its own operations. It reports funds raised and directed, about 19 dollars directed per dollar spent in 2024. It has said that it does not try to measure its impact against a world without GiveWell.

- **The share it caused.** Funds directed counts every dollar. We count only the giving GiveWell caused, and half of that as new money.
- **The next dollar.** Funds directed per dollar spent is an average. We take 21% of it for the next dollar.
- **Staff time.** We credit staff for pay they give up, as in every model on the site (credit by share of resources, off by default).

### Biggest uncertainties

- How much of its donors' giving would have reached equally good charities without it. The only survey is old and comes from engaged donors.
- Whether a dollar for its operations adds research, or only frees another donor's unrestricted dollar for grants.
- The value of the newer programmes it funds, many of them pilots, research or scoping grants read at GiveWell's figure for the organisation.

### What isn't modelled

- Money GiveWell raised in earlier years and granted in 2024, and the interest it earns while waiting ($10.6 million in 2024). The interest roughly offsets the wait before money is granted, so neither is counted.
- GiveWell's influence on how other evaluators and governments judge programmes, beyond the 10% side effect.

### How this model was checked

Built from GiveWell's audited 2024 accounts and its 2024 metrics report, with its grantees read live. An independent audit then checked the sources, the arithmetic and the judgments; the rounds and what changed are kept in audits/givewell-charityevals/.
