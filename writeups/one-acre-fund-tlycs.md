### Summary

One Acre Fund sells smallholder farmers seed and fertiliser on credit, with training and delivery, and runs farm shops, tree-seedling and extension programmes with governments. The Life You Can Save puts its return at 4.43 dollars of extra farm profit per dollar spent, from One Acre Fund's 2024 figures; valuing that profit as a rise in the household's consumption, that is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="one-acre-fund-tlycs">7.4</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**.

The model reproduces every calculated cell of The Life You Can Save's sheet. The Life You Can Save calls its figures highly approximate.

### What the charity does

In its core programme, field officers organise farmers into groups, deliver seed and fertiliser on credit, train them in planting and harvesting, and collect repayments. Its partnerships reach more farmers more lightly: rural shops, free tree seedlings, buying high-value crops, and extension run with governments. In 2024 it reached 5.5 million farmers in eight countries, half of them in Rwanda.

### How the impact happens

1. **Extra profit:** each season One Acre Fund weighs sampled harvests of its farmers and of comparable non-clients, values them at market prices and subtracts all farm costs, loan interest included; trees and other products add their value over their life. Across all programmes this comes to 51 dollars a farmer in 2024.
2. **Cost:** One Acre Fund's net cost after farmers repay their loans, 11.53 dollars a farmer across the organisation.
3. **Return:** 51.09 ÷ 11.53 = 4.43 dollars of profit per dollar, The Life You Can Save's figure.
4. **Value (our step):** the profit raises what the farmer's household consumes that year.

### How the model works

The Life You Can Save's sheet comes first: organisation-wide, for the core programme and partnerships, and by country. Our steps follow:

- **Consumption.** The site values an income gain by how much it raises the household's consumption, in ln terms. The households' consumption is the World Bank's survey mean for each country, carried to 2024 dollars, times the rural household size, weighted by where the farmers are. Burundi and Nigeria have no recent survey mean and take the others' mix.
- **One season.** The profit is for the year's harvest, counted once, after a short wait until harvest.
- **What would have happened anyway:** already in One Acre Fund's figure, which is measured against matched non-clients.
- **Beyond the profit:** deaths averted by higher household incomes (the site's shared figure) and a small side effect for practices kept after farmers leave, better diets and the trees' wider benefits.
- **Credit and staff pay:** with credit by share of resources on, the partners' resources in the partnerships share the credit and staff's pay below market counts as part of the cost.

### What drives the result

- **The profit measurement:** One Acre Fund's own, against matched non-clients rather than a randomised comparison.
- **The households' consumption**, which sets how much a 51-dollar gain is worth to them.
- **The value of a healthy year against income.**

### Where we differ from The Life You Can Save

- **The unit.** The Life You Can Save stops at dollars of profit per dollar; we value the profit as a rise in household consumption, the site's way for every income gain.
- **Deaths averted by higher incomes and benefits beyond the profit,** which the sheet leaves out (the shared 20% and our judged 10%).
- **Discounting** the wait until harvest at the shared rate; the sheet has no discounting.
- **Staff pay and partners' credit,** with credit by share of resources on.

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether matched non-clients are a fair comparison: farmers who join may differ in ways the matching misses.
- How the organisation-wide 51 dollars is put together across programmes and products: it is One Acre Fund's weighted average, given to The Life You Can Save.
- The partnerships' results, where governments and partners share the work.

### What isn't modelled

- Farmers' gains in later seasons from what they learned, beyond the small side effect.
- The countries' figures separately: the result uses One Acre Fund's organisation-wide figures.
