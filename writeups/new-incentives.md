### Summary

New Incentives pays caregivers in northern Nigeria small cash incentives for bringing infants to clinics for their routine vaccinations. GiveWell models 40 state-level programs. This page shows Bayelsa, the median: **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="new-incentives">4.4</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**, about the same as GiveDirectly.

As with vitamin A, location dominates: from about 1.8 years of healthy life per $1,000 (Ebonyi) to about 64 (Sokoto). GiveWell funds the northern states where few children are vaccinated and disease burden is high; those are at the top of the list.

The model reproduces all 100 of GiveWell's intermediate figures, and those of every other state.

### How the impact happens

1. Incentives raise the share of children who complete their vaccinations above what would have happened anyway. GiveWell estimates the increase from a randomised trial and from coverage data.
2. Extra vaccinated children avoid deaths from measles, pneumococcus, rotavirus, pertussis and other vaccine-preventable diseases, weighted by each disease's local burden and each vaccine's efficacy.
3. Benefits extend to older ages and to long-term income.

### What drives the result

- **The probability that an unvaccinated child dies of a vaccine-preventable disease** in the state: GiveWell's 25th–75th percentile moves the result by about ±30%.
- **The share of disease averted by the vaccines**.
- Your moral weight on saving lives.

### Where we differ from GiveWell

Nothing beyond the shared accounting choices.

### What isn't modelled

Effects on vaccination after the program ends, room for more funding by state, and organisational strength.
