### Summary

Giving What We Can asks people to pledge part of their income to effective charities, recommends where to give and runs a donation platform. Our model of all its work in 2023 and 2024 comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per $1,000**. Each dollar it spends leads to about nine dollars of giving it caused, valued at what the charities its donors actually chose achieve on this site. The figure moves most with the discount rate, because a pledge pays out over decades, and with how much the next dollar does compared with the past average.

### What the charity does

Giving What We Can spent $3.9 million over 2023 and 2024 (in 2024 prices). Until August 2024 it was a project of Effective Ventures; since then it runs as two charities of its own, Giving What We Can UK and Giving What We Can USA. No single set of audited accounts covers its spending for these years, so the model uses its own figures across all the entities that ran it: $1,991,339 in 2023 and $1,869,506 in 2024. The UK charity's audited accounts, for its first months to December 2024, show £239,842 of its own spending. They also show £404,443 of donors' money it passed on to charities, which is not its spending.

Its work is one programme of effective giving:

- **The 10% Pledge and the Trial Pledge.** 1,347 more people held the 10% Pledge at the end of 2024 than at the start of 2023, after resignations. 1,259 people took a Trial Pledge.
- **The donation platform.** Pledgers record their giving on it. Other donors gave $23.2 million through it over the two years, not counting the largest donors.
- **Research and recommendations.** It evaluates the evaluators and recommends charities and funds in global health, animal welfare and catastrophic risk.

### How the impact happens

People give more, and give better, because of Giving What We Can. The model counts only the giving it caused. Its own surveys of donors put that at 33% of pledgers' giving and 28% of other donors' giving through the platform. The rest these donors say they would have given to effective charities anyway.

A pledge is valued by the giving it leads to over the pledger's working life. Pledgers who record their giving give $8,491 a year on average. The share still giving falls from 59% in the first year towards a long-term 28%, using the site's shared pledge-persistence figures, which come from its cohort data. A new pledger gives for 35 years, and each future year is discounted at the site's rate. Pledgers also give about 14% more than they record.

A dollar moved is worth what its recipients achieve. Of the giving, 82% went to charities and funds that Giving What We Can's own review rates high-impact. By cause, that was $40.2 million to global health and wellbeing, $9.8 million to animal welfare and $11.5 million to catastrophic risk, plus some to climate and to other effective-giving organisations. Each recipient is read live at the figure the rankings show for it, under your values:

- **Global health funds** that follow GiveWell (GiveWell's own funds, EA Funds' Global Health and Development Fund, its own Global Health and Wellbeing Fund) are valued at GiveWell's top charities. Named charities with a model here are read directly: Against Malaria Foundation, GiveDirectly, Malaria Consortium, Helen Keller Intl, New Incentives, Deworm the World (at Evidence Action's figure), Unlimit Health and StrongMinds. Other global health recipients are valued at the average of these.
- **Animal welfare** is valued at The Humane League's figure on the rankings (ACE's). It is the only animal charity among the top recipients that the site models, and the animal funds are valued at its figure.
- **Catastrophic risk** recipients (the Long-Term Future Fund, Longview, its own Risks and Resilience Fund) have no model here. They are valued at the catastrophic-risk portfolio: the charities Coefficient Giving's catastrophic-risk funds paid, weighted by their grants.
- **Climate**, where no model of the work these donors fund is published here, and **other effective-giving organisations** are valued at the average of the rest.
- **The remaining 18%**, to charities it couldn't place, is valued as an ordinary charity.

Half the giving it caused is counted as new money. The other half would have gone to other charities, so it counts only for the gain over where it would have gone. These are the site's shared figures for every organisation that moves money.

### How the model works

Money moved = new pledges × yearly giving × discounted years of giving × recording × the share it caused, plus the same for Trial Pledges and for other donors' platform giving. That comes to {{row:caused_donations}} over the two years, against $3.9 million of spending. For {{row:givewell_shared_share}} of the giving, GiveWell's research decided where the gift went: its funds, funds that follow it, and its Top Charities. Giving What We Can and GiveWell both moved that money, so they share the credit for it, {{row:givewell_credit_share}} to Giving What We Can. Pledgers' gifts through Effektiv Spenden's platform are shared the same way. That leaves {{row:money_moved}} credited to it. Each dollar moved is valued at its recipients' figures on the rankings, net of where shifted money would have gone. A 5% side effect is added, our judgment, for giving it inspires but doesn't record, less a 10% norm that may anchor some who would give more. The result is then multiplied by how much the next dollar does compared with the past average: {{row:marginal_vs_past_average}}, Giving What We Can's own figure. Money moved is counted, not estimated, so no branch is discounted for missing data.

### What drives the result

- **The discount rate.** At 0.1% a pledge's 35 years count almost in full and the result is nearly three times higher. At 8% it falls by about two fifths.
- **The next dollar** ({{row:marginal_vs_past_average}}, from 20% to 85%). Spending grew about 2.5 times from 2020–2022 while pledge growth slowed, and the organisation itself expects diminishing returns.
- **The value of a life** and **where shifted money would otherwise have gone** (one times GiveDirectly, from 0 to 3 times).
- **The share it caused** (33% and 28%), from donor surveys that rely on what people say they would have done.

### Where we differ from Giving What We Can's own evaluation

Its own estimate is a giving multiplier of 6x: $6 of value for effective charities per dollar spent in 2023–2024.

- **What a dollar moved is worth.** It counts dollars to charities that pass its bar as equal. We value each recipient by its figure on the rankings. We also count only half the money as new.
- **Charities below its bar.** It counts this giving as zero. We value it as an ordinary charity.
- **Pledges.** It averages two methods (this period's recorded giving, and new pledges' lifetime giving). We count only the new pledges' lifetime giving, because a pledge added this period is a flow. Its two methods give similar totals ($19.3 million and $21.6 million).
- **Discounting.** It discounts at 3.5%; we use the site's 4%.
- **Staff time.** It subtracts the good its staff could do earning elsewhere and donating half of it. We credit staff instead for pay they give up, as in every model on the site (credit by share of resources, off by default).
- **The next dollar.** It reports an average. We take {{row:marginal_vs_past_average}} of it for the next dollar.

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether the 28% long-term floor holds beyond the ten years of data, and whether pledgers who stop recording still give.
- Donor surveys of what people would have done. Its conservative case (26% and 17%) gives a far lower result.
- The value of the catastrophic-risk and animal giving, which rests on the site's models of the catastrophic-risk charities and of The Humane League, and on the species-weight and risk sliders.

### What isn't modelled

- The Company Pledge, which its evaluation leaves out of its own multiplier.
- The largest donors (over $140,000 a year on the platform, or pledgers giving over $1 million a year). Its surveys found little sign it caused their giving, so they count as zero.
- Effects of its research on donors who never use its platform, beyond the {{row:moved_side_effects}} side effect.

### How this model was checked

Four audit rounds (audits/giving-what-we-can-charityevals/). Round 1 found no high-severity problem. Its fixes widened the next-dollar range up to the organisation's own target, weighted the high-impact share by the money each stream moves, and gave the spending figures the ±$200,000 margin the evaluation states. Round 2 checked those changes and passed. Round 3 checked the move to reading each recipient at its figure on the rankings, and the two recipients now read by their own figures, and passed. Round 4 checked the move of the catastrophic-risk stand-in to the site's live cause average, and passed.
