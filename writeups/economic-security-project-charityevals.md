### Summary

Economic Security Project campaigns for cash policies in the US: state child and earned income tax credits, guaranteed income, the IRS's free Direct File service, and public options such as city-run grocery stores. We count its tax-deductible arm's spending over 2021 to 2024 against its share of what those years' campaigns won. Our model comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="economic-security-project-charityevals">0.09</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**, close to the median of the site's global-health models. Almost all of it is the state tax credits. The result is mostly uncertainty: how much of the credits the advocates caused, and how much of that was Economic Security Project's, are judgments.

### Which organisation

Economic Security Project started in 2016. Economic Security Project Inc, its 501(c)(3), was set up in November 2020 and is the one modelled here. Its sister, Economic Security Project Action, is a 501(c)(4) that lobbies, and gifts to it aren't tax-deductible. Only the 501(c)(3)'s spending and its share of the wins count.

### What the charity does

In 2024 the 501(c)(3) spent $10.2 million, $8.2 million of it on programmes:

- **Campaigns** ($2.5 million): state tax credits, IRS Direct File, and Economic Security California.
- **Guaranteed income** ($1.8 million): it runs the national coalition, the Guaranteed Income Now conference and research on direct cash.
- **Shaping markets** ($1.6 million): grants for anti-monopoly and public-option work, and the public grocery store campaign in Illinois.
- **Convenings, state affiliates and media** ($2.3 million): this work serves all three.

$1.7 million of the total went in grants to state and national partners.

### How the impact happens

- **State tax credits.** States enacted or improved child and earned income tax credits each year from 2021 to 2024, worth $275 million, $636 million, $2.2 billion and $801 million a year to households (Economic Security Project's own sums). The average credit is about $1,500 a household. Economic Security Project funds, coaches and coordinates the state campaigns.
- **Guaranteed income pilots.** US cities and funders paid $481 million to about 41,000 people in 122 pilots between 2017 and 2025, about $7,200 a year each. Economic Security Project paid for the first pilots (Stockton, Jackson) and now convenes the movement.
- **IRS Direct File.** The free filing service ran for the 2024 and 2025 seasons (141,000 and 309,000 returns). Treasury suspended it in October 2025.
- **Public grocery stores.** It paid for a feasibility study of a city-run grocery store in Chicago. No store has opened yet.

### How the model works

Each branch counts the money that reached people, valued on the site's log-income scale at the receivers' consumption.

- **Tax credits.** Recipient families' consumption per person is about 36% of the US average. That comes from the two lowest income fifths' spending in the Consumer Expenditure Survey, spread over a family of 3.6. What the credits cost state taxpayers is subtracted, using the shared cost of public money and how state taxes fall. Each year's credits count for the site's shared number of years a won reform counts, discounted, after a year's wait. We judge 15% of the credits wouldn't have come, or not for years, without the advocates, and Economic Security Project's share of the advocates' resources at 15%. We trust 75% of the counted money as real and lasting.
- **Pilots.** The money would otherwise have funded something else, so the gain is the cash's value less 40% for that alternative. Each year's payments count once. Economic Security Project's share of the movement is 6%.
- **Direct File.** It saved its users about $18 million in filing fees. It cost the Treasury $73 million, so on the log scale it did a little net harm, and the model counts that.
- **Grocery store.** A store is worth a little to the residents it serves, net of the city's subsidy, times a 30% chance it opens.

The cost is the 501(c)(3)'s average real spending over the four years, 95% of 2024's. The next dollar counts at 42% of the past average. The organisation added $4.5 million to its reserves in 2024, and its next campaigns defend credits already won. Every branch takes the shared missing-data discount.

Four side effects are rows of their own:

- Children's long-run gains from family income add 10% of the credits' value.
- Work effects of the credits add 1%.
- The pilots' evidence and lasting programmes add 30% of their value.
- The rest of the shaping-markets work adds a small judged amount.

### What drives the result

About 95% of the value is the state tax credits. The largest swings are the years a won reform counts, the missing-data discount, how much of the credits the advocates caused, Economic Security Project's share of them, the value of a healthy year in income, and the next-dollar figure.

### Biggest uncertainties

- How much of the 2021-2024 credits the state campaigns caused, as against governors' plans and record state surpluses.
- Economic Security Project's share of the state campaigns. They were run mostly by state fiscal policy groups it funds and coaches.
- Whether the credits last. Some are being rolled back after the 2025 federal tax law.

### What isn't modelled

- The expanded federal child tax credit of 2021 is left out. It passed before this organisation was running and lapsed after a year.
- Tax-preparation firms' lost margins from Direct File are left out. They would lower its value slightly.
- The 2025 credits (about $1.5 billion a year) are left out. They were bought with spending not yet filed.
