### Summary

Dansk Vegetarisk Forening (DVF) promotes plant-based food in Denmark, including training chefs and influencing public funding. ACE's analysis (2024 evaluation, recommendation renewed in 2025) estimates SADs only for the chef education program: **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="dansk-vegetarisk-forening">0.87</span> years of healthy life per $1,000** you give. That uses our default exchange rates between suffering and healthy life, which is where most of the uncertainty is.

In ACE's own view the model reproduces all 21 of ACE's intermediate figures, which come to 20.

### How the impact happens

About 50 chefs a year are trained; each prepares thousands of plant-based meals a year for some years (ACE: ten; our view: the shared "Years a won reform counts before it would have come anyway", 5 by default); some share of those replace meat meals; each replaced meal averts some suffering, less the extra others eat as prices fall. ACE discounts for other organisations' involvement (70%) and for time.

### What drives the result

**How much a fish can suffer relative to a chicken** (about 0.17 to 5.4 years of healthy life per $1,000 on its own) and **suffering spared per meal swapped** (0.10 to 1.7) move the result most: most of a replaced meal's suffering falls on fish. Next come the years counted (0.19 to 2.7 over the slider's 1 to 20 years) and how a Danish meal compares with the Latin American reference meal (0.44 to 1.7, our labelled judgment).

### What isn't modelled

DVF's work influencing public funding has no SADs estimate in ACE's sheet, so it isn't in the bottom line.

### Where we differ from ACE

- **Suffering per meal (changed for consistency).** ACE uses 2.58 days of suffering per meal swapped (1.05–4.11); we use the shared 0.90 (ACE's figure for Sinergia Animal's canteen meals), so the same change counts the same in every model.
- **A Danish meal (ours, a labelled judgment).** The shared per-meal figure is a Latin American canteen meal. ACE publishes per-country meal estimates only for Brazil and the US, so the reference meal stands in for a Danish one (1, from half to double).
- **Production response (changed for consistency).** A meal not eaten cuts production by 0.76 of a meal (prices fall and others eat a little more), as in every diet-change model; ACE counts 1.
- **Years counted (changed for consistency).** ACE counts ten years of each chef's cooking (filed as the charity's data); we use the shared slider, 5 years by default.
- **Species weights.** Ambitious Impact's weights come out of ACE's figures and Rethink Priorities' go in, relative to a chicken; the chicken slider (default 33%) sets how a chicken's pain compares with a person's.
- **Salary sacrifice (off by default).** Turn on "Credit by share of resources" and we count staff's forgone pay as an in-kind donation: we guess staff are 65% of program cost and earn 35% less than in comparable for-profit jobs, about 35% of cash spending. That leaves each donated dollar about 74% of the credit ACE gives it. Both numbers are guesses with wide ranges; replace them with the charity's real figures when we have them.
- **Credit for shared outcomes.** ACE credits 70% of the meals to others involved in the training, and we keep that (filed as a judgment, not data). With "Credit by share of resources" on, DVF gets its share of the resources behind the training instead: our judgment, 30% (15% to 50%).
