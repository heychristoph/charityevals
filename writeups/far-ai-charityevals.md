### Summary

FAR.AI works to make advanced AI safe. It does technical research on how AI systems can be attacked and broken, runs a research hub in Berkeley, holds workshops for alignment researchers and funds research by others. Our model of all four programmes in 2024 comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per $1,000**. That figure rests on forecasts of how likely an AI catastrophe is and on an estimate of what a typical dollar of AI safety work does to that risk. Both are deeply uncertain, so the plausible range runs from {{low}} to {{high}}.

### What the charity does

FAR.AI spent $8.6 million in 2024, according to its Form 990 (its audited statements aren't published). Of its $7.5 million of programme spending:

- **Research: $3.0 million.** Twelve papers in 2024, among them attacks that strip a frontier model's refusals by fine-tuning, the compute an attacker needs to break a language model's defences, attacks on and defences of superhuman Go AIs, and how a neural network learns to plan. It also red-teamed frontier AI systems and worked with government AI security initiatives.
- **Events: $2.0 million.** Its largest Alignment Workshops yet, in Vienna and the Bay Area, and smaller technical sessions. The Safe AI Forum, then a FAR.AI project, ran two International Dialogues on AI Safety, where leading scientists from China and the West agreed joint statements.
- **Research grants: $1.5 million.** One grant to a European research organisation, from a $12 million regranting programme funded by Open Philanthropy.
- **FAR.Labs: $1.1 million.** A research hub and coworking space in Berkeley with 40 to 50 members, weekly seminars and a programme for new organisations.

### How the impact happens

AI carries a yearly chance of catastrophe, spread evenly to 2100. By default there is a 3% chance by 2100 that AI ends humanity's future; this is a slider. The forecasting tournament's experts expect three AI catastrophes the world survives for every one that ends it. A catastrophe the world survives is taken to kill a quarter of people.

FAR.AI's spending cuts that yearly risk by the share a typical AI project does per dollar. That share comes from Rethink Priorities' cross-cause model, including its chance that a project backfires. Rethink has one typical project for work against misuse and one for work against AI that doesn't do what its makers intend. The cut counts for the years such progress would otherwise have taken to come (5 by default), and each year is discounted.

The deaths averted are valued like every death on the site, across the world's age groups. A catastrophe that ends humanity's future also takes the lives of everyone not yet born. Those future lives count only through the discount rate.

### How the model works

All of FAR.AI's spending is on AI. The model's pandemic and nuclear areas are set to zero, and so are its policy-win and training branches. No treaty or law was won in 2024, and FAR.AI ran no training programme.

Over a quarter of the work is against misuse: about a third of the research (two of its twelve papers, on jailbreaks and data poisoning, and the red-teaming of safeguards) and about three quarters of the grant, which most likely went to ETH Zurich for research on attacks such as data poisoning and model stealing. The rest is valued as work against misalignment.

| Adjustment | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| How far a typical project's effect applies to FAR.AI's work | 1.02 | Research 1.2 (developers act on its attacks), events 1, grants 1, the hub 0.6 (it supports others' work), weighted by spending |
| Work no one else would have done now | 0.65 | Universities, the labs and government institutes also study robustness and red-team models |
| FAR.AI's share of the resources behind its work | 0.81 | Fees paid by others, co-authors, co-hosts and attendees' time |
| Funging by large funders | 1 | Counted once, in the next-dollar figure |
| Missing data | slider | Nothing about FAR.AI's effect on the risk is measured |

Side effects: other benefits add 10% (robustness work also guards against smaller harms; new organisations started at the hub). Two harms subtract 3% and 5%. Robustness research also makes AI products more reliable. Published attacks teach misusers as well as defenders.

FAR.AI ended 2024 with net assets of $22.5 million, about two and a half years of spending, and raised over $30 million more in 2025. The research grants run on a restricted $12 million grant from Coefficient Giving, so a donor's next dollar pays only for the other programmes. So the next dollar counts at 20% of the past average.

### What drives the result

The main reason is the quarter of its work aimed at misuse. For catastrophes the world survives, Rethink's typical misuse project cuts the deaths about 450 times more per dollar than its typical misalignment project, because in its model most of those deaths come from misuse. So the misuse work carries almost all of the value. Deaths of people alive now are 99% of it: those catastrophes don't take the future, and at the default 4% discount rate the future counts for only about 8 years of the long-run population anyway.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How much a dollar of misuse work cuts the risk.** Rethink's figures are placeholders, and the range runs ten times either way: from 0.067 to 4.6.
- **How likely an AI catastrophe is.** The setting's range runs from 0.38% (the superforecasters) to 50%. Across it the result moves from 0.036 to 4.8.
- **How much of the work is against misuse.** FAR.AI publishes no split. From 11% to 40% moves the result from 0.20 to 0.68.
- **The years a cut counts and the discount rate.** These are shared sliders.

### What isn't modelled

- FAR.AI's work before and after 2024 is not valued here. This includes red-teaming for the GPT-5 system card in 2025.
- Losses beyond deaths, such as survivors' lost income and health, are a shared figure set to 0.
- The forecasts are spread evenly over the century, though forecasters put much of the AI risk later.

### How this model was checked

The model was built from the catastrophic-risk template. Round 1 of the independent audit found that the share of misuse work rested on four highlighted papers: rebuilt from all twelve 2024 papers, the red-teaming and the grant's likely topic, it fell from 25% to 23%, lowering the result by 8%. Round 2 passed with two medium findings, both fixed: the grant's project is three quarters misuse work, raising the share to 28%, and the next dollar can't buy the restricted grants, lowering it from 32% to 20%. Together they lower the result by a quarter. A verification round checked the changes. The funging adjustment was set to 1 in November 2026 because the next-dollar figure already asks whether money is the constraint. Independent audits are in audits/far-ai-charityevals/.
