### Summary

TerraPraxis works to re-power the world's coal plants with advanced nuclear reactors, so the sites, grid connections and jobs outlive coal. It does this through designs, software and convening rather than by building anything itself. Our model counts what its work of 2022-2024 adds to the deployment of advanced nuclear power, against what it spent in those years. It comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="terrapraxis-charityevals">0.02</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**. The result is mostly uncertainty. No coal plant has been re-powered yet, and every step from TerraPraxis's work to a working reactor is a judgment.

### What the charity does

TerraPraxis Inc, a US non-profit founded in 2021, spent $2.01 million in 2024, $1.62 million of it on its one reported programme (Form 990). It had eight staff and paid $0.82 million to contractors. Its UK sister company, TerraPraxis CIC, had one or two staff and about £20,000 in the bank. It is not a registered charity and files no income statement, so its work runs through the US entity's spending. Over 2022-2024 TerraPraxis spent $6.3 million in all. By the end of 2024 its net assets were down to $58,560.

The programme has these parts:

- **REPOWER (coal repowering).** Standard designs for replacing a coal plant's boilers with mass-made reactors ("heat boxes"), developed with the architects Bryden Wood. Site-assessment software built with Microsoft from 2022. The REPOWER consortium of more than 38 utilities, vendors, financiers and governments, with yearly retreats.
- **The Ten-Terawatt Initiative.** Launched in 2024 and presented at Davos in January 2025. It sets out what would let clean firm power grow fast enough to replace fossil fuels, and it includes AI tools for nuclear licensing built with Microsoft.
- **Smaller work.** Papers on synthetic fuels, industrial heat and nuclear-powered shipping, and pages on advanced geothermal and energy modelling.

### How the impact happens

TerraPraxis doesn't campaign for laws. It tries to make reactors cheaper and faster to deploy. It produces the designs, tools and working groups that utilities and governments would otherwise have to build for themselves, and it argues that reactors should replace coal on the sites coal leaves. US national laboratories found that four in five US coal sites could host advanced reactors, and that reusing a site saves 15% to 35% of construction costs. If that work brings reactors forward, the tonnes come when they replace coal or gas power, mostly in the late 2030s and 2040s.

### How the model works

The model has two lines, each starting from a yardstick rather than a forecast.

- **Coal repowering.** The world's 2,175 GW of coal plants × the share on suitable sites (80%) × the share re-powered with nuclear by about 2050 (4%, our judgment). That comes to about 70 GW, which runs at the world fleet's 83% capacity factor and would avoid a tonne of CO₂ per MWh if it replaced coal. Only part of that is a real cut. We count 40% of the coal-site reactors as extra reactors rather than reactors moved from other sites. Each extra reactor replaces power with half of coal's CO₂.
- **Advanced nuclear elsewhere.** The COP28 pledge to triple 2020's 392 GW × the share built (35%), less the coal-site reactors already counted. The grids these reactors join have 35% of coal's CO₂ per MWh.

Both lines then take these steps:

- All groups' non-commercial enabling work in the period gets credit for 4% of the deployment.
- TerraPraxis's share of that work is 8% on coal sites and 0.6% elsewhere.
- The tonnes count for the shared number of years a result counts, discounted after the shared 15-year wait for a new technology.
- The shared evidence and missing-data steps apply.

A small share is added for the remaining work (5%) and for jobs kept in coal towns. A small share is taken off for nuclear risk. The total is divided by a typical year's spending in 2022-2024. The next dollar is valued at 48% of that average: money is short, but the founding steps of the period are done.

### What drives the result

The coal-repowering line gives about three quarters of the result. Its biggest inputs are the shared years a result counts, the 4% of deployment credited to enabling work, and the share of coal capacity re-powered. The air-pollution step matters too. Coal and gas burned in Asia in the 2040s carry more smoke per tonne than US power does today, and the model puts that at 0.14 of the average fossil-fuel tonne.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **Whether coal repowering happens at all.** Utilities have announced studies. One reactor is going up beside a Wyoming coal plant, but no conversion is running.
- **How much design and convening work moves deployment.** Reactors turn mostly on cost, finance, demand and policy. Nobody has measured how much enabling work changes that.
- **TerraPraxis's share.** It is one of the few organisations focused on coal repowering, but national laboratories, the IAEA and industry bodies do much of the same work with far larger budgets.
- **Its finances.** Revenue fell two years running and reserves are almost gone. That is part of the next-dollar figure.

### What isn't modelled

- Methane from coal mines that are no longer needed: no source sizes it for these plants, so it is left out.
- Any extra cost to electricity customers if nuclear power is dearer than the alternatives: TerraPraxis's designs aim at the opposite, and nothing has been built to price it.
