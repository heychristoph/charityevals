### Summary

SilverLining funds and coordinates research on solar radiation modification: cooling the planet by reflecting a little sunlight, for example with fine particles high in the atmosphere. It also presses governments to research and govern the idea. Our model values the research it added in 2019-2024 by what that research could be worth: better decisions about whether and how cooling is ever used. Against what SilverLining spent over those years, that comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="silverlining-charityevals">0.085</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**. Every step is a judgment that no study settles, so the range runs from almost nothing to many times the best guess.

### What the charity does

SilverLining spent $6.5 million in 2024, $5.3 million of it on programmes (Form 990; audited accounts). The return splits the programmes:

- **Research and innovation: $1.9 million (35%).** Grants from its Safe Earth System Research Initiative: climate-model studies of stratospheric aerosols (the ARISE-SAI simulations with NCAR) and marine cloud brightening (University of Washington), field measurements, and grants to groups in Africa and the Arctic. It reports over $7 million in grants in its first five years and 30 peer-reviewed papers.
- **Policy: $1.4 million (27%).** Research and analysis on US and international policy, convening and education. A separate organisation, SilverLining Policy ($0.4 million), does its lobbying.
- **Stakeholder engagement: $1.1 million (21%).** Media, events and outreach.
- **Other programmes: $0.9 million (17%)**, with no description on the return.

### How the impact happens

Cooling would not cut emissions. If it were used, it would hold back some warming, and the heat deaths that come with it, while it lasted. Research doesn't cool anything. It changes whether, when and how cooling is used: a careful design instead of a crude one, or a harmful use avoided. SilverLining adds to that research in three ways, each a branch of the model:

- **Research it funds or does** (about 28% of the result): its grants and its own science, 2019-2024.
- **Money from other funders it directed** to research it coordinates: $15 million gross, as it reports it (about 20%).
- **The US federal research line** (about 53%): Congress began funding NOAA's Earth's Radiation Budget programme in late 2019, $4 million at first and about $9 million a year since. SilverLining was one of only two organisations to disclose lobbying on the subject in the decade to 2023, and its director advised on the bill. We give it 30% of the work behind the line, plus its other policy results (the White House's research plan, UN reports its research informed), counted as 30% more.

### How the model works

First, what a typical dollar of research in the field is worth. We judge a 30% chance that cooling is used at scale this century. That is about half the 66% of IPCC-contributing researchers who expect an attempt, because an attempt is not a sustained use. If it is used, we put the cooling at 0.6°C for 50 years. This follows the most studied design, which holds warming at 1.5°C under a middle emissions path. The heat deaths of that cooling equal those of about 240 billion tonnes of CO₂: the cooling, divided by how much a tonne warms (the IPCC's 0.45°C per trillion tonnes) and by the years the site counts a tonne's heat deaths. The site's shared conversion then values those tonnes as it values every climate charity's. We judge that research adds a tenth of the benefit by improving the decision. Spread over all the field's funding until a decision (about $44 million a year now, doubling, for 20 years), a field dollar is worth about 4 tonnes.

SilverLining's dollars enter at that value. Each branch then takes the share that wouldn't have happened anyway and SilverLining's share of the work. The federal line counts for the site's shared years for a won reform. The model takes the shared evidence (70%) and missing-data steps and three side effects: science useful beyond cooling (+10%), weaker effort to cut emissions (−3%) and a hasty or ungoverned use made likelier (−8%). The total is divided by a typical year's spending in 2019-2024. The next dollar is valued at 50% of that average: SilverLining has almost no reserves, but its biggest win came first.

### What drives the result

The result is far above the climate median, more than ten times its peers. Cooling would offset the warming of hundreds of billions of tonnes at once, and the field whose research shapes that decision is small. Its peers' tonnes are each one policy's emissions cut. After all its steps the model is near the climate funds' best picks ($0.50-3.50 a tonne). The biggest swings are the share of the benefit research adds, the shared discount rate (the heat deaths come decades from now), the chance cooling is used, and its size and length.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **Whether research improves the decision at all, and by how much.** No study measures this; our 10% runs from 2% to 30%.
- **Whether cooling is ever used.** The survey shows what researchers expect, not how likely a sustained use is.
- **SilverLining's part in the federal line.** Congress decided, with NOAA's scientists and the National Academies behind it.
- **The sign.** Research could make an early, ungoverned use likelier; we count that harm at 8%, but some critics think it outweighs the benefit.

### What isn't modelled

Climate damage other than deaths stays at the site's shared zero, as for every climate model. So do cooling's effects on crops and on tipping points such as melting ice sheets, where its supporters see most of its value. If cooling were used, its own harms (air pollution from the particles, rainfall shifts) count inside the share research adds, not separately. Federal funding after fiscal 2024 counts only through the shared years for a won reform.
