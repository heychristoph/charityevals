### Summary

SilverLining funds and coordinates research on solar radiation modification: cooling the planet by reflecting a little sunlight, for example with fine particles high in the atmosphere. It also works with governments and others on how the idea is researched and governed. Our model values what it added to that field in 2019-2024 by what the field's work could be worth: a better-designed use of cooling, if there is one. Against what SilverLining spent over those years, that comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="silverlining-charityevals">0.14</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**. Whether cooling is used at all is one event, so at the default risk setting the counted figure is near zero. Every step is a judgment that no study settles.

### What the charity does

SilverLining spent $6.5 million in 2024, $5.3 million of it on programmes (Form 990; audited accounts). The return splits the programmes:

- **Research and innovation: $1.9 million (35%).** Grants from its Safe Earth System Research Initiative. Most fund climate-model studies of stratospheric aerosols (the ARISE-SAI simulations with NCAR) and marine cloud brightening (University of Washington), and field measurements. About a third went to groups in Africa and the Arctic for engagement and communications. It reports over $7 million in grants in its first five years and 30 peer-reviewed papers.
- **Policy: $1.4 million (27%).** Research and analysis on US and international policy, convening and education. A separate organisation, SilverLining Policy ($0.4 million), does its lobbying.
- **Stakeholder engagement: $1.1 million (21%).** Media, events and outreach.
- **Other programmes: $0.9 million (17%)**, with no description on the return.

### How the impact happens

Cooling would not cut emissions. If it were used, it would hold back some warming, and the heat deaths that come with it, while it lasted. Research and engagement don't cool anything. They change when and how cooling is used: a careful, governed design instead of a crude one. SilverLining adds to that work in four ways, each a branch of the model:

- **Research it funds or does** (about 29% of the result): its research grants and its own science.
- **Engagement** (about 23%): its stakeholder-engagement programme and the grants abroad.
- **Money from other funders it directed** to research it coordinates (about 28%): $15 million gross, as it reports it.
- **The US federal research line** (about 20%). Congress began funding NOAA's Earth's Radiation Budget programme in late 2019: $4 million at first, about $9 million a year since. SilverLining was one of only two organisations to disclose lobbying on the subject in the decade to 2023. Its director advised Rep. McNerney's office on his bill for a formal NOAA programme. Advocates as a whole get the site's shared share of a won climate policy (30%), and SilverLining 60% of that. Only the part of the line that the field's funding tracker would count as cooling research is counted (55%). Its other policy results add 15%.

### How the model works

First, what a typical dollar of the field's work is worth. We judge a 30% chance that cooling is used at scale this century. That is about half the 66% of IPCC-contributing researchers who expect an attempt, because an attempt is not a sustained use. If it is used, we put the cooling at 0.6°C for 50 years. This follows the most studied design, which holds warming at 1.5°C under a middle emissions path.

The heat deaths of that cooling equal those of about 240 billion tonnes of CO₂. The figure is the cooling divided by how much a tonne warms (the IPCC's 0.45°C per trillion tonnes) and by the years the site counts a tonne's heat deaths. The site's shared conversion values those tonnes as it values every climate charity's. These deaths come about 24 years sooner than a tonne's, which the model corrects at the shared discount rate. We judge that the field's work adds a tenth of the benefit by improving the use. Spread over all the field's funding until a decision (about $44 million a year now, doubling, for 20 years), a field dollar is worth about 4 tonnes, or 10 after the timing correction.

SilverLining's dollars enter at that value. Each branch takes the share that wouldn't have happened anyway and SilverLining's share of the work. The federal line counts for the site's shared years for a won reform. Then come the shared evidence (70%) and missing-data steps and three side effects: science useful beyond cooling (+10%), weaker effort to cut emissions (−3%), and a hasty or ungoverned use made likelier (−8%). The total is divided by a typical year's spending in 2019-2024. The next dollar is valued at 50% of that average. SilverLining has almost no reserves, but its biggest win came first.

### What drives the result

The result is far above the climate median, more than ten times its peers. Cooling would offset the warming of hundreds of billions of tonnes at once, and the field whose work shapes that use is small. Its peers' tonnes are each one policy's emissions cut. After all its steps the model comes to about 90 cents a tonne, near the climate funds' best picks ($0.50-3.50 a tonne). The biggest swings are the share of the benefit the field's work adds, the shared discount rate, the chance cooling is used, and its size and length.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **Whether cooling is ever used.** If it isn't, this model gives the work no value. The survey shows what researchers expect, not how likely a sustained use is.
- **How much the field's work improves a use.** No study measures this; our 10% runs from 2% to 30%.
- **SilverLining's part in the federal line.** Congress decided, with NOAA's scientists and the National Academies behind it.
- **The sign.** Research could make an early, ungoverned use likelier. We count that harm at 8%, but some critics think it outweighs the benefit.

### What isn't modelled

Climate damage other than deaths stays at the site's shared zero, as for every climate model. So do cooling's effects on crops and on tipping points such as melting ice sheets, where its supporters see most of its value. Value in worlds where cooling is never used (a harmful use talked out of) is not counted. If cooling were used, its own harms (air pollution from the particles, rainfall shifts) count inside the share the field's work adds, not separately. Federal funding after fiscal 2024 counts only through the shared years for a won reform.

### How this model was checked

Independent audits are in audits/silverlining-charityevals/. Round 1 found the cooling's heat deaths discounted as if they came at a tonne's later delay; a timing correction raised the result about 2.6-fold. It also made the chance of use a single event and gave the federal line the shared advocates' share of a won policy. It counted only the cooling-research part of the line, and based SilverLining's credit on its policy work alone rather than its whole budget. Engagement got a branch of its own, and an overlap was taken out of the other policy results.
