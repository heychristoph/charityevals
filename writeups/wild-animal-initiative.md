### Summary

Wild Animal Initiative (WAI) funds and runs academic research on the welfare of wild animals, and builds the field around it. ACE doesn't estimate suffering averted for WAI: the benefits depend on what future research makes possible, which nobody can yet quantify. Its 2025 analysis counts outputs instead. This model follows it, and ranks WAI through one shared, deliberately wide assumption: suffering-adjusted days per peer-reviewed paper.

ACE's figures work out to about **7.2 peer-reviewed papers per $1 million** across the grants and research programs, or **5.3 per $1 million you give** after our salary-sacrifice adjustment. The model reproduces all 25 of ACE's intermediate figures, including projects completed, welfare metrics validated and outreach outputs per $1 million.

### How to read it

Papers are a proxy. Whether WAI is a good use of money depends on how much you think establishing wild animal welfare as a research field is worth, which is a judgment no spreadsheet settles. ACE's review discusses this qualitatively.

The default of 100,000 SADs per paper is our rough Fermi estimate, explained with the assumption; its range runs from 1,000 to 10 million. Where WAI ranks depends almost entirely on where you set it.

### What drives the result

WAI's estimates of how many funded projects will be completed and published, and ACE's estimate of operational costs per program.

### Where we differ from ACE

Only the salary-sacrifice adjustment (see the "Your dollar's share" section).
