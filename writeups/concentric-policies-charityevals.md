### Summary

Concentric Policies advises the governments of Liberia and Guinea-Bissau on taxing tobacco and alcohol. Its first year comes to about **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**. Most of that rests on one claim found nowhere but the charity's own posts: that it stopped an executive order cutting Liberia's alcohol and tobacco taxes. The model counts that claim as a {{row:eo_p_success}} chance that the order was real and stopped, with {{row:eo_attribution_share_shared}} of the credit for stopping it Concentric's. The rest is a small share of Liberia's 2025 tax act and a chance that Guinea-Bissau applies a tobacco tax rise it passed in 2022. It counts the next dollar at {{row:marginal_vs_past_average}} of the average dollar.

### What the charity does

Concentric Policies was founded in September 2023 through Ambitious Impact's (then Charity Entrepreneurship's) incubation programme. It is a fiscally sponsored project of Players Philanthropy Fund, a US 501(c)(3), with no accounts of its own. In its first year (about October 2023 to September 2024) its two co-founders spent about $80,000, by their own account, on pay, travel and the sponsor's fees. By their count, 8.1 months of full-time work went to Liberia and 3.4 to Guinea-Bissau.

- **Liberia**: technical help to the Ministry of Finance on health taxes, including modelling for an IMF loan application. It also backed an alcohol tax rise it says passed the House of Representatives, and says it stopped an executive order that would have cut alcohol and tobacco taxes.
- **Guinea-Bissau**: work with the health ministry and customs on applying the tobacco taxes passed in 2022, and a national coordination body for tobacco control.
- **Outputs**: memoranda with ministries and NGOs, five briefs and evidence reviews, and a preprint on health workers' advice to quit smoking.

### How the impact happens

A higher tax raises the price of cigarettes or beer. Some smokers quit and fewer young people start, and heavy drinkers drink less. Over the following years that means fewer deaths from heart disease, cancers, lung disease, injuries and liver disease, and less second-hand smoke. A tax cut stopped keeps prices where they were. A tax only works where it is collected, and Liberia collects little tobacco excise.

### How the model works

It is one model, with a branch for each change and each product.

- **Cost**: the first year's spending as the founders report it; {{row:program_share}} of it on the country work (our judgment), the rest management and fees.
- **The stopped tax cut (2024)**: Liberia's smoking burden (27,071 DALYs a year in GBD 2023) and alcohol burden (888 deaths a year). The order's rates are unknown; we judge it would have cut {{row:eo_excise_cut_share}} of the excise. Excise was 46.5% of a pack's price, so cigarettes would have been {{row:eo_tob_price_change}} cheaper; for beer and spirits, whose excise share we judge lower, {{row:eo_alc_price_change}}. The chance that the order was real, was on its way to taking effect, and was stopped is {{row:eo_p_success}}: no text, date or press report of it was found, and the finance ministry and the IMF were both pushing for more revenue. Concentric's share of the credit for stopping it is {{row:eo_attribution_share_shared}}. The Legislature then cut beverage excise anyway from April 2025, so the alcohol part counts only about a year; the tobacco part counts the usual years.
- **Liberia's 2025 tax act**: in force from April 2026, it raises the excise on beer and raw tobacco and indexes excise to inflation from 2027. No source credits Concentric. It gets {{row:act_attribution_share_shared}} of the credit among those who pressed for it, and the first year {{row:act_period_share}} of that.
- **Guinea-Bissau**: a {{row:gnb_p_adopted}} chance that the 2022 tobacco taxes are applied within a few years. Nothing had been applied by November 2026, and a military takeover in late 2025 makes government action less predictable.
- **From price to health**: shared figures. 1% higher cigarette prices cut the smoking burden by {{row:tob_burden_per_price}}% in the long run, half through quitting (gains about 8 years later) and half through never starting (about 40 years later). 1% higher alcohol prices cut alcohol's burden by {{row:alc_burden_per_price}}%, heavy drinking's price response in a meta-analysis, with gains about 3 years later.
- **Counting**: a changed tax counts for the site's standard number of years, after its wait, discounted. Deaths are valued by age; the illness part stays in DALYs.
- **Enforcement**: the share of a tax change that reaches prices. For tobacco in Liberia it is {{row:lbr_tob_implementation_adj}}: tobacco import excise fell from over $3 million in 2013 to $6,000 in 2023, and the founders write that almost none of the market pays tax. For alcohol it is {{row:lbr_alc_implementation_adj}}: WHO puts recorded (taxed) drinking at about a third of the total, the rest home-made cane spirits and other untaxed drink.
- **Credit**: for the stopped order, {{row:eo_attribution_share_shared}} among the outside advisers who argued against it. For Guinea-Bissau, {{row:gnb_attribution_share_shared}} among those working on the taxes. Governments, which set and collect the taxes, are credited through the mobiliser's share.
- **Side effects**: second-hand smoke averted (GBD's second-hand-smoke burden over smoking's) and the harm heavy drinking does to others. Household spending on tobacco is left at nothing: quitters save, but those who keep smoking pay more.
- **Missing data**: every branch is discounted, because no change in prices, smoking or drinking from Concentric's work is measured.

### What drives the result

The stopped order is about two thirds of the result and Liberia's 2025 act about a quarter; Guinea-Bissau is about a twentieth. Tobacco and alcohol count about equally: tobacco's stopped cut counts the full years, alcohol's only one, but alcohol taxes are better collected. The biggest swings are:

- the mobiliser's share and the years a won tax counts;
- the missing-data discount;
- the chance the order was real and stopped, and Concentric's share of stopping it;
- the share of the excise the order would have cut;
- how much of a tobacco tax change reaches prices in Liberia;
- the price response of heavy drinking.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **Whether the order existed and was stopped by Concentric.** The founders report it and Ambitious Impact repeats it, but nothing else records it. The founders first valued it at $42 per DALY and later, counting alcohol only, at $192.
- **How much of a tax reaches prices.** Liberia's tobacco excise is barely collected, so a change in rates may move little; most alcohol drunk is untaxed.
- **Price changes.** Neither the order's rates, Liberia's beverage excise before 2025, nor the act's effect on cigarette prices was found.
- **The first year's cost.** It is the charity's own rounded figure, without audited accounts.

### What isn't modelled

- Tax revenue, which is a transfer from smokers and drinkers to the government.
- The second year onwards, whose spending isn't published; only what the first year's work bought.
- Benin, where Concentric has only a letter of intent.
- Its briefs, memoranda and the cessation preprint, except through the branches.

### How this model was checked

Independent audits are in audits/concentric-policies-charityevals/. Round 1 found the stopped order's alcohol part counted for five years although the Legislature cut beverage excise a year later (now about one year), and Concentric's part in stopping it counted twice (the chance is now about the event only). It also led to a lower share of tobacco tax reaching prices, from the founders' own figures; GBD's deaths by age in place of judged shares; the order's price change built from the excise's share of the price; the alcohol enforcement share from WHO's recorded and unrecorded drinking; a neutral next-dollar room, the last funding statement having run out; and no household-spending side effect for a tax. The result moved from 0.065 to 0.031. Round 2, a scoped verification, passed: it re-checked WHO's recorded and unrecorded drinking and the founders' figures; its low findings (wording and links) were fixed.
