### Summary

icddr,b is a health research institute in Bangladesh that also runs health programmes, among them Reach Up, in which health workers visit parents of young children and run parenting groups. The Happier Lives Institute estimates Reach Up creates 50 WELLBYs per $1,000; on our scale that is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="icddr-b-hli">1.9</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

HLI found no way for donors to fund this programme directly, so it does not recommend it; the estimate is here because HLI published it. HLI publishes no spreadsheet for it: the model is built from the figures in HLI's report, each quoted on its row, and reproduces HLI's 50.

### What the charity does

In Reach Up, health workers visit families with children under three and run group sessions, showing parents how to play, read and talk with their children and how to avoid harsh punishment.

### How the impact happens

HLI asks whether better parenting in early childhood leads to happier adults:

1. **Effect:** from two trials of home visiting in poorer countries and four causal studies of preschool stimulation in rich ones, weighted equally, HLI estimates the children's later depression scores improve by 0.23 standard deviations for 32 years: 14.5 WELLBYs for the child and 7 for the rest of the household.
2. **Adjustment:** HLI cuts this by 77%, mainly because it doubts the long-run results would replicate, to about 5 WELLBYs.
3. **Cost:** $98 per child.

5 WELLBYs for $98 is 50 WELLBYs per $1,000.

### How the model works

HLI's figures come first, ending at WELLBYs per $1,000. Our steps follow: the benefit is spread over the 32 years HLI gives it and discounted at the shared rate, and credit by share of resources counts staff's forgone pay when that is on. The site converts WELLBYs to healthy years at the shared exchange rate.

### What drives the result

- **HLI's 77% discount**, a judgment about replicability; at no discount the estimate would be more than four times higher.
- **How long the effect lasts** (32 years), which also sets how much our discounting cuts.
- **The cost per child** ($98), which HLI expects to fall if icddr,b scales up.

### Where we differ from HLI

- **Exchange rate (changed for consistency):** every HLI model converts WELLBYs at one rate, set so that a fall in depression counts the same in HLI's analyses as in the site's other depression models (0.13 doubled-income years per WELLBY; "1 point more life satisfaction for a year vs doubled-income year" under Your values). HLI compares charities with GiveDirectly's cash transfers, which implies about 1.15, nine times higher; the evaluator view converts at that rate.
- **Discounting.** HLI counts a WELLBY decades from now the same as one now. Our view discounts the 32 years of benefit at the shared rate, which cuts the figure by about two fifths at the default 4%.
- **Staff pay:** with credit by share of resources, staff's forgone pay counts as an in-kind contribution.

### Biggest uncertainties

The long-run evidence on wellbeing is thin: two small trials in poorer countries (426 people in all) and preschool studies from rich countries. HLI calls its estimate speculative.

### What isn't modelled

Effects on the children's health, schooling and earnings other than through wellbeing, and icddr,b's research and other programmes.
