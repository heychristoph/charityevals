### Summary

Feeding America is the national office of the US network of more than 250 food banks and 60,000 pantries and meal programmes. Our model of its food network work in the year to June 2025 comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="feeding-america-charityevals">0.018</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**. Its policy, research and awareness work has a model of its own ([advocacy](/feeding-america/feeding-america-charityevals-advocacy), <span data-figure="headline" data-model="feeding-america-charityevals-advocacy">0.1</span>), weighted by its spending, about a fifth of the total.

The site values food and money on one log-income scale, and the people helped live in the US: about $90 of food a person a year is a small share of what even a poor US household spends. Most of the resources behind the food are not Feeding America's: the food companies' donated food (about $5.7 billion at retail) and the food banks' and pantries' cost of handing it out dwarf Feeding America's own $289 million. The advocacy does more per dollar because federal food programmes move billions, but the coalition caused only a small share of what they gained.

### What the charity does

Feeding America signs contracts with about 600 national food manufacturers and retailers, whose surplus food goes to its member food banks (2.4 billion pounds in the year, about 2 billion of the network's 5.9 billion meals). It buys food and transport for members, who pay it back; gives them grants for trucks, cold storage, food sourcing, child hunger and food-as-medicine programmes and operations ($195 million of grants); helps people apply for SNAP; and campaigns on federal food programmes.

### How the impact happens

The food saves the households who eat it what they would otherwise spend on groceries, which on the site's scale is income, and keeps some food out of landfill. SNAP help enrols households who would not otherwise have applied. Advocacy, with the rest of the anti-hunger coalition, wins or keeps federal food money.

### How the models work

**Food network** (cost: Feeding America's own cash on member services, food procurement and programs, less what members paid back for food bought for them, plus its share of management and fundraising: $289 million):
- **Food**: 2.4 billion pounds × the audited wholesale value ($1.90 a pound) × 1.25 for retail prices × 0.8 for food chosen by others, spread over the 50 million people who get charitable food, at the shared consumption of US food-aid clients (30% of the US average per person). Credited by Feeding America's share of the resources behind it (about 4%: its cash against the donors' food at retail and the food banks' and pantries' cost), half of it counted as caused by Feeding America, 95% reaching households, discounted for missing data.
- **SNAP**: 312,000 applications helped, 60% approved, enrolled households' benefits at 0.8 of the average for 1.5 years, a third of the enrolments caused by the help (a trial of SNAP outreach found two thirds), credited by Feeding America's SNAP cost against $150 per assisted application from all funders, net of taxpayers' cost; discounted for missing data.
- **Landfill**: 30% of the donated food would otherwise be landfilled at about half a tonne of CO₂e a tonne, nearly all methane, with the food branch's credit, through the site's climate conversion.

**Advocacy** (cost: policy and advocacy, research and analysis and public awareness, the average of FY2021 to FY2025 in 2025 prices, $45 million a year, plus overhead): three wins over those five years, each with its own share caused by the coalition: the 2021 Thrifty Food Plan re-evaluation (21% higher SNAP benefits, about $17 billion a year; 12%), permanent Summer EBT ($2.7 billion a year; 25%) and the 2025 law's smaller SNAP cut than the House passed (about $10 billion a year; 5%). Each counts the site's years for a won reform, valued for SNAP households net of what it costs the taxpayers (federal taxes fall mostly on richer households; raising a dollar costs 1.2), with Feeding America a quarter of the coalition, its next dollar at 39% of the past average, discounted for missing data.

### What drives the result

Food network: the missing-data discount, the value of a healthy year, how much of the food would have reached people anyway (25% to 80%), and how poor the people served are. Advocacy: the share of each win the coalition caused, Feeding America's share of the coalition, and the next dollar against the past average.

### Biggest uncertainties

How much of the donated food would have reached people without Feeding America's contracts; how poor pantry clients are; how many SNAP enrolments the help causes; and whether the coalition caused much of the federal money at all.

### What isn't modelled

Health effects of food (no controlled evidence; part of the money value) and wellbeing beyond the money value (it would count the same change twice). Donations the awareness work brings to food banks (no count is published).

### How this model was checked

Built on 13 November 2026 from the audited accounts and annual report for the year to June 2025. Round 1 of the independent audit found 2 high and 6 medium issues in the food network model and 2 high and 5 medium in the advocacy model. Fixed: the clients' consumption now reads a shared figure for US food-aid clients (the Salvation Army build reads it too: 0.6 → 0.3 of the average), the donors' food enters the credit at retail, landfill emissions go through the climate conversion, the SNAP credit follows its cost, SNAP's value against cash, its administration and the federal tax incidence are shared figures, and the marginal cost of public funds is the site's shared fact. The advocacy model now counts each win with its own source and counterfactual, including the Thrifty Food Plan re-evaluation it had missed, against the same five years' average spending. Food network 0.010 → 0.018, advocacy 0.016 → 0.10. Later rounds: see the audits folder. Round 3 verified the site-wide methane change (the landfill tonnes, 95% methane, get methane's own timing and ozone) and the removal of two unused smoke rows; result unchanged at three figures.
