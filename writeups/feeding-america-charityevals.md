### Summary

Feeding America is the national office of the US network of more than 200 food banks and 60,000 pantries and meal programmes. Our model of its food network work in the year to June 2025 comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="feeding-america-charityevals">0.01</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**. Its policy, research and awareness work has a model of its own ([advocacy](/feeding-america/feeding-america-charityevals-advocacy), <span data-figure="headline" data-model="feeding-america-charityevals-advocacy">0.016</span>), weighted by its spending, about a fifth of the total.

Both are far below the global-health charities on the site. The site values food and money on one log-income scale, and the people helped live in the US: about $90 of food a person a year is a small share of what a poor US household spends. And most of the resources behind the food are not Feeding America's: the food companies' donated food ($4.6 billion at wholesale) and the food banks' and pantries' cost of handing it out dwarf Feeding America's own $289 million.

### What the charity does

Feeding America signs contracts with about 600 national food manufacturers and retailers, whose surplus food goes to its member food banks (2.4 billion pounds in the year, about 2 billion of the network's 5.9 billion meals). It buys food and transport for members, who pay it back; gives them grants for trucks, cold storage, food sourcing and operations ($195 million of grants); helps people apply for SNAP; and campaigns on federal food programmes.

### How the impact happens

The food saves the households who eat it what they would otherwise spend on groceries, which on the site's scale is income. SNAP help enrols households who would not otherwise have applied, and each enrolment brings about $2,200 a person a year, net of what it costs taxpayers. Advocacy protects or adds federal food money.

### How the models work

- **Cost**: Feeding America's own cash spending (audited, before donated goods) on member services, food procurement and programs, less what members paid back for food bought for them, plus its share of management and fundraising: $289 million.
- **Food**: 2.4 billion pounds × the audited wholesale value ($1.90 a pound) × 1.25 for retail prices × 0.8 for food chosen by others, spread over the 50 million people who get charitable food; valued on the log-income scale at the clients' consumption (60% of the US average per person). Credited by Feeding America's share of the resources behind it (about 5%), half of it counted as caused by Feeding America, 90% eaten, and discounted for missing data.
- **SNAP**: 312,000 applications helped, 60% approved, 1.9 people each, 1.5 years of benefits, a third of them caused by the help (a trial of SNAP outreach found two thirds), Feeding America a quarter of the resources behind the network's SNAP help, net of taxpayers' cost; discounted for missing data.
- A side-effect share of +10% covers the members' own food that Feeding America's grants also help move, food kept out of landfill and other programmes.

### What drives the result

The missing-data discount, the value of a healthy year, how much of the food would have reached people anyway (25% to 80%), and how poor the people served are.

### Biggest uncertainties

How much of the donated food would have reached people without Feeding America's contracts; whether pantry clients are as poor as assumed; how many SNAP enrolments the help causes; for the advocacy model, whether the coalition caused any of the federal money at all.

### What isn't modelled

Health effects of food (no controlled evidence; part of the money value) and wellbeing beyond the money value (it would count the same change twice).

### How this model was checked

Built on 13 November 2026 from the audited accounts and annual report for the year to June 2025; mechanical checks pass. The independent audit loop had not started when this build stopped (see the audits folder).
