### Summary

Protect Democracy works to keep the United States from sliding into a more authoritarian government, through lawsuits, election protection, state laws, tools and public education. Our model of its 2024 work comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="protect-democracy-charityevals">0.014</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**, about a fifth of the site's global-health median. It is a very uncertain figure: the main step, how much a year of the whole pro-democracy field's work lowers the chance of the US leaving the democracies, is our judgment.

### What the charity does

Protect Democracy was founded in 2016 by former government lawyers. Its 501(c)(3), Protect Democracy Project, spent $34.4 million in 2024, $28.3 million of it on its programme. That included $7.2 million of grants to other groups. It sues to stop attempts to overturn elections and executive overreach. It brought the defamation case in which a jury awarded two Georgia election workers $148 million against Rudy Giuliani. It runs VoteShield, which watches 167 million voter records in 26 states for suspicious changes. It convenes election officials and police, and it helped Arizona, Minnesota, Michigan and Wisconsin pass laws protecting elections and election workers. A smaller part of its work promotes proportional representation.

### How the impact happens

Forecasters give the US about a 15% chance of falling to a "hybrid regime" before 2030. If that happened, people would lose part of what democracy brings. Across 175 countries, becoming a democracy raises income per person by about a fifth in the long run. People would also lose freedoms and fair treatment by the state. Protect Democracy's work, with that of hundreds of other groups, aims to lower that chance: through the courts, election officials and state laws.

### How the model works

- **Cost:** the 2024 Form 990 (the audited statements aren't public). Management and fundraising are spread over the programme. Grants count as the work they fund.
- **Backsliding branch (about 90% of spending):**
  - the stake: the whole US population keeps the income gain of democracy, at the share reached in the five years a win counts on the site. The gain builds over about 25 years, so the share is small;
  - a slide to a hybrid regime counts as 60% of the full loss, and the cross-country effect counts at 0.8 for a rich country;
  - a year of the field's work removes a judged 5% of the 15% chance;
  - Protect Democracy's credit is its share of the money behind that work: its spending over $3 billion of yearly foundation money for democracy, of which 40% goes to work like its own. That is about 2.4%;
  - the years counted are discounted at the shared rate plus the shared chance that a democratic gain is undone later.
- **Proportional-representation branch (about 10%):** about 300,000 people a year newly governed under proportional representation in expectation, as when Portland, Oregon, adopted it in 2022. The reform is worth a judged 5% of democracy's gain. Protect Democracy has a 10% share of the movement.
- **Side effects:** the good of democracy beyond income (+30%), political violence averted (+5%), effects on democracy and aid abroad (+20%) and backlash (−5%), each a share of the income value.
- **Next dollar:** 35% of the average. Protect Democracy held $93 million of net assets at the end of 2024, about 2.7 years of spending.

### What drives the result

1. The share of the backsliding chance a year of the field's work removes (1% to 20%).
2. The share of democracy's long-run income gain reached in the years counted (5% to 20%).
3. The share of the field's money doing work like Protect Democracy's (20% to 70%), and the forecast chance itself (7% to 30%).

Proportional representation adds only about 3% of the result.

### Biggest uncertainties

- No study measures how much litigation and election protection lower the chance of democratic breakdown. Our 5% is a judgment between two facts. Democracies that survive a slide do so mostly through courts and elections, which is where this work acts. But resources explain little of who wins on contested US policy.
- The forecast is from thin prediction markets on an index the Economist publishes. Expert forecasts have moved by half within months.
- The income effect comes from mostly poorer countries changing regime. A rich democracy sliding part-way may lose less.

### What isn't modelled

- Work of the sister 501(c)(4), Protect Democracy United, which spent $5.0 million in 2024 on lobbying.
- Any value per vote, voter record protected or lawsuit won: these count only through the chance of a slide.
- Single 2025 cases against the administration: the model is of 2024 spending and its work.

### How this model was checked

Built from the template and audited by independent reviewers (rounds in audits/protect-democracy-charityevals/).
