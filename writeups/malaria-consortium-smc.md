### Summary

Malaria Consortium delivers seasonal malaria chemoprevention (SMC): monthly courses of antimalarial drugs for children aged 3–59 months during the four or five months of peak transmission. GiveWell models twenty locations. This page shows Nigeria's Federal Capital Territory, the median one: **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="malaria-consortium-smc">25</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**; GiveDirectly comes to about <span data-figure="headline" data-model="givedirectly">4.4</span>. Across locations the result runs from about 8.8 (Northern Bahr el Ghazal, South Sudan) to 100 (Kebbi, Nigeria).

The model reproduces all 88 of GiveWell's intermediate figures, and those of every other location.

### How the impact happens

1. Malaria Consortium, governments and other funders pay for drugs and door-to-door delivery; the model counts all of it.
2. Children receive up to four or five monthly cycles. The share of malaria deaths that fall in the treated season sets how much of the year's risk the drugs can address.
3. Treated children die of malaria less often; GiveWell takes the reduction from trials and adjusts it for local conditions.
4. Smaller benefits follow for untreated older people (less transmission) and for treated children's adult incomes.

### What drives the result

- **Malaria mortality in the location**, the single biggest uncertainty: about 15 to 34 years of healthy life per $1,000 across GiveWell's 25th–75th percentile.
- **Upstream costs per child**: what it really costs to deliver all cycles.
- **Seasonality**: the share of malaria deaths in the months the program covers.
- Your moral weight on saving lives.

### Where we differ from GiveWell

Nothing beyond the shared accounting choices. Malaria Consortium's staff salary gap isn't estimated yet.

### What isn't modelled

Drug resistance trends beyond GiveWell's adjustments, room for more funding, and organisational strength.
