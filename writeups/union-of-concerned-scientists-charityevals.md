### Summary

The Union of Concerned Scientists (UCS) puts scientists' and engineers' analysis behind advocacy: against the risk of nuclear war, for climate and clean-energy policy, and for independent federal science. We model all its programmes in the year to September 2025 in three parts. Its nuclear-weapons and science-and-democracy work comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per $1,000**, almost all from the nuclear work; the plausible range runs from {{low}} to {{high}}. Its climate, transport and western states work comes to **{{result:union-of-concerned-scientists-charityevals-climate}}**, a small net harm: it averts a tonne of CO₂e for about $190, but most of its tonnes come from the Inflation Reduction Act, whose public money costs taxpayers more than the tonnes are worth on the site's scale. Its food and farm work comes to **{{result:union-of-concerned-scientists-charityevals-food}}**. All three rest on judgments about how much of each outcome is due to UCS.

### What the charity does

UCS spent $49.8 million in the year to September 2025, $44.0 million of it on programmes:

- **Climate and Clean Energy: $19.0 million.** Clean power and the grid in the states, regional grid operators' plans, the science behind lawsuits against fossil-fuel companies, and nuclear power safety.
- **Clean Transportation: $6.5 million.** Analysis and advocacy on vehicle standards, electric cars and trucks, and public transport.
- **Global Security: $5.9 million.** Nuclear weapons: the new ICBM, plutonium pit production, missile defence, nuclear testing, and compensation for people harmed by weapons production and testing.
- **Center for Science and Democracy: $4.6 million.** Defending federal science and public comment on rules, and evidence on elections.
- **Food and Environment: $4.5 million.** Farm conservation, fertilizer, farmworkers and healthy food.
- **Western States: $3.2 million.** Climate and pollution policy in California and the West.
- **Legislative work: $0.3 million**, spread over the programmes by cost.

### How the impact happens

**Nuclear war.** A nuclear war carries a yearly chance of catastrophe, from the domain experts' forecasts in the 2022 Existential Risk Persuasion Tournament. A typical project cuts that risk by a share per dollar, from Rethink Priorities' cross-cause model. The cut counts for the years such progress would otherwise have taken (5 by default), each year discounted. A war the world survives kills 45% of people, valued like every death on the site; a war that ends humanity's future also takes everyone not yet born, counted through the discount rate.

**Science and democracy.** This counts as better use of US public money: the federal budgets the work touches, the share of their value lost to waste, the small part of that the work closes, valued as a dollar to the average American.

**Climate.** Five policies won from October 2021 to September 2025 have published tonnes: the Inflation Reduction Act, EPA's 2024 vehicle and power-plant standards, California's Advanced Clean Cars II rule, and its 2025 law extending its carbon market to 2045. Each counts the tonnes it would avert, the 30% of a won policy due to advocacy at all, and UCS's share of that advocacy. Tonnes become deaths averted through the site's one conversion. The act also won public money for US households, its home rebates and credits. Like every model of public money won, a dollar counts as the recipients' gain less the taxpayers' cost. UCS gets half its share of the act for this money, since its work was on clean power and farms.

**Food and farms.** UCS estimated that the Upper Midwest's wetlands avert about $23 billion of flood damage a year. That analysis was part of the case for federal wetland protections, which a court upheld in May 2025. The value is the flood damage that draining farmed wetlands would bring, times the chance the lawsuit would have won, times UCS's small share of the defence.

UCS is credited with 6% of the advocacy behind EPA's vehicle standards, 10% behind California's car rule, 4% behind the carbon-market extension, 3% behind the power-plant standards and 1% behind the Inflation Reduction Act. EPA has since repealed the vehicle standards and is repealing the power-plant standards; Congress revoked California's waiver in 2025, now in court; the 2025 budget law cut much of the act's credits. Each counts only what survives, by shared figures.

Some value is added as shares. The nuclear part adds 7%: work against a limited nuclear war and the 2025 expansion of radiation compensation. The climate part adds 15%: other state laws, grid plans and the science behind climate lawsuits. It takes off 2% for safety campaigns that may slow nuclear power. The food part adds 50% for its fertilizer, groundwater, farm bill and farmworker work.

UCS's net assets were about 17 months of spending in September 2025, and its donations grew that year. The next dollar of its nuclear, science and food work counts at {{row:marginal_vs_past_average}} of the past average. Its climate work now faces harder ground, so its next dollar counts at 33%.

### What drives the result

The nuclear work is about half the first model's cost and almost all its value. Only half this spending is on nuclear risk, and the next dollar counts for less.

About three-fifths of it comes from the Inflation Reduction Act, a fifth of it from the public money the act won for households. The food result is low because the lawsuit was unlikely to win, the government and four other groups fought it, only farmed wetlands were at stake and they drain slowly, and the flood damage averted is money for US households.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How much a dollar of nuclear work cuts the risk.** Rethink's figures are placeholders, and the range runs ten times either way: 0.91 to 43.
- **How many years a cut or a won policy counts.** The shared slider runs from 1 to 20 years.
- **How likely and how deadly a nuclear war is.** The share of people who die in a war the world survives runs from 15% to 85%.
- **UCS's share of each climate win, and how much of each survives.** These are judgments.
- **UCS's share of the wetland defence.** It was not a party to the case; its 1% share moves the food result ten times either way.

### What isn't modelled

- Losses beyond deaths, such as lost income and health, are a shared figure set to 0.
- The tonnes of UCS's other state laws are not estimated; they enter only as a share.

### How this model was checked

The three models were built from their family templates: catastrophic risk, climate policy and policy. The vehicle standards and the Inflation Reduction Act, including the act's methane share, are shared figures. The first climate audit asked for the carbon-market extension as its own win and the food work as its own model, both done. The audit rounds are in audits/ under each model's name.
