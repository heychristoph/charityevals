### Summary

The Union of Concerned Scientists (UCS) puts scientists' and engineers' analysis behind advocacy: against the risk of nuclear war, for climate and clean-energy policy, and for independent federal science. We model all its programmes in the year to September 2025 in two parts. Its nuclear-weapons and science-and-democracy work comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="union-of-concerned-scientists-charityevals">14</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**, almost all from the nuclear work; the plausible range runs from about 0.6 to about 330. Its climate, transport and food work comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="union-of-concerned-scientists-charityevals-climate">0.0012</span>**, about $170 per tonne of CO₂e averted. Both rest on judgments about how much of each outcome is due to UCS.

### What the charity does

UCS spent $49.8 million in the year to September 2025, $44.0 million of it on programmes:

- **Climate and Clean Energy: $19.0 million.** Clean power and the grid in the states, regional grid operators' plans, the science behind lawsuits against fossil-fuel companies, and nuclear power safety.
- **Clean Transportation: $6.5 million.** Analysis and advocacy on vehicle standards, electric cars and trucks, and public transport.
- **Global Security: $5.9 million.** Nuclear weapons: the new ICBM, plutonium pit production, missile defence, nuclear testing, and compensation for people harmed by weapons production and testing.
- **Center for Science and Democracy: $4.6 million.** Defending federal science and public comment on rules, and evidence on elections.
- **Food and Environment: $4.5 million.** Farm conservation, fertilizer, farmworkers and healthy food.
- **Western States: $3.2 million.** Climate and pollution policy in California and the West.
- **Legislative work: $0.3 million**, spread over the programmes by cost.

### How the impact happens

**Nuclear war.** A nuclear war carries a yearly chance of catastrophe, from the domain experts' forecasts in the 2022 Existential Risk Persuasion Tournament. A typical project cuts that risk by a share per dollar, from Rethink Priorities' cross-cause model. The cut counts for the years such progress would otherwise have taken (5 by default), each year discounted. A war the world survives kills 45% of people, valued like every death on the site; a war that ends humanity's future also takes everyone not yet born, counted through the discount rate.

**Science and democracy.** This counts as better use of US public money: the federal budgets the work touches, the share of their value lost to waste, the small part of that the work closes, valued as a dollar to the average American.

**Climate.** Four policies won from October 2021 to September 2025 have published tonnes: the Inflation Reduction Act, EPA's 2024 vehicle and power-plant standards, and California's Advanced Clean Cars II rule. Each counts the tonnes it would avert, the 30% of a won policy due to advocacy at all, and UCS's share of that advocacy. Tonnes become deaths averted through the site's one conversion.

### How the model works

| Adjustment | Nuclear | Science and democracy | Climate wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| How far the estimate applies | 0.8 | 1 | 0.8 |
| Work no one else would have done, or would have come anyway | 0.7 | 0.6 | 0.6 to 0.8 (shared) |
| UCS's share of the work | 0.9 | 0.5 | 1% to 10% |
| Share of the win still delivered | | | 5% to 35% (shared) |
| Missing-data discount | 0.5 | 0.5 | 0.5 |

UCS is credited with 6% of the advocacy behind EPA's vehicle standards, 10% behind California's car rule, 3% behind the power-plant standards and 1% behind the Inflation Reduction Act. EPA has since repealed the vehicle standards and is repealing the power-plant standards; Congress revoked California's waiver in 2025, now in court; the 2025 budget law cut much of the act's credits. Each counts only what survives, by the same shared figures our other climate models use.

Some value is added as shares. The nuclear part adds 8%: work against a limited nuclear war, the 2025 expansion of radiation compensation, and the democracy work. The climate part adds 60%: California's 2025 cap-and-invest extension and other state laws, grid plans, the science behind climate lawsuits, and the farm work. It takes off 2% for safety campaigns that may slow nuclear power.

UCS had about 17 months of spending in reserve in September 2025 and grew its donations that year. Its climate work now faces harder ground. The next dollar counts at 36% of the past average.

### What drives the result

The nuclear work is about half the first model's cost and almost all its value. The result is below the Arms Control Association's 41, because only half this spending is on nuclear risk and the next dollar counts for less. It is above the Federation of American Scientists' 1.4, which spends only about 3% on nuclear work.

The climate result is near our other climate advocacy models: the Environmental Defense Fund 0.0018 and Clean Air Task Force 0.0067. Three-quarters of it comes from the Inflation Reduction Act.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How much a dollar of nuclear work cuts the risk.** Rethink's figures are placeholders, and the range runs ten times either way: 2.6 to 124.
- **How many years a cut or a won policy counts.** The shared slider runs from 1 to 20 years.
- **How likely and how deadly a nuclear war is.** The share of people who die in a war the world survives runs from 15% to 85%.
- **UCS's share of each climate win, and how much of each survives.** These are judgments.

### What isn't modelled

- Losses beyond deaths, such as lost income and health, are a shared figure set to 0.
- The tonnes of California's 2025 laws are not estimated; they enter only as a share.

### How this model was checked

Both models were built from their family templates: the catastrophic-risk one, as for the Federation of American Scientists, and the climate policy one, as for the Environmental Defense Fund. The Environmental Defense Fund's model now reads the same shared figures for the vehicle standards and the Inflation Reduction Act. The audit rounds are in audits/union-of-concerned-scientists-charityevals/ and audits/union-of-concerned-scientists-charityevals-climate/.
