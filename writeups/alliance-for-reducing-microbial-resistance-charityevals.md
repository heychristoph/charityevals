### Summary

ARMoR (the Alliance for Reducing Microbial Resistance) argues in Europe for paying drug makers for new antibiotics through subscriptions. Our model counts what it spent from its launch in September 2023 to December 2025 against what it won in that time: a voluntary framework for member states' joint subscriptions in the EU pharmaceutical package, which it had made its key ask. It comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**. The figure is very uncertain: whether member states ever fund the framework, how many new drugs a reward brings, and ARMoR's share of the credit are all judgments.

### What the charity does

ARMoR started in September 2023 from Ambitious Impact's incubation programme. It is a small team, fiscally sponsored by the Players Philanthropy Fund, and publishes no accounts: it received $226,477 to March 2025, and its 2026 budget is $280,000. Drug makers have little reason to develop new antibiotics, because new ones are kept in reserve and sell little. A subscription pays a fixed sum for access to a new drug, however little is used.

ARMoR writes analyses of what pull incentives would return to the EU and to single countries (Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Germany), and takes them to officials. It spoke at a European Commission training for member states, gave evidence to the German Bundestag and coordinated a joint EU position of non-profits. It also does paid research for the World Bank, the Novo Nordisk Foundation, GARDP, SECURE and the Union for International Cancer Control, and assessed G7 commitments. In May 2026 it started a programme on access to antibiotics in low- and middle-income countries.

### How the impact happens

The EU agreed its new pharmaceutical law in December 2025. Next to a voucher for new antibiotics, it sets up a voluntary framework for member states to buy new antibiotics jointly by subscription, a Parliament proposal that ARMoR had pressed for. If member states fund it, drug makers expect more revenue from new antibiotics and bring more of them to market. Each new antibiotic averts deaths from drug-resistant infections for years, mostly among older people, where patients can get it. The payments come out of member states' budgets, and the drugs also save hospitals money.

### What drives the result

The size of a joint subscription moves the result most. Next come the mobiliser's share, ARMoR's share of the credit, the additional share of the payments and the years a won reform counts. Then the discount rate, the chance member states fund the framework, the missing-data discount, the share of patients new drugs reach and trust in the effect per drug.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **Whether member states fund the framework:** it is voluntary and unfunded, and the EU's last joint scheme for antibiotics stalled.
- **How many new drugs a reward brings:** the reward a drug needs is an estimate, and how much of a subscription goes to drugs that would exist anyway isn't measured.
- **ARMoR's part:** the clause came from the Parliament, and no one else has said what ARMoR contributed to it.

### What isn't modelled

The transferable exclusivity voucher in the same law isn't credited to ARMoR. It was the Commission's and Council's design, and ARMoR argued against its cap. National schemes still to come, the access programme begun in 2026 and the use of its research by its clients count only through the next dollar.

### How this model was checked

Audit rounds are listed in audits/alliance-for-reducing-microbial-resistance-charityevals/. Round 1 found two high and four medium issues. Deaths were valued at the world's ages, though the drugs mostly reach high-income countries, where the deaths are of older people; they are now valued at the ages of the deaths reached. The reward per drug is now in 2025 prices. The trust in the effect per drug was re-anchored on the study's own sensitivity runs, and the wait for a drug's benefits now follows from its life. A Swiss branch was considered and left at zero, as Switzerland's legal basis predates ARMoR's work there. Together these took the result from 0.068 to 0.051. Round 2 passed; its one low, the Swiss consultation's date, now has a source. Removing Ambitious Impact's pre-founding estimate of ARMoR is left to the site's owner.
