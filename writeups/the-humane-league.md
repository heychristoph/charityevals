### Summary

The Humane League (THL) gets food companies to honour the cage-free and broiler-welfare pledges they have already made, by tracking progress publicly and campaigning against laggards. ACE's 2025 analysis covers two accountability programs, one global cage-free and one for Better Chicken Commitment pledges in the UK and EU. In our view it comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="the-humane-league">120</span> years of healthy life per $1,000** you give. That uses our default exchange rates between suffering and healthy life, which is where most of the uncertainty is.

In ACE's own view the model reproduces all 44 of ACE's intermediate figures.

### How the impact happens

1. Companies covering about 114 million hens a year have cage-free pledges in THL's global program; some report progress, some don't.
2. THL's pressure brings implementation forward by some years. ACE estimates how many, separately for companies that report progress and those that don't (3 to 15 years); our view uses the shared "Years a won reform counts before it would have come anyway" (5 by default) for every group, counted from the date companies are now expected to comply and discounted from there, as ACE does.
3. Each year a hen spends in an aviary instead of a battery cage spares her about 17.5 days of suffering, weighted by intensity (Ambitious Impact's current figure, which ACE used for Sinergia Animal). ACE's THL sheet uses 7.9, from its 2025 SADs, which build in a lower weight for a hen; on the same basis as 17.5 that is 10.6.
4. ACE discounts for other organisations' contributions, for the share of the impact that would have happened anyway, and for the time value of future benefits.

### What drives the result

After the cross-cause exchange rates and your weight on animal suffering, the largest levers are **how many years a won reform counts** (about 26 to 370 years of healthy life per $1,000 over the slider's 1 to 20 years), the **suffering spared per hen** (about 74 to 200; the low end is ACE's 7.9, restated as 10.6), the **share of pledges companies keep** (about 51 to 130) and the **share of impact ACE attributes to the year evaluated** rather than THL's work in other years.

### Where we differ from ACE

- **Suffering per hen (changed for consistency).** ACE uses 7.9 days of suffering per hen-year out of a battery cage; we use the shared 17.5, so the same change counts the same in every model. With the points below, they take ACE's own figure, 94, to <span data-figure="headline" data-model="the-humane-league">120</span>.
- **Years brought forward (changed for consistency).** ACE's dates "if not for accountability" are 3 to 15 years after the expected ones; we use the shared slider for every group (5 years by default), so THL is credited the same span as every other won reform.
- **Pledges kept (changed for consistency).** ACE's own probabilities (90% and 80% for cage-free, 30% and 25% for broilers) become the shared figures: 92% of cage-free commitments fulfilled (Chicken Watch) and 30% for Better Chicken Commitment pledges.
- **A chicken against a person.** ACE's figures count a hen's suffering at 35% of a person's (Ambitious Impact's weight in ACE's 2025 SADs; 46% in the 2024 SADs behind the 17.5). We take out whichever weight each figure was built with. We count days of a chicken's pain and let the chicken slider (default 33%) set how they compare with a person's, as in every animal model.
- **Salary sacrifice (off by default).** Turn on "Credit by share of resources" and we count staff's forgone pay as an in-kind donation: we guess staff are 65% of program cost and earn 35% less than in comparable for-profit jobs, about 35% of cash spending. That leaves each donated dollar about 74% of the credit ACE gives it. Both numbers are guesses with wide ranges; replace them with the charity's real figures when we have them.
- **Credit for shared outcomes.** ACE's discounts for other organisations' contributions are built into its inputs here, and we keep them. They are ACE's estimate of the other groups' share of the work, so they are also what "Credit by share of resources" uses.
- **Pounds to dollars (changed for consistency).** ACE converts the charity's spending in pounds at its own rate, $1.2781 a pound; we use the 2024 IRS yearly average, $1.277, as for every model with costs in pounds. The difference is under 0.1%.
- **Past average, not the next dollar (ours):** ACE's estimate divides The Humane League's past campaign spending by the progress those campaigns won, an average. The next dollar does less than the average did, since an organisation pursues its best opportunities first: like every model built on an organisation's past spending over its past wins, our view counts the shared 50% of the average (18% to 100%, a judgment). The evaluator view keeps 100%.

### Biggest uncertainties

- How much earlier companies comply because of THL rather than other pressure.
- Several inputs are confidential data THL shared with ACE; the sheet shows them but they can't be checked independently.
