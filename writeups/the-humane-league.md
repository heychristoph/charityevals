### Summary

The Humane League (THL) gets food companies to honour the cage-free and broiler-welfare pledges they have already made, by tracking progress publicly and campaigning against laggards. ACE's 2025 analysis covers two accountability programs, one global cage-free and one for Better Chicken Commitment pledges in the UK and EU. In our view it comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="the-humane-league">130</span> years of healthy life per $1,000** you give. That uses our default exchange rates between suffering and healthy life, which is where most of the uncertainty is.

In ACE's own view the model reproduces all 44 of ACE's intermediate figures.

### How the impact happens

1. Companies covering about 114 million hens a year have cage-free pledges in THL's global program; some report progress, some don't.
2. THL's pressure brings implementation forward by some years. ACE estimates how many, separately for companies that report progress and those that don't.
3. Each year a hen spends in an aviary instead of a battery cage spares her about 17.5 days of suffering, weighted by intensity (Ambitious Impact's current figure, which ACE used for Sinergia Animal). ACE's THL sheet uses 7.9, from an older version.
4. ACE discounts for other organisations' contributions, for the share of the impact that would have happened anyway, and for the time value of future benefits.

### What drives the result

After the cross-cause exchange rates and your weight on animal suffering, the largest factual levers are the **share of impact ACE attributes to the years THL brings forward**, the **implementation dates** companies would otherwise have reached, and the **suffering spared per hen** (about 57 to 130 years of healthy life per $1,000 on its own; the low end is ACE's 7.9).

### Where we differ from ACE

- **Suffering per hen (changed for consistency).** ACE uses 7.9 days of suffering per hen-year out of a battery cage; we use the shared 17.5, so the same change counts the same in every model. This and the next point together take ACE's own figure, 94, to <span data-figure="headline" data-model="the-humane-league">130</span>.
- **A chicken against a person.** ACE's figures count a hen's suffering at 46% of a person's (Ambitious Impact's weight). We count days of a chicken's pain and let the chicken slider (default 33%) set how they compare with a person's, as in every animal model.
- **Salary sacrifice (off by default).** Turn on "Credit by share of resources" and we count staff's forgone pay as an in-kind donation: we guess staff are 65% of program cost and earn 35% less than in comparable for-profit jobs, about 35% of cash spending. That leaves each donated dollar about 74% of the credit ACE gives it. Both numbers are guesses with wide ranges; replace them with the charity's real figures when we have them.
- **Credit for shared outcomes.** ACE's discounts for other organisations' contributions are built into its inputs here, and we keep them.

### Biggest uncertainties

- How much earlier companies comply because of THL rather than other pressure.
- Several inputs are confidential data THL shared with ACE; the sheet shows them but they can't be checked independently.
