### Summary

The Lead Exposure Elimination Project (LEEP) persuades governments in low- and middle-income countries to ban lead in paint, and helps manufacturers switch. Founders Pledge estimated in 2023 that it costs $1.66 to prevent one child's lead exposure, in expectation. Valuing a prevented exposure with GiveWell's lead figures, that is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="lead-exposure-elimination-project-founders-pledge">18</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

Founders Pledge's figure is a cost per child; what a prevented exposure is worth is our bridge.

### What the charity does

In each country LEEP measures lead in paint on sale, shares the results with the government to win a commitment to regulate, helps manufacturers reformulate, and checks compliance afterwards. It has won commitments in Malawi, Madagascar, Pakistan, Sierra Leone, Zimbabwe and others.

### How the impact happens

Children exposed to lead paint have higher blood lead, which lowers IQ, and lower IQ means lower earnings. Lead also raises heart disease in adults.

### How the model works

Founders Pledge's $1.66 per child comes first. Our bridge follows: a prevented exposure lowers a child's blood lead by about 1.5 micrograms per decilitre throughout childhood (0.5 to 4, our judgment); GiveWell's Pure Earth figures turn that into IQ (0.15 points per microgram), earnings (0.67% a year per point, from 15 years on for 40 years, discounted at the shared rate), shared with the household, plus GiveWell's 20% for lead's other health effects.

### What drives the result

- **The fall in blood lead per child** (0.5 to 4): the result is proportional to it.
- **The discount rate:** the earnings come 15 years and more later.
- **How income and health are weighed** (Your values).

### Where we differ from Founders Pledge

- **The bridge** is ours; Founders Pledge publishes no sheet.
- **Credit by share of resources:** when on, staff's forgone pay counts as cost. Off by default.

### Biggest uncertainties

What a "prevented exposure" means in blood lead: Ambitious Impact's LEEP model attributes about a quarter of children's lead to paint, and GiveWell's Pure Earth analysis expects a fall of about 1 microgram across a covered population. Whether regulations are enforced.

### What isn't modelled

Exposure of adults, and benefits beyond the children counted in Founders Pledge's figure.
