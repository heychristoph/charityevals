### Summary

The Iodine Global Network (IGN) helps governments and salt producers keep salt iodized, so that children are not born and raised short of iodine. Our model counts what its 2025 work achieved: **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="iodine-global-network-charityevals">4.4</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**. The figure is very uncertain: it rests on judgments of how many births each piece of work protects and on how much mild iodine deficiency harms a child's development.

### What the charity does

IGN, formerly ICCIDD, is a small organisation with a core team of seven and a network of 11 regional and 140 national coordinators, most of them working in governments, universities and hospitals. It spent US$1.32 million in 2025. It doesn't make salt or run programmes itself: it gives technical help to salt producers and inspectors, reviews countries' iodization laws and standards, builds and tests tools for checking how a country's programme works (its Program Assessment Tool, sentinel surveillance, a processed-food assessment), and publishes research.

### How the impact happens

The brain is most sensitive to iodine in the womb and in the first years of life. A child whose mother lacks iodine in pregnancy, or who lacks it as an infant, scores lower on cognitive tests and, later, earns less. Iodized salt is the cheapest way to prevent this, but it only works if producers add enough iodine, the salt in shops and processed food is iodized, and governments keep the rules and check them. IGN's work is on those links.

### How the model works

- **Cost:** the average of IGN's 2024 and 2025 spending in 2025 prices, as the results of 2025 build on both years.
- **Value of a child protected:** 7.5 IQ points lost to iodine deficiency (a meta-analysis of trials and cohorts), halved because the deficiency at stake in IGN's countries is mostly mild to moderate; 0.67% higher earnings per IQ point (GiveWell's figure) for 40 working years starting about 15 years after birth, shared within the household and discounted; plus 10% for health gains (goitre, hypothyroidism) and less 2% for hyperthyroidism in older adults, a side effect.
- **Five pieces of work**, each counted in births a year:
  - **Lebanon:** the three main producers back to standard by early 2025; half the supply restored, half of the children reached otherwise deficient.
  - **Producer quality assurance:** small producers supplying 11.3 million people in Bolivia and 10 million in northern Argentina, and a tenth of the people in Madagascar, Mozambique and Peru; a tenth of these households move to adequately iodized salt.
  - **Vietnam:** the Ministry of Health kept iodized salt compulsory in processed foods, which carry most of the salt people eat; a tenth of births protected, a 30% chance (a single event) that the decree would have been weakened without the evidence IGN gave, half the foods complying.
  - **Davao City:** a restarted salt task force with a multi-year enforcement plan; a twentieth of the city's births moved to adequate salt if the plan is carried out.
  - **Policy reviews and tools** in ten more countries (India, Germany, the Maldives, South Sudan, Bhutan, Cameroon, Sri Lanka, Armenia, Tanzania, Moldova): 0.05% of their 28 million births a year protected, anchored on Moldova's law requiring iodized salt in bread, which followed IGN's processed-food assessment.
- **Credit:** IGN gets 20% to 40% of each result, as UNICEF, WHO, ministries, universities and the producers themselves put in resources too; each result counts for the years a won change counts on the site, and is discounted by the shared missing-data slider, as no change in children's iodine status is measured.
- **The next dollar** is valued at 56% of the average: money is tight after aid cuts, but the 2025 accounts close with a surplus.

### What drives the result

The years a won change counts, the discount rate and the missing-data slider move the result most; of the model's own judgments, the share of births the policy reviews and tools protect and how much milder deficiency costs in IQ.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **Mild deficiency:** the meta-analysis's studies are mostly of moderate to severe deficiency; supplements given to mildly deficient pregnant women in India and Thailand showed no clear effect on children's development. We take half the loss; a quarter or nine tenths are as defensible.
- **What a year's work moves:** no study measures how many children's iodine status changed because of IGN; the shares of births protected are our judgments from what the annual report describes.
- **India:** India is most of the births in the policy-review branch, so a review there that changes nothing, or a lot, moves the result.

### What isn't modelled

IGN's research publications, its regional meetings and its work in Europe, the US and Canada enter only through the policy reviews and tools branch's countries, not on their own.
