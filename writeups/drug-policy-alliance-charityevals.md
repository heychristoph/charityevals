### Summary

Drug Policy Alliance (DPA) campaigns to end the war on drugs in the United States. Counting its spending over fiscal years 2021 to 2025 against a share of the wins of those years, our model comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="drug-policy-alliance-charityevals">0.001</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**. That is far below most of the site's models. Ending arrests counts for little on a scale set by deaths. Marijuana legalisation's health cost, more people with cannabis use disorder, nearly cancels what it gains. And DPA's share of each coalition is small. The result could be below zero or about ten times higher.

### What the charity does

DPA is based in New York, with offices in California, New Jersey, New Mexico and Washington, D.C. In the year to May 2025 it spent $12.2 million, $10.1 million of it on programmes. Most of that is public policy and legal affairs ($6.3 million): lobbying lawmakers, drafting model laws and joining court cases. The rest is communication ($2.3 million), grants to partner campaigns ($0.6 million), the biennial International Drug Policy Reform Conference ($0.4 million) and health and harm reduction ($0.5 million). Its sister organisation Drug Policy Action is a 501(c)(4). It runs ballot campaigns and lobbying, gifts to it aren't tax-deductible, and its spending isn't counted here.

### How the impact happens

The model has three branches, one for each kind of win in the period.

- **Marijuana legalisation** in New York, New Jersey and New Mexico (all signed in early 2021). About 40,000 possession arrests a year stopped. About 760,000 old records were cleared. Adult users gain from legal, tested supply. Against that, studies of the states that legalised earlier find more cannabis use disorder, counted as healthy years lost. DPA's share of the coalitions behind the three laws is taken as 15%.
- **Overdose prevention.** New York City's two overdose prevention centres, the first sanctioned in the US, opened in 2021 and reverse about 500 overdoses a year. About 3% of those would have been deaths. Other harm-reduction laws DPA helped win (Rhode Island, Vermont, California's drug-checking law) add half as much again. The centres are paid for and run by OnPoint NYC, so DPA's share is 5%.
- **Oregon's Measure 110** decriminalised drug possession from February 2021 until it was repealed in September 2024. About 12,000 possession arrests a year stopped while it was in force. One study finds 182 more overdose deaths in 2021 because of it, but two later studies find none once fentanyl's arrival is accounted for. We count 15% of that study's figure as a harm. DPA's share is 8%: the ballot campaign was paid for by Drug Policy Action and others.

### How the model works

Every win counts the site's shared years before it would have come anyway, five, discounted. Measure 110 counts only its years in force. The value of a whole win is spread over the five years of spending and set against an average year's cost, $12.5 million in 2025 prices. Time spent in custody is valued with the site's shared figures for a year in prison, both lost wellbeing and the deaths it causes. Overdose deaths are valued at the ages of US overdose deaths. Income gains, users' gains and deaths go through the site's shared conversions. The cannabis use disorder rise, the users' gains, the centres' other services, families spared an arrest and Measure 110's overdoses are side effects, so the side-effect slider changes how much they count. Each branch is discounted for missing data. DPA has about a year of spending in available assets, so the next dollar is taken to do 49% as much as the average one.

### What drives the result

Most of the gain is records cleared and users' gains from legal supply. Most of the loss is the rise in cannabis use disorder. These nearly cancel. The overdose prevention centres and the arrests ended add a little, and Measure 110's possible overdose deaths take some away.

### Biggest uncertainties

- How much legalisation raises cannabis use disorder. We count a third of the rise one study finds; two others find no harm on average.
- What legal supply is worth to users, and what a cleared record is worth to the person who held it.
- DPA's share of each coalition.
- Whether Measure 110 caused any overdose deaths.

### What isn't modelled

Measure 110's treatment money. It moved cannabis tax from other public uses, and no study yet shows better outcomes from it. Campaigns that stalled elsewhere after Measure 110's repeal are a lost future gain, not a harm. Federal work with no change in the period, such as the proposal to reschedule marijuana, counts nothing.

### How this model was checked

Built from DPA's audited statements and published studies, then checked by independent audit rounds (audits/drug-policy-alliance-charityevals/).
