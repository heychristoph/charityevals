### Summary

Drug Policy Alliance (DPA) campaigns to end the war on drugs in the United States. We count its spending over fiscal years 2021 to 2025 against the share of that period's wins its work in those years bought. On that basis our model comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per $1,000**. That is a small net harm. The harms we count are cannabis use disorder and traffic deaths after legal sales, and overdose deaths Measure 110 may have caused. They outweigh what the wins gained: arrests ended, records cleared, users' gains from legal supply and overdose deaths averted. Each side is small and uncertain, so the true result could be anywhere from a larger harm to a small gain.

### What the charity does

DPA is based in New York, with offices in California, New Jersey, New Mexico and Washington, D.C. In the year to May 2025 it spent $12.2 million, $10.1 million of it on programmes. Most of that is public policy and legal affairs ($6.3 million): lobbying lawmakers, drafting model laws and joining court cases. The rest is communication ($2.3 million), grants to partner campaigns ($0.6 million), the biennial International Drug Policy Reform Conference ($0.4 million) and health and harm reduction ($0.5 million). Its sister organisation Drug Policy Action is a 501(c)(4). It runs ballot campaigns and lobbying, gifts to it aren't tax-deductible, and its spending isn't counted here.

### How the impact happens

The model has three branches, one for each kind of win in the period.

- **Marijuana legalisation** in New York, New Jersey and New Mexico, all signed in early 2021.
  - About 40,000 possession arrests a year stopped, counted from the states' 2020 levels.
  - About 380,000 people had records cleared. New York counts 203,000 charges and New Jersey 362,000 cases, at about 1.5 per person. More than 1,200 people in New Jersey were also released from probation.
  - Users aged 21 and over gain from legal, tested supply once sales began in 2022.
  - Against that, studies of the states that legalised earlier find more cannabis use disorder and more traffic deaths.
  - DPA's share of the coalitions behind the three laws is taken as 15%. Those campaigns ran for years before June 2020, so we count only 30% of DPA's work for them as falling in the period.
- **Overdose prevention.** New York City's two overdose prevention centres, the first sanctioned in the US, opened in November 2021. They reverse about 500 overdoses a year, and about 3% of those would have been deaths. Other harm-reduction laws DPA helped win (Rhode Island, Vermont, California's drug-checking law) add half as much again. The centres are paid for and run by OnPoint NYC, so DPA's share is 5%. Of DPA's work for them, 45% falls in the period: 30% for the centres, more for the later laws.
- **Oregon's Measure 110** decriminalised drug possession from February 2021 until it was repealed in September 2024. About 9,500 possession arrests a year stopped while it was in force, after allowing for the fall in policing in the pandemic. One study finds 182 more overdose deaths in 2021 because of it. Two later studies find none once fentanyl's arrival is accounted for, so we count 15% of that figure as a loss in each year in force. DPA's share is 8%, because the ballot campaign was paid for by Drug Policy Action and others, and 85% of its work for it falls in the period.

### How the model works

- **Years counted.** Every win counts the site's shared years before it would have come anyway (five), discounted. Measure 110 counts only its years in force.
- **Cost.** The value of a whole win is spread over the five years of spending. It is set against an average year's cost, $12.5 million in 2025 prices.
- **Time in custody.** Valued with the site's shared figures for a year in prison: both lost wellbeing and the deaths it causes, which are discounted for the wait until they come.
- **Deaths and income.** Overdose deaths are valued at the ages of US overdose deaths, traffic deaths at ages 15 to 49. Income gains, users' gains and deaths go through the site's shared conversions.
- **Side effects.** Four things count as side effects, so the side-effect slider changes how much they weigh:
  - the rise in cannabis use disorder;
  - traffic deaths;
  - the centres' other services;
  - families spared an arrest.
  Measure 110's possible overdose deaths are the outcome it meant to improve, so they stay in its branch.
- **Adjustments.** Each branch is discounted for missing data and for DPA's share. The doubt about the harm per arrest applies to the arrest terms only: the harms, users' gain and records have their own.
- **The next dollar.** DPA has about a year of spending in available assets, so the next dollar is taken to do 49% as much as the average one.

### What drives the result

Most of the gain is users' gains from legal supply, then the overdose deaths the centres averted and the records cleared. Most of the loss is the rise in traffic deaths and in cannabis use disorder, then Measure 110's possible overdose deaths. The losses are more than twice the gains.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **Users' gain from legal supply.** We take it as about 5% of what a user spends a year, about $110. No study prices it, and at zero the net harm grows by nearly half.
- **How much legalisation raises cannabis use disorder and traffic deaths.** We count a third of the use-disorder rise one study finds; two others find no harm on average. For traffic deaths we take about 5 more a year per million people while sales go on, and count 60% of them as caused: three of four studies find a rise, one of them a rise that fades within about 18 months.
- **Measure 110 and overdose deaths.** Whether it caused any is unsettled.
- **DPA's role.** Both its share of each coalition and how much of its work for each win fell in the period are uncertain.

### What isn't modelled

- **Measure 110's treatment money.** It moved cannabis tax from other public uses, and no study yet shows better outcomes from it.
- **Campaigns stalled after the repeal.** Decriminalisation campaigns elsewhere that stalled after Measure 110's repeal are a lost future gain, not a harm.
- **Federal work without a change.** Federal work that changed nothing in the period, such as the proposal to reschedule marijuana, counts nothing.

### How this model was checked

The model was built from DPA's audited statements and published studies, then checked by independent audit rounds (audits/drug-policy-alliance-charityevals/). Round 1 changed the result's sign. Its fixes:

- only the share of each campaign's work done in the cost period counts;
- traffic deaths after legal sales are added;
- New York's count of records leaves out the 2019 sealings, and records are counted per person;
- users aged 21 to 25 are counted, along with Cerdá et al.'s finding for 18 to 25;
- users' gains are counted as a purpose of legalisation, not a side effect, from the start of legal sales;
- the arrest counts allow for the pandemic;
- custody deaths are discounted for the wait until they come.

Round 2's fixes:

- the doubt about the harm per arrest no longer discounts the harms, which have their own;
- Measure 110's overdose deaths moved from side effect to the branch;
- the traffic rate is read in its sources' units;
- the users' gain arithmetic was corrected;
- probation ended in New Jersey was added.

Round 3 added a five-year study of traffic deaths, gave Measure 110's share of DPA's work in the period a range, and counted the later harm-reduction laws' campaigns as mostly inside the period.
