### Summary

Pallium India provides palliative care in Kerala and helps other Indian states build pain relief and opioid access for people with serious illness. The Happier Lives Institute's rough estimate for Pallium helping Karnataka catch up with Kerala is 16 to 108 WELLBYs per $1,000; we take the middle of that range, which on our scale is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="pallium-india-hli">20</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

HLI calls this "a highly uncertain BOTEC" (back-of-the-envelope calculation) and plans a fuller report. It gives only the range and no spreadsheet; the model takes HLI's two ends, quoted, and uses their geometric mean as the central figure, which is our choice.

### What the charity does

Pallium India grew out of a palliative care society in Calicut, Kerala, which now covers about two thirds of the people who need such care. It trains clinicians, supports state governments and works on the rules and supply chains that keep morphine from patients. HLI models a hypothetical project in which it does the same in Karnataka.

### How the impact happens

From HLI's appendix:

1. **The project:** about $200,000 a year for 5 years, bringing Karnataka to Kerala's coverage 5 to 8 years sooner than the state would manage alone, so that 1.1 to 3.2 million more patients receive palliative care over 10 years.
2. **Effect per patient:** pain falls by 4.5 points on a 0–10 scale, which HLI converts to 2.72 points of wellbeing, for the 15 days of care Pallium reports; with household spillovers and a replication discount, 0.07 WELLBYs a patient.
3. **Risks:** a 50% chance the project fails, and a 1% chance of an opioid overuse crisis.

### How the model works

HLI's two ends come first and their geometric mean is the central figure. Our steps follow: the patients are reached over 10 years, so the WELLBYs are spread over those years and discounted at the shared rate, and credit by share of resources counts staff's forgone pay when that is on. The uncertainty tools vary the central figure across HLI's range.

### What drives the result

- **How far and how fast coverage rises** because of Pallium, which HLI's two ends mostly differ on.
- **How long each patient benefits.** HLI uses Pallium's 15 days; it notes a founder once put the average at 3 months, which would multiply the effect.
- **Our choice of central figure** within HLI's range.

### Where we differ from HLI

- **A central figure.** HLI gives 16 to 108 and no best guess; we use the geometric mean, 42. HLI's 2025 giving season update quoted an earlier, wider range, 26 to 163.
- **Discounting** over the 10 years the patients are reached.
- **Staff pay:** with credit by share of resources, staff's forgone pay counts as an in-kind contribution.

### Biggest uncertainties

Everything about the project is hypothetical: whether Pallium expands to Karnataka, how much faster coverage grows, and how long patients benefit. HLI says its 0.07 WELLBYs per patient "should be understood as a plausible lower bound".

### What isn't modelled

Pallium's existing care in Kerala (paid for by restricted funding, HLI says), relief for families beyond HLI's spillover, and any effect in states other than Karnataka.
