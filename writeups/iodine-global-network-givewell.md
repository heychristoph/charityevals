### Summary

The Iodine Global Network (formerly ICCIDD) advises governments and salt producers on iodizing salt, so that children grow up without the iodine deficiency that lowers IQ. GiveWell's salt iodization analysis, built in 2014 for GAIN and ICCIDD and updated with its 2021 moral weights, gives **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars** in our view (GiveWell's own figure: about 7 times cash transfers). It is the same sheet as on GAIN's page, credited here to the Iodine Global Network.

### How the model works

A year of iodized salt costs about 10 cents per person. Children who grow up with it gain about a quarter of a standard deviation in IQ, worth higher wages for 40 working years once they start earning, discounted. GiveWell then allows for coverage short of the whole population, fade-out, children who don't benefit, the trials' validity, and a 50% chance that the organisation's work makes a difference at all.

### Where we differ from GiveWell

- **Wages per IQ point**: the site's one figure, GiveWell's current 0.67% per point, where the 2014 sheet takes about 1%.
- **Household sharing**: the site counts a long-term income gain as shared with the household (GiveWell's current multiplier, 2); the 2014 sheet counts the earner only.
- **Value of income and discounting**: the site's shared sliders (GiveWell's 1.44 per unit of log income and 4% at the defaults).
- **Missing data**: a model of salt iodization generally, with no count of whose salt the Iodine Global Network's support changed, so the shared missing-data step applies.

### Biggest uncertainties

GiveWell noted that ICCIDD advises and advocates rather than running programmes, so its 50% chance of impact is a guess; with the IQ gain and how much of it lasts, it drives the result. The analysis is over five years old, so it is shown on the charity's page but not ranked.
