### Summary

Fistula Foundation pays partner hospitals in Africa and Asia to repair obstetric fistula, an injury from obstructed labour that leaves women leaking urine or faeces. The Life You Can Save estimates $76 per DALY averted from Fistula Foundation's 16,587 surgeries in 2024; with the full cost of a surgery and the repairs that fail or would have happened anyway, that is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="fistula-foundation-tlycs">3.8</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**.

The model reproduces every cell of The Life You Can Save's sheet. The Life You Can Save calls its figures highly approximate.

### What the charity does

Fistula Foundation funds surgery at partner hospitals in about 30 countries, trains fistula surgeons, and pays for outreach that finds women living with fistula and brings them to treatment. A fistula is a hole between the birth canal and the bladder or rectum, left by days of obstructed labour without a caesarean; women live with constant incontinence, often shunned by their families and communities, until it is repaired.

### How the impact happens

1. **Surgeries:** 16,587 repairs in 2024 (Fistula Foundation's annual report), 85% vesicovaginal and 15% rectovaginal, as in a published series of repairs.
2. **Health:** each repair spares a woman the fistula's disability (weights 0.34 and 0.50) for the rest of her life: about 39 years from age 32, the average of the 31 countries' life tables, 22.8 years once discounted at 3%.
3. **Cost:** 633 dollars per surgery, from Fistula Foundation's monitoring data for 2024-25.

### How the model works

The Life You Can Save's sheet comes first, ending at its cost per DALY averted. Our steps follow:

- **Full cost.** The sheet divides by the direct cost of a surgery; a donated dollar also pays administration and fundraising. The sheet's own monitoring row gives 1,189 dollars a surgery with these, which matches Fistula Foundation's total spending in 2024.
- **Repairs that work:** we judge that 80% of repairs end the incontinence; surgical series close most fistulas at the first operation, and some women still leak afterwards.
- **What would have happened anyway:** we judge that 75% of the counted years wouldn't have come without Fistula Foundation, since some women would have been repaired later through other funders.
- **Beyond health:** a small side effect for women's return to work and family life.
- **Discounting** the remaining years at the shared rate rather than 3%.
- **Staff pay and others' money:** with credit by share of resources on, staff's pay below market and the partner hospitals' own resources count as part of the cost.

### What drives the result

- **The full cost per surgery**, which nearly doubles the cost the sheet uses.
- **How many women would otherwise have lived with fistula for the rest of their lives.**
- **The discount rate**, since the benefit runs for nearly four decades.
- **How often a repair ends the incontinence.**

### Where we differ from The Life You Can Save

- **Cost:** 1,189 dollars a surgery with administration, against the sheet's 633.
- **Failed repairs** (80% work) and **what would have happened anyway** (75%), our judgments, steps the sheet leaves out.
- **Discounting** at the shared rate (The Life You Can Save's 3% in the evaluator view).

### Biggest uncertainties

- The disability weights and the split between the two kinds of fistula are taken from elsewhere, not measured for Fistula Foundation's patients.
- How long women repaired would otherwise have waited for surgery.
- The surgeries are Fistula Foundation's own count, not audited.

### What isn't modelled

- Effects on later pregnancies and births.
- Fistula Foundation's training of surgeons, beyond the surgeries it pays for.
