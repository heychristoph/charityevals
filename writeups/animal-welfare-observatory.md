### Summary

Animal Welfare Observatory (Observatorio de Bienestar Animal) campaigns in Spain for corporate pledges: the European Chicken Commitment for broilers and cage-free eggs. ACE's 2025 analysis works out to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="animal-welfare-observatory">26</span> years of healthy life per $1,000** you give. That uses our default exchange rates between suffering and healthy life, which is where most of the uncertainty is.

In ACE's own view the model reproduces all 28 of ACE's intermediate figures.

### How the impact happens

Companies make pledges; some implement them, on a timeline the campaigns can bring forward; each affected chicken or hen lives with less suffering. ACE discounts for implementation risk, other organisations' role and time.

### What drives the result

**How many years a won reform counts** before it would have come anyway moves the result most among the inputs that aren't about animals' weight (about 6 to 81 years of healthy life per $1,000 over the slider's 1 to 20 years; ACE's own spans run from 1 to 15 years). Next come the suffering a hen is spared out of an enriched cage (about 16 to 48) and the share of cage-free pledges kept (about 14 to 27).

### Where we differ from ACE

- **A chicken against a person.** ACE's figures count a hen's suffering at 35% of a person's (Ambitious Impact's weight in ACE's 2025 SADs); we take that weight out. We count days of a chicken's pain and let the chicken slider (default 33%) set how they compare with a person's, as in every animal model.
- **Years brought forward (changed for consistency).** ACE's dates "if not for" the campaigns are 1 to 15 years after the expected ones; we use the shared "Years a won reform counts before it would have come anyway" (5 by default) for every group, counted and discounted from the expected date as ACE does.
- **Pledges kept (changed for consistency).** ACE's own probabilities (30% for the European Chicken Commitment, 80% to 90% for cage-free) become the shared figures: 30% for broiler pledges and 92% of cage-free commitments fulfilled (Chicken Watch).
- **Salary sacrifice (off by default).** Turn on "Credit by share of resources" and we count staff's forgone pay as an in-kind donation: we guess staff are 65% of program cost and earn 35% less than in comparable for-profit jobs, about 35% of cash spending. That leaves each donated dollar about 74% of the credit ACE gives it. Both numbers are guesses with wide ranges; replace them with the charity's real figures when we have them.
- **Credit for shared outcomes.** ACE's discounts for other organisations' contributions are built into its inputs here, and we keep them. They are ACE's estimate of the other groups' share of the work, so they are also what "Credit by share of resources" uses.
- **Past average, not the next dollar (ours):** ACE's estimate divides the Observatory's past year of campaign spending by the pledges it won, an average. The next dollar does less than the average did, since an organisation pursues its best opportunities first: like every model built on an organisation's past spending over its past wins, our view counts the next dollar at the share of the average set under Your values, "How much the next dollar does compared with the average dollar so far" (50% by default, our judgment; 18% to 100%). The evaluator view keeps 100%.
