### Summary

The Center for Global Development (CGD) is a think tank whose economists research and argue for better aid and development policy. Our model counts its work since 2002 against three changes it helped bring about: the advance market commitment that brought pneumococcal vaccine to poor countries early, the development banks' recent capital reforms, and the design of the US Millennium Challenge Corporation. It comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**, mostly because the vaccine branch counts child deaths averted across a programme reaching tens of millions of children a year, of which a small credited share still outweighs CGD's budget. It is a very uncertain figure: CGD's share of the credit and the years a win counts are our judgments.

### What the charity does

CGD was founded in 2001 in Washington, with a London arm, CGD Europe, since 2014 (a UK registered charity, consolidated in CGD's accounts). Together they spent US$23.9 million in 2025, about two thirds of it on staff. Its researchers write on aid, development banks, global health financing, migration, education and trade, and work with governments and agencies to turn proposals into policy. Its working group on vaccine markets (2003-2005) designed advance market commitments; its staff shaped the Millennium Challenge Account; it launched the International Initiative for Impact Evaluation (3ie) in 2008; and its experts sat on the G20 panel that reviewed the development banks' capital rules in 2022.

### How the impact happens

Ideas become policy when governments and agencies adopt them. An advance market commitment promised vaccine makers a market if they supplied poor countries at a low price, so pneumococcal vaccine reached them a year after rich countries rather than a decade or more. Capital reforms let development banks lend more from the same capital. A better-designed aid agency spends its budget on countries and projects more likely to use it well.

### How the model works

- **Cost:** an average year of spending since 2002 from the audited accounts (CGD Europe included from 2014), in 2025 prices, with 2002-2004 judged before the shared price index begins. That comes to 80% of 2025's spending.
- **Pneumococcal vaccine (about 80% of the result):**
  - 33 million children a year got the vaccine with Gavi's support in 2015-2019; the AMC's evaluation estimates 260,000 child deaths averted for the first 49 million children;
  - each death averted is valued at the site's value of a child's death, with the children's later income gain and GiveWell's shared supplements (illness, treatment costs, investment of income, protection of the unvaccinated, less replacement by other strains), together about 80% more;
  - the money spent is subtracted at what it would otherwise have done: the doses (US$3.00 each) and the donors' US$1.5 billion top-up at GiveWell's figure for a dollar of Gavi spending, delivery at its figure for a country's own health spending;
  - the AMC gets 40% of the credit for the faster spread (the evaluation names other causes too), CGD 15% of the AMC;
  - we trust the modelled deaths at 70%.
- **Development banks' capital reforms (about 17%):** the banks' US$200 billion of extra lending capacity, trusted at 80%, lent over about ten years, 60% of it used; a dollar lent is worth its grant element, judged 15% of a dollar of aid, at the site's shared value of aid of unknown use; CGD gets 5% of the credit; extra debt risk takes away 5%.
- **Millennium Challenge Corporation (3%):** its US$18 billion of compacts signed since 2005, about US$1.2 billion a year in 2025 prices, judged 10% better used than other aid because of the design CGD argued for, CGD credited with 10%; reforms countries make to qualify add 2% of MCC's whole value.
- **All three** count for the years a won reform counts on the site, after a discounted wait, are spread over the 24 years of work, and are discounted by the shared missing-data slider, as no study measures CGD's part. Its other work (3ie, the Commitment to Development Index, labour mobility, Nigeria's debt relief, health technology assessment, education) adds a judged 40%, anchored on the spread of the three modelled wins.
- **The next dollar** is valued at {{row:marginal_vs_past_average}} of the average: CGD holds about 21 months of spending in reserve and its biggest wins are behind it.

### What drives the result

The years a won reform counts, the missing-data slider and the next dollar's value move the result most, then CGD's share of the AMC and the AMC's share of the vaccine's faster spread.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **Credit:** Michael Kremer and Rachel Glennerster's academic work, the Gates Foundation, Italy, the UK, the World Bank and Gavi all made the AMC happen; our 15% for CGD could be a third or twice that.
- **What the AMC changed:** the evaluation itself says the results can't be attributed to the AMC alone, and its deaths are modelled, not measured.
- **The banks' lending:** the US$200 billion is the banks' own estimate of capacity, and what a dollar of non-concessional lending is worth to a borrower is a judgment.

### What isn't modelled

CGD's other wins enter only as a share of the three modelled; none is valued on its own.

### How this model was checked

Audit rounds are listed in audits/center-for-global-development-charityevals/. Round 1 found two high, two medium and four low issues. The vaccine branch now counts the children's later income and GiveWell's shared supplements on the deaths' value, and values the vaccine money at Gavi's and governments' figures, since its use is known. The banks' and MCC's evidence adjustments no longer discount twice; the dose price, MCC's yearly funding (from compacts signed) and the size of the MCC effect were corrected, and the other work's share is anchored. Together these took the result from 1.0 to 1.6. Round 2 passed; its fixes (GiveWell's supplements on the income gain too, the other work's range, an anchor for CGD's share of the AMC, MCC's compacts in 2025 prices and before terminations, two quotes) took it to 1.7, checked in a verification round.
