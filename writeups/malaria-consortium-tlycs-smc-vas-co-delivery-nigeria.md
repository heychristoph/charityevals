### Summary

Malaria Consortium added vitamin A capsules to the door-to-door seasonal malaria chemoprevention (SMC) round in two areas of Bauchi state, Nigeria, raising vitamin A coverage from 1% to 83% of young children. The Life You Can Save estimates $11 per DALY averted; valuing each child's death averted by age and counting only what the one round costed buys, that is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="malaria-consortium-tlycs-smc-vas-co-delivery-nigeria">65</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**.

The model reproduces every calculated cell of the sheet's Nigeria tab. The Life You Can Save calls its figures highly approximate.

### What the charity does

Malaria Consortium delivers SMC, monthly antimalarial drugs for young children in the rainy season, through community distributors. In October 2021, in Giade and Katagum, it gave vitamin A to children 6 to 59 months in the same visit, reaching 1.2 million children, at $1.1 a child for both against $0.9 for SMC alone (the published study).

### How the impact happens

1. **Coverage added:** vitamin A from 1.1% to 82.3% (Malaria Consortium's surveys); SMC from 54% (a baseline The Life You Can Save takes from the literature) to 89%.
2. **Vitamin A:** 12% fewer deaths from all causes (the Cochrane review), applied to Nigeria's under-five death rate among the children newly reached.
3. **SMC:** 4.66 deaths and 13.85 hospital admissions averted per 1,000 children protected (a trial in Mali).
4. **Cost:** $1.18 per child reached.

### How the model works

The Life You Can Save's sheet comes first, ending at its cost per DALY. Our steps follow:

- **Deaths valued by age.** The sheet counts a child's death averted as the remaining life expectancy, discounted at 3%; the site values it by GiveWell's moral weight for a child under five and the value-of-a-life slider.
- **One round's effect.** The cost is for one SMC round with vitamin A, but the Mali trial's effects are for a full season of four monthly rounds, and the vitamin A effect is for children dosed every six months. Our view counts a quarter of a season's SMC effect and half a year's vitamin A effect.
- **What would have happened anyway:** ×1, since the sheet credits only the coverage added over the baseline.
- **Staff pay:** with credit by share of resources on, staff's pay below market counts as part of the cost.

### What drives the result

- **How much protection one round gives** (our quarter of a season, up to half; our half-year of vitamin A, up to a full year).
- **The value of a child's death averted**, nearly all the healthy years gained.
- **The SMC baseline:** The Life You Can Save chose 54% from the literature; Malaria Consortium's monitoring reported 91%, which would credit almost no added SMC coverage.

### Where we differ from The Life You Can Save

- **Deaths valued by age** with the site's moral weights, rather than discounted life expectancy (28.9 years in the sheet).
- **One round's protection:** a quarter of the season's SMC deaths and admissions averted and half the year's vitamin A deaths averted, where the sheet counts the full effects against one round's cost.
- **Staff pay** with credit by share of resources on: a step the sheet leaves out.

### Biggest uncertainties

- The SMC baseline coverage, which The Life You Can Save set itself.
- Whether Nigeria's national under-five death rate fits the children reached in Bauchi.

### What isn't modelled

- Effects of vitamin A on illness and blindness.
- The integration's later use in other states.
