### Summary

The same GiveDirectly program, delivered in Malawi. It comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="givedirectly-malawi">6.4</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**, against <span data-figure="headline" data-model="givedirectly">4.4</span> for GiveDirectly's Kenya program. Our adjustments change it as little as they change Kenya.

### Why Malawi comes out higher

The model is identical to the Kenya one; only the inputs that describe Malawi differ.

- **Recipients are poorer:** baseline consumption is about $1.29 a day against $1.79 in Kenya. On a log scale the same transfer is worth more to them.
- **The transfer is larger** ($1,000 against $865) and buys more, at 2.9 PPP dollars per US dollar against 2.4.
- **Gains last longer relative to year one** (136% against 123%): the invested share is larger relative to baseline consumption.
- **Spillovers are discounted more** (external validity 70% against 80%), because Malawi is further from the Western Kenya setting where they were measured.

Child mortality is slightly lower, so the mortality benefit is a smaller share of the total (9% against 12%).

### How this page is built

This model inherits every row, explanation and source from GiveDirectly (Kenya) and overrides only the eight inputs above. Malawi's purchasing-power and child mortality figures come from the shared Malawi facts. If GiveWell revises a part of the model common to both countries, both pages update from a single change.

### Biggest uncertainties

The same as Kenya. In addition, the spillover estimate travels further from its evidence base here. GiveWell's own range on it runs from 28% to 112% of the Kenya-based effect.
