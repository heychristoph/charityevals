### Summary

The END Fund raises money for, and helps governments run, programs against neglected tropical diseases, including mass deworming of children against schistosomiasis and soil-transmitted worms. SoGive's 2022 analysis of its deworming work, the only public cost-effectiveness model of it we have, comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="end-fund-deworming-sogive-2022">9.3</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

That is SoGive's figure on our scale; SoGive's own figure is £40 per year of doubled consumption. Almost all of the value comes from higher adult earnings decades after treatment, and SoGive trusts that link much less than GiveWell does.

### What the charity does

The END Fund pools money from donors and passes it, with technical help, to national programs that hand out deworming pills, mostly through schools and communities. SoGive's model covers its deworming in Ethiopia, Angola, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Rwanda and Zimbabwe, weighted by where the END Fund's money goes. The END Fund also works on other neglected tropical diseases (river blindness, trachoma, lymphatic filariasis), which the model doesn't cover.

### How the impact happens

1. Children who are dewormed carry fewer worms for the rest of the year.
2. The model assumes this lifts their earnings as adults, starting about eight years later and lasting about 40 years, based on the Kenyan deworming trial's 20-year follow-up.
3. That income gain is valued on a log scale, so it counts for more for poorer people, then discounted back to today.
4. Small extra effects (deaths averted, other health effects, drug resistance, worse areas reached at the margin) enter as supplementary adjustments.

### How the model works

SoGive took GiveWell's 2022 deworming model for each charity and changed one main thing: the replicability adjustment, which scales the Kenyan trial's earnings effect down to what SoGive expects a program to achieve elsewhere. Our page traces every cell from SoGive's bottom line through the END Fund tab and the shared tabs (moral weights, GiveDirectly, value of other funders' money). Every calculated row reproduces SoGive's sheet in the evaluator view.

### What drives the result

- **The replicability adjustment.** SoGive uses 7.2% of the Kenyan effect, against GiveWell's 13% in the same 2022 model. SoGive builds it from the mechanisms that could carry the effect (weight gain, schooling, cognition), each combined with the Kenyan trial's earnings result after correcting for "economic losers" in the treatment group. The result moves roughly in proportion to it.
- **The discount rate.** The benefits arrive 8 to 48 years after treatment, so the result is very sensitive to how much later years count (Your values).
- **How a doubled income compares with a healthy year** (Your values), since the whole benefit is income.
- **The worm burden adjustment** for each country, which scales the effect by how infected children are now compared with the Kenyan trial.

### Where we differ from SoGive

- **Discount rate:** linked to the shared rate under Your values; SoGive's sheet uses GiveWell's 4%, pinned in the evaluator view.
- **Value of income and of a life:** linked to the shared assumptions; SoGive uses GiveWell's 2022 moral weights, pinned in the evaluator view.
- **Credit by share of resources:** when on, each location's value per dollar is SoGive's value per dollar of everyone's spending on the program, instead of GiveWell's leverage and funging adjustment for what governments' and other funders' money would have done otherwise.
- **Staff pay:** with credit by share of resources, staff's forgone pay counts as an in-kind contribution (our default for delivery programs: staff pay about 15% of cost, a 10% pay gap).

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether deworming raises adult earnings at all outside the Kenyan trial. SoGive's and GiveWell's adjustments differ by almost two times, and both are judgments.
- The country weights and costs are from GiveWell's 2022 figures; the END Fund's mix of countries changes from year to year.
- The sheet has no END Fund data for two inputs on the result's path: the share of treatments that reach children is the SCI Foundation's overall figure (79%), and the cost per treatment is one figure ($0.81) for every country.

### What isn't modelled

The END Fund's work on other neglected tropical diseases, room for more funding, and organisational strength. SoGive's own rating also weighs these.
