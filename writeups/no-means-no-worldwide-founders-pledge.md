### Summary

No Means No Worldwide designs IMPower, a course that teaches schoolgirls to recognise, avoid and fight off sexual assault, and licenses partners to teach it in Kenya and elsewhere. Founders Pledge estimated in 2018 that it prevents a case of sexual assault for about $62. Counting the post-traumatic stress a case causes, that is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="no-means-no-worldwide-founders-pledge">3.1</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

Founders Pledge's figure reproduces exactly; the step from cases prevented to healthy years is ours.

### What the charity does

IMPower is a six-session course, with refreshers, on assertiveness, de-escalation and self-defence for girls aged about 10 to 20. No Means No Worldwide trains instructors and licenses local organisations to run it; it costs about $4 a girl.

### How the impact happens

1. Trials in Nairobi schools found the yearly rate of sexual assault fell by about 6.5 percentage points among girls who took the course, against those who didn't.
2. Founders Pledge assumes half the protection is left each later year.
3. It halves the effect for programs not yet evaluated, and gives No Means No Worldwide full credit for courses its partners teach.
4. A case prevented spares a survivor the illness that often follows: about 41% of survivors still have post-traumatic stress disorder a year on.

### How the model works

Founders Pledge's rows come first and reproduce its $62 per case. Our bridge follows, in its own section: the share of survivors with PTSD a year on (41%, Dworkin et al. 2023), a disability weight standing in for PTSD (0.133, the Global Burden of Disease's moderate anxiety), and how many years the illness lasts (4, from 1 to 15; Kessler et al. 1995 and later surveys), discounted at the shared rate. That gives about 0.2 healthy years per case, so about 0.003 per dollar.

### What drives the result

- **The bridge's disability weight** (0.03 to 0.4) and **years of illness** (1 to 15): together they move the result about 200-fold.
- **The trials' effect** and **how long it lasts** (Founders Pledge's conservative and optimistic cases).
- **Credit for partners' courses** (0.5 to 1.5).

### Where we differ from Founders Pledge

- **The bridge:** Founders Pledge stops at cases prevented; the healthy years are ours. No standard conversion exists for this outcome, so we map it to the nearest one.
- **Discounting:** Founders Pledge counts later years' protection undiscounted; our view discounts them at the shared rate (Founders Pledge's view pins it at 0).
- **Credit by share of resources:** off by default; when on, instructors' forgone pay counts as cost.

### Biggest uncertainties

The bridge counts mental illness only, and the PTSD figures come mostly from high-income countries. The trial effects come from Nairobi schools and may not hold in other places or with other instructors.

### What isn't modelled

Physical injury, HIV and other infections, pregnancy, harm to schooling, and the suffering of survivors without a diagnosable illness; all would raise the result. Effects on boys' behaviour (No Means No Worldwide also runs a boys' program) aren't counted.
