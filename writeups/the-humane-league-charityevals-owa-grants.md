### Summary

The Humane League passes more than a quarter of its programme spending on as grants: in 2025, $4.26 million to Open Wing Alliance groups abroad, to its sister charity The Humane League UK, and to US groups in its Animal Policy Alliance. Counting each grant as what its recipients did with the money, we estimate **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="the-humane-league-charityevals-owa-grants">0.0002</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**. Every branch rests on counts or chances we had to judge, so each is counted at half by the site's shared missing-data slider. THL's own accountability campaigns are modelled [here](/the-humane-league-charityevals-corporate-campaigns).

### What the grants do

- **Open Wing Alliance groups abroad** ($1.76 million, Form 990 Schedule F: Sub-Saharan Africa, South America, Europe outside the UK, East and South Asia, the Middle East and North Africa). All for corporate cage-free and broiler campaign work. The alliance's 82 members won 216 new cage-free policies in 2025, got 16 stalled pledges restored or re-dated after its Fair and Fowl report (REWE's, covering 7 million hens, among them) and saw 18 broiler policies implemented.
- **The Humane League UK** ($2.15 million, the audited statements' related-party note). About a year of THL UK's spending. In its latest audited year it won Tesco's and Aldi's commitments to give 240 million chickens a year more space (both carried out by May 2025), pressed UK supermarkets to keep their cage-free pledges, lobbied for a ban on cages (the government's December 2025 strategy promised a consultation, opened in January 2026) and lost its appeal against the broiler welfare code while winning the court's reading of the law.
- **Animal Policy Alliance members in the US** ($0.35 million to about a dozen groups). Lobbying against federal preemption of state cage-free sales laws (the EATS and "Save Our Bacon" bills) and local wins: plant-based meals by default at Hennepin County events, soy milk in school meals, a foie gras ban in Brookline. A $50,000 gift to a legal defence fund for activists is counted with the side effects.

### How the model works

Each kind of win is a branch, all in suffering-adjusted days (SADs) on the site's shared figures per change:

- **New pledges abroad**: policies won outside the US × hens per policy (our judgment, 10,000, anchored on the named wins) × the share still caged, split between battery cages (Africa, Latin America, Asia) and enriched cages (Europe, a fifth); the site's rate of pledges kept, the shared years a win counts after a four-year wait.
- **Pledges restored abroad**: REWE's hens not yet cage-free (17% of 7 million) and the other 15 wins at 30,000 hens each, of which 30% wouldn't have switched yet without the pressure.
- **Broiler policies abroad**: 18 policies at 200,000 broilers each, partly to the full standard and partly to more space alone.
- **UK space commitments**: Tesco's and Aldi's 240 million chickens a year at the shared figure for more space alone (a new shared judgment: about a sixth of what a slower-growing breed does, since a broiler's worst pain comes from its breed), delivered within a year (so no pledges-kept rate).
- **UK cage-free switches**: the UK hens that left cages in 2025 (45 million hens, 78.6% to 82.7% cage-free), a fifth of them sooner because of the pressure.
- **UK cage ban** and **the breed case**: the UK's caged hens still caged when a ban would bite and its billion fast-growing broilers, at the chance a ban becomes law (60%) and the chance the legal route ends fast-growing breeds (3%), each drawn as happening or not, and THL UK's year's small share of all the work behind them.
- **US sales laws defended**: the hens supplying states with cage-free sales laws, the share that would go back into cages under preemption, and the five points the year's opposition takes off the chance it passes (drawn together as one event).
- **US food-policy wins**: meals replaced, at the shared figure per meal.

Credit is shared by resources: the alliance grants get their share of everything spent on the alliance's wins abroad (525 campaign staff, seven in ten outside the US, plus THL's own running of the alliance), about a tenth; the UK grant is about nine tenths of a year of THL UK's spending, whose wins are shared with the other UK groups; the US grants are a hundredth of the opposition to preemption. Three side effects are counted through the side-effect slider: stronger groups win more later (+10%), wins spill over to laws and suppliers (+10%), and cage eggs sold elsewhere and dearer eggs (−10%). Because these are 2025's wins against 2025's grants, the next dollar counts at THL's own next-dollar figure, as in THL's other models.

### What drives the result

The animal slider (how a chicken's suffering compares with a person's), the years a win counts, the missing-data slider and THL's next-dollar figure move it most. Among the model's own judgments: how much more space alone does for a broiler, THL UK's share of the supermarket campaigns, the chance of the breed case and THL UK's share of it, and the hens behind an average new pledge abroad. The UK branches are about 85% of the result (the space commitments alone two thirds), the alliance grants about an eighth, the US grants 1%.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **Hens per new pledge abroad.** The alliance publishes counts of policies, not hens; one Ghanaian supplier alone has some 600,000 hens.
- **Space without slower breeds.** Welfare Footprint quantifies only the breed change; no study gives more space alone a figure.
- **The long shots.** The breed case is a seventh of the result at its expected value but, drawn as one event at a 3% chance, it is zero in most simulated worlds; the cage ban is likelier but THL UK's share of it is small.
- **Who else paid.** The alliance's resources abroad are our estimate from its staff count.

### How this model was checked

Built with charityevals' corporate-campaign template on 13 November 2026; the mechanical audit and the site's invariants pass. Independent audit, round 1 (1 high, 4 medium, 7 low): a funging step taken off on top of credit by resources was removed; the shared figure for more space alone now rests on what Welfare Footprint measured (the breed change); the breed case's evidence step no longer splits the effect; with credit by resources, the grantees' forgone pay counts among the resources; THL UK's spending is in 2025 prices; the caged hens a ban would reach allow for the trend. Round 2 (1 high, 1 medium, 4 low): Tesco's and Aldi's space commitments were already carried out, so they are counted as delivered, not as pledges; THL UK's staff take the advocacy pay gap. Round 3 (verification: no high, no medium, 3 low) found those fixes sound; the delivery sources were added here. When THL's accountability campaigns were modelled (November 2026), for consistency across THL's three models: the next dollar reads THL's own figure, the overhead the Form 990's figures, the UK's 2024 and 2025 cage-free shares are drawn together in the simulation, and the reason for crediting 30% of pressed switches is stated.
