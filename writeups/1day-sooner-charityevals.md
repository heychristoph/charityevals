### Summary

1Day Sooner speaks for people who volunteer to be deliberately infected in medical studies (challenge studies), and uses that voice to push for faster vaccines and better pandemic preparedness. Our model of all its work in 2024 comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per $1,000**. About 70% of that comes from its pandemic work, valued as a typical pandemic project's effect on the forecast risk. Its malaria vaccine campaign adds about a sixth, its hepatitis C work and its part in stopping a California bill against far-UV air cleaners the rest. Every part is uncertain, so the plausible range runs from {{low}} to {{high}}.

### What the charity does

1Day Sooner started in 2020, when about 40,000 people signed up to say they would join a COVID-19 challenge study. It spent $2.4 million in 2024 and had 14 employees, plus part-time staff in Europe and Africa (Form 990; its statements are not audited). The 990 reports a single programme line. Its 2025–2026 funding proposal splits the work into five programme areas. Our shares of the 2024 spending are judgments, because the 990 gives no split:

- **Pandemic work** (about {{row:bio_cost_share}}). Under *Warp Speed 2.0* it designed a preparedness programme for the FDA, the Emerging Pathogens Preparedness Program. The programme made it into a bipartisan bill, the Senate draft of the pandemic preparedness act and report language in both appropriations bills, but it got no funding. The *pandemic challenge* programme wrote on the ethics of challenge studies with pandemic viruses, and its recommendations went into the 100 Days Mission's roadmap. It also proposed a pathogen biobank and an agreement to have high-containment beds ready in the UK. The *indoor air* programme is building a "Safe Air Research Alliance" for far-UV and other air cleaning.
- **The California far-UV fight** (about 4%). In 2024 1Day led the campaign against California's SB 1308. The bill would have set indoor ozone limits below federal standards, which would in effect have banned far-UV devices in the state. It was withdrawn before the committee vote.
- **Hepatitis C** (about 27%). 1Day works inside the Toronto team planning a hepatitis C challenge study, and it campaigns for a hepatitis C vaccine. It says the design changes it proposed, approved by the team, cut about 1.5 years from the study.
- **Malaria vaccines** (about 26%). The campaign pushes for faster rollout of the R21 and RTS,S vaccines, with part-time advocates in Burkina Faso, Ghana, Kenya, Tanzania and Zambia, plus op-eds, polling and a congressional letter. Its own account calls 2024 "marked more by frustration than success".

Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy) aims to provide about 40% of its funding.

### How the impact happens

**Pandemics.** The shared forecasts give a yearly chance that a pandemic ends humanity's future (about 1% by 2100) or kills more than a tenth of people (about 4% by 2100), and a 2% yearly chance of a COVID-scale pandemic. A typical dollar of pandemic work cuts those risks by the share in Rethink Priorities' cross-cause model. The cut counts for the shared number of years (5) and is discounted. 1Day's own outputs adjust it: 0.8 of a typical project, since the FDA programme went unfunded and the UK proposals were still proposals.

**The California win.** We take everyone's resources behind the bill's defeat (about $300,000) at a typical pandemic dollar's effect. The site's figure for far-UVC research is 0.8 of a typical project, so a won defence of the market counts at 1. 1Day gets a 35% share. Without it, the bill had about an even chance of passing.

**Hepatitis C.** About 900,000 people are infected each year and about 239,000 die (WHO). Bringing a vaccine forward by a year averts a year of infections, and with them the deaths those infections would cause 20 to 30 years later, mostly at 50 or older. The deaths are discounted for that wait and for the 12 years or so before a vaccine could be in use. The model credits 2024 with half of the 1.5 years. It assumes 40% of the study time saved reaches the vaccine, a 20% chance that a vaccine is licensed at all (the only candidate to reach an efficacy trial failed in 2021), and that a vaccine in use averts 15% of infections. Fewer people per infection will die by then, because more will be cured.

**Malaria vaccines.** A published model finds about 632 deaths averted per 100,000 children fully vaccinated. We judge that all advocacy for faster rollout in 2024 adds about 167,000 children a year, under 2% of Gavi's target, for the shared years a win counts. Gavi's money would otherwise do good elsewhere, so each child counts net of that, at GiveWell's value of a Gavi dollar: about 78% of the value is left. 1Day's share of the advocacy is about 5%.

### How the model works

| Branch | Share of the result | Main adjustments |
|---|---|---|
| Pandemic work | 67% | Outputs 0.8 of a typical project; 0.7 would not have happened anyway; 0.8 its share |
| Malaria vaccines | 16% | 0.8 trust in a modelled effect; 0.8 others would not have filled its part; 5% its share of the advocacy |
| California far-UV win | 7% | 0.5 chance the bill would have passed without it; 0.35 its share |
| Hepatitis C | 9% | 0.85 trust in its 1.5-year figure; 0.85 its share among those who pressed for the changes |

Every branch takes the shared missing-data discount, since none of the effects is measured. Funging by the main funder is counted once, in the next-dollar figure.

Side effects add a net 7%. Other benefits add 10%: air cleaning against everyday infections, an FDA programme that would also speed peacetime vaccines, hepatitis C treatment costs averted and malaria's later income gains. Harms subtract 3%. They are a 2% risk from the pandemic challenge proposals (pathogen biobanks, challenge models for pandemic virus families), a 1% risk of friction with Gavi and others it criticises, and a small risk to volunteers.

At the end of 2024 1Day had about ten months of spending in hand, after a $1 million deficit. Its previous proposal to Coefficient Giving brought $3 million over three years; for 2025–26 it asked for $5 million. The next dollar goes to new work (a service supporting academic challenge trials, a revolving fund) and to programmes with mixed 2024 results, so it counts at 57% of the past average.

### What drives the result

Like theirs, most of it rests on Rethink's typical pandemic project. 1Day is small ($2.4 million), so no correction for size is needed. Its hepatitis C and malaria work count for less per dollar than its pandemic work, because they save lives today rather than cutting a catastrophic risk.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **The discount rate.** With the Long-run value set (a 0.1% discount rate, lives that never start in full) the result is about 19; at an 8% discount rate it is about 1.2.
- **The years a cut counts.** At one year the result is 0.46; at twenty it is 4.6.
- **How much a typical pandemic dollar cuts the risk.** Rethink's figures run ten times either way: the extinction figure alone moves the result from 1.0 to 6.7.
- **How much the next dollar does.** From 24% to 99% of the past average, the result moves from 0.67 to 2.8.
- **The malaria children added.** From 33,000 to 833,000 a year, the result moves from 1.3 to 2.7.

### What isn't modelled

- 2025 and 2026 work (the 2025 Form 990 is not yet filed), including the planned trial-support service.
- Its sister organisations: 1Day Sooner Action Project (a 501(c)(4) that Coefficient Giving funds), 1Day Africa and 1Day UK. Their accounts are separate.
- Earlier wins it took part in, such as WHO's prequalification of R21 in December 2023 and Project NextGen. They come before the year modelled, and 1Day says it cannot tell whether it influenced them.
- Losses beyond deaths in a pandemic are a shared figure set to 0.

### How this model was checked

The model was built from the catastrophic-risk template, with the CEPI, Johns Hopkins Center and Blueprint models as worked examples. Round 1 of the independent audit found two high, five medium and three low findings, all fixed. Displaced Gavi money now counts at GiveWell's shared figure, which raised the malaria share from 40% to 78%. The California win is calibrated on both sizes of pandemic, with its multiple anchored on the site's far-UVC figure (2 to 1). Malaria advocacy counts children per year for the shared years a win counts. Hepatitis C's doubts are each counted once, and its death ages and illness come from WHO's estimates. The result moved from 5.7 to 5.3. Independent audits are in audits/1day-sooner-charityevals/.
