### Summary

Ambitious Impact, formerly Charity Entrepreneurship, finds promising ideas for new charities and trains people to found them. Our model of all its programmes in the year to March 2025 comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="ambitious-impact-charityevals">20</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**. Each organisation it founds goes on to raise and spend money for years, and the model values that money at what the charities it has launched achieve on this site. The figure moves most with how much the next dollar does compared with the past average, the discount rate, how much its charities will raise over their lives and how much of their existence Ambitious Impact caused.

### What the charity does

Ambitious Impact is a UK charity (charity 1195850). In the year to 31 March 2025 it spent £3,953,275. Of that, £1,969,975 were seed and follow-on grants it passed on to the organisations it had incubated. That is donors' money for the new charities and part of their own spending, so the model counts it there, not as Ambitious Impact's cost. Its own spending was £1,983,300, about $2.5 million.

Its programmes in the year:

- **Incubation Program.** Two cohorts founded nine charities: ACTRA, Rahi Impact, Lead Research for Action, Oxygen Access Project, Respira Health, Scale Welfare, the Radius Institute for Road Safety, LABRI and the Institute for Development Innovation. They received about $1.3 million of seed funding. Its research team recommended the 12 ideas behind its cohorts, nine in human well-being and three in animal welfare.
- **Effective Giving Initiatives.** A one-off pilot launched five national giving organisations.
- **Founding to Give.** Eleven for-profit companies were launched by founders who commit to give much of what they earn.
- **Funding circles.** Groups of funders it runs granted $5.8 million.
- **Impactful Grantmaking.** It trained 14 grantmakers who control about $40 million a year.
- **AIM Research Program.** It trained 28 researchers.

The accounts split spending by type of cost, not by programme, so all of it is one model with a branch for each programme.

### How the impact happens

A new charity does good with the money it raises over its life. The model counts that money as money Ambitious Impact moved, like the money an effective-giving organisation moves:

- **How much the charities raise.** Its 60 charities have raised $107 million since 2019, about $1.8 million each so far, including the ones that closed. They are young and the survivors grow, so a charity's whole life is taken to raise 2.5 times that (1.5 to 5 times). Half of it is spent about four years after launch, which is discounted at the site's rate. The effective giving organisations are smaller: 30% of a charity each.
- **How much of that Ambitious Impact caused.** Half (25% to 80%). Some of these charities would have existed anyway, later or in another form. The founders also give up other good work they could have done.
- **What a dollar to its charities does.** Each charity it has launched that the site ranks is read live at its figure on the rankings, under your values. That is GiveWell's figure for ten of them, including Suvita, Family Empowerment Media, Fortify Health, Ansh and Taimaka. ACTRA is the Happier Lives Institute's, and eleven younger ones are Ambitious Impact's own idea-stage estimates. The animal charities are Shrimp Welfare Project (Animal Charity Evaluators') and Animal Policy International. Charities are weighted equally within each cause, and the causes nine to three, as in the year's ideas.
- **New and shifted money.** Half of what its charities raise is counted as new money. The other half would have been given anyway, so it counts only for the gain over an ordinary charity of the same cause. These are the site's shared figures for every organisation that moves money.

Its funding circles count for 30% of their grants, the site's shared share for money a meta charity records without its own donor survey. Founding to Give counts $100,000 of expected donations per company ($10,000 to $1 million), since most start-ups never pay out. The grantmaker training and its published research change how others spend money they would spend anyway. For those, the model counts 4% and 3% of the money informed, valued only for the gain and discounted for weak evidence and missing data. About a quarter of the research fellows moved into high-impact research. Those careers are valued by the site's shared career-change step.

### How the model works

Money raised = (9 charities + 5 giving organisations × 30%) × $1.8 million × 2.5 × the discounted wait × 50%, plus 30% of the circles' $5.8 million and the companies' expected donations. That is about $22 million, against $2.5 million of its own spending. Each dollar is valued at its charities' figures, net of where shifted money would have gone. The changed spending of the grantmakers and readers of its research, its fellows' careers and a 10% side effect are added. The side effect covers founders who go on to lead elsewhere and ideas others use, less partners' time spent on charities that fail. The total is then multiplied by how much the next dollar does compared with the past average: 36%, our figure for Ambitious Impact. It has over a year of spending in reserve, and says founders and ideas are as much its constraint as money.

### What drives the result

- **The next dollar** (36%, from 15% to 75%).
- **The discount rate.** The charities' money comes over years, so at 0.1% the result is about three times higher.
- **How much its charities raise over their lives** (2.5 times what they have raised so far, from 1.5 to 5).
- **How much of their existence it caused** (50%, from 25% to 80%).
- **Where shifted money would otherwise have gone** (one times GiveDirectly, from 0 to 3 times), and the value of a life.

### Biggest uncertainties

- The charities' lifetime fundraising. The $107 million is Ambitious Impact's own count, and the multiple for the years to come is our judgment.
- How much of each charity's existence it caused, which no study has measured.
- The figures of its youngest charities, which are Ambitious Impact's own forecasts made before the charities ran. Its older charities are read at GiveWell's figures, which are lower on average.

### What isn't modelled

- Its charities that the site doesn't model, such as LEEP (whose only model here is unpublished), FarmKind, Rahi Impact and Scale Welfare. They are valued at the average of their cause.
- The effective giving organisations' own leverage. Their spending is valued at the charities their donors fund, not at money moved per dollar.
