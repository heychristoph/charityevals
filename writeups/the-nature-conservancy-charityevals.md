### Summary

The Nature Conservancy (TNC) buys and protects land and water, brokers debt-for-nature deals with governments, and works on clean energy, food systems, fresh water and fisheries in the US and about 80 other countries. Our model of its fiscal year 2025 comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="the-nature-conservancy-charityevals">0.0004</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

That is under a tenth of the site's other climate models, for two reasons:

- Most of TNC's work keeps carbon in land. On the site's scale, a tonne of CO₂ kept out of the air is worth little in healthy years.
- TNC is a small share of the money and people behind its big deals, rules and policies.

Nearly half of the result comes from PRANA, which helps farmers in Punjab and Haryana stop burning crop residue, whose smoke kills tens of thousands of people a year in northern India. Burning has fallen across both states, so most of the fall among PRANA's farmers would have come anyway. A third comes from Pará's deforestation-free cattle rule, two-thirds of that from its least grounded step: a small cut in pandemic risk from the forest kept. The rule's roll-out has barely begun: under 0.2% of the state's herd had been tagged by July 2025.

Every programme is modelled from the research on that kind of work, using TNC's own spending and the reach it reports. None of them is measured against a comparison group, so every one takes the site's missing-data discount, and the result is mostly uncertainty.

### What the charity does

TNC is the world's largest conservation charity by spending: $1.69 billion in its fiscal year 2025 (July 2024 to June 2025), counting $206 million of land and easement purchases. Its audited statements list nine pathways under three priorities for 2030:

- **Tackling climate change:** clean energy, natural climate solutions, adaptation.
- **Protecting ocean, land and fresh water:** transformative protection, Nature Bonds, Enduring Earth.
- **Providing food and water:** fresh water, regenerative food, fisheries.

TNC publishes no split of its spending by pathway. So the model counts all of the spending, and each pathway's work is one or more branches, each with its own outcomes and its own adjustments.

### How the impact happens

The model has 13 branches, each with its own chain.

**Protection**

1. **The Ecuador Amazon deal** (Nature Bonds, December 2024): 1.8 million hectares of new protection and 4.6 million better managed. Protected areas lose forest 41% more slowly than similar unprotected land (Wolf et al. 2021). The forest kept holds carbon, and less tropical clearing may cut pandemic risk a little.
2. **Local incomes in the year's deals:** people in and around the Ecuadorian Biocorridor, and in The Bahamas under its new marine protected areas.
3. **Land bought:** about 40,000 hectares, mostly in the US, kept from development.
4. **Protection policy** (natural climate solutions, transformative protection): national protection and Indigenous tenure beyond the deals, at a guessed 1 million hectares of tropical forest a year.

**Clean energy**

5. **Mine-land solar:** the Neyveli project in India and 17 planned projects in Appalachia. The model counts only the share of the power that wouldn't have been built yet.
6. **Renewable siting maps** in India and Europe: a guessed 3 GW a year planned with them, 5% of it brought forward, about 30% of it used and built.

**Food**

7. **Deforestation-free cattle in Pará:** a state rule, launched in 2023 after years of TNC's work, requiring every animal to be traced. If all buyers apply it, cattle-driven clearing could fall by about half (Levy et al. 2023).
8. **Zero-conversion soy in the Gran Chaco:** traceability and finance for soy grown without clearing.
9. **Regenerative foodscapes:** cover crops and no-till, which store a little soil carbon (Poeplau & Don 2015; contested by Chaplot & Smith 2023), much of which comes back out when farmers stop.
10. **No-burn farming in India (PRANA):** 300,000 farmers enrolled. TNC's midline survey found 25% less burning. Fewer fine particles mean fewer deaths (Lan et al. 2022), and less methane and nitrous oxide.

**Water and climate risk**

11. **Restoration:** forests, riparian strips and wetlands replanted or left to regrow, which take up carbon.
12. **Fisheries rules:** the Seychelles' fisheries law and the tuna bodies' monitoring standards, which may slowly raise coastal fishers' incomes.
13. **Climate adaptation:** reef insurance in Mexico and Hawai'i and mangrove restoration, which cut flood and storm losses.

### How the model works

The [model](/the-nature-conservancy/the-nature-conservancy-charityevals) follows the site's conservation template. Each outcome is counted in its own unit (tonnes of CO₂, or units of value for incomes and deaths) and converted to healthy years by the site's shared rates.

**Cost.** TNC's conservation spending was $1,276 million: $1,070 million of activities plus $206 million of land purchases. Administration and fundraising add 33%. The whole $1,694 million is set against the outcomes of all the branches together.

**One adjustment per question.** Each branch has its own evidence, counterfactual, credit, delivery and missing-data adjustments. The protection branches count the forest at risk before protection, then apply the protection effect once, in their counterfactual, so a hectare that would be lost anyway is counted out once, not twice. The cattle and soy branches count the clearing the rules remove, so their counterfactual is 1. Where the shared years a win counts already ask whether a change would have come anyway (adaptation, fisheries), the counterfactual is 1 too.

**Years.** A won deal, rule or policy counts the site's shared years a win counts (5 by default), discounted, after its wait. Restoration counts the years a regrowing forest takes up carbon (20), and foodscapes the years a farm keeps the practice (8). PRANA counts one year of avoided burning against one year's spending.

**Credit.** TNC's share of all the resources behind each outcome:

| Branch | Share |
|---|---|
| Ecuador deal, protection policy, Chaco soy | about 5% each |
| Ocean deal, Pará cattle rule | about 10% each (the cattle rule's is built from the resources of the state, ranchers, meatpackers, retailers and other funders) |
| Fisheries rules, adaptation | about 20% each |
| Foodscapes | about 25% |
| Clean energy, siting maps | about 30% each |
| PRANA | about 35% (of the resources behind its own increment, after the fall that India's machinery subsidies and burning bans brought anyway) |
| Restoration | about 40% |
| Land bought | about 56% (agencies and other buyers repay the rest) |

**The next dollar.** The won deals, rules and policies are a year's spending set against wins that took years to prepare. They read the shared next-dollar slider, like other past averages. Land purchases, restoration, foodscapes and PRANA are bought unit by unit and don't.

### What drives the result

At the defaults the branches contribute:

| Branch | Share of the result |
|---|---|
| PRANA | 48% |
| Pará cattle rule | 32% (about two-thirds of it the pandemic-risk step) |
| Adaptation | 6% |
| Ecuador deal, fisheries rules, local incomes, protection policy, restoration and Chaco soy | about 1.4-2.8% each |
| Land bought, siting maps, foodscapes and mine-land solar | under 1% each |

The biggest swings:

- **Shared settings:** the missing-data discount, the years a win counts, the value of a healthy year in units of value, the discount rate and the next-dollar slider.
- **The cattle rule's inputs:** TNC's share, the years of work behind it, and the share of land-use disease risk that scales with tropical clearing.
- **PRANA's counterfactual and credit:** how much of the burning cut would have come anyway, and TNC's share of what caused the rest.

### Where we differ

There is no evaluator model of TNC to differ from. TNC's own goals count hectares "conserved" and tonnes "avoided or sequestered" without a counterfactual or shared credit. We count only what each programme changes against what would have happened anyway, for the shared years a win counts, at TNC's share of the resources.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **Pandemic risk.** We assume the risk falls in proportion to tropical forest kept. No study has measured that, and it carries much of the cattle rule's result.
- **PRANA.** The burning cut is TNC's own before-and-after survey. Punjab's fire counts fell from about 71,000 in 2021 to about 11,000 in 2024, so most of the cut is likely the statewide trend. The deaths per tonne come from a model of India's air.
- **The reach of the unreported work.** Protection policy, siting, foodscapes, restoration, fisheries and adaptation publish no counts, so their hectares and people are judgments.

### What isn't modelled

- **Biodiversity for its own sake.** Species and habitats have no value on the site's scale, except through what they do for people and animals.
- **Wild animals' welfare.** TNC claims no effect on it, and the sign is unknown.
- **Effects counted only as shares of each branch.** On the plus side: clean water for cities from the water funds, illness from crop smoke, fire protection from forest thinning, and grassland, wetland and ocean protection under the policy pathways. On the minus side: leakage of clearing onto land the rules don't cover.
- **Room for more funding**, and how TNC would spend an extra dollar across its priorities.

### How this model was checked

Built with the build-cea skill on 2026-11-03 and rebuilt on 2026-11-04 to the current rules.

**The first build** valued three-quarters of the spending at the results of other charities on the site. Its two audit rounds found 2 high, 3 medium and 10 low findings, all fixed in that version.

**The rebuild** removed those borrowed values. It built each pathway from the research and gave every branch its own adjustments and missing-data discount. It also split the ocean deal's credit from the Amazon deal's, which the first build's last audit had left open.

**Rebuild, audit round 1:** 2 high, 6 medium and 7 low findings, all fixed.

- **Borrowed value (high).** The food pathway's flagships had been scaled up to stand for the rest of the pathway, and the energy projects to stand for the siting work. The rest now has branches of its own: Chaco soy, foodscapes and siting maps.
- **PRANA's counterfactual (high).** It is now anchored on the statewide fall in burning: 15%, down from 50%.
- **PRANA's other inputs.**
  - Partial burning is no longer discounted twice.
  - TNC's share is anchored on India's machinery subsidies: 20%, down from 40%.
  - The deaths are Lan et al.'s yearly average, and the two states' share is taken from the paper.
  - Smoke illness is at GBD's share, and the methane and nitrous oxide are counted in tonnes.
- **Adaptation.** It is now its own branch.
- **Fisheries.** The branch counts only binding rules: Chile's recreational limits and the Amazon map are out, and the Seychelles is counted once.
- **Restoration.** Its anchor is restated (the Rio Grande figure was acres of thinning), and its uptake rate is lowered to temperate regrowth.
- **Smaller fixes:**
  - The cattle rule's leakage and loophole are each discounted once.
  - The pandemic step is anchored on Allen et al. 2017.
  - Evidence rows hold only validity questions.
  - Beneficiaries are split by people.
  - Reading-only rows are labelled.

The result fell from 0.0017 to 0.0004 years of healthy life per $1,000.

**Rebuild, audit round 2:** no high findings, 3 medium and 7 low. All of them were fixed.

- **PRANA's credit (medium).** The subsidies had been discounted twice, once in the counterfactual and again in the credit. The credit now counts only the resources behind PRANA's own increment: 35%, up from 20%.
- **Pará's credit (medium).** It is now anchored on who pays for the rule (the state, the industry, other funders): 10% (4-20%).
- **Foodscapes' soil carbon (medium).** It now counts only the 30% that stays stored. The guessed hectares were lowered to the anchor's scale.
- **Smaller fixes:**
  - The Chaco soy branch no longer excludes cattle twice.
  - Siting maps' counterfactual moved to its own row, with a discount for planned capacity that is never built.
  - Adaptation's counterfactual is now 1, and it has a wait.
  - Mangrove flood protection is counted once.
  - Source dates corrected (India's subsidy reply, the Seychelles Act), and Lan et al.'s sensitivities quoted.

The result rose to 0.0005.

**Rebuild, audit round 3 (the last):** no high findings, 1 medium and 2 low, all fixed.

- **Pará's roll-out (medium).** The state's animal-health agency had tagged under 0.2% of the herd by July 2025, against a decree for the whole herd by December 2026. The delivery share is now 40%, down from 60%, and the explainers name both schedules (the decree's 2026, TNC's 2030).
- **Ear tags (low).** The 2 million pairs were donated by TNC and JBS together; the credit row now says so and puts amounts on the resources behind the rule. The credit stays 10%.
- **Fuel share (low).** The share of tonnes displacing fossil power is updated to 2%.

The result is 0.0004 years of healthy life per $1,000 (combined range 0.00005-0.0035).
