### Summary

Ploughshares Fund is a San Francisco foundation that funds and connects the groups working to lower the chance of nuclear war. Our model of all its work in the year to June 2025 comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per $1,000**. That figure rests on forecasts of how likely a nuclear war is and on an estimate of what a typical dollar of such work does to the risk. Both are deeply uncertain, so the plausible range runs from about 1.2 to about 480.

### What the charity does

Ploughshares spent $7.6 million in the year to June 2025. Its one programme cost $5.7 million:

- **Grants: $3.1 million to 46 organisations.** These fund arms-control research and advocacy (the Arms Control Association, the Federation of American Scientists, the Stimson Center, RAND), lobbying for diplomacy in Congress, talks on Korea, nuclear-policy media, watchdogs of the weapons laboratories and groups for people harmed by nuclear testing.
- **Its own work: $2.6 million.** It runs the field's coalitions (on the new arms race, Iran and North Korea), a day lobbying Congress with about 30 partners, the Story Center and other communications, and a new network of women security leaders.
- **The Nuclear Futures Fellowship.** Run with Horizon 2045, it took in a second group of nine mid-career fellows. It paid $10,000 to each first-year fellow's organisation for a final project.

### How the impact happens

Nuclear war carries a yearly chance of catastrophe, taken from the domain experts' forecasts in the 2022 Existential Risk Persuasion Tournament and spread evenly to 2100: an 8% chance by 2100 of a war that kills over 10% of people, and 0.55% of one that ends humanity's future. The superforecasters' lower figures are the low end of each range.

A typical project cuts that risk by a share per dollar, taken from Rethink Priorities' cross-cause model, including its chance that a project backfires. The cut counts for the years such progress would otherwise have taken to come (5 by default), with each year discounted. A war the world survives is taken to kill 45% of people, mostly through famine, valued across the world's age groups like every death on the site. A war that ends humanity's future also takes the lives of everyone not yet born. Those future lives count only through the discount rate.

### How the model works

A grant dollar counts as a typical dollar of the grantee's nuclear work, except for the four grantees with a model on the site: the Arms Control Association ($178,000), the Union of Concerned Scientists ($120,000), the Federation of American Scientists ($90,000) and the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists ($80,000). Those $468,000 are valued at each grantee's own figure on the rankings, live, which already holds that grantee's own adjustments, so only Ploughshares' share of the credit is applied on top. ICAN's only money from Ploughshares is a fellowship capstone grant; Back from the Brink and Global Zero have no model. Ploughshares' own coordination and communications count on the same path. The fellowship counts the work it adds: about 1 in 10 fellows staying longer in the field or moving up because of it, over five years. The fellowship's share of the credit for that work is its cost against the fellows' added work, about a third.

Each part has its own adjustments:

| Adjustment | Grants | Own work | Fellowship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Share of the spending counted | 0.84 | 0.75 | 1 |
| How far a typical project's effect applies | 0.8 | 0.6 | 1 |
| Work other groups would not have done now | 0.7 | 0.7 | 0.8 |
| Work other funders would not have paid for | 0.8 | 1 | 1 |
| Ploughshares' share of the resources | 0.95 | 0.6 | 0.6 |

Grants for people harmed by past testing don't cut the risk, so they don't count. Grants for diplomacy and war prevention that only partly concern nuclear weapons count at half. A quarter of Ploughshares' own work is running the grants, whose result is already counted in the grants. Ploughshares' coalitions run on partners' unpaid staff time, so it gets only a share of their credit. The fellowship is shared with Horizon 2045. Every part takes the missing-data discount, because nothing about the work's effect on the risk is measured.

Side effects add 13%. They cover a limited nuclear war or a single detonation, which has no shared forecast, war prevention beyond nuclear weapons, compensation won for people harmed by testing, and Ploughshares' influence on what other funders give. No harm is added beyond a typical project's chance of backfiring, which is already in the cut per dollar.

Ploughshares holds almost no unrestricted reserves. It has drawn on its restricted capital pool through two years of deficits, and funding for the field is shrinking. The next dollar counts at 53% of the past average.

### What drives the result

The grants bring about three fifths of the result, Ploughshares' own work under a third and the fellowship about 8%. Catastrophes the world survives count for more than extinction does: at the default 4% discount rate, the future counts for about 8 years of the long-run population. At a 0.1% discount rate, the future counts far more and the result rises to about 83.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How much a dollar of this work cuts the risk.** Rethink's figures are placeholders, and the range runs ten times either way. This is the largest swing, from 5.0 to 210.
- **How many years a cut counts before it would have come anyway.** The shared slider runs from 1 to 20 years.
- **How likely and how deadly a nuclear war is.** The superforecasters put the chance at half the experts' figure. The share who die in a war the world survives runs from 15% to 85%.
- **How far the grantees' work beats or trails a typical project.** Nothing about it is measured.

### What isn't modelled

- Losses beyond deaths, such as survivors' lost income and health, are a shared figure set to 0.
- The forecasts are spread evenly over the century, though forecasters put much of the risk later.

### How this model was checked

The model was built from the catastrophic-risk template, with each part's adjustments applied to its own value. Round 1 of the independent audit passed with two medium findings, both fixed. The first was the cost of running the grants, which had been counted again as Ploughshares' own work. The second was a missing question: would the grantees' other funders have paid. Fixing them moved the result from 32 to 26. Round 2 passed with small source and wording fixes, which a verification round checked. The rounds are in audits/ploughshares-fund-charityevals/.
