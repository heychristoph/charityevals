### Summary

The Brennan Center for Justice is a nonpartisan law and policy institute that researches, litigates and campaigns for fair elections, checks on presidential power and an end to mass incarceration in the United States. Our model of its work in the year to June 2025 comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**.

### What the charity does

The Center was founded in 1995, is affiliated with NYU School of Law and has offices in New York and Washington. In the year to 30 June 2025 it spent $57.6 million, with its lobbying affiliate, the Brennan Center Strategic Fund: $19.4 million on its Democracy programme (voting rights, election security, redistricting, money in politics, courts), $3.9 million on Liberty and National Security (emergency powers, surveillance), $3.2 million on Justice (incarceration, sentencing, bail), $12.5 million on communications, $2.7 million on its fellows and policy advocacy, and $15.9 million on management and fundraising. In 2025 it sued with others to block the elections executive order, led the campaign against the SAVE Act, challenged the Justice Department's demands for state voter files and filed briefs in the tariff case.

### How the impact happens

The Center's democracy work aims to keep US elections free and fair and the president within the law. If US democracy broke down, the country would in time lose part of the income that democracies gain over autocracies. Its briefs against the emergency tariffs helped end a tax that shrinks the US economy and its trading partners'. Its justice work aims to cut the number of people in prison and jail. People kept out of prison are happier and live longer.

### How the model works

- **Cost:** the audited 2025 accounts, without donated legal services. Communications, fellows, policy advocacy and overhead are split by direct spending: {{row:dem_cost_share}} to the democracy work, 12% to justice. The democracy work is then split into direct work against a slide (election protection, defending election officials, courts, and most of Liberty and National Security: judged about a third) and reform work (voting rights and access, redistricting, money in politics), which is valued in its own branch and never also against a slide.
- **Breakdown risk:** the shared forecast that the US falls to a hybrid regime before 2030 ({{row:dem_p_breakdown}}). A year of the whole US pro-democracy field's work is judged to cut that chance by about a twentieth. A slide would cost 60% of democracy's long-run income gain (the shared figure, about a fifth of income), building up over 25 years and reversed at about 5% a year; averting it puts that whole path off. Only its direct work counts here: its share of the field's resources, about $2.6 billion a year once weighted by how directly they act on a slide, comes to under 1%.
- **Reform work:** the rest of its democracy spending (about $33 million) is valued at the site's shared figures for reforms within a democracy: the US redistricting movement's wins of 2008-2025 (79 million people, each commission worth {{row:ref_gain_share}} of democracy's income gain, built up and reversible), a Brennan dollar judged to do a quarter of what a movement dollar does. Its reform spending is about one and a half times the movement's yearly spending, so value scales per dollar and no diminishing returns are modelled.
- **Tariff ruling:** the Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling against the emergency tariffs restores about 0.12% of long-run US GDP, net of the replacement tariffs, and 1.6 times as much again abroad, valued the same way (mostly China's). The Center filed six of about 75 amicus briefs for the challengers (about 37 at the Supreme Court alone); the amici are judged to have carried 15% of the litigation, so the Center is credited with {{row:tar_attribution_share}}, half of it in the year modelled.
- **Justice:** the national fall in incarceration, about 23,000 fewer people a year from 2013 to 2019. Policy changes are judged to have caused 60% of it, and the reform field 40% of those changes. Each year out of prison is worth over a point of life satisfaction (the shared figure) and a twentieth of a death averted a few years later. The Center is credited with its share of the field's spending in those years, about $430 million a year in 2025 prices.
- **Adjustments:** evidence; missing data (every branch); side effects as shares in each branch (backlash, democracy's value beyond income, repression and the pull abroad; prisoners' families and earnings, crime, rollbacks); and the next dollar against the past average ({{row:marginal_vs_past_average}}: the Center holds about two years of spending in liquid reserves).

### What drives the result

The tariff ruling is four fifths of the result, the reform branch about a tenth and the direct work against a slide under a tenth. The tariff figure is a small share of a ruling that touches the income of every American and of hundreds of millions abroad, more than half of it in China. The breakdown branch counts all 340 million Americans too, but only a small chance and a small share of the field's effect. The justice branch is smaller, mostly through the deaths that prison causes. Every branch rests on the Center's share of a large field or case, about 1%.

### Biggest uncertainties

- The Center's share of the tariff litigation: how much of it the amici carried.
- How much a year of the field's work cuts the chance of a breakdown.
- How much a reform dollar does against a redistricting dollar (a quarter, judged), and how much of the democracy programme is direct work against a slide (a third, judged).
- How many years a win should count (the site's slider: at 20 years the result is about three times higher).
- How much of democracy's income gain a fall to a hybrid regime would cost a rich country, and how soon it would be reversed.
- How much of the fall in incarceration reform caused, and whether it continues: it has reversed since 2021.

### What isn't modelled

Only as shares of each branch's value: the worth of civil liberties beyond income, repression averted, US democracy's effect abroad, backlash, families of people in prison, their earnings, crime and reforms rolled back. Wins other than the tariff ruling are not modelled one by one: the model counts the Center's share of what its fields achieve in a year.

### How this model was checked

Independent audit rounds are in audits/brennan-center-for-justice-charityevals/. Round 1 counted a postponed breakdown's whole loss path instead of the build-up within the years counted (and dropped the repeal hazard there), added the tariff ruling, made the breakdown chance a judgment anchored on the market and the severity a third of the gain (a hybrid regime is milder than a coded reversal), split the field's cut into the routes it works on and the share it decides, applied each branch's side effects in that branch, paired the incarceration decline with the field's spending of the same years, and discounted prison deaths for their delay. Round 2 valued the trading partners' gains from the Budget Lab's regional figures the way the US gain is valued (1.6 times it, in the main chain), built the Center's share of the tariff litigation from its six briefs, and dropped a discount on the tariff estimate that had no direction. Round 3 passed; its findings were fixed after it: the challengers' amicus briefs counted from the Supreme Court docket (75 across the courts, not 60), the amici's share anchored on the briefs' word limits, and the reading rows' scope said. After merging main, the breakdown branch was aligned with the family's new shared rule: the shared forecast and field figures, the continuous shifted ramp with a reversal hazard, the severity, external validity and field cut.
