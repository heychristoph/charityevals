### Summary

The Brennan Center for Justice is a nonpartisan law and policy institute that researches, litigates and campaigns for fair elections, checks on presidential power and an end to mass incarceration in the United States. Our model of its work in the year to June 2025 comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="brennan-center-for-justice-charityevals">0.08</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**, about the site's global-health median.

### What the charity does

The Center was founded in 1995, is affiliated with NYU School of Law and has offices in New York and Washington. In the year to 30 June 2025 it spent $57.6 million, with its lobbying affiliate, the Brennan Center Strategic Fund: $19.4 million on its Democracy programme (voting rights, election security, redistricting, money in politics, courts), $3.9 million on Liberty and National Security (emergency powers, surveillance), $3.2 million on Justice (incarceration, sentencing, bail), $12.5 million on communications, $2.7 million on its fellows and policy advocacy, and $15.9 million on management and fundraising. In 2025 it sued with others to block the elections executive order, led the campaign against the SAVE Act, challenged the Justice Department's demands for state voter files and filed briefs in the tariff case.

### How the impact happens

The Center's democracy work aims to keep US elections free and fair and the president within the law. If US democracy broke down, the country would in time lose part of the income that democracies gain over autocracies. Its briefs against the emergency tariffs helped end a tax that shrinks the US economy and its trading partners'. Its justice work aims to cut the number of people in prison and jail. People kept out of prison are happier and live longer.

### How the model works

- **Cost:** the audited 2025 accounts, without donated legal services. Communications, fellows, policy advocacy and overhead are split by direct spending: 88% to the democracy work, 12% to justice.
- **Breakdown risk:** the chance that the US falls to a hybrid regime before 2030 (12%, a little above a forecasting market's 11%). A year of the whole US pro-democracy field's work is judged to cut that chance by a twentieth: it works on half the routes to a breakdown and decides a tenth of their outcomes. A breakdown would cost a third of democracy's long-run income gain (the shared figure, about a fifth of income), building up over 25 years; averting it postpones that whole path. The Center is credited with its share of the field's spending, about $4.5 billion a year.
- **Tariff ruling:** the Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling against the emergency tariffs restores about 0.12% of long-run US GDP, net of the replacement tariffs, and 1.6 times as much again abroad, valued the same way (mostly China's). The Center filed six of about 60 amicus briefs for the challengers; the amici are judged to have carried 15% of the litigation, so the Center is credited with 1.5%, half of it in the year modelled.
- **Justice:** the national fall in incarceration, about 23,000 fewer people a year from 2013 to 2019. Policy changes are judged to have caused 60% of it, and the reform field 40% of those changes. Each year out of prison is worth over a point of life satisfaction (the shared figure) and a twentieth of a death averted a few years later. The Center is credited with its share of the field's spending in those years, about $430 million a year in 2025 prices.
- **Adjustments:** evidence; missing data (every branch); side effects as shares in each branch (backlash, democracy's value beyond income and abroad; prisoners' families and earnings, crime, rollbacks); and the next dollar against the past average (32%: the Center holds about two years of spending in liquid reserves).

### What drives the result

The tariff ruling is nine tenths of the result: a small share of a ruling that touches the income of every American and of hundreds of millions abroad, more than half of it in China. The breakdown branch counts all 340 million Americans too, but only a small chance and a small share of the field's effect. The justice branch is smaller, mostly through the deaths that prison causes. Every branch rests on the Center's share of a large field or case, about 1%.

### Biggest uncertainties

- The Center's share of the tariff litigation: how much of it the amici carried.
- How much a year of the field's work cuts the chance of a breakdown.
- How many years a win should count (the site's slider: at 20 years the result is about three times higher).
- How much of democracy's income gain a fall to a hybrid regime would cost a rich country.
- How much of the fall in incarceration reform caused, and whether it continues: it has reversed since 2021.

### What isn't modelled

Only as shares of each branch's value: the worth of civil liberties beyond income, US democracy's effect abroad, backlash, families of people in prison, their earnings, crime and reforms rolled back. Wins other than the tariff ruling are not modelled one by one: the model counts the Center's share of what its fields achieve in a year.

### How this model was checked

Independent audit rounds are in audits/brennan-center-for-justice-charityevals/. Round 1 counted a postponed breakdown's whole loss path instead of the build-up within the years counted (and dropped the repeal hazard there), added the tariff ruling, made the breakdown chance a judgment anchored on the market and the severity a third of the gain (a hybrid regime is milder than a coded reversal), split the field's cut into the routes it works on and the share it decides, applied each branch's side effects in that branch, paired the incarceration decline with the field's spending of the same years, and discounted prison deaths for their delay. Round 2 valued the trading partners' gains from the Budget Lab's regional figures the way the US gain is valued (1.6 times it, in the main chain), built the Center's share of the tariff litigation from its six briefs, and dropped a discount on the tariff estimate that had no direction.
