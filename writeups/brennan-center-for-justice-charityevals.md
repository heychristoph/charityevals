### Summary

The Brennan Center for Justice is a nonpartisan law and policy institute that researches, litigates and campaigns for fair elections, checks on presidential power and an end to mass incarceration in the United States. Our model of its work in the year to June 2025 comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="brennan-center-for-justice-charityevals">0.003</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**, far below the site's global-health median.

### What the charity does

The Center was founded in 1995, is affiliated with NYU School of Law and has offices in New York and Washington. In the year to 30 June 2025 it spent $57.6 million, with its lobbying affiliate, the Brennan Center Strategic Fund: $19.4 million on its Democracy programme (voting rights, election security, redistricting, money in politics, courts), $3.9 million on Liberty and National Security (emergency powers, surveillance), $3.2 million on Justice (incarceration, sentencing, bail), $12.5 million on communications, $2.7 million on its fellows and policy advocacy, and $15.9 million on management and fundraising. In 2025 it sued with others to block the elections executive order, led the campaign against the SAVE Act, challenged the Justice Department's demands for state voter files and filed briefs in the tariff case.

### How the impact happens

The Center's democracy work aims to keep US elections free and fair and the president within the law. If US democracy broke down, the country would in time lose part of the income that democracies gain over autocracies. Its justice work aims to cut the number of people in prison and jail. People kept out of prison are happier and live longer.

### How the model works

- **Cost:** the audited 2025 accounts, without donated legal services. Communications, fellows, policy advocacy and overhead are split between the two branches by their direct spending (88% democracy).
- **Democracy:** the chance that the US stops being a democracy before 2030 (15%, above a forecasting market's 11%). A year of the whole US pro-democracy field's work is judged to cut that chance by a twentieth. A breakdown would cost half of democracy's long-run income gain (the shared figure, about a fifth of income), building up over 25 years. The Center is credited with its share of the field's spending, about $4.5 billion a year.
- **Justice:** the national fall in incarceration, about 20,000 fewer people a year from 2012 to 2019. Policy changes are judged to have caused 60% of it, and the reform field 40% of those changes. Each year out of prison is worth a point of life satisfaction and a twentieth of a death averted (about two years of life lost per year served). The Center is credited with its share of the field's spending, about $600 million a year.
- **Adjustments:** evidence; missing data (both branches); side effects as shares (backlash, democracy's value beyond income, its effect abroad; prisoners' families and earnings, crime, rollbacks); and the next dollar against the past average (32%: the Center holds about two years of spending in liquid reserves).

### What drives the result

An averted breakdown counts for the five years the site counts any won change. Over those years the income loss would only have reached a tenth of its long-run size, so the democracy branch is small, despite counting all 340 million Americans. The justice branch is as large in total, from an eighth of the cost, mostly through the deaths that prison causes. Both rest on the Center's share of a large field, about 1%.

### Biggest uncertainties

- How much a year of the field's work cuts the chance of a breakdown.
- How many years an averted breakdown should count (the site's slider: at 20 years the result is several times higher).
- The chance of a breakdown, and how much of democracy's income gain it would cost a rich country.
- How much of the fall in incarceration reform caused, and whether it continues.

### What isn't modelled

Only as shares of the modelled value: the worth of civil liberties beyond income, US democracy's effect abroad, backlash, families of people in prison, their earnings, crime and reforms rolled back. Wins are not modelled one by one: the model counts the Center's share of what its fields achieve in a year.
