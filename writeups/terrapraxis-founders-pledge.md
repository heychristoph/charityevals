### Summary

TerraPraxis works to re-power existing coal plants with advanced nuclear heat, so that their sites, grid connections and jobs can outlive coal. Founders Pledge's Climate Fund paid for a TerraPraxis event at COP26. Its best guess is that the grant avoided a tonne of CO₂ for $0.02; under what it called extremely conservative assumptions, $1.10. Taking the conservative case, our judgment, that is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="terrapraxis-founders-pledge">2.1</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

The figure is a back-of-the-envelope estimate for one event, not a model of TerraPraxis's work as a whole; it rests on three guessed probabilities.

### What the charity does

TerraPraxis promotes "re-powering": replacing a coal plant's boiler with a clean heat source, mostly advanced nuclear reactors, while keeping the turbines, transmission and workforce. Most of the world's coal plants are young and in Asia; re-powering is a way to stop their emissions without closing them. Founders Pledge first funded TerraPraxis with a $250,000 seed grant in 2020.

### How the impact happens

Founders Pledge's conservative case: the COP26 event makes re-powering happen five years sooner for 1 in 10,000 of today's coal emissions (14.8 billion tonnes a year), and paying for it philanthropically, rather than leaving it to the nuclear industry, makes a 1% difference. That is about 74,000 tonnes, and the page puts the cost at $1.10 a tonne.

### How the model works

The model rebuilds both of Founders Pledge's cases from the page: the page doesn't give the grant's size (the conservative case implies about $81,000), and the linked sheet models a different TerraPraxis grant. On that implied grant, the best-guess assumptions give about $0.022 a tonne, Founders Pledge's $0.02. Founders Pledge's view uses the best guess; ours the conservative $1.10. Our steps follow:

- **Tonnes per dollar:** one over the cost per tonne.
- **Air pollution:** every tonne here is coal not burned, so its smoke goes too. Coal power in Europe causes about a fifth of the air-pollution deaths per tonne of CO₂ that the shared rate assumes for fossil fuels on average (Markandya and Wilkinson 2007); the plants re-powering targets are mostly younger Asian plants with less flue-gas cleaning, so we take 0.4 of the average (0.2 to 1, our judgment).
- **Staff pay:** with credit by share of resources on, staff's forgone pay counts as cost.

The site then turns tonnes into healthy years through the deaths they avoid, heat deaths discounted over the decades until they come, as for every climate model.

### What drives the result

- **Which of Founders Pledge's cases to take.** Its best guess is $0.02 a tonne, 55 times cheaper; at that price the result would be about 115 at the default settings, more than 30 times any other climate model on the site.
- **"Deaths caused by one more tonne of CO₂"** and the value of a life (Your values).
- **How dirty the avoided coal power is** (0.2 to 1 of the average).

### Where we differ from Founders Pledge

- **Conservative case, not best guess.** Founders Pledge's bottom line is $0.02 a tonne, and it reproduces from the page. Our view takes its conservative $1.10 instead: a single event, three guessed probabilities, and a result that would otherwise be more than 30 times every other climate model. This is our judgment, shown as a changed row.
- **Air pollution:** Founders Pledge counts only tonnes; we add the coal smoke they carry.
- **Credit by share of resources:** off by default; when on, staff's forgone pay counts as cost.

### Biggest uncertainties

Whether one event changes when coal plants are re-powered at all; the three probabilities are guesses, and Founders Pledge says so. The tonnes come in the 2030s or later, but, as in Founders Pledge's estimate, the wait until they are avoided isn't discounted (the heat deaths each tonne causes are discounted from emission onward), and the air-pollution deaths they avoid are counted at once.

### What isn't modelled

TerraPraxis's other work (its design tools, partnerships with utilities), and any value of the event beyond re-powering.
