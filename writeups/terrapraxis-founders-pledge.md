### Summary

TerraPraxis works to re-power existing coal plants with advanced nuclear heat, so that their sites, grid connections and jobs can outlive coal. Founders Pledge's Climate Fund paid for a TerraPraxis event at COP26. Its best guess is that the grant avoided a tonne of CO₂ for $0.02; under what it called extremely conservative assumptions, $1.10. Taking the best guess, as for every evaluator on the site, and discounting the wait until the tonnes are avoided, that is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="terrapraxis-founders-pledge">2.1</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

**Why it is more than ten times every other climate model on the site.** It isn't an organisation's work over years, like the others, but a single event (an estimated $81,000 grant), and its cost per tonne rests on three probabilities Founders Pledge guessed: how much of the world's coal emissions the event brings forward (1 in 1,000), by how many years (5), and how much paying for it philanthropically mattered (5%). Change any one and the result moves in proportion; Founders Pledge's own conservative case is 55 times lower. We keep its best guess, as for every evaluator, but read the figure as a guess about one event, not as evidence that TerraPraxis outperforms the rest.

### What the charity does

TerraPraxis promotes "re-powering": replacing a coal plant's boiler with a clean heat source, mostly advanced nuclear reactors, while keeping the turbines, transmission and workforce. Most of the world's coal plants are young and in Asia; re-powering is a way to stop their emissions without closing them. Founders Pledge first funded TerraPraxis with a $250,000 seed grant in 2020.

### How the impact happens

Founders Pledge's best guess: the COP26 event makes re-powering happen five years sooner for 1 in 1,000 of today's coal emissions (14.8 billion tonnes a year), and paying for it philanthropically, rather than leaving it to the nuclear industry, makes a 5% difference. That puts the cost at $0.02 a tonne. Its conservative case (1 in 10,000 and 1%, about 74,000 tonnes) puts it at $1.10.

### How the model works

The model rebuilds both of Founders Pledge's cases from the page: the page doesn't give the grant's size (the conservative case implies about $81,000), and the linked sheet models a different TerraPraxis grant. On that implied grant, the best-guess assumptions give about $0.022 a tonne, Founders Pledge's $0.02. Both views use the best guess, with the conservative $1.10 as the end of the range. Our steps follow:

- **Years the re-powering counts:** Founders Pledge credits five years of re-powering brought forward. Like every won reform on the site, we count the years set under Your values ("Years a won reform counts before it would have come anyway", 5 by default, 1 to 20), each year after the first discounted, and scale Founders Pledge's tonnes by that over its five.
- **Tonnes per dollar:** one over the cost per tonne, discounted for the wait until the tonnes are avoided (10 years, 5 to 20, our judgment: re-powering needs advanced reactors that aren't commercial yet).
- **Air pollution:** every tonne here is coal not burned, so its smoke goes too. Coal power in Europe causes about a fifth of the air-pollution deaths per tonne of CO₂ that the shared rate assumes for fossil fuels on average (Markandya and Wilkinson 2007); the plants re-powering targets are mostly younger Asian plants with less flue-gas cleaning, so we take 0.4 of the average (0.2 to 1, our judgment).
- **Staff pay:** with credit by share of resources on, staff's forgone pay counts as cost.

The site then turns tonnes into healthy years through the deaths they avoid, heat deaths discounted over the decades until they come, as for every climate model.

### What drives the result

- **The cost per tonne.** Founders Pledge's conservative case, $1.10 a tonne, is 55 times its best guess; three guessed probabilities for a single event set it (filed as judgments). That is why the result is more than ten times every other climate model on the site, and why the range is wide.
- **How much less a marginal dollar does than this past grant's average** (Your values: "How much the next dollar does compared with the average dollar so far"; 50% by default, from 18% to 100%, one slider for every model built on a past average).
- **The years a won reform counts** (Your values, 5 by default): the tonnes scale with them.
- **The discount rate and the wait** until the tonnes are avoided.
- **"Deaths caused by one more tonne of CO₂"** and the value of a life (Your values).
- **How dirty the avoided coal power is** (0.2 to 1 of the average).

### Where we differ from Founders Pledge

- **Deaths valued by age.** Each death a tonne avoids is valued at GiveWell's moral weight for the age it comes at, over the ages of today's heat and air-pollution deaths (GBD 2023: most are of people over 50), about 30 and 24 healthy years, where the site used GiveWell's all-ages adult value (36.6) until late October 2026: the same rule as every death on the site.
- **A marginal dollar, not the past average.** Founders Pledge's figure is one past grant (a COP26 event) over its effect: an average. The next dollar does less; our view counts it at the share set under Your values, "How much the next dollar does compared with the average dollar so far" (50% by default, our judgment; 18% to 100%), one slider for every model on the site built on a past average (The Life You Can Save's leverage ratio, the other retrospective climate models). The evaluator view keeps 100%.
- **Years counted:** Founders Pledge counts five years of re-powering brought forward, undiscounted; we count the shared "Years a won reform counts" (5 by default), each year after the first discounted, as for every won reform (the evaluator view keeps Founders Pledge's five).
- **Discounting:** Founders Pledge doesn't discount the wait until the tonnes are avoided; we do, at the site's discount rate, as for every future benefit.
- **Air pollution:** Founders Pledge counts only tonnes; we add the coal smoke they carry.
- **Credit by share of resources:** off by default; when on, staff's forgone pay counts as cost.

### Biggest uncertainties

Whether one event changes when coal plants are re-powered at all; the three probabilities are guesses, and Founders Pledge says so. The tonnes come in the 2030s or later; how long the wait is matters through the discount rate.

### What isn't modelled

TerraPraxis's other work (its design tools, partnerships with utilities), and any value of the event beyond re-powering.
