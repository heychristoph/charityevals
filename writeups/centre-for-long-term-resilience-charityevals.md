### Summary

The Centre for Long-Term Resilience (CLTR) is a London think tank that advises the UK government on extreme risks, mainly pandemics and advanced AI. Our model of all its work in the year to 30 November 2025 comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="centre-for-long-term-resilience-charityevals">5.7</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**. About 85% of that comes from the biosecurity work, about half the cost. A tenth comes from the AI work and the rest from risk management. The plausible range runs from about 0.3 to about 95.

### What the charity does

CLTR was founded in 2019. It is a non-profit company limited by guarantee (Alpenglow Group Limited, company 12308171), not a registered charity. It is funded by donations and takes no government money. It had 19 staff on average in the year, up from 15. Its work runs through three policy units:

- **Biosecurity.** Advice on carrying out the UK's 2023 Biological Security Strategy, including an independent assessment two years on (October 2025). Also the case for mandatory screening of synthetic DNA, preparing for mirror life, medicines supply, the European Biotech Act, and a risk index for AI-enabled biological tools with RAND Europe.
- **AI.** Advice on UK frontier AI regulation, misuse and open-source risks, risk management at AI companies, and how government responds to AI incidents (Preparing for AI security incidents, September 2025).
- **Risk management.** How the UK government assesses risks and prepares for them, including its Resilience Action Plan (July 2025) and risk exercises.

### How the impact happens

Pandemics and AI carry a yearly chance of catastrophe, taken from the shared forecasts. A typical project cuts that risk by a share per dollar, taken from Rethink Priorities' cross-cause model. The cut counts for the years such progress would otherwise have taken to come (5 by default), with each year discounted. Deaths count across the world's age groups, like every death on the site; a COVID-scale pandemic's deaths count at COVID's age mix. A catastrophe that ends humanity's future also takes the lives of everyone not yet born, counted only through the discount rate.

### How the model works

CLTR files small-company accounts with no income and expenditure account, so its spending isn't published. We build it from the 19 staff in its accounts and the pay in its job adverts: about £75,000 on average, plus 25% for National Insurance, pension and benefits. Staff are taken as 80% of all costs. That makes about $2.9 million (£2.2 million). Its known grants and the year's £0.6 million surplus give about the same.

No split by unit is published either. One senior adviser and some leadership time go on risk management, 12% of the cost. Of the rest, 55% goes on biosecurity, by headcount on the unit pages. A quarter of the AI work is aimed at misuse, mostly AI and biology. Each part has its own adjustments:

| Adjustment | Biosecurity | AI | Risk management |
|---|---|---|---|
| How far the effect applies | 0.85 | 0.64 | 0.4 |
| Work others would not have done now | 0.75 | 0.6 | 0.7 |
| CLTR's share of the resources | 0.85 | 0.8 | 0.75 |

The AI figure includes a 15% cut for its work on chronic and societal AI risks, which the forecasts don't cover. Risk management counts at 0.4 because better government planning is a step further from the risk, and much of its value is in ordinary emergencies. Every part is discounted 50% for missing data.

Side effects add 10%, mostly risk management's value in ordinary emergencies and the AI unit's societal work. Harms take off 3.5%: a little faster progress in the technologies, and published analyses of how they could be misused.

CLTR holds about three months of spending in its own funds. Coefficient Giving offered in October 2024 to match other donors' gifts, up to £3 million, and CLTR says it turns down most of what it is asked to do. The next dollar counts at 53% of the past average.

### What drives the result

After each part's adjustments, the biosecurity work brings about 85% of the result, the AI work a tenth and risk management about 5%. A typical dollar of pandemic work cuts far more deaths in Rethink's model than a typical dollar of AI work aimed at loss of control. The AI work's value comes mostly from its misuse share.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How much a dollar of pandemic work cuts the risk.** Rethink's figures are placeholders, and the range runs ten times either way, moving the result from about 3 to 30.
- **How many years a cut counts before it would have come anyway.** The shared slider runs from 1 to 20 years.
- **How much the next dollar does.** From 21% to 90% of the past average.
- **How far the typical project applies to the biosecurity work.** From 0.3 to 1.3.

### What isn't modelled

- Losses beyond deaths, such as survivors' lost income and health, are a shared figure set to 0.
- No law, treaty or standard won in the year is credited to CLTR, so all the work is valued as typical field work.
- The spending is our estimate, not a published figure. The result per dollar barely depends on it.

### How this model was checked

The model was built from the catastrophic-risk template, with the branch layout of our Council on Strategic Risks model. Round 1 of the independent audit checked every source, recomputed the branch shares and passed. Its smaller findings were fixed: the staff share of costs is now taken from CLTR's own accounts (the tax owed at the year end and the year's surplus) rather than a peer; employer costs include the advertised benefits; the other job adverts are linked; text left over from the Council on Strategic Risks model is gone. A verification round checked those fixes and passed. CLTR's share of the resources behind its biosecurity work was then raised from 0.8 to 0.85, as for the Johns Hopkins Center for Health Security, since most of that work is its own staff's analysis; a second verification round checked that change. The rounds are in audits/centre-for-long-term-resilience-charityevals/.
