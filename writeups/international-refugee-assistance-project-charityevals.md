### Summary

The International Refugee Assistance Project (IRAP) gives refugees free legal help to reach safety, and litigates and campaigns to keep those pathways open. Our model of its legal services in the year to August 2025 comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**. Its litigation and policy work, modelled separately, comes to **{{result:international-refugee-assistance-project-charityevals-advocacy}}**.

### What the charity does

IRAP began as a student project at Yale Law School in 2008 and now has offices in New York, Washington, Berlin, Amman and Beirut. In the year to 31 August 2025 it spent $32.5 million: $17.2 million on legal services, $2.5 million on litigation, $2.7 million on policy, $1.5 million on communications and $2.4 million on other programmes (family reunification from Latin America, human rights defenders, legal fellows). It served 4,884 clients, reached about 290,000 people with legal information online, and worked with over 670 pro bono lawyers and 630 law students. It takes no government money.

### How the impact happens

A refugee waiting in Jordan, Lebanon, Kenya or Afghanistan lives on a few dollars a day, often in danger. IRAP's lawyers help them through resettlement, family reunification and visa processes. A client who reaches the US, Canada or Europe consumes several times more for the rest of their life, and leaves the danger behind. IRAP's lawsuits and advocacy open or defend pathways for many people at once.

### How the model works

- **Cost:** the audited 2025 accounts. Donated legal services and written-off pledges are left out; management and fundraising are spread over the programmes.
- **Legal services:** of the year's clients, the share who reach safety in a year (our judgment: 10%). Each move is valued by the rise in consumption, from what refugees live on while they wait to what resettled refugees consume, over 30 years and discounted. The excess deaths over three years of danger are added.
- **Litigation and policy:** a past average over fiscal years 2021-2025, with three wins:
  - IRAP's defence and revision of the court-ordered plan that speeds Special Immigrant Visas for Afghan and Iraqi allies (about 27,000 visas a year, judged a quarter of a year sooner);
  - a share of the rebuilt US refugee programme (100,034 admissions in 2024 against 11,411 in 2021), counted only for the two years it lasted;
  - the 80 refugees resettled under the 2025 injunctions in Pacito v. Trump.
- **Adjustments:**
  - evidence;
  - moves that would have happened anyway (those that would have come later count for the years brought forward);
  - credit shared with volunteers, governments and co-counsel;
  - the wait until arrival;
  - missing data: IRAP publishes no count of clients who reached safety, and the value of each move, in both models, is our judgment;
  - the next dollar against the past average (46%), for the litigation and policy work.

### What drives the result

The value of one move is large, about 50 units of value, because a move multiplies a very poor person's consumption for decades. The legal services' result is low anyway, because casework costs about {{row:cost_per_person}} a client and few clients get out in a year. That was especially true after the US suspended refugee admissions in January 2025. The litigation and policy result is higher because a won pathway moves thousands of people for the cost of one campaign. But it rests on how much of the US rebuild advocacy caused, and IRAP's share of that advocacy.

### Biggest uncertainties

- The share of clients the year's casework brings to safety.
- How many would have got out without IRAP.
- How much of the refugee programme's rebuild advocacy caused, and IRAP's share of the coalition.
- How much sooner the court's plan brought the visas.

### What isn't modelled

These are counted only as shares of the modelled value: legal information to about 290,000 people, green cards and protection from removal, the relief of safety and reunited families, remittances, host countries' early costs, and backlash. Wins not modelled one by one are counted as a share of the three that are: family reunification parole, the Central American Minors programme, European family-reunification rulings and state laws.

### How this model was checked

Independent audit rounds are in audits/international-refugee-assistance-project-charityevals/ and audits/international-refugee-assistance-project-charityevals-advocacy/. Round 1 of the legal-services model raised the share of clients reaching safety to the yearly flow (10%), credited moves brought forward, weighted Central American families' higher consumption, valued pro bono hours at a lawyer's wage and fixed the legal-information share. Round 1 of the litigation model credited only the period's defence of the SIV plan (the plan itself dates from June 2020), discounted all three branches for missing data and asked whether the extra refugees would have reached safety another way.
