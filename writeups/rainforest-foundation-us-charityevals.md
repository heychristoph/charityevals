### Summary

Rainforest Foundation US (RFUS) backs Indigenous peoples in Peru, Brazil, Guyana and Panama to secure and defend their forest. Our model of its 2025 spending comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="rainforest-foundation-us-charityevals">0.009</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

Most of that is not the forest's carbon:

- A little over half is a small cut in the chance of a new pandemic from tropical forest kept standing. That is the least grounded step in the model.
- About 30% is the pay RFUS's community patrollers bring home, in some of the poorest households of the Amazon.
- The forest's carbon is the rest. Land carbon is worth little in healthy years, as everywhere on the site.

The forest effect rests on a randomised trial of RFUS's own monitoring programme in Peru. Nothing measures the 2025 territories against comparable land, so every branch takes the site's missing-data discount.

### What the charity does

RFUS, founded in 1988, works through Indigenous federations and councils. It does three things, which its tax return reports as three programmes:

- **Land rights.** Legal and mapping work for communal titles. In 2025 it helped secure titles for 67,762 acres in Peru, and strengthened tenure on about 2 million acres in all, through boundary work and title claims.
- **Rainforest protection.** It trains and pays community patrollers who act on monthly satellite deforestation alerts. In 2025 it had more than 400 patrollers, covering 19.6 million acres in Guyana, Brazil, Peru and Panama.
- **Strengthening Indigenous organisations.** Governance, administration and fundraising support, and grants, for 22 Indigenous organisations. In Guyana, for example, it supports district councils that pursue illegal gold miners.

In 2025 it spent $11.25 million: $9.29 million on programmes, $0.84 million on management and $1.12 million on fundraising. The audited accounts don't split the programmes. In the 2024 Form 990, land rights took 16% of programme spending, rainforest protection 22% and strengthening organisations 61%.

### How the impact happens

The model has three branches, set against the same cost.

1. **The territories.** Patrols, alerts and stronger organisations deter loggers, miners and settlers, so a little less of the territories' forest is cleared that year.
2. **Titles.** A title makes invasion and clearing harder. RFUS's work brings titles forward by some years.
3. **The patrollers' pay.** Patrollers are paid for the days they patrol, which raises what their households consume.

The forest kept holds carbon. Less clearing of tropical forest may also cut the chance of a new pandemic a little.

### How the model works

The [model](/rainforest-foundation-us/rainforest-foundation-us-charityevals) follows the site's conservation template.

**Cost.** All $9.29 million of programme spending, plus management and fundraising spread over it: $11.25 million in all.

**The territories.** We start from the 7.9 million hectares under monitoring and multiply by:

- **The share lost each year without the support.** We judge it by country, about 0.1% a year overall. Guyana loses about 0.06% a year by its national monitoring, and Guyana is 60% of the area. The trial's communities in Peru lost 0.25%.
- **The share that stays lost** rather than being delayed: half.
- **Carbon per hectare lost:** the tropics' average, about 650 tonnes of CO₂.

Then the adjustments:

- **What the support changes: 20% of that loss.** In the randomised trial (Slough et al. 2021, 76 communities in Loreto), alerts, training and paid patrols cut forest loss by 52% in the first year and 37% over two years. That trial had about 2,200 hectares per monitor. In 2025 each patroller covers about 20,000, so the effect is likely smaller.
- **Credit: 60%.** The communities, their federations, other funders and governments count too.
- **Delivery:** 90%.
- **Evidence:** 0.7. The trial's estimate is imprecise, and its result is applied to other countries.

**Titles.** The 27,400 hectares titled in 2025, plus a tenth of the 780,000 hectares whose titling advanced. We multiply by:

- the share of untitled community forest lost each year (0.25%);
- the share a title prevents (35%). Blackman et al. 2017 found titling in Peru cut clearing by more than three quarters for two years. In Brazil, full property rights cut deforestation and demarcation alone didn't;
- the site's shared years a won reform counts (five).

RFUS's credit here is 40%: the regional government grants the titles, and the federations file the claims.

**Pandemic risk.** The forest kept in both branches cuts the risk by the same shared figures as the site's other forest models.

**The pay.** We take 400 patrollers × 60 patrol days a year (our judgment) × the $8 a day the trial paid. Half of that is a net gain, since patrol days take time from fishing and farming. That is about $100,000 for about 1,500 people. We put their consumption at 35% of Peru's average, as for the site's other model of Amazonian Indigenous communities. Two side effects are added, as for other cash: deaths averted (+12%) and gains to local traders (+10%).

**A unit cost.** More money pays for more patrollers, titles and partner support at about the same cost, so the model has no next-dollar factor.

### What drives the result

The biggest swings are:

- the missing-data discount;
- the share of the territories' loss the support prevents;
- how much of the pandemic risk from land use scales with forest loss;
- how much of the forest kept stays standing;
- the discount rate.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **What the patrols do at 2025's scale.** The trial covered dense monitoring in threatened Peruvian communities. Most of 2025's area is Guyana's lightly threatened forest, watched much more thinly.
- **Pandemic risk from forest loss.** This is a shared judgment, and here it is over half of the result.
- **The patrollers' pay.** RFUS doesn't publish how many days it pays for or at what rate today.
- **Titles' lasting effect.** The Peruvian evidence covers two years after a title. Brazil's evidence is mixed.

### What isn't modelled

These are shares in each branch, not separate rows:

- **RFUS's global work:** the Indigenous direct-finance mechanism of the Tropical Forest Forever Facility and the COP30 land pledge. Together with less mercury from illegal mining, fire brigades, biodiversity and water, a small positive share in the territories branch.
- **The communities' own gains from secure land,** a positive share in the titles branch.
- **Patrollers' skills and standing,** and the dangers they face, in the pay branch.
- **Leakage,** clearing pushed elsewhere, a negative share.
- **Money RFUS helps partners raise:** more than $10.5 million in 2025. It is not counted as extra, since most of it would fund forest work elsewhere.
