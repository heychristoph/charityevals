### Summary

Ubongo makes educational cartoons for TV and radio (Akili and Me, Ubongo Kids) that teach early reading and maths to children across sub-Saharan Africa. Founders Pledge's page gives about £0.25 per child reached and learning gains of 0.17 standard deviations in two trials. After discounts for fade-out and for noise in small trials, and turned into income with Founders Pledge's own method, that is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="ubongo-founders-pledge">50</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

Founders Pledge's own figure stops at test scores per pound. The fade-out discount, the shrinking and the step to income are ours, in both views.

### What the charity does

Ubongo produces cartoon series aligned with national curricula, in several languages, and broadcasts them free through partner TV and radio stations reaching about 43 million households. Its cost per child is very low because broadcast is cheap per viewer.

### How the impact happens

Two evaluations of Akili and Me, in Rwanda and Tanzania, found pre-schoolers gained about 0.17 standard deviations in counting, number and letter recognition after four weeks of viewing. If some of that lasts, it raises later learning and earnings.

### How the model works

Founders Pledge's figures come first: 0.17 standard deviations for £0.25, 0.68 per pound. Our steps follow: a persistence discount, keeping 20% (5% to 50%) of the measured gain, because early-childhood gains typically fade by half to two-thirds within a year or two and a child reached by broadcast watches less than a child in a trial; pounds to dollars; a 50% weight on Founders Pledge's 0.1 prior, as the evidence is two short trials; then Founders Pledge's own 2023 education method turns test scores into income: 19.1% higher earnings a year per standard deviation, of which it keeps 17.5% for how well the evidence replicates, from a few years after school for 40 working years, shared with the household, discounted at the shared rate. The income gain is then valued like every income gain on the site. Because the children are pre-schoolers, the wait until work is 13 years rather than Founders Pledge's 8.

### What drives the result

- **The persistence discount** (5% to 50%): it moves the result tenfold.
- **The discount rate.**
- **The weight on the prior.**

### Where we differ from Founders Pledge

- **Persistence:** the page says Founders Pledge applies discounts for persistence and generalizability but doesn't give them; ours is a stand-in.
- **The wait until work:** 13 years for pre-schoolers rather than the method's 8.
- **From test scores to healthy years:** Founders Pledge's page stops at learning; we apply its own 2023 method.
- **Credit by share of resources:** when on, staff's forgone pay counts as cost. Off by default.

### Biggest uncertainties

How much of a short burst of viewing lasts into school and work: almost nothing is known about long-run effects of edutainment in Africa. The result's very low cost per child is per child reached, not per child who watched as much as in the trials.

### What isn't modelled

Effects on older children's programs (Ubongo Kids), and on parents and siblings who watch.
