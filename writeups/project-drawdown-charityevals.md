### Summary

Project Drawdown researches which climate solutions matter most and helps funders, companies and investors put their money there. Our model values that advice by the money it changed in 2023-2025. Each dollar counts only for how much better its new use is than its old one. Against what Drawdown spent over those years, that comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="project-drawdown-charityevals">0.0016</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**. Most of it comes from foundation and family giving it redirected. The result is very uncertain: nobody has counted the decisions it changed, so the money, the gain per dollar and Drawdown's part in them are all our judgments.

### What the charity does

Project Drawdown spent $6.0 million in 2025, $4.7 million of it on programmes (audited figures in its year-end report). The 2025 tax return splits the programmes:

- **Science: $2.9 million (66%).** Research on climate solutions and Drawdown Explorer (2025), a free platform covering about 150 solutions, their costs and where they matter most. Its "emergency brake" work puts fast cuts in methane and other short-lived pollutants first. Drawdown Food covers food and farming emissions.
- **Drawdown Labs: $0.8 million (17%).** Work with companies (job-function action guides, training, the Drawdown Business Coalition) and the Drawdown Capital Coalition for impact investors and philanthropists.
- **Drawdown Stories: $0.4 million (8%).** Video and storytelling for the public: Drawdown's Neighborhood, the Global Solutions Diary, and SHIFT, a guide to effective personal action.
- **Communications: $0.3 million (8%).** Media, marketing and sharing the research.

### How the impact happens

Drawdown cuts no emissions itself. It changes where other people's climate money and effort go. The model has a branch for each kind of reader:

- **Funders.** Foundations and individuals gave $11.7-18.4 billion for climate mitigation in 2024 (ClimateWorks). We judge that funders newly reallocate about 0.3% of that a year after using Drawdown's work, about $45 million gross. Its testimonials come from family foundations, ReFED's food-waste funders and the Toniic investor network, but no funder names an amount.
- **Companies.** Corporate climate budgets that companies working with Drawdown Labs move from offsets and carbon accounting toward real cuts: $15 million a year (our judgment).
- **Investors.** Climate funds built on its list of solutions, such as Carbon Collective's ETF: $100 million a year. Buying climate shares instead of others changes very little, so each dollar is worth only a small gain.
- **The public.** About 3,000 people a year making a lasting change (diet, flights, driving, heating), worth half a tonne a year each.

### How the model works

This is a value-of-information model. For each branch: money reallocated a year × the difference in tonnes per dollar between the new and old use × the years a changed choice counts (the shared years a won reform counts, discounted, after a year's wait until the money is spent). An ordinary climate grant or corporate climate dollar is put at 0.01 tonnes a dollar (0.002 to 0.04). The site's own models of large climate advocacy groups are a cross-check, not an input. A dollar reallocated with Drawdown's help is judged to do 30% more (3% to 150%). Drawdown long ranked solutions by their total potential, not by what one more dollar does, and funders stay within their own missions.

Each branch then takes the share that wouldn't have happened anyway (40-50%) and Drawdown's share of the work behind the change. The funders' own staff, advisers and other guides (ClimateWorks, Giving Green, Founders Pledge) do the rest. Then come the shared evidence and missing-data steps, and two side effects: other good not modelled, such as policy advice and research others cite (+15%), and companies using the partnership as cover (−3%). The total is divided by a typical year's spending in 2023-2025. The next dollar is valued at 42% of that average. Drawdown holds about a year's cash, and new money goes to programmes not yet tried (Drawdown Capital, Explorer 2.0).

Each tonne counts for its heat deaths. The methane in the tonnes (about a quarter, from its "emergency brake" focus) counts for methane's faster warming and its ozone. The fuel tonnes count a little air pollution, mostly in the US and Europe.

### What drives the result

Funders' giving carries about four-fifths of the modelled tonnes, companies about an eighth and investors a twentieth. The public adds little. The three biggest swings are how much better a redirected dollar does, how much giving it changes, and the shared years a changed choice counts. Each moves the result about tenfold across its range.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How much money it really moves.** Drawdown says its work has "helped guide billions of dollars", but that is a total since 2017 and includes money that would have gone the same way. No funder has published what it changed.
- **Whether its advice improves a dollar.** Its rankings have favoured big, mature solutions that markets already fund. The gain could be near zero, or larger where it pushes funders to methane and food, which get little climate funding.
- **The value of an ordinary climate dollar.** This is judged from abatement costs and the site's advocacy models, and spans twentyfold.

### What isn't modelled

Its influence before 2023 is left out, along with its cost: the 2017 book and the funds and companies started because of it. The policy advice it gives governments has no named win, so it sits in the +15% for other good. Climate damage other than deaths is left at the shared zero.
