### Summary

Village Enterprise gives groups of three people living in extreme poverty in Uganda and Kenya a small business grant, training and a year of mentoring. Founders Pledge's 2018 analysis, which we rebuilt row by row, comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="village-enterprise-founders-pledge">2.0</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

Founders Pledge's own figure is $250 to double one person's consumption for a year. The model reproduces all 9 of its calculated figures. Founders Pledge analysed Village Enterprise in its 2018 report; it isn't among Founders Pledge's recommended charities.

### What the charity does

Village Enterprise trains business groups, gives each a grant of a few hundred dollars to start a small enterprise (often livestock or a shop), and mentors them for a year, with savings groups alongside. In the randomised trial Founders Pledge relies on, the program cost $232 per household.

### How the impact happens

1. The grant and training start or expand a small business.
2. The business raises the household's consumption; the trial (Sedlmayr, McKenzie and Smith 2017) measured a gain of about 4% a year later.
3. Founders Pledge values the gain on a log scale, like GiveWell: doubling one person's consumption for one year is one unit of value, the site's common unit.

### How the model works

The [model](/village-enterprise/village-enterprise-founders-pledge) follows Founders Pledge's sheet. It sets the trial's consumption gain against the control group's consumption, assumes the gain lasts 3 years (2 to 5), discounts later years at 4.5%, and divides the cost per person by the total.

### What drives the result

- **How long the gain lasts**, the one input Founders Pledge gives a range for: at 2 years the result is about 30% lower, at 5 years about 60% higher.
- **The exchange rate between a healthy year and a doubled-income year** (Your values).

### Where we differ from Founders Pledge

- **Discount rate:** our view uses the shared rate under Your values (4% by default); Founders Pledge's view keeps its 4.5%.
- **Staff pay:** with credit by share of resources on, staff's pay below what they could earn elsewhere counts as part of the cost. We guess staff pay is 30% of the cost (the grant itself is much of the rest) and the pay gap 15%. Off by default.

### Biggest uncertainties

- The consumption gain is small (about 4%), comes without a range in the sheet, and was measured a year after the grant; whether it lasts is the main question, and the trial can't answer it.
- The trial ran in Uganda in 2014–2016; the program and its costs have changed since.

### What isn't modelled

Gains in assets and savings that don't show up in consumption, and any spillovers to neighbours. Founders Pledge's sheet counts consumption only.
