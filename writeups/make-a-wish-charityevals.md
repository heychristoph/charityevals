### Summary

Make-A-Wish America and its 58 chapters grant wishes to children with critical illnesses: a trip, a meeting with a hero, a gift or an experience the child chooses. Our model of the whole US network in fiscal 2024 comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="make-a-wish-charityevals">0.0016</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**.

That is far below the site's other wellbeing and global-health models. A wish costs about $21,600 with fundraising and management, as much as therapy for dozens of people, and the one randomised trial of a wish found that it lifts a sick child's quality of life for some months. An observational study suggests it may save some hospital costs; nothing in the evidence suggests it changes whether the child survives. The biggest uncertainties are the size of the effect (one small trial) and how long it lasts (measured once, five weeks after the wish).

### What the charity does

The national office in Phoenix charters the chapters, passes them money and donated travel, runs national partnerships and campaigns, and handles referrals; the chapters, with more than 20,000 volunteers, meet the families and grant the wishes. In the year to 31 August 2024 the network granted 16,295 wishes to children aged 2½ to 17 and spent $352.5 million (donated advertising excluded): $201.4 million on programmes (wish granting $175.8 million, chapter support $23.0 million, public information $2.1 million, training $0.5 million), $81.9 million on fundraising and $69.2 million on management. The national office also received $67.8 million of donated public-service advertising.

### How the impact happens

The child chooses a wish, waits some months while it is planned, and has the experience, usually with the family. The trial's authors credit the anticipation and hope as much as the event. Parents and siblings share in it.

### How the model works

The model follows the site's template for wellbeing:

- **Cost**: the network's operating expenses, $352.5 million, over 16,295 wishes. The donated advertising, valued at half its reported rate-card value, and the volunteers' hours at the US volunteer wage are others' resources: they lower Make-A-Wish's share of the credit to about 85%.
- **Effect**: health-related quality of life rose by 0.59 standard deviations against a wait-list in the one randomised trial of a wish (66 Israeli children with cancer, Make-A-Wish Israel). We count about three weeks at the full effect for the months of anticipation before the wish (unmeasured: the trial's controls were waiting for a wish too) and about five months after it (still there five weeks after the wish, then fading), and turn it into WELLBYs at the site's shared rate. Each other member of the household, about 2.8 more people in a family with children, gains the site's shared share of the child's effect.
- **Hospital costs**: 496 wish children at one hospital had fewer unplanned admissions and emergency visits over two years than matched controls. We judge a wish saves about $750 of hospital costs (from nothing, if healthier children were simply the ones granted wishes, to $4,000), valued as money spread over the many who pay for US health care on the site's log-income scale, about a quarter of the result.
- **Adjustments**: half the trial's effect survives our evidence adjustment (one small, unblinded trial with many measures; cancer patients aged 5 to 12 in Israel against US children of 2½ to 17 with many illnesses, some too young or too ill to take in a wish as the trial's did); 95% of wishes wouldn't have come from another wish charity. The trial is of the same intervention, so the missing-data discount doesn't apply.
- **Not modelled, as a share**: family outside the household (+), postponed wishes and children who die first (−): 2%.

### What drives the result

- **The effect size**: the trial's 95% interval runs from 0.10 to 1.08 standard deviations, which moves the result about tenfold.
- **How long the effect lasts after the wish** (two to twelve months at the full effect), and how much of it comes before the wish.
- **The hospital costs saved** (nothing to $4,000 a wish).
- **The exchange rate between WELLBYs and healthy years**, a shared slider.
- **How far to trust one small trial** (30-80%).

### Biggest uncertainties

- One trial of 66 children, measured once, five weeks after the wish. No replication, and no follow-up to show how long the gain lasts.
- The trial's children had cancer and were 5 to 12; Make-A-Wish's children are younger and older, with many illnesses, and some are too young or too ill to take part as the trial's did.
- Parents' and siblings' gains are not measured; the shared household share comes from psychotherapy.
- The hospital savings rest on one observational study that reports odds, not amounts, and charges, not costs.
- The combined enterprise figures are Make-A-Wish's own sum of its audited statements; the audited national statements give the chapters' totals, which agree to within 2%.

### What isn't modelled

Siblings and grandparents outside the household, and the disappointment of wishes postponed or never granted, together a 2% share. The donors' and volunteers' own satisfaction is not counted.

### How this model was checked

The mechanical audit (lint, invariants, shared figures) passes. Round 1 of the independent audit found 1 high, 3 medium and 5 low issues: the hospital-use study, then a share of a catch-all, now has rows of its own valued as money saved; the household is a family with children (new shared sizes from the same survey as the US average), not the US average of 2.4; the duration is split into the unmeasured months before the wish and the measured weeks after it; a doubt counted twice (children too young or ill) is now counted once; and five sources and reasons were tightened. Round 2 found the round-1 fixes in place and 1 high, 1 medium and 5 low issues: the hospital savings are now spread over the two years observed and discounted, take every adjustment but the quality-of-life trial's evidence discount, and are paid by a payer mix judged from who pays for children's hospital care; the remaining lows were sources and wording. The result moved from 0.0008 to 0.0013 healthy years per 1,000 dollars over the two rounds.
