### Summary

The Arms Control Association is a Washington DC membership organisation that works to lower the chance of nuclear war through research, its journal Arms Control Today, the press and Congress. Our model of all its work in the year to June 2025 comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per $1,000**. That figure rests on forecasts of how likely a nuclear war is and on an estimate of what a typical dollar of such work does to the risk. Both are deeply uncertain, so the plausible range runs from {{low}} to {{high}}.

### What the charity does

The Association spent $1.7 million in the year to June 2025, with 12 staff. Its programmes cost $1.6 million:

- **Research, publications and special projects: $0.59 million.** Issue briefs and reports on US-Russian and Chinese nuclear arms, the nuclear test ban, Iran and North Korea. It also runs the Physicists Coalition for Nuclear Threat Reduction, whose scientists lobby Congress.
- **Arms Control Today: $0.56 million.** The field's journal of record, written largely by outside experts.
- **Communications: $0.18 million.** Media work: its staff are quoted by Reuters and the New York Times.
- **Membership: $0.16 million.** Members' service and dues. The return books no fundraising costs.
- **Meetings, workshops and outreach: $0.10 million.** The annual meeting, briefings, and a December 2024 retreat of more than 50 campaigners from 28 organisations.

### How the impact happens

Nuclear war carries a yearly chance of catastrophe, taken from the domain experts' forecasts in the 2022 Existential Risk Persuasion Tournament and spread evenly to 2100: an 8% chance by 2100 of a war that kills over 10% of people, and 0.55% of one that ends humanity's future. The superforecasters' lower figures are the low end of each range.

A typical project cuts that risk by a share per dollar, taken from Rethink Priorities' cross-cause model, including its chance that a project backfires. The cut counts for the years such progress would otherwise have taken to come (5 by default), with each year discounted. A war the world survives is taken to kill 45% of people, mostly through famine, valued across the world's age groups like every death on the site. A war that ends humanity's future also takes the lives of everyone not yet born. Those future lives count only where you count lives that never start (0% by default).

### How the model works

Every dollar counts as a typical dollar of nuclear work, except about 2% on biological weapons, which counts as pandemic work, and about 7% on conventional and chemical weapons, which cuts no global risk. Then come the adjustments:

| Adjustment | Value |
|---|---|
| How far a typical project's effect applies | 0.8 |
| Work other groups would not have done now | 0.7 |
| The Association's share of the resources | 0.9 |
| Missing-data discount | 0.5 |

The first two are our judgment. The share of resources is below 1 because outside authors write for the journal and scientists give their time unpaid. Ploughshares' two grants ($178,000) are part of the Association's costs, so a donor here and Ploughshares each get credit only for their own dollars.

Side effects add 10%: work against a single detonation or a limited war, which has no shared forecast, and the conventional and chemical arms work. No harm is added beyond a typical project's chance of backfiring.

The Association has under nine months of reserves and ran deficits in both of the last two years, while the field's large funders are leaving. The next dollar counts at {{row:marginal_vs_past_average}} of the past average.

### What drives the result

Deaths in a war the world survives bring almost all of the result. By default the future does not count (lives that never start: 0%). With the Long-run value set (a 0.1% discount rate, lives that never start in full) the result rises to about 44. Nearly all the Association's work is on nuclear risk, none of it is grants that another funder might have paid for, and its smaller reserves make the next dollar count for more.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How much a dollar of this work cuts the risk.** Rethink's figures are placeholders, and the range runs ten times either way. This is the largest swing, from 2.2 to 110.
- **How many years a cut counts before it would have come anyway.** The shared slider runs from 1 to 20 years.
- **How likely and how deadly a nuclear war is.** The superforecasters put the chance at half the experts' figure. The share who die in a war the world survives runs from 15% to 85%.
- **How far the Association's work beats or trails a typical project.** Nothing about it is measured.

### What isn't modelled

- Losses beyond deaths, such as survivors' lost income and health, are a shared figure set to 0.
- The forecasts are spread evenly over the century, though forecasters put much of the risk later.

### How this model was checked

The model was built from the catastrophic-risk template. Round 1 of the independent audit passed with six small findings, all fixed: a worked count of the unpaid time behind the work and a better source for the absence of a policy win. It also checked that Ploughshares' grants to the Association are not counted twice. A verification round checked the fixes. The rounds are in audits/arms-control-association-charityevals/.
