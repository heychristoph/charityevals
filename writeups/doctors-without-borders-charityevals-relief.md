### Summary

Besides medical care, Médecins Sans Frontières hands out relief items to displaced families (653,300 families in 2025) and runs search and rescue in the Mediterranean. We estimate **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="doctors-without-borders-charityevals-relief">0.70</span> years of healthy life per $1,000** for this work, about 1.7% of MSF's programme spending. The result is mostly uncertainty: MSF doesn't publish what the distributions cost or how many people it rescued in 2025 beyond the first week's press reports, and no study measures how many deaths household items avert, so both branches are counted at half by the site's shared missing-data slider. MSF's main model, its medical care, is [here](/doctors-without-borders/doctors-without-borders-charityevals).

### How the model works

**Relief distributions.** The cost is the families reached times our estimate of 45 euros a family (items and delivery; UK for UNHCR prices a full family survival kit at £65). The items' market value is 70% of that, less the nets (about 5%), which are counted as deaths averted instead; the rest is counted on the site's log-income scale for people consuming about $590 a year (the World Bank's figure for the DRC's poor, in the PPP the goods are converted at). The nets are counted on their own: half the kits carry nets, a third of the children sleep under them for about a year, 60% of those wouldn't otherwise have slept under a net, and nets cut children's deaths from all causes by 17% where malaria is common (Cochrane, Pryce et al. 2018; per child using a net, about 23%), at the normal death rate plus the 60% of the crisis's excess that lasts the year. The other items (shelter, blankets, jerrycans, soap) avert an estimated 0.75% of displaced people's excess deaths for half a year (80% of the peak excess lasting that long), at a crisis death rate of 1.5 per 10,000 a day (Sphere's emergency threshold is 1, MSF's Zamzam survey found 2.5), and a little illness (malaria, diarrhoea and respiratory episodes at GBD's disability weights). A 72% counterfactual reflects the badly underfunded shelter and non-food-item clusters: OCHA's tracking shows them 21% funded in the DRC, 24% in Sudan and 34% in South Sudan in 2025. Prices and normal death rates mix the DRC's, Sudan's and South Sudan's (45%, 30%, 25% of the families, our judgment; two country files added to the site's facts); the DRC's household size stands in for all three.

**Search and rescue** (3.2 million euros). MSF's new ship, the Oyvon, sailed only from mid-November 2025 and rescued at least 68 people in its first week (press reports); we estimate about 150 for the year (68 to 300, the high end at the pace of MSF's previous ship, 2,280 people in 2024). A rescued person's chance of dying without any rescue is 2.5 times the central Mediterranean's average (1,342 dead or missing for 66,296 arrivals in Italy in 2025, about 2%), since NGO ships reach boats in distress; half of them would have been reached by coastguards, merchant ships or other NGOs in time. Deiana, Maheshri and Mastrobuoni find that state rescue operations led smugglers to send more dangerous boats; for a single NGO ship we take off 15%. The branch adds only 0.2% of the result: in 2025 the ship sailed for seven weeks against a full year's cost, including preparing it, so the branch is shown at its start-up year.

### What drives the result

The missing-data slider (0.14 to 1.41), the kits' cost (0.97 to 0.51), the crisis death rate (0.56 to 1.04), how long the nets protect (0.56 to 0.99), the counterfactual (0.49 to 0.83) and how many children sleep under the nets (0.59 to 0.95).

### How this model was checked

Built on 3 November 2026 and rebuilt on 4 November under the new rules for missing data: search and rescue, valued before at the site's lifeboat model (a proxy, now retired), is a branch built from the route's death rate and the literature on rescue at sea; both branches carry the missing-data marker; and the health effects' evidence adjustment no longer also discounts for "no study of kits" (that is the missing-data step now, 0.5 → 0.8). The rebuild also closed what the first build's last audit left open: the nets are no longer counted twice (as goods and as deaths averted); illness is built from GBD's disability weights (two added to the site's facts) instead of a typed figure; and the staff-pay share is built for this work (the items' delivery cost and search and rescue's own personnel) instead of MSF's average. The rebuild's independent audit (round 1: 1 high, 3 medium, 9 low) led to: the nets counted on their own, on all children's deaths for their own year (the high finding: they had been counted only on the excess deaths over the other items' half year; 0.63 → 1.0); the year's rescues anchored on press reports; the consumption baseline anchored on the World Bank's poverty data, in the right PPP base; the three countries' prices and death rates mixed; and labels, stale figures and the UNHCR kit's contents corrected. Round 2 (no high, 3 medium, 6 low), all on the nets: children who already had a net now gain nothing (60% new coverage), the trial's effect is set per child using a net (the trials' 75% use) so non-use isn't counted twice, and the crisis's excess death rate fades over the nets' year (60%); together 1.0 → 0.73. The relief figures the medical model also reads are one shared MSF fact. Left as they are: Sudan's 2017 official exchange rate (which understates the goods' value there; the site has no parallel-market rate) and the DRC's poor and household size standing in for all three countries. Round 3 (no high, no medium, 8 low): the other items' excess death rate now fades too (80% over their half year; 0.73 → 0.70), search and rescue's spending is a shared MSF fact, and units and wording fixed. The first build's independent audits (three rounds: no high, 4 medium, 5 low; 1 high, 2 medium, 6 low; no high, 2 medium, 5 low) had anchored the crisis death rate, the counterfactual (OCHA's 2025 cluster funding), the kit cost and the waits.
