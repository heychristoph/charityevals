### Summary

Besides its accountability campaigns and its grants, The Humane League (THL) spent about $5.2 million of its 2025 programme budget on four kinds of work: mobilising volunteers and supporters to press companies to keep their cage-free commitments, winning new corporate commitments, defending US state cage-free and crate-free laws and supporting the Animal Policy Alliance's local campaigns, and training Open Wing Alliance members. Our estimate of a dollar given for this work is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="the-humane-league-charityevals-other-programmes">0.0004</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**, below the median animal model on the site.

Most of it is THL's four new commitments (1.6 million hens) and the grassroots work's share of the hens that came out of cages at companies THL pressed; the defence of state laws against a federal override and the Alliance members' wins the training helped add a little. Each part is a share of an outcome many groups, and THL's other lines, work on. THL measures none of it beyond its own counts, so every branch takes the site's shared discount for missing data.

### What the charity does

THL's Form 990 reports one programme; its annual report splits 2025's into Global Programs ($5.2 million, the THL UK grant included), Education & Mobilization ($4.7 million), US Public Policy & Corporate Engagement ($2.5 million) and grants to the Open Wing Alliance and the Animal Policy Alliance ($2.0 million). Its other lines are the grants (to Alliance groups, THL UK and US groups) and the accountability campaigns ACE models. What is left:

- **Volunteers and grassroots mobilisation** (about $3.8 million, mostly Education & Mobilization): about 1,300 changemakers took 6,180 actions and sent 1.48 million messages, mostly at companies breaking their promises (Subway, Ahold Delhaize, Walmart), with protests, investigations and the communications that carry them.
- **New corporate commitments** (about $0.3 million, our judgment): four companies, 1.6 million hens.
- **US public policy and the Animal Policy Alliance** (3% and 4% of THL's spending by ACE's 2024 breakdown: $0.44 million and $0.28 million after the Alliance's $300K of grants, which are in the grants line). THL and its coalition opposed the EATS and "Save Our Bacon" language in the Farm Bill (228 members of Congress signed letters against it) and defended Proposition 12 and Massachusetts's Question 3 in court; THL counts 10 major legislative wins for farmed animals with the Alliance's 31 member groups, such as plant-based-by-default catering in Hennepin County.
- **Open Wing Alliance training** outside ACE's accountability costs (about $0.4 million, our judgment): four in-person trainings, webinars, campaign chats and mentoring (27 mentees); members won 216 new cage-free policies.

### How the model works

One model, the site's corporate-campaign template, with a branch per part; all end in the site's shared figures per hen-year, sow-year or meal and the site's years a won reform counts.

- **Volunteers**: the year's 7.5 million hens out of cages, less those at companies Alliance members pressed and THL UK's (both credited in the grants line), the 30% that wouldn't have switched yet without pressure, THL's 86% share among the groups (ACE's figures for the same campaigns), and the mobilisation's share of THL's own resources on those campaigns: its spending against the accountability line's $5.1 million and the volunteers' and supporters' unpaid time at the US volunteer-hour value. A charityevals model of the accountability line would take the rest of THL's share.
- **New commitments**: hens under them × share still caged × the shared rate of pledges kept × the discounted years after a four-year wait; THL's share among the groups pressing the same companies (80%), less what later accountability pressure will add (credited to that work).
- **State-law defence**: the hens supplying states with cage-free sales laws (23% of the US flock) and the 700,000 sows supplying California, the 30% that would go back into cages if the laws fell, and the five-point fall in the chance of an override (30% to 25%), one event drawn as happens or doesn't. Credit: THL's spending over the coalition's (about $8 million).
- **Training**: members' other 212 policies at about 10,000 hens each, plus half again in pressed commitments; credit by the training's share of all resources behind the members' campaigns (525 campaigners at about $45,000 each).
- **Local wins**: about 100,000 meals a year replaced, 70% of them served, at the site's per-meal figure for a US meal.
- **Side effects**, through the side-effects slider: wider gains from won commitments and laws (+10% of the animals' gain) and caged eggs sold elsewhere (−10%).

The year's spending is set against the year's wins, so the next dollar counts at THL's own next-dollar figure.

### What drives the result

- The site-wide sliders: a chicken against a person, the years a won reform counts, how much an unmeasured result counts; and THL's next-dollar figure.
- The shared hen figure, the share of switches that wouldn't have come yet without pressure, and the rate of pledges kept.
- The chances of a federal override with and without the coalition.

### Biggest uncertainties

- How much grassroots pressure adds to direct negotiation, and how much training adds to members' own work: nobody has measured either.
- The split of the money between the parts, which sets the mobilisation's and the training's credit.
- THL's counts (hens under the new commitments, hens spared) are its own.
- Whether the 2025 opposition changed the chance of an override at all; drawn as one event, the defence counts mainly at high risk settings.

### How this model was checked

Three rounds of independent review. Round 1 (3 high, 5 medium, 6 low): the next dollar now reads THL's own figure; ACE's pound costs were typed in (THL UK's costs are now left out, being in its grant); the volunteers branch, which ACE's theory of change places inside the accountability programme, now takes only the mobilisation's share of THL's own resources on those campaigns (ACE's model of that line is ACE's figure, apart from ours); the money is split by THL's annual report; evidence adjustments no longer repeat the missing-data step; the new sow figure was relabelled for Proposition 12's change (group housing). Round 2 (no high, 2 medium, 7 low): switches pressed by Alliance members are taken out (the grants line credits them); the sow figure's low end is a stated judgment; supporters' messages count in the volunteers' time. A verification round (no high, 1 medium, 4 low) found THL UK's switches counted in both lines; they are now taken out here, and a second verification (no high or medium, 3 low) led to the remaining switches' battery-cage share being raised to 0.9. Open, for the owner: the line's dollars in the charity record ($5.25 million here) and one pool per shared outcome with the grants line's model.
