### Summary

Learning Alliance helps primary schools in Eastern Uganda, mostly government ones, teach reading, with daily scripted phonics lessons for teachers, training and regular coaching. Our model of its first year, 2025, gives **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**. The result rests on a small comparison with matched schools that the charity ran itself, which found a large gain in reading letter sounds but none yet in reading words, and on how much of an early-grade reading gain lasts into adult earnings.

### What the charity does

In 2025 Learning Alliance worked with the district education office in Iganga District: 75 primary schools (about two thirds government, one third low-cost private, mostly rural), 278 teachers of Primary 1 to 3 and over {{row:pupils_2025}} pupils. Each teacher got a book of daily phonics lesson plans localised for Uganda, three days of training and a classroom visit from one of six coaches about once a month, who recorded each visit on a phone. Fifty schools started in February and 25 joined in July. A 32-day teachers' strike closed government schools in the third term. The charity spent about {{row:program_expenses}}, all in, of which about 5 dollars a pupil was the field programme and the rest headquarters (lesson design, management, monitoring and evaluation); its fiscal sponsor keeps {{row:fiscal_sponsor_fee}} of donations on top. In 2026 it serves 72 schools.

### How the impact happens

Children who learn to decode letters and sounds in the first grades go on to read, and adults who read better earn more. Part of a learning gain fades in later years; the part that lasts raises earnings over a working life, shared with the household.

### How the model works

The cost is 2025's all-in spending plus the fiscal sponsor's fee, against the {{row:pupils_2025}} pupils of that year. The effect is the charity's own comparison, in February 2026, of 26 programme schools with 26 non-programme schools in the district matched on size, pupil-teacher ratio, rurality and past exam results: pupils read {{row:spm_effect_sd}} standard deviations more letter sounds a minute, but no more whole words; Primary 3 pupils, whom the design can't measure, are given the same effect. Counting letter sounds and words equally, in units of the combined score, gives an overall reading gain of {{row:learning_gain_sd}} standard deviations a pupil-year. Each standard deviation of reading skill that lasts into adult life raises earnings by {{row:edu_earnings_per_sd}}, and {{row:edu_persistence}} of the gain is counted as lasting; the earnings come after about {{row:years_until_work}} years and are discounted. Then the model asks how far to trust the comparison, what would have happened anyway, what share of the resources behind the outcome are Learning Alliance's, and how much of a year the schools that joined in July had ({{row:july_dose_share}} of a full year: the strike closed the government schools among them, while private ones kept teaching). The charity's own count of pupils meets its own controlled evaluation of the same year's programme, so the result isn't discounted for missing data.

### What drives the result

The value of later earnings dominates, so the discount rate, the share of the gain that lasts and the earnings gain per standard deviation move the result most. Of the model's own inputs, the weight given to letter sounds against word reading moves it most, then the trust placed in the comparison and the two measured effects.

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether the comparison is right. Schools weren't randomised, there are only 26 pairs, and the charity designed and ran the analysis after the year. The testing after the year-end break and the extra month of teaching in the comparison schools push the estimate down; the matching can miss differences between schools in either direction.
- Whether letter sounds become reading. After one year the gain is in decoding, not yet in words read; fewer pupils couldn't read a single word (a fall of 10 points in Primary 1 and 20 in Primary 2). The 2026 midline has no comparison schools; the endline in November 2026 will.
- How much of a gain in the early grades lasts into adult earnings: the evidence linking test scores to wages comes from adults, and childhood gains fade.
- What a year costs. The first year carried the fixed costs of a new organisation over {{row:pupils_2025}} pupils; the charity expects costs a pupil to fall as it grows. That projection isn't used.

### What isn't modelled

Literacy's benefits beyond earnings, such as better health for the children's own future children, and teachers keeping the routines with later classes, against time taken from other subjects, are one judged share ({{row:unmodelled_benefits_adj}}); pupils helping siblings and classmates read is a small judged share ({{row:spillover_ratio}}). Later years of the programme for the same children are counted in their own years, not here.

### How this model was checked

Independent audits (audits/learning-alliance-charityevals/). Round 1 passed with no high findings; the fixes that followed: the reading gain is now in units of the combined two-subtask score, as the earnings figure needs (with a judged correlation between the subtasks), and the weighting's anchor restated; the fiscal sponsor's fee on donations added to the cost; the dose of the schools that joined in July raised because about half were private schools that taught through the strike; and the extrapolation to Primary 3 pupils stated. Round 2 confirmed the fixes.
