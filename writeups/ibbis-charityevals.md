### Summary

IBBIS works to stop DNA synthesis being used to make pandemic pathogens: it gives synthesis companies a free tool to screen what they are asked to make, and works on the standards and rules that make screening the norm. Our model of all its work in 2025 comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per $1,000**. That figure rests on forecasts of how likely catastrophic pandemics are, and on an estimate of what a typical dollar of such work does to the risk. Both are deeply uncertain, so the plausible range runs from about 0.3 to about 240.

### What the charity does

The International Biosecurity and Biosafety Initiative for Science is a Swiss foundation in Geneva. NTI | bio incubated it from 2019 and launched it in February 2024. Its 2025 work:

- **Screening tools.** commec, a free, open-source tool that screens DNA orders and runs on the provider's own computers. Providers in China, Latin America, the US and Europe use it. With the US National Institute of Standards and Technology it runs a portal that tests how well screening tools work. It also publishes customer-screening forms and training.
- **Standards.** It convenes two expert groups. One, about 30 experts, is writing a guide to put the ISO standard on DNA synthesis into practice. The other, over 60 experts, is defining which sequences should be flagged.
- **The Global DNA Synthesis Map.** A public map of where DNA is made, who screens it and which rules apply, launched in December 2025.
- **Policy and workshops.** Briefings and submissions in the EU (screening is in the draft EU Biotech Act), the UK and the US, and workshops for companies and regulators in Tanzania, Nigeria and South Africa.

IBBIS publishes no accounts. A Swiss foundation need not, and its website, annual letter and register entry give no figures. Its spending is built from its staff: about 7.5 people on average in 2025, growing to a full-time team of 10, plus fellows, part of whose time it pays. Each work-year is costed at the site's shared figure, $250,000 with overheads, plus a fifth for events and grants. That comes to about $2.3 million.

### How the impact happens

Pandemics carry a yearly chance of catastrophe, from the domain experts' forecasts in the 2022 Existential Risk Persuasion Tournament, spread evenly to 2100. There is also a 2% yearly chance of a pandemic as deadly as COVID-19.

IBBIS's spending cuts that yearly risk by the share a typical biosecurity project does per dollar. That share comes from Rethink Priorities' cross-cause model, including its chance that a project backfires. The cut counts for the years such progress would otherwise have taken to come (5 by default), with each year discounted.

The deaths averted are valued like every death on the site, by age. A COVID-scale pandemic kills mostly older people, so its deaths take COVID's age mix. A catastrophe that kills a share of the world takes the world's age mix. One that ends humanity's future also takes the lives of everyone not yet born, counted only through the discount rate.

### How the model works

All the work is valued as pandemic work. No law or standard was won in 2025: the EU Biotech Act was still a draft. IBBIS runs no programme that moves people into careers in the field.

| Adjustment | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| How far a typical project's effect applies | 0.8 | Providers use commec and it passed an independent benchmark. But only a handful of providers use it, and the standards and rules aren't in force yet |
| Work no one else would have done now | 0.7 | Others also build screening tools and standards. Few do outreach to providers outside the US and EU |
| IBBIS's share of the resources | 0.8 | Unpaid expert time in its two consortia, Microsoft's lead on the 2025 study, NIST's test sets, workshop co-hosts |
| Missing data | slider | Nothing about IBBIS's effect on the risk is measured, and even its spending is estimated |

Side effects: other benefits +5%. Screening also catches orders for toxins, and the map helps export-control authorities. The map also shows a would-be attacker where screening is missing, and the 2025 study showed AI could redesign toxins to get past screening, though tools were patched before it was published. That costs −3%.

IBBIS is raising money in small grants for named work, and Founders Pledge's 2023 recommendation was for funding its first three years (2024 to 2026). The next dollar counts at 60% of the past average.

### What drives the result

Pandemics that end humanity's future bring about half the result, mostly through the deaths of everyone then alive: at the default 4% discount rate the future of people not yet born counts for only about 8 years of the long-run population. Catastrophes the world survives bring about a third and COVID-scale pandemics the rest. At a 0.1% discount rate the result rises to about 36. The budget estimate hardly matters: every dollar is valued at the same rate, so a larger or smaller budget changes the cost and the benefit alike.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How many years a cut counts before it would have come anyway.** The shared slider runs from 1 to 20 years: 1.9 to 27.
- **How much a dollar of this work cuts the risk.** Rethink's figures are placeholders and the range runs ten times either way: about 4.5 to 52 for the pandemic figure.
- **How much the next dollar does.** 26% to 95% of the past average: 3.9 to 14.
- **How likely catastrophic pandemics are.** The superforecasters put an extinction-level pandemic at about a hundredth of the experts' chance.

### What isn't modelled

- Toxin screening and the help to export-control authorities are a 5% share of the benefits, not a branch of their own.
- Losses beyond deaths, such as survivors' lost income and health, are a shared figure set to 0.
- Credit for the screening tool's development under NTI (2019 to 2023) belongs to those years, not to 2025.

### How this model was checked

The model was built from the catastrophic-risk template. Round 1 of the independent audit checked the figures against IBBIS's annual letter and its Manifund page and found one high issue: ten rows still carried the template's placeholder sources. They now cite the forecasting tournament and IBBIS's own pages. A medium issue was in this write-up: it said most of the result came from catastrophes the world survives, but about half comes from pandemics that end humanity's future. That was corrected. The low issues were fixed too: the evidence wording and fellows paid by their own programmes, now left out of IBBIS's cost. Round 2 passed with two low issues. Fellows may already be in the team of 10, so fewer are added, and Sentinel Bio's 2026 grant is now in the next-dollar note. A verification round checked those changes. The rounds are in audits/ibbis-charityevals/.
