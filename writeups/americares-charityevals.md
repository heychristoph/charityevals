### Summary

Americares, based in Stamford, Connecticut, gets donated medicines and supplies to clinics and patients in the US and about 90 other countries, runs its own clinics, and responds to disasters. Our model of its medicine and health-services programmes, 93 million dollars of its own (cash) spending in the year to June 2025, gives **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="americares-charityevals">0.015</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**; its emergency programmes (40 million dollars) give **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="americares-charityevals-emergency">0.04</span>**. Both are far below the site's other global-health charities, mostly because the donated medicine and partners' own staff, not Americares' cash, are nearly all the resources behind the medicine it handles, and drug companies would most likely run their patient-assistance programmes without it.

### What the charity does

In its fiscal year 2025 Americares spent 1.92 billion dollars by its audited accounts, but 1.75 billion of that is donated medicines and supplies passed on, recorded at US wholesale list prices. Its own spending was 170.7 million dollars: 133.1 million on programmes and 37.6 million on management and fundraising. Its three programmes:

- **Access to Medicine** (34.4 million dollars cash, 1.73 billion of donated goods): the patient-assistance programmes it runs for drug companies, which mail free brand-name medicine to uninsured US patients (1.35 billion of the donated value), and shipments of donated medicine to partner clinics and hospitals abroad and to US free clinics and health centres (1.9 million patients helped, by its estimate).
- **Clinical Services and Community Health** (58.5 million cash): its own clinics in Colombia, El Salvador, Mumbai and Connecticut (364,468 consultations) and 270 community health projects in 36 countries (15,889 health workers trained).
- **Emergency Programs** (40.1 million cash, 10.4 million donated goods): 30 emergencies in 22 countries, 684,700 survivors helped.

### How the impact happens

Medicine and care avert deaths and illness: a patient whose clinic would have run out of an antibiotic or blood-pressure drug, or who couldn't have afforded an inhaler, gets treated. In emergencies, Americares restores health care (medicine for clinics that lost stock, mobile clinics, mental-health support) and hands out water, hygiene supplies and cash.

### How the model works

Each programme branch counts patients, the risk of death the care removes and the illness it averts, valued at the site's shared figures for a death by age and a healthy year. Then come four questions per branch: would the patient have been treated anyway (the counterfactual), what share of the resources behind the care were Americares' own (its credit share), how much medicine expires, and how much to trust a judged effect.

- **Donated goods count in the credit share, not the cost.** The medicines are drug companies' resources, valued at what they would cost where they're used (half of US list price for US brand drugs, about a third for medicine shipped abroad), and partner clinics' staff are theirs. Americares' cash is credited with its share of all of that: about 2% for the assistance programme, 8% for donated medicine to clinics, 85% for its own clinics.
- **The assistance programme** (about 224,000 patient-years of medicine, our estimate from its value) adds little: the companies would most likely run their programmes in-house or through another administrator without Americares.
- **Its clinics and training** carry most of the result: clinic patients at outpatient-care risks as in our model of Doctors Without Borders' outpatients, trained health workers from the research on training (Rowe et al. 2018).
- **Emergencies** are a humanitarian-relief model: excess deaths during the crisis, a share averted by the health care, illness treated, and goods, water and cash on the site's log-income scale over the months they cover, in four stand-in places (the US a third). The goods take no evidence step, being valued at their cost.

Every branch's effect is our judgment, not measured, so each is discounted by the shared missing-data slider.

### What drives the result

The missing-data slider, then how much a trained health worker's patients gain, the clinic patients' risk of death, and the price of donated medicine where it's used. Valuing donated medicine at recorded US list prices would make Americares' share even smaller; valuing it at zero (much of it near-expiry stock the companies would have destroyed) would raise the donated-medicine branch over ten times but leave the result well below the median.

### Biggest uncertainties

- What donated medicine does for patients against a stock-out: no study measures it.
- How many of the 1.9 million patients the medicine helped would have been treated anyway.
- What a trained health worker's patients gain: the training branch is over a quarter of the result and rests on the research on training alone.
- Where the emergency money went: the accounts give no split by country.

### What isn't modelled

Supply-chain and disaster-preparedness support to partner clinics, recovery projects after emergencies and healthier adults working more (benefits); near-expiry donations partners must destroy, donations displacing local purchases, and patient-assistance programmes steering patients to brand drugs while companies take tax deductions (harms): each is a share in the models' unmodelled-benefits rows.

### How this model was checked

Each model had an independent audit (audits/americares-charityevals/ and audits/americares-charityevals-emergency/). Round 1 passed both with no high findings. The fixes that followed: the assistance programme's deaths split by age from the shared cardiovascular split instead of our guess, the other age splits anchored on the shared splits, the emergency goods share anchored on the programme's own expense lines, the cash programme total cited from the audited statement, and the overlaps between branches stated. A verification round checked those changes. A later site-wide pass counted the emergency goods over the months they cover and without an evidence step (0.037 to 0.041); a third emergency round verified it with no high or medium finding.
