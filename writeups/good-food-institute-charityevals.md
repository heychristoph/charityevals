### Summary

The Good Food Institute (GFI) works to make plant-based, cultivated and fermentation-made meat good and cheap enough to take part of the meat market. Our model credits its 2024 spending with its share of what the whole alternative-protein field may achieve, and comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="good-food-institute-charityevals">0.0027</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**. At the site's default weights most of that is the greenhouse gases and farm air pollution of the meat not produced, about a third the eaters' health, and a few percent the animals spared. Nothing in the chain is measured, so the figure is very uncertain.

### What the charity does

GFI is a US non-profit founded in 2016 with six affiliates (Europe, Brazil, Asia Pacific, Israel, India, Singapore). In 2024 it spent $35.7 million, $26.5 million of it on programmes (audited consolidated accounts):

- Science and technology, $10.2 million: open-access research on the technical problems of alternative proteins, research grants ($3.7 million in 2024), and courses and student groups that bring scientists into the field.
- Policy, $5.9 million: public funding for open research, and challenges to product bans and labelling rules.
- Corporate engagement, $4.5 million: advice and data for companies, investors and suppliers.
- Communication, $3.6 million: reports, media and events for the field.
- International, $2.4 million: support for the affiliates and international grants.

It holds about 14 months of spending in reserve.

### How the impact happens

If alternative proteins match meat on taste and price, people eat less meat, and less meat is produced. Fewer chickens, pigs and fish are raised. Livestock emissions fall: methane from cattle, nitrous oxide and carbon dioxide from feed and land. Less manure and fertiliser means less ammonia in the air, which forms fine particles that kill people near farms. Eaters who eat less red and processed meat may be a little healthier.

GFI does not make products. It works on what the whole field needs, so its effect is to bring the field's progress forward a little.

### How the model works

All five programmes are one chain, because they all push the same field forward:

- **The gain:** world meat production in 2024 (FAO, 378 million tonnes) and the fish and seafood eaten (FAO, 165 million tonnes), counted as meals (0.15 kg a meal), times the extra share of that market alternative proteins take once the work now under way pays off: 3 points, our judgment (half a point to 15). Milk, cheese and egg alternatives add half as many meals again (our judgment, 0.2 to 1). Today plant-based meat and seafood sell $6.1 billion a year, about half a percent of the meat market, and US plant-based food sales have fallen since 2022.
- **What it displaces:** half an animal-product meal for every alternative-protein meal (our judgment, 0.2 to 0.8), times the shared production response.
- **When and for how long:** the gain comes after the site's shared wait for new technologies (15 years), discounted, and counts the site's number of years a won change counts.
- **GFI's share:** one year of GFI's spending over everything the field spends until the gain: the $19 billion companies have raised so far (in 2024 prices, about $21 billion), about $3 billion of public, philanthropic and company money, and today's $2.4 billion a year for 15 more years. That gives GFI 0.06%. A GFI dollar counts 1.5 times an average dollar into the field (our judgment, 0.5 to 5), for open research, policy work and information the whole field uses.
- **Animals:** the site's one figure for suffering spared per meal replaced, split by the shared species shares, in chicken pain days.
- **Climate and air:** 15 kg of greenhouse gases per kg of meat or fish displaced, net of the alternative, and 0.5 kg per dairy or egg meal (from Poore and Nemecek's global means). About 45% of it is methane. The farm ammonia counts as 0.2 of a fossil-fuel tonne's air pollution, from US estimates of the deaths it causes. These go through the site's climate conversion.
- **Health:** 0.00001 healthy years per kg of meat displaced, a side effect, from the burden the Global Burden of Disease puts on red meat, cut for poultry, weak evidence and the processed substitutes.
- **Your dollar:** GFI's own figure for the next dollar against its past average, 49%: no stated funding gap and a reserve above its target.

### What drives the result

The market share alternative proteins gain moves the result most, then the years a change counts, how far a GFI dollar goes against an average one, the missing-data slider and the discount rate. The health side effect and the climate rates move it about threefold each. Weighting animals more ("One person's suffering counts as much as … chickens'") raises the animal part. At Rethink Priorities' weights it is the largest part.

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether alternative proteins reach taste and price parity at all. Sales have stalled since 2022 and investment fell again in 2024. Forecasts made in 2019–2021 ran far ahead of what happened.
- GFI's share of the credit. We split it by money, and the leverage row is a guess with a tenfold range.
- What an alternative-protein meal replaces: meat, or beans and other plant foods.
- The health effect. It could be slightly negative if the substitutes' salt and processing outweigh less red meat.

### What isn't modelled

- Land freed from feed and grazing, and what grows back. That helps wild species, but the effect on wild animals' welfare could go either way.
- Antibiotic resistance and pandemic risk from livestock.
- Jobs and farm incomes that shift from meat to alternative proteins.

### How this model was checked

An independent auditor checked each version against the sources and the site's rules. Round 1 found two serious problems. Only the meat market was counted, though GFI's credit counted money spent on seafood, milk and egg alternatives too, so seafood, dairy and eggs were added. Forecasts running ahead of sales were discounted twice, so the evidence adjustment was set to 1. It also corrected the greenhouse gases per kg (18 to 15), FAO's 2024 meat figure, a wrong link, and past investment now counts in 2024 prices. The leverage judgment now has an anchor. Together these changes doubled the result.
