### Summary

The British Heart Foundation (BHF) is the UK's largest charitable funder of research into heart and circulatory disease. It also runs health information, support, CPR training and the national defibrillator network, funds pilots of new care in the NHS, and campaigns on policy. This is charityevals' own model of its research, its NHS pilots and its policy work in the year to 31 March 2026. Its information, support and CPR work is a separate model, [health and support](/british-heart-foundation-charityevals-health).

We estimate **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="british-heart-foundation-charityevals">0.025</span> years of healthy life per $1,000** you give to this work. The health-and-support model gives **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="british-heart-foundation-charityevals-health">0.005</span>**. The charity's figure in the rankings weights the two by spending: about two thirds this model, one third health and support. Almost all of the research figure rests on judgment: how much of the world's heart disease a year of research averts, how many years sooner, and what share of the field BHF is. The range runs across several orders of magnitude. None of the three branches has measured outcomes for BHF's own spending, so each is discounted for missing data.

### What the charity does

BHF spent £150.4m on its charitable work in 2025-26:

- **Research, £96.2m (64%):** 247 new grants worth £103.3m, mostly fellowships, programme and project grants and its research centres, across 45 UK institutions. In 2022 BHF paid for about half of the UK's public and charity cardiovascular research.
- **Health, £54.2m (36%):** health information (35 million web page views), the Heart Matters magazine, the nurse-led Heart Helpline (26,000 people), CPR training through the free RevivR app (190,000 people trained in the year), The Circuit (the defibrillator map ambulance services use), pilots of new care through the Healthcare Implementation Fund, and policy work in all four UK nations.

The accounts don't split health spending by activity. We judge policy at about 6% of it and the implementation pilots at about 5% (the fund has awarded £5.74m since it began, in awards of up to £500,000). Both are in this model; the rest of health spending is the health-and-support model.

BHF's 651 shops lost £6.7m on trading this year, so they don't pay for the charitable work. We count them by that net result, not their £240m of trading costs. Raising money cost £50.3m. Both costs are spread over the charitable spending, about 38p on each pound.

### How the impact happens

**Research.** Research leads to new drugs, devices, tests and guidelines, which reach practice about 17 years after the money is spent. A year of the world's cardiovascular research brings those advances forward. BHF gets credit for its share of that research.

**Implementation pilots.** The fund pays NHS teams to test new ways of delivering care: cholesterol-lowering injections in community pharmacies, home blood-pressure checks after high blood pressure in pregnancy, health checks for taxi drivers. A pilot that works and spreads brings that care to more people sooner.

**Policy.** BHF campaigned for decades for tighter tobacco control, and it part-funds Action on Smoking and Health. The Tobacco and Vapes Act became law in April 2026. It bans tobacco sales to anyone born on or after 1 January 2009. Each year the ban arrives sooner, one more birth cohort grows up with less smoking. That cohort is about 820,000 people, and its health gains come decades later.

### How the model works

Research is the medical-research template's chain:

- **Burden:** the world's cardiovascular burden, 436 million DALYs a year (GBD 2023).
- **Share averted:** what a year of the field's research averts once adopted, 0.3% a year (one judgment, 0.05% to 1%). Coronary deaths in England and Wales fell about 4% a year from 1981 to 2000, and treatments explain 42% of that. Rich countries carry a fifth of the world's burden, uptake elsewhere is lower, and about half of the gain is uptake of older advances rather than new research.
- **Timing:** brought forward 5 years, after a 17-year wait.
- **Adjustments:** our trust in the evidence (0.7), then BHF's share of the world's cardiovascular research funding (about 1%).
- **Check:** a 9% return in UK health gains with a 17-year lag, the figure for UK cardiovascular research, implies about the same healthy years per pound as the chain. The model shows both rows.

The implementation pilots follow the same chain at UK scale:

- **Burden:** the UK's cardiovascular burden, 3.35 million DALYs a year (GBD 2023).
- **Pilots:** the fund's spending over about £200,000 a pilot, about 13 pilots a year.
- **Each pilot:** serves a group carrying about 0.2% of the UK burden, and its model of care at scale averts 5% of that group's burden. The anchor is the trial evidence on what the pilots deliver: a 5 mmHg fall in blood pressure cuts major heart and stroke events by about 10%.
- **Adoption:** 15% of pilots are adopted widely. Of the NHS's five nationally funded Vanguard care models, only one was scaled up.
- **Timing:** brought forward 3 years, after a 5-year wait.
- **Adjustments:** trust 0.6, and BHF's share of the resources behind the piloting 60%. As for research, credit is for the piloting, not for the NHS's later cost of delivery.

The policy branch follows the government's own impact assessment of the law:

- **Effect:** 8 percentage points of each covered cohort kept from smoking. The assessment's central scenario says 9.2.
- **Health gain:** 3 healthy years lost per young smoker, arriving about 40 years later and discounted.
- **Credit:** a fifth of the whole campaign took place this year, and BHF had a twentieth of the resources behind the law.
- **Years counted:** the site's shared years a won reform counts.

**Deaths by age.** The DALYs in every branch are mostly years lost to deaths, and these deaths are mostly at old ages. As everywhere on the site, each DALY is split into its illness part and the deaths behind the rest. Each death is valued at the site's value of a death by age, using GBD's age split: two thirds of the world's cardiovascular deaths come at 70 or older, and four in five of the UK's. So the value-of-a-life setting moves this model, and each DALY counts about 0.6 to 0.75 of a healthy year.

All three branches use one cost: research plus our estimates of the policy and pilot shares of health spending. Each branch is multiplied by the shared "How much we count a programme whose results aren't measured" (50% by default), because none has measured outcomes: the research chain is calibrated judgment, the law's effect is modelled, and this year's pilots have no results yet.

The result divides spending by what past work of the kind achieved, so it is a past average. Like every such model, it counts the next dollar at the share of the average set under Your values (50% by default).

### What drives the result

- **The share of the burden a year of research averts:** moves the result about 14 times across its range.
- **The missing-data discount and the next dollar against the past average** (your value judgments).
- **Years brought forward:** 2 to 15 years.
- **BHF's share of the field:** 0.5% to 2%.
- **The discount rate:** a 17-year wait makes the result sensitive to it.

Research is about 93% of the result, policy about 5% and the pilots about 2%. The policy gains come 40 years out and BHF is one of many groups behind the law. The pilots are a small budget, and few pilots spread.

### Biggest uncertainties

- No study measures what one funder's research is worth. The chain is calibrated on returns-to-research studies: 9% a year in health gains for UK cardiovascular research, 10% for cancer. Their authors call these best-case figures, and they count only UK gains.
- Credit is split by share of funding. Industry pays for more than half of all cardiovascular research, and we count all of it; BHF is about 1% of the total.
- The smokefree-generation effect is modelled, not observed. New Zealand repealed its own ban before it took effect.
- The pilots are new and report no outcomes yet. How often a pilot spreads, and how much its care achieves outside a trial, are our judgments.

### What isn't modelled

- Research workforce and infrastructure that later research uses (in a small unmodelled share).
- Economic gains from research (not health).
- The Act's vaping provisions and second-hand smoke.
- BHF's other policy work, such as the cardiovascular framework in England, survival funding in Scotland and blood-pressure work in Wales. These are in the policy share's cost and get a small named share (about 2%) of the unmodelled benefits, not a chain of their own.

### How this model was checked

Mechanical checks: lint, the normalization invariants and the reference class (`scripts/audit-model.ts`). The research-to-practice lag of about 17 years was above the lint's generic 15-year bound for a reform's wait. The medical-research family now allows up to 25 years, as its own reference file expects.

First build (2026-11-03), independent audit round 1 (3 high, 4 medium, 11 low). What changed:
- **Proxy peers:** the health model's proxy read two SoGive upper bounds. It then read ranked best guesses only.
- **Programme patterns:** these matched both models. They are now anchored, so each programme has its own model.
- **Reference row:** a reading row now holds the returns-to-research check.
- **Merged judgment:** the share of the burden averted is one judgment instead of two independent ranges.
- **Smaller fixes:** a counterfactual for trainees who would have trained elsewhere, a lower defibrillator survival gain for home arrests, and clearer anchors and sources.

First build, independent audit round 2: no high findings, 3 medium, 7 low. What changed:
- **BHF's share of the field:** now counts all of industry's research, which leaves it at 1%.
- **Smoking cut:** re-anchored on the impact assessment's scenarios (8 points, 2.5 to 9.2), with leakage counted once.
- **Healthcare Implementation Fund:** left proxied then, which this rebuild has fixed.

Rebuild (2026-11-04), to the rule that no programme is valued at other models' results and every branch carries the missing-data step:
- **Implementation pilots:** now a branch of this model, built from the UK burden and the trial evidence on what the pilots deliver, instead of being valued at other charities' models.
- **Missing data:** research, policy and the pilots each take the shared discount, which halves the result at the default.
- **Health and support:** the information and helpline work is now built from trials of patient education (see its write-up). The health result fell from 0.05 to 0.005.

Rebuild, independent audit round 1 (1 high, 6 medium, 7 low). What changed:
- **Deaths by age:** the DALYs in every branch are now split into illness and deaths, with the deaths valued by age, as the site does everywhere. New shared facts give the ages of cardiovascular and smoking deaths (GBD 2023). This moved the result from 0.033 to 0.025, because the deaths are mostly at old ages. The family's reference file now says the step applies.
- **The pilots:** the chain is now worked out: pilots per year from the spending, each group's share of the burden, the effect of the new care, and a 15% chance of adoption, from the Vanguard programme. BHF's credit follows the research rule (60%).
- **Sources:** the UK and world burdens now come from GBD 2023. The research anchors (IMPACT, Moses et al., CardioScape, NIH) are cited, and the 0.3% is worked out.
- **Other policy work:** now a named share of the unmodelled benefits.
- **Spending on the pilots:** the anchor now says what the accounts don't itemise, with a wider range.
- **Smaller fixes:** the smoking check uses 8%, the expert count is stated, and the staff share covers the full cost.
- **Left as they are:** two reading-only reference rows the lint flags as unused, which hold the returns-to-research check. Research's spread by world population is the site's rule for global research.

Rebuild, independent audit round 2: no high findings, 1 medium, 10 low. The auditor checked every new death-age fact against the source. The medium is that the family's other charityevals research model (St Jude) doesn't yet split its DALYs into deaths valued by age. The lows each move this result by 3% or less, so they are left as they are: weighting the research deaths by where the gains fall, five-year weights at 50–69, a UK illness share for smoking, and judged over-80 shares where data exists. Two wording fixes were made.
