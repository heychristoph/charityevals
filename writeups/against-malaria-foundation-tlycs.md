### Summary

The Against Malaria Foundation (AMF) pays for insecticide-treated bednets handed out in mass campaigns run by governments and partners. The Life You Can Save estimates $93 per DALY averted in the DRC, $50 in Nigeria and $85 in South Sudan from AMF's 2024 distributions; pooled by money, valuing each death averted by age and discounting the nets' later years, that is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="against-malaria-foundation-tlycs">17</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**.

The model reproduces every calculated cell of The Life You Can Save's sheet. The Life You Can Save calls its figures highly approximate.

### What the charity does

AMF raises money for long-lasting insecticide-treated nets and funds them for national campaigns, mostly in Africa; governments and distribution partners hand them out household by household, and AMF checks afterwards that the nets are hung and used. In 2024 it funded 23.8 million nets in the three countries in the sheet, 22.9 million of which reached households.

### How the impact happens

1. **Nets reach people:** 11.6 million nets in the DRC, 5.7 million in Nigeria and 5.6 million in South Sudan, each covering about two people (AMF's monitoring).
2. **Cases among users:** the sheet takes the country's malaria cases per person (World Malaria Report 2024), the share of people who use their nets, and three years of a net's life, less deductions for nets that wear out.
3. **Deaths averted:** 5.6 children's lives saved a year per 1,000 children protected (the Cochrane review of nets), applied to the under-fives' cases, and 24% fewer deaths among the older people's cases.
4. **Illness averted:** two weeks of moderate malaria for each case that isn't a death.
5. **Cost:** AMF's spending on the nets in each country, about $3.50 to $4.40 a net.

### How the model works

The Life You Can Save's sheet comes first, ending at its cost per DALY for each country. Our steps follow:

- **Pooled by money.** The sheet gives no overall figure, so the three countries' costs are added and divided by their DALYs.
- **Deaths valued by age.** The sheet counts a death averted as the remaining life expectancy, discounted at 3%. The site values every death the same way, by GiveWell's moral weights for the age and the value-of-a-life slider; deaths at five or older are spread over the ages of sub-Saharan Africa's malaria deaths.
- **The nets' later years discounted** at the shared rate.
- **What would have happened anyway:** we judge that 85% of the nets wouldn't have reached households without AMF's money, since other funders also buy nets for these campaigns.
- **Who pays:** with credit by share of resources on, the governments' and partners' in-kind campaign costs and staff's pay below market count as part of the cost.

### What drives the result

- **Deaths per case.** The sheet's deaths come from the countries' malaria deaths per case, applied to every case among the nets' users, rather than from the fall in cases the nets bring.
- **The value of a child's death averted**, most of the healthy years gained.
- **How many nets others would have funded.**
- **The discount rate** on the nets' second and third years.

### Where we differ from The Life You Can Save

- **Deaths valued by age** with the site's moral weights, rather than discounted life expectancy (28 years for a young child, about 24 for an adult in the sheet).
- **Pooling** the three countries by money, where the sheet stops at a figure for each.
- **Discounting** the nets' later years at the shared rate; the sheet adds them up at face value.
- **What would have happened anyway** (85%, our judgment) and, with credit by share of resources on, **AMF's share of the campaign cost** (94.5%, GiveWell's figure) and **staff pay**: steps the sheet leaves out.

### Biggest uncertainties

- The sheet counts every malaria case among people using the nets as averted over three years; the incidence reductions it lists (44% to 47%) don't enter its bottom line.
- For the DRC the sheet reads the World Malaria Report's row for the Republic of Congo.
- For Nigeria the sheet's discounted life expectancy of a child (47.65) is the undiscounted figure for an adult; our view doesn't use it, since deaths are valued by age.

### What isn't modelled

- Fewer malaria infections among people who don't sleep under a net (the community effect of many nets).
- Income gained from less illness.
