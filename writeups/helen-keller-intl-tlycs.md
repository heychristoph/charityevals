### Summary

Helen Keller Intl supports governments' twice-yearly campaigns that give young children vitamin A capsules. The Life You Can Save estimates a cost per DALY averted in each of 13 African countries from Helen Keller Intl's 2024 costs and children reached; pooled by money, its figures give $30 per DALY. Valuing the deaths averted the site's way and taking out the doses that would have been given anyway, that is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="helen-keller-intl-tlycs">44</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**.

The model reproduces every calculated cell of The Life You Can Save's sheet. The Life You Can Save calls its figures highly approximate.

### What the charity does

Helen Keller Intl helps health ministries plan, run and monitor vitamin A supplementation for children aged 6 to 59 months, usually in campaigns twice a year. Between January and June 2024 the programmes it supported reached 45 million children in the 13 countries counted.

### How the impact happens

1. **Reach:** children given a capsule in the first half of 2024, from Helen Keller Intl's monitoring data.
2. **Deaths averted:** each country's yearly under-five death rate (UNICEF deaths over UN population figures), cut by 12% (the Cochrane review's estimate) for the six months a dose protects.
3. **DALYs:** each death averted counts the remaining life expectancy at age 2.5, discounted at 3%.
4. **Cost:** Helen Keller Intl's own costs in each country for the year July 2023 to June 2024.

### How the model works

The Life You Can Save's sheet comes first, one section per country, ending at a cost per DALY for each. The sheet has no overall figure, so our first step pools the 13 countries by money: all costs over all DALYs. Our steps follow:

- **Deaths valued the site's way.** Each child's death averted counts like every child's death on the site.
- **What would have happened anyway:** governments run the campaigns and coverage was already high before the year counted; we judge that 70% of the doses wouldn't have been given without Helen Keller Intl.
- **Beyond deaths:** a small side effect for night blindness and the shorter illnesses vitamin A prevents.
- **Central costs:** a judged 10% for headquarters costs the country figures may leave out.
- **Credit by share of resources:** with it on, Helen Keller Intl's staff's pay below market and the governments' and donors' costs count as part of the cost.

### What drives the result

- **The 12% fall in deaths.** It comes from trials, many decades old, in places where vitamin A deficiency was common; the sheet applies it in every country, whatever the share of children deficient there.
- **The death rate it applies to:** all deaths of children under five, newborns included, though the capsules go to children from six months.
- **How much of the coverage is Helen Keller Intl's doing.**
- **The value of a child's life**, the site's slider.

### Where we differ from The Life You Can Save

- **Value of a death.** The Life You Can Save counts 27.9 to 28.9 discounted years of life; we count GiveWell's value of a death under five at the value-of-a-life slider.
- **Pooling.** The sheet gives country figures only; we pool them by money.
- **What would have happened anyway** (70%, our judgment), **central costs** (10%, our judgment) and **the side effect** (5%, our judgment): steps the sheet leaves out.
- **Staff pay and others' costs** under credit by share of resources.

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether vitamin A still cuts child deaths by 12% where deficiency has fallen and other health care has improved.
- The share of the campaigns that would have gone ahead without Helen Keller Intl.
- The reach and costs are Helen Keller Intl's own monitoring figures, which the sheet notes weren't validated.

### What isn't modelled

- Helen Keller Intl's other programmes (neglected tropical diseases, eye health, nutrition beyond vitamin A).
- The second round of the year: the sheet counts one round's children against the whole year's costs.
