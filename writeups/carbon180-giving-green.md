### Summary

Carbon180 is a US policy nonprofit for carbon removal. Giving Green estimated in 2021 that its federal policy work from 2019 to 2021 removed or kept out of the air a tonne of CO₂ for about 66 cents, through the 45Q carbon capture tax credit and direct air capture hubs. On our scale that is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="carbon180-giving-green">0.23</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**: captured and removed CO₂ avoids only heat deaths, which come decades from now and are discounted like any future good.

The model reproduces all 5 of Giving Green's calculated figures in its spreadsheet.

### What the charity does

Carbon180 writes policy for removing CO₂ from the air and storing it: federal tax credits, research funding, public purchasing, and support for direct air capture, soil and forest carbon. It works with Congress and federal agencies.

### How the impact happens

Giving Green's chain, for three federal measures passed or proposed in 2020–21:

1. A two-year extension of the 45Q carbon capture tax credit (98 million tonnes from 2022 to 2028), a higher 45Q payout proposed in the Build Back Better Act (121 million, from Energy Innovation's modelling), and direct air capture hubs in the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (0.25 million): 219 million tonnes in all.
2. Carbon180 changed the chance of these provisions passing by 3% (1% to 5%), Giving Green's guess after talking to carbon removal experts.
3. The policy and government-affairs share (60%) of Carbon180's 2019–21 budgets, $4.4 million, divided by the expected 6.6 million tonnes.

### How the model works

Giving Green's rows come first, ending at its bottom line in dollars per tonne. Our steps follow:

- **Tonnes per dollar:** one over the cost per tonne, discounted for the years until the tonnes are avoided.
- **Tonnes without fuel smoke:** the shared climate rate, "Deaths caused by one more tonne of CO₂", counts deaths from heat and from the fine particles that come with burning fossil fuels. CO₂ captured at plants that keep running, or pulled from the air, comes with no less smoke, so the model's row "Air pollution these tonnes cut, relative to an average fossil-fuel tonne" is 0 (0–0.05, our judgment), and the site values them for their heat deaths.
- **Staff pay:** with credit by share of resources on, staff's forgone pay counts as part of the cost.

The site turns these tonnes into years of healthy life through the heat deaths they avoid: 23% of "Deaths caused by one more tonne of CO₂", each valued by the age it comes at like every death on the site, discounted at "Next year's good counts less by" over "Years until the heat deaths from a tonne of CO₂".

### What drives the result

- **Change in the chance of the bills passing because of Carbon180** (3%; 1% to 5%), Giving Green's guess, and the result moves in proportion.
- **The higher 45Q payout** (121 million tonnes; 28 to 258), which was proposed in Build Back Better; that bill did not pass, though the Inflation Reduction Act later raised 45Q.
- **Deaths caused by one more tonne of CO₂** and the discounting of heat deaths, values you set.

### Where we differ from Giving Green

- **Deaths valued by age.** Each death a tonne avoids is valued at GiveWell's moral weight for the age it comes at, over the ages of today's heat and air-pollution deaths (GBD 2023: most are of people over 50), about 30 and 24 healthy years, where the site used GiveWell's all-ages adult value (36.6) until late October 2026: the same rule as every death on the site.
- **A marginal dollar, not the past average.** Giving Green's figure is Carbon180's 2019–21 policy spending over the bills it influenced: an average. The next dollar does less; our view counts it at the share set under Your values, "How much the next dollar does compared with the average dollar so far" (50% by default, our judgment; 18% to 100%), one slider for every model on the site built on a past average (The Life You Can Save's leverage ratio, the other retrospective climate models). The evaluator view keeps 100%.
- **Tonnes become healthy years** through the shared climate rate. Giving Green stops at dollars per tonne.
- **Captured and removed tonnes count for their heat deaths only**, because they come with no less fuel smoke.
- **Future deaths are discounted.** Like every model on the site, ours discounts future good at the shared rate.
- **The wait for the tonnes is discounted.** Giving Green counts a tonne the same whenever it is avoided; the bills' cuts run from 2022 to 2028, after the 2019–21 spending, about 5 years later (3 to 8, our judgment from Giving Green's dates). Like every future benefit on the site, the tonnes are discounted for that wait at the shared rate, in both of the site's views, since Giving Green takes no view on discounting.
- **Staff pay:** with credit by share of resources, staff's forgone pay (our judgment for policy work) counts as an in-kind contribution.

### Biggest uncertainties

- The estimate counts the higher 45Q payout as if it passed in Build Back Better, which it didn't; the Inflation Reduction Act raised 45Q in 2022, so the effect came by another route.
- Much of the CO₂ captured under 45Q is used to pump more oil from old wells, which adds emissions back; neither Giving Green nor we subtract them.
- Giving Green stopped recommending Carbon180 in 2022, citing its fundraising success, and this 2021 estimate has not been updated. Its deep dive is no longer online; the spreadsheet is.

### What isn't modelled

Effects after 2028, Carbon180's work on soil and forest carbon and on public purchasing of carbon removal, and its role in building the carbon removal field.
