### Summary

The Campaign for Tobacco-Free Kids campaigns for laws that cut smoking: tobacco taxes, smoke-free places, warnings, bans on advertising and flavours. Its work comes to about **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="campaign-for-tobacco-free-kids-charityevals">0.17</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**, above the median of the site's global-health models. Smoking kills about 1.6 million people a year in the ten countries it works in most, and a tax or law once won keeps cutting smoking. But the figure rests on judgments about how much a year's wins cut smoking and how much of the credit is CTFK's. It also counts the next dollar at a third of the average dollar, because a donor's dollar mostly funds the US work while Bloomberg Philanthropies pays for the global programme.

### What the charity does

CTFK is a Washington-based 501(c)(3) founded in 1996, with a sister 501(c)(4), the Tobacco-Free Kids Action Fund, for lobbying. In the year to March 2025 it spent $47.7 million, $44.0 million of it on three programmes in its Form 990:

- **Global tobacco** ($29.5 million, including $9.6 million of grants): money and technical help for campaigners and governments in ten priority countries (Bangladesh, Brazil, India, Indonesia, Mexico, Pakistan, the Philippines, Turkey, Ukraine and Vietnam) and in Africa. It is a lead partner of the Bloomberg Initiative to Reduce Tobacco Use, and Bloomberg's restricted grants pay for most of this work.
- **US tobacco** ($8.4 million): federal, state and local campaigns on taxes, flavoured products and e-cigarettes, and on FDA regulation, plus litigation and research reports.
- **Global Health Advocacy Incubator** ($6.1 million): the same kind of campaigning outside tobacco, on food and nutrition, heart health, road safety, drowning, overdose, immunization and health data, in about 40 countries.

### How the impact happens

A tax rise, a smoke-free law or a ban on advertising leads some smokers to quit and some young people never to start. Over the following years that means fewer deaths from heart disease, stroke, cancers and lung disease, and less second-hand smoke for the people around them. The policy would probably have come some day without the campaign; the campaign brings it forward.

### How the model works

It is one model, with a branch for each programme.

- **Cost**: all three programmes plus their share of overhead, at the average yearly spending of April 2020 to March 2025 in today's prices (5% above the latest year's). This is the period whose wins are counted.
- **Wins**: each branch takes a burden and the share of it that a typical year's wins avert once fully in effect.
  - US: the US smoking burden, 9.4 million DALYs a year in GBD 2023, and 0.3% of it averted.
  - Abroad: the ten countries' smoking burden, 47.8 million DALYs, and 0.5% of it averted. The anchor is Lyle and Hendrie's estimate that the world's top-level tobacco policies of 2007–2020 cut smokers by about 0.5% a year, and by 0.2% a year in 2016–2020.
  - GHAI: the world's burden from obesity and salty diets, 174 million DALYs, and 0.02% of it averted, plus half as much again for its other areas.
- **Counting the wins**: a won policy counts for the site's standard number of years before it would have come anyway, after a year's wait until it takes effect and a lag of about ten years (five for diet) until the health gain. Every wait is discounted.
- **Deaths**: the DALYs are split into illness and the deaths behind them. The deaths are valued by age, from GBD's smoking death rates by age times the UN's population.
- **Credit**: CTFK's share of everyone behind the wins. That is 12% in the US, where ACS CAN, the heart and lung associations, Truth Initiative, health departments and its own Action Fund also campaign. It is 15% abroad, where governments, the other Bloomberg partners and WHO do much of the work.
- **Adjustments**: enforcement (90% in the US, 70% abroad) and the strength of the evidence (0.7 in the US, 0.6 abroad, 0.5 for GHAI). The missing-data discount applies to every branch, because no change in smoking from CTFK's own wins is measured.
- **Side effects**: second-hand smoke averted (+28%, the ratio of GBD's second-hand-smoke burden to smoking's). Smokers kept from switching by the e-cigarette bans CTFK backs (−3%). Household money no longer spent on tobacco (+3%).
- **The next dollar**: 33% of the average dollar's result. Room for more money is 0.6: four months of unrestricted reserves, with most net assets restricted by donors. The use is 0.55, because a donor's unrestricted dollar mostly funds the US work, which in this model does about half as much per dollar as the global work Bloomberg pays for.

### What drives the result

About three quarters of the result comes from the global branch, 15% from GHAI and 9% from the US. The biggest swings are:

- how many years a won policy counts;
- the missing-data discount;
- the next-dollar figure;
- the share of the ten countries' smoking burden a year's wins avert (0.08 to 0.40 years per 1,000 dollars across its range);
- CTFK's share of the credit abroad.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How much a year's wins cut smoking.** No study measures this for CTFK's countries. The anchor is a model of what policies of a given strength should do (SimSmoke-style projections), and the recent rate of wins is lower than the 2007–2014 rate.
- **Credit.** Bloomberg's partners, governments and local groups all claim the same wins, and CTFK's share of the resources is a judgment.
- **From fewer smokers to fewer deaths.** The model treats a lasting cut in smoking as an equal cut in the smoking burden, reached after about ten years. Youth who never start gain only decades later.
- **GHAI.** Its food policies' effect on weight and disease is much less sure than tobacco taxes' effect on smoking.

### What isn't modelled

- Tax revenue, which is a transfer.
- The Action Fund's own lobbying spending, which is not CTFK's: it lowers CTFK's credit instead.
- Wins in Africa and outside the ten countries, whose smoking burden is small. They are inside the global branch's judged share.
- Industry counter-campaigns triggered by wins.

### How this model was checked

Independent audits, in audits/campaign-for-tobacco-free-kids-charityevals/.
