## Summary

Aquatic Life Institute (ALI) pushes retailers, certifiers and policymakers to adopt welfare standards for farmed fish. ACE's analysis (2024 evaluation, renewed in 2025) estimates SADs for its corporate and institutional outreach: **481 SADs averted per dollar**, or **357 per dollar you give, about 91 times GiveDirectly**, the second highest on the site after Sinergia Animal. Converted with our default exchange rates between suffering-adjusted days (SADs) and GiveWell's units of value, which is where most of the uncertainty is.

The model reproduces all 18 of ACE's intermediate figures.

## How the impact happens

Companies such as retailers commit to standards (for example stunning at slaughter); some implement them; the number of fish affected is very large, so even small per-fish improvements add up.

## What drives the result

Whether specific large commitments are implemented: **how certain Tesco's stunning commitment is** is the largest factual lever in ACE's sheet (about 73× to 109× GiveDirectly on its own).

## What isn't modelled

ALI's policy feedback program has no SADs estimate and is left out of the bottom line.

## Where we differ from ACE

- **Salary sacrifice (on by default).** We guess staff are 65% of program cost and earn 35% less than in comparable for-profit jobs, so the forgone pay is an in-kind donation of about 35% of cash spending. That leaves each donated dollar about 74% of the credit ACE gives it. Both numbers are guesses with wide ranges; replace them with the charity's real figures when we have them.
- **Credit for shared outcomes.** ACE's discounts for other organisations' contributions are built into its inputs here, so the full-credit switch doesn't change this model yet.
