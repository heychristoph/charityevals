### Summary

Effektiv Spenden runs a donation platform for Germany, Switzerland and Austria. It passes tax-deductible gifts on to charities that evaluators recommend, and it also runs its own funds and gives donors advice. Our model of all its work in 2025 comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**. Each dollar it spends leads to about eight dollars of giving it caused. That giving is valued at what the causes its donors chose achieve on this site. About half went to fighting poverty, and the value comes from that half. Under the site's default values, the giving to the other causes adds slightly less than nothing. The figure moves most with how much the next dollar does compared with the past average, and with the discount rate.

### What the charity does

Effektiv Spenden works through three non-profits: a German company (UES gGmbH, eight staff), a Swiss association and, since September 2024, an Austrian association. Together they spent $1.34 million in 2025, at the year's average exchange rates. The German accounts are compiled by a tax adviser and the Swiss accounts are audited. The donations passed on to charities, €20.7 million in Germany and CHF 7.0 million in Switzerland, are donors' money. They count as the recipients' cost, not Effektiv Spenden's.

Its work is one programme of effective giving:

- **The donation platform and its funds.** About 16,000 donors gave almost €30 million in 2025, by its own count. In the accounts, that is $32 million received for charities. It had six funds in 2025. Each one follows an outside evaluator or adviser: GiveWell's All Grants Fund, Founders Pledge's Climate Fund, the EA Animal Welfare Fund, Longview Philanthropy for catastrophic risks and for AI safety, and Power for Democracies.
- **Donor advice.** In 2024 its advice steered about €3 million straight to recommended charities, outside the platform.
- **Outreach.** Its website, newsletter, book and press work, and the 10% Pledge it runs with Giving What We Can.

### How the impact happens

Donors give, and give to better charities, because of Effektiv Spenden. The model counts only the giving it caused. Its post-donation survey (4,714 answers over 2019–2022) found that:

- 19% of respondents would not have given at all;
- 47% would have given to another, non-recommended charity;
- the rest would have given to recommended charities anyway, or came through its partners.

The giving it caused is the first two groups, 66% of respondents. Donors who didn't answer are counted at half the respondents' influence, the site's shared rule for any organisation's own survey. Donors giving over €100,000 a year gave a third of the money in 2024, and they are counted at 30% of the others' influence. Most of them were committed effective givers already. Together this puts the share it caused at about 31% of platform giving. That is close to its own best guess of 32%. Advised gifts take the site's shared 30%, because no survey covers them.

A dollar moved is worth what its recipients achieve. Each cause is read live, under your values, at its recipients' figures on the rankings. Within each cause, two lists of recipients are blended:

- the whole organisation's 2024 split, the latest it publishes;
- the Swiss association's 2025 grants by recipient, from its audited accounts.

The Swiss list gets its share of the 2025 money, 27%. In both lists, recipients without a model here take the average of the modelled ones in their cause.

- **Fighting poverty (48%).** Its poverty fund (40% of the 2024 poverty giving) is valued at GiveWell's top charities. GiveDirectly (27%), Malaria Consortium, Against Malaria Foundation and Deworm the World (at Evidence Action's figure) are read directly. In the Swiss list, Evidence Action, Helen Keller Intl, GiveDirectly, StrongMinds and the others are each read at their own figure.
- **Climate (21%).** These are valued at Clean Air Task Force's figure, our own model and the one recipient with a published model here. Founders Pledge's and Giving Green's models of its other recipients are not published.
- **Animal welfare (7%).** In 2024 these are valued at The Humane League's figure on the rankings (ACE's), the one modelled recipient. In the Swiss list, The Humane League, Sinergia Animal and Wild Animal Initiative are each read at their own figure.
- **Catastrophic risks (12%).** No 2024 recipient has a model here, so that part is valued at the catastrophic-risk charities on the rankings, weighted equally. In the Swiss list, METR is the one modelled recipient.
- **Democracy (9%).** These are valued at the democracy charities on the rankings, weighted equally. None of its recipients has a model here.
- **Other effective-giving organisations (2.4%)** take the average of the rest.

Shifted money to these causes therefore counts slightly below zero: it was assumed to come from an ordinary charity valued at GiveDirectly's level. So the poverty half carries the whole result. Half the platform giving it caused counts as new money. The other half would have gone to other charities, so it counts only for the gain over where it would have gone. Both are the site's shared figures for every organisation that moves money.

### How the model works

Money moved = donations received for charities × the share it caused, plus advised gifts × the shared 30% its advice is taken to have redirected. People who ask for advice were giving anyway, so the advised gifts count only for the gain over where they would have gone, after the site's evidence and missing-data steps (the rule for advice that changes where money goes). That comes to about $11 million against $1.34 million of costs. Platform donations wait about a third of a year before reaching the charities, because its funds grant twice a year (our judgment, 0.1 to 0.6 years). That wait is discounted at the site's rate. Each dollar moved is valued at its recipients' figures, net of where shifted money would have gone. A 5% side effect is added (our judgment) for giving its recommendations inspire outside the platform and advice. The result is multiplied by how much the next dollar does compared with the past average: 32%. Its German company holds about two years of costs in reserve after two surpluses, and Open Philanthropy covers half its costs, so a further gift does less than the average dollar so far. Platform money is counted, not estimated, so it takes no missing-data discount; the advised gifts, whose redirection is judged, do.

### What drives the result

- **The next dollar** (32%, from 14% to 66%).
- **The discount rate.** It matters through the destinations' own long-run benefits and the wait for the funds' grants.
- **How far the survey's non-respondents were influenced** (half the respondents' influence, from a quarter to all).
- **Where shifted money would otherwise have gone** (one times GiveDirectly, from 0 to 3 times) and **the value of a young child's life**.

### Where we differ from Effektiv Spenden's own evaluation

Its own figures are a ratio of donations to costs of about 21 for 2024 (23 with advised gifts) and a best-guess counterfactual multiplier of 13 for 2020–2022.

- **What a dollar moved is worth.** It treats all its recommended charities as equally good. We value each cause by its recipients' figures on the rankings.
- **The share it caused.** It counts a third of the donors who would have given elsewhere as moved from charities five times less effective, and the rest as nothing. We count all of them as caused, and the site's shared rows then credit only the gain over an ordinary charity. We also count non-respondents and the largest donors at less influence, where its evaluation makes no such adjustment.
- **New and shifted money.** We use the site's shared 50%. Its survey alone would put new money at about 29% of the giving it caused.
- **Advised gifts.** Its evaluation leaves them out. We count them, using the 2024 figure, as redirected money: only the gain over where they would have gone.
- **Staff time.** It subtracts what its staff could have given by earning more elsewhere. We credit staff for the pay they give up instead, as in every model on the site (credit by share of resources, off by default).
- **The wait and the next dollar.** It reports an undiscounted average. We discount the time money waits in its funds and take 32% of the average for the next dollar.

### Biggest uncertainties

- The survey: it covers 2019–2022 and relies on what donors say they would have done. Its response rate isn't published (we judge 20%, from 8% to 40%).
- How much its largest donors were influenced.
- The value of the climate, animal and catastrophic-risk giving. It rests on one or a few models per cause, and on the site's species-weight and risk sliders.
- The within-cause splits. The organisation-wide one is from 2024, and only the Swiss association publishes its 2025 grants by recipient.

### What isn't modelled

- Gifts to its recommended charities from people who read its recommendations but never use the platform or ask for advice, beyond the 5% side effect.
- The interest its held donations earn (€135,000 in 2025), which pays part of its own costs. This counts only as part of the wait and in the side effect.

### How this model was checked

Six audit rounds (audits/effektiv-spenden-charityevals/). Round 1 found no high-severity problem and checked every cost, reach and fact figure against its source. Its fixes:

- Blended the Swiss association's 2025 grants by recipient into each cause. This brought in METR, Sinergia Animal and Wild Animal Initiative, which the organisation-wide 2024 split leaves out.
- Lowered the wait for the funds' grants below the year-end balances, which December's giving inflates.
- Corrected the write-up.

Round 2 verified those changes and passed. Its two low findings were explainer wording. Round 3 checked those fixes. Round 4 checked the move of two equal-weight lists to the site's live cause averages, and passed. Round 5 checked the advised gifts' move to the site's rule for advice (redirected money, counted only for the gain, with the evidence and missing-data steps), made when Longview Philanthropy's model was built, and passed; round 6 checked the wording that followed.
