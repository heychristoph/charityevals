### Summary

Winning Start gives primary-school children in South Africa daily remedial lessons at their own level, on the Teaching at the Right Level model. Founders Pledge's 2019 education report values it through the extra earnings better-taught children go on to make: **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="winning-start-founders-pledge">9.8</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

The model reproduces all 10 of Founders Pledge's calculated figures for Winning Start, including its bottom line: an increase in ln(income) of 0.0034 per dollar, or about $200 to double one person's income for a year (Founders Pledge's middle case).

### What the charity does

Trained facilitators take children who have fallen behind out of class for an hour a day of literacy (and numeracy) sessions grouped by level rather than by age.

### How the impact happens

1. Test scores rise by about 0.15 standard deviations per child (5 to 30 hundredths across Founders Pledge's cases), based on the evidence for Teaching at the Right Level.
2. Each standard deviation raises earnings: Founders Pledge's 2019 method assumes 10% a year, 80% of it new income; our view uses its 2023 method, 19.1% a year times 17.5% for replicability, doubled for the household.
3. The gain starts about eight years later and lasts five years in the 2019 method's middle case; 40 years, a working life, in the 2023 method our view uses.
4. The cost is about $11 per child.

### How the model works

Founders Pledge's sheet has a pessimistic (10th percentile), middle and optimistic (90th percentile) case. The middle case is the model; the other two give each input's range. Founders Pledge discounts the earnings gain at its rate of 5% over its duration, and the wait by a simple factor of 1 / (1 + 8 × 5%); our view uses the shared rate (4% by default), compounded, with Founders Pledge's 2023 earnings figures (below). The result, in ln(income) per dollar, is valued with GiveWell's weight on income: a year of doubled income is one unit of value.

### What drives the result

- **How much earnings rise per standard deviation** and **how long the gain lasts**: Founders Pledge's 2023 education figures, shared by every education model on the site (19.1% × 17.5%, 40 years). They carry no published range, so the ranges shown leave them out.

### Where we differ from Founders Pledge

- **Earnings from learning (changed for consistency):** Founders Pledge's 2019 method, which this analysis used, counts 10% more earnings a year per standard deviation of test scores, 80% of it additional, for 5 years, for the earner alone. Founders Pledge's newer education analyses (2023: Central Square Foundation, EIDU, Imagine Worldwide, Teaching at the Right Level, Ubongo) count 19.1% times a 17.5% replicability adjustment, over a 40-year working life, doubled for the household. Our view uses Founders Pledge's latest for every education model, so a standard deviation of learning is worth the same wherever it comes from; it compounds the eight-year wait at the discount rate, as the 2023 method does, where the 2019 sheet used a simple factor. Per standard deviation this is about 3.7 times the 2019 value at 4%. The evaluator view keeps the 2019 figures.
- **Discount rate:** our view uses the shared rate under Your values (4% by default) instead of Founders Pledge's 5%.
- **Credit by share of resources:** when on, the government's spending counts as a contributor's cost, and staff's pay below market counts as theirs (our default for staff-delivered programs: about half of cost, paid about 15% below market). Off by default.

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether test-score gains from a year of remedial lessons last into adult earnings. The evidence linking the two in low- and middle-income countries is thin, and gains often fade.
- Founders Pledge's figures date from 2019; Winning Start's costs and reach may have changed.

### What isn't modelled

Benefits other than earnings (wellbeing, health, children's own children) and any benefit to children not in the sessions.
