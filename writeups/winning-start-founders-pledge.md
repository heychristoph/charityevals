### Summary

Winning Start gives primary-school children in South Africa daily remedial lessons at their own level, on the Teaching at the Right Level model. Founders Pledge's 2019 education report values it through the extra earnings better-taught children go on to make: **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="winning-start-founders-pledge">2.7</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

The model reproduces all 10 of Founders Pledge's calculated figures for Winning Start, including its bottom line: an increase in ln(income) of 0.0034 per dollar, or about $200 to double one person's income for a year (Founders Pledge's middle case).

### What the charity does

Trained facilitators take children who have fallen behind out of class for an hour a day of literacy (and numeracy) sessions grouped by level rather than by age.

### How the impact happens

1. Test scores rise by about 0.15 standard deviations per child (5 to 30 hundredths across Founders Pledge's cases), based on the evidence for Teaching at the Right Level.
2. Each standard deviation is assumed to raise earnings by 10% a year, of which 80% is new income rather than income taken from others.
3. The gain starts about eight years later and lasts five years in the middle case.
4. The cost is about $11 per child.

### How the model works

Founders Pledge's sheet has a pessimistic (10th percentile), middle and optimistic (90th percentile) case. The middle case is the model; the other two give each input's range. Founders Pledge discounts the earnings gain at its rate of 5% over its duration, and the wait by a simple factor of 1 / (1 + 8 × 5%); our view uses the shared rate (4% by default) in the same formula. The result, in ln(income) per dollar, is valued with GiveWell's weight on income: a year of doubled income is one unit of value.

### What drives the result

- **How much earnings rise per standard deviation** (1% to 25%) and **how long the gain lasts** (2 to 20 years): shared assumptions from Founders Pledge's 2019 education work, and by far the biggest swings.
- **Government money:** in Founders Pledge's middle case, the government was expected to spend about $50 per child alongside Winning Start ($2 million in 2018/19), but Founders Pledge counts none of it as a cost. With credit by share of resources on, it counts, and the result falls about sixfold.

### Where we differ from Founders Pledge

- **Discount rate:** our view uses the shared rate under Your values (4% by default) instead of Founders Pledge's 5%, which is why our figure is a little higher than in the evaluator view. Founders Pledge discounts the eight-year wait with a simple factor rather than compounding, which counts the wait a little less; we keep its formula.
- **Shared figures:** the earnings gain, the share that is additional, the wait and the duration read the shared facts for Founders Pledge's 2019 education work; same numbers.
- **Credit by share of resources:** when on, the government's spending counts as a contributor's cost, and staff's pay below market counts as theirs (our default for staff-delivered programs: about half of cost, paid about 15% below market). Off by default.

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether test-score gains from a year of remedial lessons last into adult earnings. The evidence linking the two in low- and middle-income countries is thin, and gains often fade.
- Founders Pledge's figures date from 2019; Winning Start's costs and reach may have changed.

### What isn't modelled

Benefits other than earnings (wellbeing, health, children's own children) and any benefit to children not in the sessions.
