### Summary

ACTRA (Acción Transformadora) runs group cognitive behavioural therapy for young men with violent or criminal backgrounds in Colombia, to cut crime and help the men themselves. The Happier Lives Institute predicts it creates 37 WELLBYs per $1,000 donated; on our scale that is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="actra-hli">18</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

HLI publishes no spreadsheet for this estimate. The model is built from the figures in HLI's report, each quoted on its row, and reproduces HLI's 37.

### What the charity does

ACTRA delivers a programme modelled on Sustainable Transformation of Youth in Liberia (STYL): about eight weeks of group therapy that helps high-risk young men practise self-control, plan for the future and see themselves differently. It is still piloting the programme in Colombia. HLI lists ACTRA as an honourable mention: promising, but small and new.

### How the impact happens

HLI reuses its model of NEPI, which ran therapy plus cash in Liberia, but takes the therapy-only arm of the Liberian trial (Blattman et al., with a 10-year follow-up):

1. **The men and their households** gain 1.04 WELLBYs: therapy alone improved the men's wellbeing less than therapy plus cash, and Colombian households are smaller.
2. **People spared from crime** gain 11.1 WELLBYs: the men committed fewer thefts over the 10 years.
3. **Cost:** $330 a participant, NEPI's cost without its cash transfer.

12.1 WELLBYs for $330 is 37 WELLBYs per $1,000.

### How the model works

HLI's rows give its bottom line in WELLBYs per $1,000. Our steps follow: the benefits are spread over the 10 years of the follow-up and discounted at the shared rate, with credit by share of resources for staff pay when that is on. The site converts WELLBYs to healthy years at the shared exchange rate.

### What drives the result

- **The benefit to would-be victims** (11.1 of the 12.1 WELLBYs), which rests on how much a theft lowers the victim's wellbeing and on the trial's count of thefts avoided.
- **The cost per participant** ($330). HLI thinks it could fall well below this, since other charities deliver therapy for under $50, but costs in Latin America may be higher than in Africa.
- **Whether the Liberian result carries over** to Colombia; HLI thinks the group format may have mattered.

### Where we differ from HLI

- **Discounting.** HLI counts a WELLBY in ten years the same as one now; our view spreads the benefits over 10 years and discounts them at the shared rate.
- **Staff pay:** with credit by share of resources, staff's forgone pay counts as an in-kind contribution.

### Biggest uncertainties

This is a prediction for a programme still being piloted, from one trial in another country. HLI's own model of NEPI, which delivered the Liberian programme, was dropped in 2025 when NEPI closed after financial irregularities; HLI says its ACTRA assessment, based on separate evidence and modelling, is unchanged.

### What isn't modelled

Effects beyond the 10-year follow-up, effects on violence other than theft, and any benefit to the communities beyond the victims HLI counts.
