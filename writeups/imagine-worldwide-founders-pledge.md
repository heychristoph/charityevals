### Summary

Imagine Worldwide puts tablets with child-led learning software for reading and maths into primary schools, and in Malawi it is scaling the program to every school, with the government planning to take it over. Founders Pledge's 2023 analysis of the Malawi scale-up comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="imagine-worldwide-founders-pledge">14</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

The model reproduces all 20 of Founders Pledge's calculated figures. All of the benefit is higher income, valued as GiveWell values income.

### What the charity does

Children work through the software on solar-charged tablets in class, at their own pace. In Malawi, Imagine Worldwide plans to bring it to all 3.4 million pupils in standards 1–4 over six years, paid for by philanthropy, after which the government has committed to run it.

### How the impact happens

1. **Learning.** Six studies of the program find learning gains of about 0.33 standard deviations a year. Founders Pledge puts half its weight on a sceptical prior of 0.1, because a national scale-up rarely matches its trials, which leaves 0.215.
2. **Income.** Each standard deviation of learning is taken to raise later earnings, after a heavy replicability discount, by about 3.3% a year for 40 years, starting 8 years later and shared with the household. Founders Pledge discounts the gain at 4% a year and values it in its own units, which it calls WELLBYs.
3. **The government phase.** With a 50% chance, the government runs the program for another 10 effective years. Founders Pledge credits the philanthropic money with that phase, net of what the government's money would have done in other education spending.
4. **Funging.** Founders Pledge keeps 70% of the result because the Malawi program might have been funded anyway.

### How the model works

The [model](/imagine-worldwide) follows the CEA tab and the income-benefits tab of Founders Pledge's sheet row by row. Budget totals and the government cost per pupil are taken from the sheet's budget tabs as inputs. Founders Pledge's bottom line is a multiple of GiveDirectly. We turn it back into its value units, then back into the income gain those units stand for, and value that gain as GiveWell values income: one unit for doubling one person's income for a year. Founders Pledge's "WELLBYs" here are its own value units, built from its moral weights (about 1.9 per doubled income-year), not life-satisfaction points measured in surveys, so we don't convert them at the WELLBY exchange rate.

### What drives the result

- **The government phase** more than doubles the result: the ramp-up alone gives about 43% of the figure with it.
- **The discount rate**: the income gains start 8 years out and last 40 years, so moving the rate from 0% to 8% moves the result about six-fold.
- **The learning-to-income link**: the replicability adjustment (17.5%) and the prior's weight are judgments that move the result in proportion.
- **How much a doubled income-year is worth against a healthy year**, a shared exchange rate that applies to every income model.

### Where we differ from Founders Pledge

- **Units:** Founders Pledge values a doubled income-year at 1.9 of its units; we count the same income gain in GiveWell's units of value, like every other income model on the site.
- **Discounting:** the income gains read the shared discount rate (Your values); Founders Pledge uses 4%, the same as our default. Founders Pledge doesn't discount the government phase (years 7 to 16), on the grounds that both its costs and benefits lie in the future. We do (changed for consistency): the donor's money is spent in the first six years, so the government phase's learning, and the government spending Founders Pledge nets off, are discounted to the start, about 67% at 4%. That lowers the result by about a fifth (Founders Pledge's own figure is 16.9 on our scale). The ramp-up years stay undiscounted, as their costs and benefits fall in the same years.
- **Credit by share of resources:** when on, the learning is shared over everyone's spending, philanthropic and government, instead of counting the government phase as leverage; and the funging discount drops.
- **Staff pay:** when on, staff's pay below market counts as an in-kind contribution. We use our default for programs that mostly buy and hand out goods.

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether the government takeover happens and keeps the program effective; Founders Pledge's 50% and 10 years are judgments.
- Whether learning gains turn into higher earnings: the evidence comes from other settings and is discounted to 17.5% for replicability.
- Founders Pledge publishes no ranges, so the tornado shows mostly shared assumptions and our staff-pay judgments.

### What isn't modelled

Benefits of learning beyond earnings (health, empowerment, later schooling) and any value of the tablets outside school hours. The government's counterfactual value comes from another Founders Pledge sheet we haven't rebuilt.
