### Summary

Boys & Girls Clubs of America (BGCA) is the national organisation of about 1,000 local Club organisations, which run nearly 5,500 Clubs (in schools, stand-alone buildings, public housing, on Native lands and on military bases) for 1.6 million members aged 5 to 18, and reach 2.57 million more children through outreach. Our estimate for BGCA's programme spending in 2025 is **{{result}} years of healthy life per $1,000** (from about 0.002 to 0.5 with every range combined).

That is low because the help goes to US families, who consume around {{row:baseline_consumption}} a person a year: on the site's one log-income scale a dollar does far less for them than for a household living on a few dollars a day, and a year at a Club costs about $1,700. No controlled evaluation of Club membership exists, so the model is built from the research on after-school programmes and counts at the site's missing-data slider (default 50%).

### What the charity does

Each local Club organisation is a separate charity; BGCA is their national office. In 2025 (audited consolidated accounts, year to 31 December) it spent $396 million, $354 million on its two programme lines:

| Spending | Amount | How it is valued |
|---|---|---|
| Grants to member Clubs (both lines) | $213 million (60%), of which $140 million federal money passed through | as what Clubs do with it: member-years at the movement's cost per member |
| BGCA's own services: training, programme design, standards, the youth outcomes survey, help for new Clubs | about $138 million | credited by share of resources, at the same cost per member (a ranged judgment, 0.4 to 1.5 of a Club dollar) |
| Advocacy for public funding (inside both lines) | about $3 million (our estimate; lobbying alone was $0.6 million in 2024) | a side effect, near zero net (below) |

### How the model works

- **Members per dollar**: the movement publishes no combined accounts; from two local Clubs' accounts and the movement's staff count, it spends about $1,700 a member a year (from $950 to $2,900), outreach and BGCA's own services included. BGCA's spending over that is its share of the movement's member-years: about 206,000 in 2025. Outreach children count at 3% of a member.
- **Children** (about 90% of the result): a year's membership is taken to raise school and behaviour outcomes by about 0.09 standard deviations, the figure the United Way model uses for after-school programmes, between the null national evaluation of 21st Century Community Learning Centers and Kremer and others' meta-analysis (2015) on one side and Durlak, Weissberg and Pachan's (2010: 0.12 on grades, 0.17 on tests) on the other; each SD is worth about 10% higher adult earnings (the site's shared figure from Chetty, Friedman and Rockoff 2014), for the site's 40 working years starting about 11 years later (members' ages), discounted at the shared rate and shared with the household. Counted at 85% for publication bias, and at 70% because members come about 1.4 days a week (445,000 a day of 1.6 million), less than the studies' participants, and outcomes rise with attendance.
- **Parents** (about 10%): Clubs are after-school and summer care. Parents who pay for after-school care pay about $99 a week (America After 3PM 2020, $123 in 2025 prices); for 42 weeks at the members' attendance, and taking 30% of that price as a real gain (many families would otherwise use free care or none), a member's year is worth about $430 to the family, valued on the log scale at the families' consumption (63% of members qualify for free or reduced-price school lunch: about 0.74 of the average person's).
- **Credit**: the movement's 160,000 volunteers (at the US value of a volunteer hour) and donated school and public-housing space lower BGCA's share to about 89%; Clubs could make up part of a lost BGCA grant (90%).
- **Missing data**: both branches × the shared slider.
- **Effects not modelled** (+10%, from −5% to +30%): less crime and violence, meals, physical activity, substance-use and teen-pregnancy prevention, against members drawn from other programmes. **Advocacy** (+3%, from −2% to +10%): BGCA counts $406 million of public funding for Clubs secured in 2025; perhaps $18 million of it is its doing (4.5% of its cost), but public money costs taxpayers about 45% of what it gains the families on the log scale, so the net is small. Its cost (about $3 million) is in the total but buys no member-years.

### What drives the result

- **The missing-data slider**: from 10% to 100% the result runs from 0.006 to 0.057.
- **The effect of a Club year**: 0.02 to 0.2 SD gives 0.009 to 0.060.
- **The discount rate**: the earnings come 11 to 51 years later (0.078 at 0.1%, 0.014 at 8%).
- **The cost per member**: $950 to $2,900 gives 0.049 to 0.017.
- **The rate between healthy years and income** (your values), as for every income result.

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether a year at a Club does what after-school programmes do in studies: Clubs are drop-in, mostly recreation with homework help and prevention programmes, and the only Club-specific studies have no control group.
- What the movement spends per member: there are no combined accounts.
- What BGCA's own national services add to the Clubs' work, credited here at the movement's average.

### What isn't modelled

Crime, health and teen-pregnancy effects beyond earnings, Clubs' meals and the parents' extra earnings beyond the price of care are in the share for effects not modelled. Wellbeing isn't counted separately: the children's social-emotional gains are valued by the earnings they predict, so counting them twice is avoided. BGCA's own fundraising is overhead.

### How this model was checked

Built 2026-11-14 from the template for social services in rich countries, then audited by independent reviewers with the audit-cea checklist (rounds in audits/boys-girls-clubs-of-america-charityevals/). Round 1 (1 high, 1 medium, 5 low) relabelled the after-school effect as our judgment (in the United Way model too), restored the template's meaning of the reach rows, kept the advocacy's cost in the total, used the full functional total of expenses, added BGCA's own services to the cost per member ($1,600 to $1,700) and worked the advocacy's net value through (+2% to +3%): 0.031 to 0.029.
