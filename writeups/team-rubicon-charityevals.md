### Summary

Team Rubicon sends veteran-led volunteer crews, called Greyshirts, to disasters across the United States to clear roads and debris, muck out flooded homes, tarp roofs and cut fallen trees, and it rebuilds a few homes for families still without one years after a disaster. Its work in 2024 comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="team-rubicon-charityevals">0.001</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

The help goes to American households, and the site's one income scale values a dollar of help to them at a small fraction of what it does for a family living on a few dollars a day. And Team Rubicon spends about $140 in cash for each volunteer hour, several times what the hour's work would cost to buy.

### What the charity does

All three programmes in its audited accounts for 2024 are modelled. The accounts book $61.1 million of programme spending, but $35.7 million of that is donated goods and services, mostly advertising space valued at rate-card prices. The cash cost was $38.8 million in all.

- **Response** ($19.3 million in cash). This covers 98 operations: 62 responses, 14 recoveries, 21 mitigation operations and one humanitarian operation, including Hurricanes Helene and Milton, the California floods and flooding in Roswell, New Mexico. It also covers 902 local service projects. Greyshirts gave 284,485 hours.
- **Resilience** ($4.2 million in cash, plus $30.9 million of donated advertising). This trains and recruits the volunteers: 9,475 people were trained in 2024, as sawyers, heavy-equipment operators and in core skills such as tarping and muck-outs.
- **Long-term recovery** ($3.8 million). Team Rubicon completed 16 rebuilt homes for 26 people, ran workforce training in construction, and gave a $1 million rebuilding grant to SBP.

### How the impact happens

The good is mostly the work itself. A household that would have had to pay a contractor, wait for another group or live with a wrecked house gets it done. That is valued like money: the work's price, on the site's log-income scale at the household's own consumption. A rebuilt home is valued the same way, as housing over the years it lasts. The volunteers, more than half of them veterans and most over 55, also gain some purpose and belonging. That is counted as a side effect in WELLBYs.

### How the model works

- **Response work.** The volunteer hours spent working for the people served, valued at what the same work would cost to buy, spread over the households served (judged at 45 hours each) and valued at their consumption. The clients are taken as a little better off than America's poorest fifth: Team Rubicon targets socially vulnerable places, but most of its clients own their homes. This branch is about three-quarters of the result.
- **Rebuilt homes.** 16 homes at a judged market cost of the rebuild, without the volunteers' own labour (that is already valued in the response work), as housing over 25 years for the 26 people living in them. The $1 million grant to SBP counts as the share of SBP's homes it paid for.
- **Volunteers' wellbeing.** About 6,000 deployed volunteers, each gaining a small amount of life satisfaction for the year, discounted for people who would have volunteered elsewhere.
- **Resilience.** Training and recruiting is what makes the response work possible, so its cost counts against the response result rather than being given a result of its own.
- **Credit.** Team Rubicon's cash is about 60% of the resources behind the work. Credit is shared with the volunteers' unpaid hours (at the US value of a volunteer hour), donated goods and services, part of the donated advertising, and partners' loaned equipment and hosting.
- **Missing data.** Every branch takes the site's missing-data step. Team Rubicon counts hours and homes, but not the households it serves or what the work is worth to them.

### What drives the result

The missing-data and income-to-health sliders move it most. After them come the value of an hour's work to those served and the share of the work that others would have done for free. How poor the clients are matters less, because a small gain is close to proportional on the log scale.

### Biggest uncertainties

- What the work is worth to those served: contractors charge more per hour than the wage, but volunteer crews do less per hour.
- What others would have done anyway. Other voluntary groups (Samaritan's Purse, Southern Baptist Disaster Relief, SBP and others) and insurers do the same work, though after the 2024 hurricanes they had long waiting lists.
- How much of the donated advertising, valued at $30.9 million at rate-card prices, really is a resource behind the work.

### What isn't modelled

The model leaves out mould-related illness averted by prompt muck-outs, injuries averted by clearing roads for emergency vehicles, careers from the workforce training, and service projects for food banks, shelters and other organisations, whose clients get the work but whose hours aren't valued. It also leaves out injuries to volunteers and work taken from local contractors. These are in one share of +10%, from −5% to +30%.

Team Rubicon International LLC brought in $987,488, but the Form 990 reports under $10,000 of programme activity outside the US in 2024, so no foreign operation is modelled.

### How this model was checked

Three independent audits, kept in audits/team-rubicon-charityevals/.

- **Round 1** found one high and one medium problem. The value of an hour's work had a best guess below its own anchors: it is now their product, 1.4 times the volunteer-hour wage. The rebuild cost per home had been worked out wrong: it is now re-anchored on rebuild prices. Seven smaller fixes followed. The rebuilt homes no longer count the volunteers' labour twice. The delivery discount now applies only to the response work. Donated supplies are no longer counted twice. The donated advertising is now discounted for its rate-card valuation only.
- **Round 2** passed. Its three small points were fixed: the home value now takes out the labour share of the price, a stock-type benefit was dropped, and this write-up was brought in line.
- **A verification round** checked those last changes.
