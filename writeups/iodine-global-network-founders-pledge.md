### Summary

Iodine Global Network helps governments and salt producers add iodine to salt, so that children grow up without the iodine deficiency that lowers IQ. Founders Pledge recommends it on a recreation of GiveWell's 2014 salt iodization model, with moral weights updated in 2021: **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="iodine-global-network-founders-pledge">12</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

The model reproduces all 8 of Founders Pledge's calculated figures, including its bottom line, 2,444 units of value per $100,000.

### What the charity does

Iodine Global Network (formerly ICCIDD) is a network of experts that advises governments on salt iodization laws, works with the salt industry, and monitors iodine levels in national surveys. It doesn't iodize salt itself; governments and producers do, at about 10 cents per person a year.

### How the impact happens

1. Children who get enough iodine throughout childhood score about 4 IQ points (a quarter of a standard deviation) higher.
2. A higher IQ means higher wages as adults: about 3.6% a year, for 40 working years.
3. A year of iodized salt gives a child one fifteenth of a childhood's benefit; children under 15 are 43% of the people covered, and a national program reaches about two thirds of households.
4. Founders Pledge gives Iodine Global Network even odds of having caused the improvement at all.

### How the model works

The model follows Founders Pledge's sheet row by row: the wage gain, delayed ten years until children start work and then counted over a working life at the discount rate; adjusted for how well the trials replicate (80%) and apply here (70%); scaled by coverage, the share of children who benefit (80%) and the age mix; then divided by the cost per person. The result is in units of value: a year of doubled income for one person. We use these units directly.

### What drives the result

- **The discount rate.** The benefits come 10 to 50 years after the salt is eaten, so the rate under Your values moves this result more than any other: at 0% it roughly triples.
- **Whether Iodine Global Network makes the difference** (25% to 75%). GiveWell found no compelling evidence that iodization rose because of this kind of advocacy.
- **Cost per person** ($0.05 to $0.10) and **leverage** (whether governments spend more because of it).
- **How much of the IQ gain lasts** and **how many children benefit** in very poor settings.

### Where we differ from Founders Pledge

- **Discount rate:** our view uses the shared rate under Your values (4% by default, the same as Founders Pledge's), applied to both the ten-year wait and the working life.
- **Weight on income:** we use GiveWell's exact 1/ln 2 (1.4427) per unit of ln(income); Founders Pledge rounds it to 1.44.
- **Credit by share of resources:** when switched on, leverage is dropped (any government money drawn in gets its own share of the credit) and staff's pay below market counts as their contribution, at our default for technical-assistance work (staff about 60% of cost, paid about 30% below market). Off by default.

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether advocacy by Iodine Global Network changes iodization rates. Founders Pledge's 50% is a judgment, not an estimate from data.
- How long the earnings gain lasts. Founders Pledge uses 40 years, borrowed from GiveWell's deworming model; GiveWell's 2014 parameters used 25, within 10 to 40.
- Founders Pledge's own estimates differ: the salt iodization tab of its 2019 education sheet, with different inputs, gives 0.0078 ln(income) per dollar in its middle case, about 5.6 years of healthy life per $1,000 at our defaults, half this model's figure.
- The model's other inputs date from 2014; only the moral weights were updated in 2021.

### What isn't modelled

Health benefits other than IQ (goitre, cretinism, pregnancy outcomes) and any benefit to adults. Both would raise the result.
