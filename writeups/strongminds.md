## Summary

StrongMinds provides group interpersonal therapy for depression, mostly to women, through trained lay facilitators in Uganda and Zambia. The Happier Lives Institute (HLI) rates it a top charity at **40 WELLBYs per $1,000**, about $25 per WELLBY, roughly **5 times GiveDirectly** with our default exchange rate.

The model reproduces all 32 of HLI's intermediate figures.

## How the impact happens

HLI combines three bodies of evidence, each run through the same chain:

1. **Effect on the person treated:** an initial reduction in depression (in standard deviations), fading over time; the area under that curve, converted to WELLBYs.
2. **Adjustments:** publication bias, response bias, follow-up timing, dosage, and differences between trial settings and StrongMinds' own delivery.
3. **Household spillovers:** other household members benefit too, at about 16% of the recipient's effect each.
4. **Combining the evidence:** HLI weights the meta-analytic prior (64%), the Baird et al. trial of StrongMinds (20%) and StrongMinds' monitoring data (16%).
5. **Cost:** about $41 per person treated, adjusted by HLI.

## Biggest uncertainties

- The Baird et al. trial found a much smaller effect than the prior and the monitoring data; how much weight it gets drives the result.
- HLI notes StrongMinds expects its cost per person to fall, which would raise the result; that projection isn't in this model.

## Comparing with other charities

HLI measures impact in WELLBYs: one point of life satisfaction on a 0–10 scale, for one person, for one year. To put it on the same scale as GiveWell's charities we use one exchange rate, calibrated so that GiveDirectly scores the same in HLI's units and in GiveWell's (about 1.15 GiveWell units of value per WELLBY). That calibration is contested, so it is a slider: at its low end these charities fall to around 2× GiveDirectly, at its high end they rise above 14×.

## Where we differ from HLI

Nothing yet. HLI publishes no ranges in this sheet, so the tornado chart shows only the exchange rate; the "100% judgment" flag reflects that gap. We haven't estimated a salary gap for this charity.
