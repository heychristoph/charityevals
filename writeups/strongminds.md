### Summary

StrongMinds provides group interpersonal therapy for depression, mostly to women, through trained lay facilitators in Uganda and Zambia. The Happier Lives Institute (HLI) rates it a top charity, at about $25 per point of life satisfaction for a year: **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="strongminds">2.5</span> years of healthy life per $1,000** with our default exchange rate. Founders Pledge's analysis of the same therapy gives <span data-figure="headline" data-model="strongminds-founders-pledge">4.9</span> and SoGive's <span data-figure="headline" data-model="strongminds-sogive-march-2024">0.57</span>.

The model reproduces all 32 of HLI's intermediate figures.

### How the impact happens

HLI combines three bodies of evidence, each run through the same chain:

1. **Effect on the person treated:** an initial reduction in depression (in standard deviations), fading over time; the area under that curve, converted to WELLBYs.
2. **Adjustments:** publication bias, response bias, follow-up timing, dosage, and differences between trial settings and StrongMinds' own delivery.
3. **Household spillovers:** other household members benefit too, at about 16% of the recipient's effect each.
4. **Combining the evidence:** HLI weights the meta-analytic prior (64%), the Baird et al. trial of StrongMinds (20%) and StrongMinds' monitoring data (16%).
5. **Cost:** about $41 per person treated, adjusted by HLI.

### Biggest uncertainties

- The Baird et al. trial found a much smaller effect than the prior and the monitoring data; how much weight it gets drives the result.
- HLI notes StrongMinds expects its cost per person to fall, which would raise the result; that projection isn't in this model.

### Comparing with other charities

HLI measures impact in WELLBYs: one point of life satisfaction on a 0–10 scale, for one person, for one year. To put it on the same scale as everything else we use one exchange rate for every HLI model, "1 point more life satisfaction for a year vs doubled-income year" under Your values. Its default is set so that depression counts the same whoever measured it: HLI counts one standard deviation of depression for a year as 2 WELLBYs, and the site counts the same fall in depression, about 4.2 points on the PHQ-9 questionnaire, as 0.13 years of healthy life by the Global Burden of Disease weights, which Founders Pledge's and SoGive's analyses also go through. That makes a WELLBY worth about 0.13 doubled-income years.

At that rate HLI's therapy charities come out near Founders Pledge's analysis of the same therapy. HLI itself values them against GiveDirectly's cash transfers, which implies a rate about 9 times higher (0.575 healthy years per WELLBY); at that rate this charity would come out about 9 times higher, and the evaluator view shows it that way. The slider runs from 0.025 to 1.5, so either view is a click away.

### Where we differ from HLI

- **Exchange rate (changed for consistency):** WELLBYs are converted at the rate that makes a fall in depression count the same as in the site's other depression models (above), 0.065 healthy years per WELLBY, not at a rate that matches HLI's view of cash transfers (0.575 healthy years per WELLBY, the evaluator view). This is the step that moves the result most: about 9 times.
- **Discounting (ours):** benefits that come later count less, at the discount rate under Your values; HLI doesn't discount (0 in the evaluator view). Therapy's effect fades within a few years, so this changes little.
- **Staff pay and credit by share of resources (ours):** with credit by share of resources on, staff's pay below market counts as part of the cost. Off by default.
- HLI's household figures (4.7 people, 16% spillover each) are now the site's shared ones for psychotherapy, which Founders Pledge's and SoGive's models of StrongMinds read too; HLI's numbers are unchanged.
- HLI publishes no ranges in this sheet, so the tornado chart shows mainly the exchange rate; the "100% judgment" flag reflects that gap.
