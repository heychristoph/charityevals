### Summary

Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF, Doctors Without Borders) runs hospitals, clinics, feeding centres, vaccination campaigns and outbreak responses in about 70 countries, mostly in war zones and epidemics. Counting what its whole 2025 medical budget did, service by service, we estimate **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="doctors-without-borders-charityevals">0.51</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

That is well below the site's top health charities, which buy one cheap intervention against one big killer. MSF publishes how many patients it treats, but not how many would have died without it, nor how many would have been treated by someone else, so every branch rests on case-fatality studies and our judgment and is counted at half by the site's shared missing-data slider (at full weight the result would be about 1.0). MSF's other work has two smaller models of its own: relief distributions and search and rescue at sea ([relief](/doctors-without-borders/doctors-without-borders-charityevals-relief), <span data-figure="headline" data-model="doctors-without-borders-charityevals-relief">0.70</span>), and awareness-raising, MSF Access and its grant to DNDi ([advocacy and research](/doctors-without-borders/doctors-without-borders-charityevals-advocacy), <span data-figure="headline" data-model="doctors-without-borders-charityevals-advocacy">0.094</span>).

### What the charity does

In 2025 MSF's 51 entities together spent 2.5 billion euros, 79% of it on its social mission: 1.58 billion on field programmes (the DRC, Sudan, Palestine, South Sudan, Nigeria, Yemen, Chad, CAR, Afghanistan and Niger the largest), 316 million on programme support, 63 million on awareness-raising and MSF Access. 98% of its income is private. Its teams gave 17 million outpatient consultations, treated 3.6 million malaria cases, admitted 1.8 million inpatients, vaccinated 2.1 million people in measles outbreaks, treated 829,000 malnourished children, 149,000 cholera patients, 30,000 new TB patients and 39,000 people on HIV treatment, assisted 405,000 births and performed 146,500 operations.

### How the impact happens

Most of the good is deaths averted: a child with severe malaria or complicated malnutrition, a cholera patient, a mother with an obstructed labour, a wounded man needing surgery would often die without care. The rest is illness averted (shorter, milder disease, disability averted by surgery, mental-health care and care after sexual violence).

### How the model works

Each service MSF counts is a branch: patients × the chance they would have died without care × the share of that risk MSF's care removes × the share who wouldn't have been treated elsewhere. The risks come from case-fatality studies (malaria: the Lives Saved Tool's review; TB: pre-treatment cohorts; measles: Portnoy et al.; cholera: WHO; malnutrition: the site's figure for the excess mortality of untreated severe wasting), each a ranged judgment where no study fits. Malaria, measles and cholera also carry the indirect deaths GiveWell counts per direct death of a young child (the site's figures). Overlaps are taken out (malaria cases and other branches' follow-up visits, about 35%, from consultations; malnutrition, birth, cholera and surgery admissions from all admissions; children moved from inpatient to outpatient feeding from the outpatient branch; caesareans from surgery). Every branch then has its own counterfactual and missing-data marker and shares the same other adjustments: trust in the evidence (0.86), MSF's share of the resources where it supports government facilities or works remotely (85%), how faithfully episode counts describe care (90%), and the wait until care (a gift waits about as long as MSF's six months of liquid reserves). Deaths are valued at the site's weights by age band, the age splits of malaria, measles, TB, HIV and diarrhoea deaths from GBD 2023 for sub-Saharan Africa. All branches are counted against the whole medical budget, with fundraising and administration spread over it, since MSF doesn't report costs by service.

Four kinds of work MSF does but doesn't count are branches built from the research literature, with our estimates of their reach: seasonal malaria chemoprevention (300,000 children, the site's shared SMC effect from Cissé et al.'s trial, measured in the population offered it), outbreak vaccination against meningitis, cholera and yellow fever (a million doses, the share of cases a late campaign averts from MSF's Katsina meningitis campaign and Delport et al.'s 210 outbreaks), drug-resistant TB (3,000 patients) and water and sanitation in camps (1.2 million people, the mortality effect from Sharma Waddington et al.'s meta-analysis). Together they add about 1% of the value: they are small next to the treatment branches.

**Missing data.** Every branch carries the shared missing-data marker. MSF counts who it treated, but no branch's effect is measured for MSF's own care against a comparison (its own controlled evaluation, or trials of the same care delivered the same way), and the four literature branches' reach is our judgment. So all of them are "thin" and read the slider (50% at the default).

### What drives the result

- **The missing-data slider** moves the whole model: from 0.10 at 10% to 1.02 at full weight.
- **Severe malnutrition and malaria**: about two-thirds of the deaths averted. How often a child with malaria would have died untreated is the biggest single question (it moves the result from about 0.42 to 0.83).
- **The counterfactuals**: on average just over half of MSF's patients are counted as people nobody else would have treated.
- **MSF's share of the resources** where it supports government facilities (60-100%: 0.36 to 0.60).
- The shared sliders (healthy year vs income, value of a life, discount rate) move it as they move every health model.

### Biggest uncertainties

- No trial measures MSF's care against no care; every risk is a case-fatality figure or our judgment, ranged widely.
- MSF reports outputs for the movement as a whole; the split between conflict trauma care, epidemics and routine care in neglected places isn't costed, so a dollar's value is the average of all of them.
- The general branches (other admissions, other consultations) are the least evidenced and together carry about a seventh of the deaths averted.

### What isn't modelled

Long-term income effects are not counted (no evidence for treatment of these episodes). A small supplementary share (+2%) covers what has no branch: development of children who survive severe malnutrition, measles immunity after the outbreak, smaller services with no count (kala-azar, snakebite, non-communicable disease and palliative care), less the harms of double counting and interrupted care. MSF's independence and witnessing are valued in its advocacy and research model.

### How this model was checked

Built with charityevals' direct-health template on 3 November 2026 and rebuilt on 4 November under the new rules for missing data: the earlier proxy for awareness-raising, MSF Access and the DNDi grant (valued at other site models' results) was retired and those programmes moved to a model of their own built from the literature; the +9% supplementary share for uncounted preventive work became the four literature branches above; and every branch got its missing-data marker. The rebuild also closed what the first build's last audit left open: mental-health care is no longer discounted twice for care elsewhere (its effect is already net of trials' control arms) and now counts the household spillover of therapy at the site's shared figure; outpatient consultations exclude other branches' follow-up visits. The rebuild's independent audit (round 1: no high, 1 medium, 9 low) led to: each branch now has its own total adjustment, so the canonical total reads like other models' (the shared part alone was shown before); follow-up visits are 35% of consultations, not 10%; children moved from inpatient to outpatient feeding are no longer counted twice; SMC's trial effect is no longer cut again for missed doses; the mental-health effect is sourced (WHO's Problem Management Plus trials) and discounted over its year; the beneficiaries are weighted by MSF's spending in its ten largest countries. Round 2 (no high, no medium, 6 low): the prevention branches now count GiveWell's share of treatment costs they save; the overhead ratio leaves out the goods sold to other organisations in all three MSF models; the relief figures and the goods sold that several MSF models read are one shared MSF fact; citations and an explainer corrected. Left out: the prevention branches' later income (under 0.5%). The first build's independent audits (three rounds: 1 high, 3 medium, 6 low; 1 high, 1 medium, 6 low; no high, 1 medium, 6 low) had added indirect deaths, the GBD age splits, the site's depression conversion and corrected overlaps.
