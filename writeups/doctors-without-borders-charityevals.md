### Summary

Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF, Doctors Without Borders) runs hospitals, clinics, feeding centres, vaccination campaigns and outbreak responses in about 70 countries, mostly in war zones and epidemics. Counting what its whole 2025 medical budget did, service by service, we estimate **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="doctors-without-borders-charityevals">0.84</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**.

MSF publishes how many patients it treats, but not how many would have died without it, nor how many would have been treated by someone else, so most branches rest on case-fatality studies and our judgment and are counted at half by the site's shared missing-data slider (at full weight the result would be about 1.3); treating malaria, TB and hepatitis C counts in full, since MSF counts those patients and the treatments' effects come from trials of the same drugs. MSF's other work has two smaller models of its own: relief distributions and search and rescue at sea ([relief](/doctors-without-borders/doctors-without-borders-charityevals-relief), <span data-figure="headline" data-model="doctors-without-borders-charityevals-relief">0.70</span>), and awareness-raising, MSF Access and its grant to DNDi ([advocacy and research](/doctors-without-borders/doctors-without-borders-charityevals-advocacy), <span data-figure="headline" data-model="doctors-without-borders-charityevals-advocacy">0.064</span>).

### What the charity does

In 2025 MSF's 51 entities together spent 2.5 billion euros, 79% of it on its social mission: 1.58 billion on field programmes (the DRC, Sudan, Palestine, South Sudan, Nigeria, Yemen, Chad, CAR, Afghanistan and Niger the largest), 316 million on programme support, 63 million on awareness-raising and MSF Access. 98% of its income is private. Its teams gave 17 million outpatient consultations, treated 3.6 million malaria cases, admitted 1.8 million inpatients, vaccinated 2.1 million people in measles outbreaks, treated 829,000 malnourished children, 149,000 cholera patients, 30,000 new TB patients and 39,000 people on HIV treatment, assisted 405,000 births and performed 146,500 operations.

### How the impact happens

Most of the good is deaths averted: a child with severe malaria or complicated malnutrition, a cholera patient, a mother with an obstructed labour, a wounded man needing surgery would often die without care. The rest is illness averted (shorter, milder disease, disability averted by surgery, mental-health care and care after sexual violence).

### How the model works

Each service MSF counts is a branch: patients × the chance they would have died without care × the share of that risk MSF's care removes × the share who wouldn't have been treated elsewhere. The risks come from case-fatality studies (malaria: the Lives Saved Tool's expert survey, times the share of the fevers counted that are really malaria; TB: pre-treatment cohorts; measles: Portnoy et al.; cholera: WHO; complicated malnutrition: fatality before the WHO protocols, against MSF's own feeding centres' 6%), each a ranged judgment where no study fits. Malaria, measles and cholera also carry the indirect deaths GiveWell counts per direct death of a young child (the site's figures). Overlaps are taken out (malaria cases and other branches' follow-up visits and the mental-health and sexual-violence consultations, about 40%, from consultations; malnutrition, birth, cholera and surgery admissions from all admissions; children moved from inpatient to outpatient feeding from the outpatient branch; caesareans from surgery). Every branch then has its own counterfactual and missing-data marker and shares the same other adjustments: trust in the evidence (0.86), MSF's share of the resources (85%: about half the care is in facilities MSF supports rather than runs, where others provide about 30% of the resources), how faithfully episode counts describe care (90%), and the wait until care (a gift waits about as long as MSF's six months of liquid reserves). Deaths are valued at the site's weights by age band, the age splits of malaria, measles, TB, HIV and diarrhoea deaths from GBD 2023 for sub-Saharan Africa. All branches are counted against the whole medical budget, with fundraising and administration spread over it, since MSF doesn't report costs by service.

Four kinds of work MSF does but doesn't count are branches built from the research literature, with our estimates of their reach: seasonal malaria chemoprevention (300,000 children, the site's shared SMC effect from Cissé et al.'s trial, measured in the population offered it), outbreak vaccination against meningitis, cholera and yellow fever (a million doses, the share of cases a late campaign averts from MSF's Katsina meningitis campaign and Delport et al.'s 210 outbreaks), drug-resistant TB (3,000 patients) and water and sanitation in camps (1.2 million people, the mortality effect from Sharma Waddington et al.'s meta-analysis). Together they add about 1% of the value: they are small next to the treatment branches.

**Missing data.** Every branch carries the shared missing-data marker. Three are measured and count in full: MSF counts the patients it treated for malaria, TB and hepatitis C, and the drugs are the ones the trials measure (artemisinin combinations, the standard TB regimen, direct-acting antivirals); the untreated risk is only the baseline. The others are "thin" and read the slider (50% at the default): their effect is a judgment with no trial of the same care (HIV treatment against none, malnutrition feeding, births, cholera, outbreak vaccination, emergency and general care, surgery, mental health), or their reach is our judgment (the four literature branches).

### What drives the result

- **The missing-data slider** moves most of the model: from about 0.45 at 10% to 1.32 at full weight.
- **Severe malnutrition and malaria**: about three-quarters of the deaths averted. How many of the malaria cases counted are really malaria rather than another fever in a child carrying parasites is the biggest single question (20-80%: 0.68 to 1.06); how often a child with complicated malnutrition would die untreated is next (20-50%: 0.72 to 1.09).
- **The counterfactuals**: on average about three-fifths of MSF's patients are counted as people nobody else would have treated.
- **MSF's share of the resources**, built from two judgments with no reference class: the share of care in facilities MSF supports (25-80%: 0.91 to 0.75) and others' share of the resources there (15-50%: 0.91 to 0.74).
- The shared sliders (healthy year vs income, value of a life, discount rate) move it as they move every health model.

### Biggest uncertainties

- No trial measures MSF's care against no care; every risk is a case-fatality figure or our judgment, ranged widely.
- MSF reports outputs for the movement as a whole; the split between conflict trauma care, epidemics and routine care in neglected places isn't costed, so a dollar's value is the average of all of them.
- The general branches (other admissions, other consultations) are the least evidenced and together carry about a twelfth of the deaths averted.

### What isn't modelled

Long-term income effects of treatment are not counted (no evidence for treatment of these episodes); seasonal malaria chemoprevention's are, at GiveWell's shared ratio. A small supplementary share (+2%) covers what has no branch: development of children who survive severe malnutrition, measles immunity after the outbreak, smaller services with no count (kala-azar, snakebite, non-communicable disease and palliative care), less the harms of double counting and interrupted care. MSF's independence and witnessing are valued in its advocacy and research model.

### How this model was checked

Built with charityevals' direct-health template on 3 November 2026 and rebuilt on 4 November under the site's rules for missing data, after three independent audit rounds on the first build and two on the rebuild. A full independent audit on 15 November 2026 (no high, 5 medium, 8 low) led to: malaria's untreated risk built from the expert survey and the share of counted cases that are malaria; complicated malnutrition's untreated risk raised to 30% and its reduction taken from MSF's own centres' death rate; HIV treatment's effect a judgment (the trial compares early with deferred treatment), so the branch reads the missing-data slider; MSF's share of the resources built from two judgments; seasonal malaria chemoprevention's rebound, later income and illness at the site's malaria-prevention figures; suicides averted by mental-health care counted; mental-health and sexual-violence consultations taken out of general consultations. Together they raised the result from 0.69 to 0.84.
