### Summary

Epoch AI tracks and forecasts how AI is developing, and publishes data, benchmarks and research on it. Our model of its work in 2025 comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per $1,000**. That figure rests on forecasts of how likely an AI catastrophe is and on an estimate of what a typical dollar of AI safety work does to that risk. Both are deeply uncertain, so on the model's own ranges the result runs from about 0.001 to about 0.9.

### What the charity does

Epoch began in 2022 as a project of Rethink Priorities and became a charity of its own (Epoch Artificial Intelligence Inc) in early 2025. Its impact report says it spent $5 million in 2025 with 21 full-time staff. No audited statement or tax return for 2025 is public yet.

- **Data.** Public databases of AI models, GPU clusters, frontier data centres, AI companies and chip sales.
- **Benchmarks and evaluations.** FrontierMath (its hardest tier commissioned by OpenAI), the Benchmarking Hub and the Epoch Capabilities Index, which combines many benchmarks into one score.
- **Research and writing.** 14 reports and papers, 38 short data pieces and 40 issues of its newsletter in 2025.
- **Paid work.** 13 commissioned projects and consultations, for Google DeepMind, xAI, METR, investors (Sequoia Capital, Bridgewater Associates), an energy research institute and the UK and EU AI offices.

Most of its money comes from Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy). Epoch says it is neutral on whether faster AI is good for society and makes no policy recommendations.

### How the impact happens

AI carries a yearly chance of catastrophe, spread evenly to 2100. By default there is a 3% chance by 2100 that AI ends humanity's future; this is a slider. The forecasting tournament's experts expect three AI catastrophes the world survives for every one that ends it. A catastrophe the world survives is taken to kill a quarter of people.

Epoch's spending cuts that yearly risk by the share a typical AI project does per dollar, from Rethink Priorities' cross-cause model, its chance of backfiring included. The cut counts for the years such progress would otherwise have taken to come (5 by default), and each year is discounted. Deaths are valued like every death on the site; the future lost counts only through the discount rate.

Epoch's work matters here when governments, labs and funders act on better information about AI: data on compute, chips and data centres, capability measures that show how fast dangerous abilities arrive, and forecasts that the field plans around.

### How the model works

All of Epoch's spending is AI work. The model's pandemic, nuclear, policy-win and training branches are zero: it campaigns for no measure and trains no one.

| Adjustment | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Share of the work bearing on catastrophic risk | 0.6 | Compute, chip and data-centre data, capability benchmarks and trend research count; the economics, power-demand and investor work doesn't |
| That work's uptake against a typical AI project | 0.8 | Widely cited by governments and central banks, but aimed at understanding AI, not at reducing its risks |
| Work no one else would have done now | 0.7 | The AI Index, OECD.AI and commercial analysts also publish trend data, but Epoch's databases are the main free source |
| Epoch's share of the resources behind its work | 0.72 | Clients' fees for commissioned work (about $1 million) are their share; the rest is mostly its own staff compiling public information, and labs give model access |
| Funging by its main funders | 1 | Counted once, in the next-dollar figure |
| Missing data | slider | Nothing about Epoch's effect on the risk is measured |

About 1.7% of spending, under 3% of the counted work, is work against misuse: tracking biological AI tools, funded by Sentinel Bio. The rest counts as work against AI escaping control.

Side effects: other benefits add 10% (its economics and energy work informs central banks and planners). Speeding up AI subtracts 9%: about 30% of the value of the work it concerns, which excludes the biological tracking. Its benchmarks give labs targets to race on, its paid work for AI companies and investors can pull money into AI, and in April 2025 a co-founder and three staff left to found Mechanize, a start-up that aims to automate all work. Information hazards subtract nothing: it publishes no threat analyses.

Epoch raised $10.3 million in 2025 and spent $5 million. Since then Coefficient Giving has given it $22.7 million of general support and more for a data-centre tracker and compute. Money is not its main constraint, so the next dollar counts at 23% of the past average.

### What drives the result

Only part of Epoch's work bears on catastrophic risk, it aims to inform rather than to reduce the risk, and its speed-up harm is among the family's largest. The small misuse share still gives about two thirds of the result: for catastrophes the world survives, Rethink's typical misuse project cuts deaths far more per dollar than its misalignment one. Deaths of people alive now are 95% of the value, the future lost 5%.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How likely an AI catastrophe is.** The slider runs from 0.38% (the superforecasters) to 20%; the result moves from 0.009 to 0.47.
- **How much a typical dollar cuts the risk.** Rethink's figures run ten times either way; the misuse figure alone moves the result from 0.026 to 0.51.
- **How much of the work counts as misuse work.** At none the result is 0.021; at 8% of spending it is 0.25.
- **Whether Epoch's information makes AI safer or faster.** The speed-up harm runs from −1.5% to −18%, and the uptake from 0.3 to 1.2 of a typical project.
- **The discount rate and the years a cut counts.** At a 0.1% discount rate the result is about 0.49.

### What isn't modelled

- Epoch's work before 2025, inside Rethink Priorities, and its 2026 work are not valued here.
- Losses beyond deaths, such as survivors' lost income and health, are a shared figure set to 0.
- The forecasts are spread evenly over the century, though forecasters put much of the AI risk later.

### How this model was checked

The model was built from the catastrophic-risk template, with METR's and the Centre for the Governance of AI's models as worked examples. Round 1 of the independent audit found one high finding and three medium ones, all fixed: clients' fees for commissioned work are now credited to the clients, the misuse share is taken over the work counted, the speed-up harm applies only to the part of the value it concerns, and a source link was corrected. Round 2 passed. Its medium finding, that fees paid across 2024 and 2025 were undercounted, was fixed, lowering the result by about a tenth, and a verification round checked the changes. Independent audits are in audits/epoch-ai-charityevals/.
