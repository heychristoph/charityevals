### Summary

GAIN helps governments and salt producers iodize salt, so that children grow up without the iodine deficiency that lowers IQ. GiveWell's salt iodization analysis, built in 2014 for GAIN and the Iodine Global Network and updated with its 2021 moral weights, gives **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="global-alliance-for-improved-nutrition-givewell">6.5</span> years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars** in our view (GiveWell's own figure: about 7 times cash transfers).

### How the model works

A year of iodized salt costs about 10 cents per person. Children who grow up with it gain about a quarter of a standard deviation in IQ, worth higher wages for 40 working years once they start earning, discounted. GiveWell then allows for coverage short of the whole population, fade-out, children who don't benefit, the trials' validity, and a 50% chance that GAIN's or the Iodine Global Network's work makes a difference at all.

### Where we differ from GiveWell

- **Wages per IQ point**: the site's one figure, GiveWell's current 0.67% per point, where the 2014 sheet takes about 1% (wage gain 2.5% against GiveWell's 3.6%).
- **Household sharing**: the site counts a long-term income gain as shared with the household (GiveWell's current multiplier, 2); the 2014 sheet counts the earner only.
- **Value of income and discounting**: the site's shared sliders (GiveWell's 1.44 per unit of log income and 4% at the defaults).
- **Credit by share of resources**: when on, leverage is 1 and staff's forgone pay counts in the cost.
- **Missing data**: the cost per person is a generic figure for iodization plants and GAIN's part is a guessed chance, so the shared missing-data step applies.

### Biggest uncertainties

GiveWell had little information on what GAIN's work changed: its 50% chance of impact, the IQ gain, and how much of it lasts drive the result. The analysis is over five years old, so it is shown on GAIN's page but not ranked.
