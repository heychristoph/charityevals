### Summary

Shrimp Welfare Project (SWP) persuades shrimp producers to stun shrimp electrically before slaughter, and works with farmers in India on welfare improvements. ACE's 2025 analysis of its two programs works out to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="shrimp-welfare-project">64</span> years of healthy life per $1,000** you give, after our salary-sacrifice adjustment. That uses our default exchange rates between suffering and healthy life, which is where most of the uncertainty is.

The model reproduces all 24 of ACE's intermediate figures.

### How the impact happens

1. **Humane Slaughter Initiative:** producers commit to installing stunners; ACE counts 9 commitments in 2024 covering about 1.7 billion shrimp a year.
2. Stunners are likely (ACE: 90%) to be deployed and used for about ten years.
3. Each stunned shrimp avoids a small amount of suffering (about 0.0045 SADs), which adds up at this scale.
4. **Sustainable Shrimp Farmers of India:** farm-level welfare improvements, modelled separately.

ACE lists retailer commitments (Tesco and others) as secondary effects it didn't add to the total.

### What drives the result

Beyond the exchange rates, SADs per stunned shrimp and the likelihood stunners are deployed. Shrimp sentience and welfare ranges are especially uncertain; they sit inside Ambitious Impact's SADs estimates.

### Where we differ from ACE

- **Salary sacrifice (on by default).** We guess staff are 65% of program cost and earn 35% less than in comparable for-profit jobs, so the forgone pay is an in-kind donation of about 35% of cash spending. That leaves each donated dollar about 74% of the credit ACE gives it. Both numbers are guesses with wide ranges; replace them with the charity's real figures when we have them.
- **Credit for shared outcomes.** ACE's discounts for other organisations' contributions are built into its inputs here, so the full-credit switch doesn't change this model yet.
