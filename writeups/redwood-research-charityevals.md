### Summary

Redwood Research studies AI systems that might work against the people who build them. It started the field of AI control: ways to use a powerful model safely even if it is secretly trying to get around its monitors. Our model of its work in 2024 comes to **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="redwood-research-charityevals">0.26</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**. That figure rests on forecasts of how likely an AI catastrophe is and on an estimate of what a typical dollar of AI safety work does to that risk. Both are deeply uncertain, so the plausible range runs from about 0.006 to about 12.

### What the charity does

Redwood spent $2.9 million in 2024, according to its Form 990 (its statements aren't audited). Its one programme, AI research, cost $2.6 million:

- **Its own research: $2.0 million.** Six employees, contractors and computing. In 2024 it showed, with Anthropic, that a production model (Claude 3 Opus) sometimes fakes alignment during training. It also published work on control protocols for untrusted models and on eliciting capabilities a model hides, and drafted a control safety case with the UK AI Security Institute. It advises AI companies, among them Google DeepMind and Anthropic.
- **A grant to Constellation: $0.7 million.** Redwood ran Constellation, a Berkeley AI safety research centre, until it became a charity of its own. In 2024 Redwood passed on $250,000 it held for the centre and the office's fit-out ($430,000).

### How the impact happens

AI carries a yearly chance of catastrophe, spread evenly to 2100. By default there is a 3% chance by 2100 that AI ends humanity's future; this is a slider. The forecasting tournament's experts expect three AI catastrophes the world survives for every one that ends it. A catastrophe the world survives is taken to kill a quarter of people.

Redwood's spending cuts that yearly risk by the share a typical AI project does per dollar. That share comes from Rethink Priorities' cross-cause model, including its chance that a project backfires. Rethink has one typical project for work against misuse and one for work against AI that doesn't do what its makers intend. The cut counts for the years such progress would otherwise have taken to come (5 by default), and each year is discounted.

The deaths averted are valued like every death on the site, across the world's age groups. A catastrophe that ends humanity's future also takes the lives of everyone not yet born. Those future lives count only through the discount rate.

### How the model works

All of Redwood's spending is on AI. The model's pandemic and nuclear areas are set to zero, and so are its policy-win and training branches. No law or standard was won in 2024, and Redwood's training bootcamps ended in 2023.

Nearly all the work is against misaligned AI. Only the paper on hidden capabilities also helps tests for misuse, so 5% of the spending counts as misuse work.

| Adjustment | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| How far a typical project's effect applies to Redwood's work | 1.05 | Research 1.2 (labs and a government institute take up its methods), the Constellation grant 0.6 (it supports others' work), weighted by spending |
| Work no one else would have done now | 0.7 | Redwood started AI control; lab teams and the UK institute now work on it too |
| Redwood's share of the resources behind its work | 0.70 | Most of the alignment-faking authors and its compute were Anthropic's |
| Funging by its main funder | 0.8 | Almost all its money comes from Coefficient Giving, which fills gaps |
| Missing data | slider | Nothing about Redwood's effect on the risk is measured |

Side effects: other benefits add 10% (control also guards against smaller harms; its researchers mentor outside scholars). Two harms subtract 5% and 2%. Control could let labs deploy more capable models sooner. Published attack strategies and alignment-faking demonstrations may end up in future models' training data.

Redwood received under $10,000 in 2024 and spent from its reserves. It ended the year with net assets of $6.5 million, over two years of spending. So the next dollar counts at 33% of the past average.

### What drives the result

The result is about a third of FAR.AI's and four times MIRI's. The gap with FAR.AI is mostly the share of work against misuse: 5% here against FAR.AI's 28%. For catastrophes the world survives, Rethink's typical misuse project cuts deaths far more per dollar than its typical misalignment project. That same 5% is most of the gap with MIRI: with no misuse work the result would be about 0.05, close to MIRI's.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How likely an AI catastrophe is.** The slider runs from 0.38% (the superforecasters) to 20%. Across that range the result moves from 0.03 to 1.7.
- **How much a dollar of misuse work cuts the risk.** Rethink's figures are placeholders, and the range runs ten times either way: from 0.07 to 2.1.
- **How much of the work is against misuse.** Redwood publishes no split. From 0 to 15% moves the result from 0.05 to 0.68.
- **The years a cut counts and the discount rate.** These are shared sliders.

### What isn't modelled

- Redwood's work before and after 2024 is not valued here. That includes AI control's first paper (December 2023) and the Ctrl-Z and BashArena results of 2025.
- Losses beyond deaths, such as survivors' lost income and health, are a shared figure set to 0.
- The forecasts are spread evenly over the century, though forecasters put much of the AI risk later.

### How this model was checked

The model was built from the catastrophic-risk template, with FAR.AI's model as the worked example. Independent audits are in audits/redwood-research-charityevals/.
