### Summary

Redwood Research studies AI systems that might work against the people who build them. It started the field of AI control: ways to use a powerful model safely even if it is secretly trying to get around its monitors. Our model of its work in 2024 comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per $1,000**. That figure rests on forecasts of how likely an AI catastrophe is and on an estimate of what a typical dollar of AI safety work does to that risk. Both are deeply uncertain, so the plausible range runs from {{low}} to {{high}}.

### What the charity does

Redwood spent $2.9 million in 2024, according to its Form 990 (its statements aren't audited). Its one programme, AI research, cost $2.6 million:

- **Its own research: $2.0 million.** Six employees, contractors and computing. In 2024 it showed, with Anthropic, that a production model (Claude 3 Opus) sometimes fakes alignment during training. It also published work on control protocols for untrusted models and on eliciting capabilities a model hides, and drafted a control safety case with the UK AI Security Institute. It advises AI companies, among them Google DeepMind and Anthropic.
- **Transfers to Constellation: $0.7 million.** Redwood ran Constellation, a Berkeley AI safety research centre, until it became a charity of its own. In 2024 Redwood passed on $250,000 it held for the centre and the office's fit-out ($430,000). The fit-out was already built and in use, so it counts for little.

### How the impact happens

AI carries a yearly chance of catastrophe, spread evenly to 2100. By default there is a 3% chance by 2100 that AI ends humanity's future; this is a slider. The forecasting tournament's experts expect three AI catastrophes the world survives for every one that ends it. A catastrophe the world survives is taken to kill a quarter of people.

Redwood's spending cuts that yearly risk by the share a typical AI project does per dollar. That share comes from Rethink Priorities' cross-cause model, including its chance that a project backfires. Rethink has one typical project for work against misuse and one for work against AI that doesn't do what its makers intend. The cut counts for the years such progress would otherwise have taken to come (5 by default), and each year is discounted.

The deaths averted are valued like every death on the site, across the world's age groups. A catastrophe that ends humanity's future also takes the lives of everyone not yet born. Those future lives count only through the discount rate.

### How the model works

All of Redwood's spending is on AI. The model's pandemic and nuclear areas are set to zero, and so are its policy-win and training branches. No law or standard was won in 2024, and Redwood's training bootcamps ended in 2023.

All the work counts as work against misaligned AI. Only the paper on hidden capabilities also helps tests for misuse, and work aimed at both counts as misalignment.

Side effects: other benefits add 10% (control also guards against smaller harms; its researchers mentor outside scholars). Two harms subtract 5% and 2%. Control could let labs deploy more capable models sooner. Published attack strategies and alignment-faking demonstrations may end up in future models' training data.

Redwood received under $10,000 in 2024 and spent from its reserves. It ended the year with net assets of $6.5 million, over two years of spending. The transfers to Constellation won't recur, so the next dollar buys only research. So the next dollar counts at 40% of the past average.

### What drives the result

All of Redwood's work is against misaligned AI. For catastrophes the world survives, Rethink's typical misalignment project cuts deaths far less per dollar than its typical misuse project. Here, deaths of people alive now carry most of the value.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **How likely an AI catastrophe is.** The setting's range runs from 0.38% (the superforecasters) to 50%. Across it the result moves from 0.0026 to 0.34.
- **How much a dollar of misalignment work cuts the risk.** Rethink's figures are placeholders, and the range runs ten times either way: from 0.0089 to 0.29.
- **Whether any of the work counts as misuse work.** If the paper on hidden capabilities counted as misuse work (about 15% of the research), the result would be 0.45.
- **The discount rate and the years a cut counts.** These are shared sliders. At a 0.1% discount rate the result is about 0.71.

### What isn't modelled

- Redwood's work before and after 2024 is not valued here. That includes AI control's first paper (December 2023) and the Ctrl-Z and BashArena results of 2025.
- Losses beyond deaths, such as survivors' lost income and health, are a shared figure set to 0.
- The forecasts are spread evenly over the century, though forecasters put much of the AI risk later.

### How this model was checked

The model was built from the catastrophic-risk template. Round 1 of the independent audit found that 5% of the work had been counted as misuse work, from a paper that serves misuse and misalignment tests alike. By the template's rule such work counts as misalignment, so the share is now 0. That lowered the result from 0.26 to about 0.05. The round also split the transfers to Constellation: the office fit-out, already in use, now counts at a sixth of the cash. Round 2 passed with two medium findings, both fixed: the next dollar buys only research now the spin-off is done, raising it from 33% to 40%, and the high end of the misuse share is now set by the paper's likely share of the research. Credit is now weighted part by part, lowering the result by 5%. A verification round checked the changes. The funging adjustment was set to 1 in November 2026 because the next-dollar figure already asks whether money is the constraint. Independent audits are in audits/redwood-research-charityevals/.
