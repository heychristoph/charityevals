### Summary

Educational Initiatives makes Mindspark, software that lets schoolchildren in India practise maths and language at their own level. Founders Pledge's 2019 education report values it through the extra earnings better-taught children go on to make: **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="educational-initiatives-founders-pledge">3.8</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**. The figure is mostly uncertainty: across Founders Pledge's own pessimistic and optimistic cases it spans more than a thousandfold.

The model reproduces all 10 of Founders Pledge's calculated figures for Educational Initiatives, including its bottom line: an increase in ln(income) of 0.0013 per dollar, or about $520 to double one person's income for a year (Founders Pledge's middle case).

### What the charity does

Mindspark adapts each exercise to what the child already knows, in after-school centres and in government schools.

### How the impact happens

1. Test scores rise by about 0.2 standard deviations per child (0.1 to 0.3 across Founders Pledge's cases), based on midline results of a randomised trial.
2. Each standard deviation raises earnings: Founders Pledge's 2019 method assumes 10% a year, 80% of it new income; our view uses its 2023 method, 19.1% a year times 17.5% for replicability, doubled for the household.
3. The gain starts about eight years later and lasts five years in the 2019 method's middle case; 40 years, a working life, in the 2023 method our view uses.
4. The cost is about $37.50 per child ($25 to $50).

### How the model works

The same as Founders Pledge's sheet for Winning Start, with Educational Initiatives' own effect and cost: the middle case is the model, and the pessimistic (10th percentile) and optimistic (90th) cases give each input's range. Founders Pledge discounts the earnings gain at its rate of 5% (our view: the shared rate, 4% by default), and the result in ln(income) per dollar is valued with GiveWell's weight on income (a year of doubled income is one unit of value).

### What drives the result

- **How much earnings rise per standard deviation** and **how long the gain lasts**: Founders Pledge's 2023 education figures, shared by every education model on the site (19.1% × 17.5%, 40 years). They carry no published range, so the ranges shown leave them out.
- **The exchange rate between health and income** under Your values.
- **The effect on test scores** and **the cost per child**.

### Where we differ from Founders Pledge

- **Earnings from learning (changed for consistency):** Founders Pledge's 2019 method, which this analysis used, counts 10% more earnings a year per standard deviation of test scores, 80% of it additional, for 5 years, for the earner alone. Founders Pledge's newer education analyses (2023: Central Square Foundation, EIDU, Imagine Worldwide, Teaching at the Right Level, Ubongo) count 19.1% times a 17.5% replicability adjustment, over a 40-year working life, doubled for the household. Our view uses Founders Pledge's latest for every education model, so a standard deviation of learning is worth the same wherever it comes from; it compounds the eight-year wait at the discount rate, as the 2023 method does, where the 2019 sheet used a simple factor. Per standard deviation this is about 3.7 times the 2019 value at 4%. The evaluator view keeps the 2019 figures.
- **Discount rate:** our view uses the shared rate under Your values (4% by default) instead of Founders Pledge's 5%.
- **Credit by share of resources:** when on, staff's pay below market counts as their contribution (our default for staff-delivered programs: about half of cost, paid about 15% below market). Founders Pledge counts no government money for this charity. Off by default.

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether test-score gains last into adult earnings: the evidence is thin and gains often fade.
- The effect comes from a trial's midline results; costs at scale, in government schools, may differ from the trial's.

### What isn't modelled

Benefits other than earnings, and any effect on teachers or schools.
