### Summary

Educational Initiatives makes Mindspark, software that lets schoolchildren in India practise maths and language at their own level. Founders Pledge's 2019 education report values it through the extra earnings better-taught children go on to make: **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="educational-initiatives-founders-pledge">1.0</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**. The figure is mostly uncertainty: across Founders Pledge's own pessimistic and optimistic cases it spans more than a thousandfold.

The model reproduces all 10 of Founders Pledge's calculated figures for Educational Initiatives, including its bottom line: an increase in ln(income) of 0.0013 per dollar, or about $520 to double one person's income for a year (Founders Pledge's middle case).

### What the charity does

Mindspark adapts each exercise to what the child already knows, in after-school centres and in government schools.

### How the impact happens

1. Test scores rise by about 0.2 standard deviations per child (0.1 to 0.3 across Founders Pledge's cases), based on midline results of a randomised trial.
2. Each standard deviation is assumed to raise earnings by 10% a year, of which 80% is new income.
3. The gain starts about eight years later and lasts five years in the middle case.
4. The cost is about $37.50 per child ($25 to $50).

### How the model works

The same as Founders Pledge's sheet for Winning Start, with Educational Initiatives' own effect and cost: the middle case is the model, and the pessimistic (10th percentile) and optimistic (90th) cases give each input's range. Founders Pledge discounts the earnings gain at its rate of 5% (our view: the shared rate, 4% by default), and the result in ln(income) per dollar is valued with GiveWell's weight on income (a year of doubled income is one unit of value).

### What drives the result

- **How much earnings rise per standard deviation** (1% to 25%) and **how long the gain lasts** (2 to 20 years): shared assumptions from Founders Pledge's 2019 education work, and the biggest swings.
- **The exchange rate between health and income** under Your values.
- **The effect on test scores** and **the cost per child**.

### Where we differ from Founders Pledge

- **Discount rate:** our view uses the shared rate under Your values (4% by default) instead of Founders Pledge's 5%. Founders Pledge discounts the eight-year wait with a simple factor rather than compounding; we keep its formula.
- **Shared figures:** the earnings gain, the share that is additional, the wait and the duration read the shared facts for Founders Pledge's 2019 education work; same numbers.
- **Credit by share of resources:** when on, staff's pay below market counts as their contribution (our default for staff-delivered programs: about half of cost, paid about 15% below market). Founders Pledge counts no government money for this charity. Off by default.

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether test-score gains last into adult earnings: the evidence is thin and gains often fade.
- The effect comes from a trial's midline results; costs at scale, in government schools, may differ from the trial's.

### What isn't modelled

Benefits other than earnings, and any effect on teachers or schools.
