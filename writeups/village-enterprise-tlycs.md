### Summary

Village Enterprise trains groups of people in extreme poverty in rural East Africa to start a small business, gives each a cash grant and sets up savings groups. The Life You Can Save puts the income gain at 3.92 dollars per dollar spent, from Village Enterprise's 2023/24 monitoring in Kenya, Rwanda and Uganda; counting the part of the gain the programme caused, over the years it lasts, that is **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**.

The model reproduces every calculated cell of The Life You Can Save's sheet. The Life You Can Save calls its figures highly approximate.

### What the charity does

Village Enterprise picks the poorest households in a village, forms businesses of three entrepreneurs, trains them for a year with business mentors, gives each business a cash grant and links the entrepreneurs into savings groups. In 2023/24 the sheet's three countries had 11,665 participating households.

### How the impact happens

1. **Consumption:** Village Enterprise surveys participants at the start and end of the programme year. Household consumption rose by 1,168 dollars in Kenya, 297 in Rwanda and 1,502 in Uganda, with smaller rises in assets and savings.
2. **Cost:** the country programmes' capital and recurrent costs, 3.2 million dollars.
3. **Return:** the rise times the households, over the cost, in each country; The Life You Can Save averages the three countries' returns: 3.92.
4. **Lasting gains (our step):** the businesses and savings keep raising consumption for some years.

### How the model works

The Life You Can Save's sheet comes first, ending at its average return. Our steps follow:

- **Consumption only, as ln(consumption).** The rise is set against the participants' own baseline consumption, the site's way of valuing an income gain; the assets and savings the sheet adds are left out, since later consumption is paid from them.
- **Pooled by money.** The three countries are weighted by the money spent in each, rather than averaging their returns.
- **What would have happened anyway:** the monitoring has no comparison group, so the rise includes what would have happened anyway. We judge that 8% of it is the programme's, from the randomised trial of the same programme in Kenya and Uganda.
- **Years:** the gain lasts about 8 years, fading 5% a year, discounted at the shared rate.
- **Beyond consumption:** deaths averted by higher household incomes (the site's shared figure) and a side effect for neighbours' gains.
- **Central costs** (a judged 20%) and, with credit by share of resources on, staff's pay below market.

### What drives the result

- **The share of the measured rise the programme caused** (8%, from 3% to 25%): the monitoring's rises are many times the trial's.
- **How long the gain lasts.**
- **The value of a healthy year against income.**

### Where we differ from The Life You Can Save

- **The unit.** The Life You Can Save stops at dollars of income per dollar; we value the consumption rise as a change in ln(consumption), the site's way.
- **What would have happened anyway** (8%, our judgment), a step the sheet leaves out; its own comment says the rise can't all be put down to the programme.
- **Years of benefit** (about 8, our judgment) against the sheet's one year, **discounted** at the shared rate.
- **Pooling** by money rather than a plain average of the three countries' returns.
- **Central costs, deaths averted by higher incomes, neighbours' gains and staff pay,** steps the sheet leaves out.

### Biggest uncertainties

- How much of the before-and-after rise the programme caused: the trial is of an earlier version of the programme in two of the three countries.
- How long the businesses keep earning.
- The monitoring figures are Village Enterprise's own, given to The Life You Can Save.

### What isn't modelled

- Village Enterprise's other programmes and countries (Ethiopia, Tanzania, the refugee programme), which the sheet doesn't cover.
- Governments' graduation programmes that Village Enterprise advises.
