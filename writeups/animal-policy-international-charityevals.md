### Summary

Animal Policy International campaigns for rules that imported eggs and pork meet the welfare standards of the country that imports them. No such rule has been won yet. This model counts what its 2025 work adds to the chance of three: in the EU, the UK and New Zealand. At the site's default weight of a chicken's pain against a person's, it comes to about **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**, a net harm. The hens and sows spared are outweighed by what shoppers would pay for dearer eggs and pork. The result turns positive only with chickens weighed about two hundred times higher.

### What the charity does

API was founded in 2023 through Ambitious Impact's (then Charity Entrepreneurship's) incubation programme. It is an Estonian non-profit association, with a New Zealand trust registered in 2025 and US donations through a fiscal sponsor. It publishes no accounts. For 2025 its EU entity declared a budget of €120,000 to the EU Transparency Register; the whole organisation spent somewhat more, about {{row:cost_total}} by our judgment. Its team page names nine staff.

- **EU**: evidence to the Commission on including imports in its coming laying-hen law, and in 2026 three reports on egg imports and their cost.
- **UK**: the first analysis of the UK's animal product imports (April 2025, with Compassion in World Farming and the RSPCA). In early 2026 it supported a Commons debate and gave evidence the Environment, Food and Rural Affairs Committee cited.
- **New Zealand**: reports and legal opinions, an 11,000-signature petition, and a member's bill (written by the Green Party) now in the ballot. It also built a farmers' alliance.

### How the impact happens

If a country requires imported eggs to be cage-free or imported pork to be free of sow stalls, some farms abroad change how they keep hens and sows. Others keep their caged hens and sell those eggs elsewhere. The country's shoppers pay a little more. API's part is the evidence and the campaigning that make such a rule more likely, sooner.

### How the model works

It is one model, with a branch for each place.

- **Cost**: the 2025 declared budget in dollars, times {{row:whole_org_ratio}} for money its New Zealand trust and US fiscal sponsor may have received outside the EU entity. {{row:program_share}} of it is programme spending.
- **EU**: about 9.3 million caged hens abroad supplied the EU's egg imports in 2025, by API's estimate. The chance the EU adopts cage-free requirements for imported eggs is {{row:eu_p_adopted}}. The Commission committed to equivalent standards for imports in February 2025, and a leaked draft of its laying-hen proposal includes them. If it passes, {{row:eu_conversion_share}} of those hens leave cages; the rest of the trade shifts to cage-free farms that already exist. By then about {{row:eu_battery_share}} of them would still be in battery cages, the rest in enriched cages. It would apply about 8 years after 2025.
- **UK**: the sows behind 6.4 million imported pigs a year. The chance of a UK rule on pork from sow stalls is {{row:uk_p_adopted}}. Most imports come from EU countries, which already limit stalls to the first four weeks of pregnancy, so a UK rule would change only {{row:uk_stall_gap_share}} of the shared crated-to-group-housing gain.
- **New Zealand**: the sows behind 540,000 imported pigs a year and 325,000 imported hens, about {{row:nz_battery_share}} of them in battery cages. The chance is {{row:nz_p_adopted}}, since the government opposes it.
- **Animals**: the shared suffering figures per hen-year out of a battery or enriched cage and per sow-year out of gestation crates. A won rule counts for the site's standard number of years, after its wait, discounted.
- **Credit**: API's share among those pressing for each rule: {{row:eu_attribution_share_shared}} in the EU, where many larger groups and the farm lobby push the same way; {{row:uk_attribution_share_shared}} in the UK; {{row:nz_attribution_share_shared}} in New Zealand, where it leads. Each is multiplied by 2025's share of its work, and by the mobiliser's share for the governments and exporters who carry the rule out.
- **Adjustments**: the animal counts are API's own conversions of trade data, with ranges; border enforcement is {{row:border_enforcement_adj}}; some caged eggs and crated pork are still sold elsewhere (the shared leakage figure). Every branch is discounted for missing data, since nothing is won or measured.
- **Compliance costs**: dearer eggs and pork, valued as money lost by the shoppers who pay. For the EU, API's own estimate of the rise in egg prices (about 0.3%) on the value of EU egg production, more for battery-caged hens. Where the trade only shifts, most of the premium is a transfer, so only {{row:eu_cost_borne_share}} of the full cost counts. For sows, the shared cost per sow from California's Proposition 12. Each is counted over the same chance, years and credit as the animals' gain.

### What drives the result

The EU branch is most of the gain and about half the cost; the UK's pork rule is most of the rest of the cost. The biggest swings are:

- the mobiliser's share and the years a won rule counts;
- the missing-data discount;
- the next-dollar figure ({{row:marginal_vs_past_average}});
- API's share among the EU's advocates, and 2025's share of its work;
- the chance the EU rule passes;
- what shoppers pay.

The weight of a chicken's pain against a person's decides the sign.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **Whether the EU rule passes, and covers egg products.** The Commission's proposal is due by December 2026; Parliament and the Council must then agree.
- **How many hens leave cages.** Exporters can meet the rule by sending the EU eggs from cage-free farms they already have and their caged eggs elsewhere.
- **API's part.** The EU's commitment came before API's EU work, and many groups push for it.
- **Spending.** API publishes no accounts; the cost rests on a self-declared budget for one of its entities.

### What isn't modelled

- Fish and broiler chickens: the rules pressed now cover eggs and pork only.
- Precedent for other countries and products, which API argues for but no evidence sizes.
- Trade retaliation against the importing countries' exports.
- Fur and foie gras, which API's UK report also names: no UK proposal on them is in play, and the site has no suffering figure for those animals.

### How this model was checked

Independent audits are in audits/animal-policy-international-charityevals/. Round 1 found the EU cost counted every caged hen behind the imports as converted, while the gain counted only those leaving cages (now the shifted trade's premium counts only in part); the cost at retail rather than producer prices; and the cost and the gain on different cage baselines (now a judged battery-cage share on both sides). It also led to a re-anchored whole-organisation ratio, the hens' laying life, ranges on the animal counts, New Zealand's hens by count and their cost by the colony move's own cost. The result moved from −0.0095 to −0.0056 healthy years per 1,000 dollars.
