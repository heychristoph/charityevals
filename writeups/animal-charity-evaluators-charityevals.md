### Summary

Animal Charity Evaluators (ACE) evaluates animal advocacy charities, recommends the ones it finds most effective, and passes donors' money to them. This model is of a gift for ACE's own work, not of the money that passes through it to other charities. It comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per $1,000**. Each dollar ACE spent led to about three dollars of giving it caused. That giving goes to animal charities, which at the site's animal weights do very little per dollar on the common scale, so ACE does too.

### What the charity does

ACE spent $6.58 million in its year to March 2025, by its audited accounts. Of that, $4.62 million was grants: donors' money passed on through its Recommended Charity Fund, gifts earmarked for a Recommended Charity, and its Movement Grants. Those grants are the recipients' cost, so they are taken out here. So is $242,000 of advertising Google donated. What is left, $1.72 million, is ACE's own spending: charity evaluations, Movement Grants grantmaking, fundraising for its Recommended Charities, and administration. Most of it is staff.

ACE says that in the same year it influenced at least $12.3 million of giving to its Recommended Charities. It puts $6.4 million of that down to its own work, $1.1 million of it from grantmakers. It also awarded $1.2 million in Movement Grants to 32 small groups, of which it counts $0.84 million as money the groups would not otherwise have got.

### How the impact happens

ACE does no animal work itself. It finds charities it thinks do the most good, and donors give to them because of it. Some of that money is new giving. Some would have gone to other animal charities anyway. Its Movement Grants fund small and new groups.

### How the model works

The model starts from the $13.5 million of giving ACE traced in the year, plus a tenth of the direct gifts for those it has no record of. It takes ACE's own estimate of the share donors gave because of it, from its surveys of donors, grantmakers and charities. Then it applies the site's rule for an organisation's own survey: people who didn't answer are counted at half the influence of those who did. ACE publishes no response rates, so we judge that about 40% of the giving was covered. That leaves about $4.6 million from donors. The $1.1 million from grantmakers is added as they reported it.

Each dollar moved is valued at ACE's Recommended Charities, read live from their figures on this site. They are weighted by what its Recommended Charity Fund paid each in the year. Eight have a figure on the rankings. The others, and the Movement Grants' small groups, take their average. Those figures are ACE's own evaluations, because that is what the rankings show, so this model is not an independent check of ACE's choices.

Half of donors' money counts as new giving. The rest, and all of the grantmakers' money, counts only for what it gains over an ordinary animal charity. By the site's rule for an organisation outside global health, an ordinary animal charity is to the animal charities on the rankings as GiveDirectly is to the global-health ones: about 60% of their average.

A further dollar is assumed to do 30% as much as ACE's average dollar so far.

### What drives the result

- **The animal weights.** Everything ACE moves goes to animals, so the result scales with how much the site counts animal suffering against human health.
- **Its next-dollar figure (30%, 10–72%).** ACE holds about 1.3 years of its own costs. Extra money would go to advertising and events, and to the restructuring of its recommendations and grantmaking it announced in October 2026.
- **The non-response step.** Counting every donor at full influence would raise donors' money moved by about two fifths.
- **The split between charities.** The Humane League's figure is about 5 times the next charity's.

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether ACE's own survey-based estimate of the giving it caused holds up. Its report on the method is not public.
- Whether direct gifts split between charities the way its fund does.
- Whether shifted money would otherwise have gone to an ordinary animal charity, or to a better or worse one.

### What isn't modelled

Side effects are one row, 10% (−10% to +30%) of the value. On the plus side: its reviews are used by funders and donors who don't report it, and its feedback helps evaluated charities. On the minus side: the staff time charities spend on its evaluations, and charities shaping their work to its criteria.

### How this model was checked

Audit rounds are recorded in audits/animal-charity-evaluators-charityevals/. Round 1 found two high and three medium problems. The Movement Grants now take the Recommended Charities' average instead of the whole animal cause. The charities are weighted by ACE's fund split instead of equally. Unrecorded gifts were added, and grantmakers' money is counted on its own. Round 2 passed, with no high findings. After it, the next-dollar figure was cut from 36% to 30% for ACE's restructuring, and unrecorded gifts were limited to direct gifts. Then shifted money moved to the site's shared rule for an organisation outside global health, against the animal cause's charities with old estimates left out: the result fell from 0.0017 to 0.0011.
