### Summary

The Environmental Defense Fund (EDF) is one of the largest US environmental groups ($319M of operating expenses in its year to September 2025). We model all of its programmes in two models: its climate and regional work (this model, 61% of programme spending with its share of core capacities) and its health and nature work (environmental-defense-fund-charityevals-health-nature, 39%). At the defaults the climate work gives about 0.0019 years of healthy life per $1,000 (about $230 a tonne of CO₂e), the health and nature work about 0.0015.

### What the charity does

EDF employs scientists, economists and lawyers to design and win environmental rules. Its accounts have five programme areas: Stabilize the Climate ($99.5M), Support People's Health ($15.9M), Strengthen People's and Nature's Ability to Thrive ($62.3M: oceans, fisheries, farms, water, resilience), Regionally Focused Work ($21.1M: China, India, Europe, US regions) and Core Capacities and Education ($80.1M: science, economics, communications, members), which we spread over the other four by their spending. The accounts consolidate its lobbying arm, EDF Action, and MethaneSAT LLC, the subsidiary that built and flew a methane-measuring satellite from March 2024 until contact was lost in June 2025.

### How the impact happens

Climate: rules and laws that cut greenhouse gas, mostly methane from oil and gas. The period's largest wins with published tonnes are EPA's 2023 oil and gas methane rule (58 million tons of methane over 2024-2038), the EU Methane Regulation's import standard (95-120 million tonnes of CO₂e a year from about 2031), the Inflation Reduction Act (2022) and EPA's 2024 power-plant and vehicle standards, both since repealed. MethaneSAT was meant to find leaks for regulators and companies to fix. Health and nature: EPA's 2024 soot standard and lead-pipe rule, fisheries reforms and farm emissions.

### How the model works

Each win is a branch: its tonnes a year (the regulator's or modeller's estimate, a shared figure every model counting the win reads) × the share of a won policy due to advocacy at all (30%) × the years a won reform counts (5), discounted with the wait until the tonnes come × what wouldn't have happened anyway × EDF's share of the advocates' resources × the share delivered since. The counterfactual and delivered shares, and the EU rule's wait, are the same shared figures Clean Air Task Force's model reads; EDF's shares of the advocacy (25% of the US methane rule, 15% of the EU rule, 10% and 8% of the power-plant and vehicle rules, 2% of the Inflation Reduction Act) leave room for Clean Air Task Force's (10%, 20%, 10%) and the many other groups; the law's carbon-capture credit, Clean Air Task Force's win, is left out of EDF's branch. The period's wins are set against its average yearly spending on these programmes (October 2021 to September 2025, each year's own programme mix, in 2025 prices). MethaneSAT's cost is counted in full: the satellite was capitalised, so most of it never passed through operating expenses; its $58.2M write-off, less $15.9M of insurance, is added to the period's cost (it lowers the result by 5%). Its benefit is a judged share of world oil and gas methane cut because of its data, for a few years.

The health and nature model values EPA's deaths at the ages of rich-country heart and lung deaths (most over 70), children's IQ points through later earnings, fishers' incomes and farm tonnes, on the same chain of advocacy share, years counted, counterfactual, EDF's share and delivery.

### What drives the result

Climate: the US methane rule gives about 55% of the value, the EU regulation about 30%, the Inflation Reduction Act about 10%; the repealed rules and MethaneSAT the rest. Methane's tonnes count for their own, earlier warming and the ozone methane forms (the shared conversion), which is why methane rules dominate. Health and nature: the lead-pipe rule gives about 60%, the soot standard most of the rest.

### Biggest uncertainties

The shared years a won reform counts, the missing-data discount, the shared advocacy share, the discount rate and EDF's next-dollar figure (31%, 14% to 71%: large reserves and a turned federal landscape). EDF's own shares are judgments; no tonne, death or income is measured for its work.

### What isn't modelled

The Inflation Reduction Act's repealed methane fee, the heavy-duty truck standards, state laws, the regional teams' work on China's carbon market and Europe's shipping, aviation and carbon market are in a share of the climate model's value (30%, 10% to 80%); toxic-chemical rules, PFAS, water and resilience in the health and nature model's (30%). The US methane rule's local smog cut is a side effect (+1.5%). Costs the rules put on industry and consumers aren't counted, as in the site's other policy models.

### How this model was checked

Independent audits are in audits/environmental-defense-fund-charityevals/ and audits/environmental-defense-fund-charityevals-health-nature/. Round 1 passed the climate model and found two highs in the health and nature model: US deaths had been valued at the world's air-pollution ages (now rich-country heart and lung ages, −46%), and the IQ earnings gain lacked the shared household-sharing multiplier. Round 1 also added the Inflation Reduction Act as a branch, used each year's own programme mix for the period's cost, restated EPA's methane tonnes at the shared warming potential (also in Clean Air Task Force's model, +1.5% there), dropped overhead on the satellite write-off, and removed farm methane from the climate model's unmodelled list (it is a branch of the health and nature model).
