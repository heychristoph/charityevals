### Summary

The Environmental Defense Fund (EDF) is one of the largest US environmental groups ($319M of operating expenses in its year to September 2025). We model all of its programmes in two models: its climate and regional work (this model, 61% of programme spending with its share of core capacities) and its health and nature work (environmental-defense-fund-charityevals-health-nature, 39%). At the defaults the climate work gives about 0.0018 years of healthy life per $1,000 (about $290 a tonne of CO₂e), the health and nature work about 0.0020.

### What the charity does

EDF employs scientists, economists and lawyers to design and win environmental rules. Its accounts have five programme areas: Stabilize the Climate ($99.5M), Support People's Health ($15.9M), Strengthen People's and Nature's Ability to Thrive ($62.3M: oceans, fisheries, farms, water, resilience), Regionally Focused Work ($21.1M: China, India, Europe, US regions) and Core Capacities and Education ($80.1M: science, economics, communications, members), which we spread over the other four by their spending. The accounts consolidate its lobbying arm, EDF Action, and MethaneSAT LLC, the subsidiary that built and flew a methane-measuring satellite from March 2024 until contact was lost in June 2025.

### How the impact happens

Climate: rules that cut greenhouse gas, mostly methane from oil and gas. The period's largest wins with published tonnes are EPA's 2023 oil and gas methane rule (1.5 billion tonnes of CO₂e over 2024-2038), the EU Methane Regulation's import standard (95-120 million tonnes a year from about 2031), and EPA's 2024 power-plant and vehicle standards, both since repealed. MethaneSAT was meant to find leaks for regulators and companies to fix.

### How the model works

Each win is a branch: its tonnes a year (the regulator's or modeller's estimate, a shared figure every model counting the win reads) × the share of a won policy due to advocacy at all (30%) × the years a won reform counts (5), discounted with the wait until the tonnes come × what wouldn't have happened anyway × EDF's share of the advocates' resources × the share delivered since. The counterfactual and delivered shares, and the EU rule's wait, are the same shared figures Clean Air Task Force's model reads; EDF's shares of the advocacy (25% of the US methane rule, 15% of the EU rule, 10% and 8% of the power-plant and vehicle rules) leave room for Clean Air Task Force's (10% and 20%) and the many other groups. The period's wins are set against its average yearly spending (October 2021 to September 2025, in 2025 prices). MethaneSAT's cost is counted in full: the satellite was capitalised, so most of it never passed through operating expenses; its $58.2M write-off, less $15.9M of insurance, is added to the period's cost (it lowers the result by 6%). Its benefit is a judged share of world oil and gas methane cut because of its data, for a few years.

### What drives the result

The US methane rule gives about 60% of the value and the EU regulation about 30%; the repealed rules and MethaneSAT about 2% each. Methane's tonnes count for their own, earlier warming and the ozone methane forms (the shared conversion), which is why methane rules dominate.

### Biggest uncertainties

The shared years a won reform counts (1 to 20 moves the result from 0.0004 to 0.0053), the missing-data discount, the shared advocacy share, the discount rate and EDF's next-dollar figure (31%, 14% to 71%: large reserves and a turned federal landscape). EDF's own shares are judgments; no tonne is measured for its work.

### What isn't modelled

The Inflation Reduction Act and its repealed methane fee, the heavy-duty truck standards, state laws, the regional teams' work in China, India and Europe, and shipping and aviation are in a share of the modelled value (40%, 15% to 100%), not branches. The US methane rule's local smog cut is a side effect (+1.5%). Costs the rules put on industry and consumers aren't counted, as in the site's other climate models.
