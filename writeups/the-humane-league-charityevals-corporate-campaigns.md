### Summary

The Humane League (THL) presses food companies to keep the cage-free promises they made, in the US and around the world. We put this work at about $5.1 million of its 2025 programme spending. Our estimate of a dollar given for it is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="the-humane-league-charityevals-corporate-campaigns">0.0004</span> years of healthy life per $1,000**, below the median animal model on the site.

The result is this work's share of the 5.5 million hens that left cages in 2025 at companies THL pressed itself. That is an outcome many groups, and THL's own grassroots work, share. THL's count is its own and what the pressure brought forward is our judgment, so the result takes the site's shared discount for missing data.

### What the charity does

THL's Form 990 reports one programme. ACE's model of THL splits out the accountability work: $3.4 million on US supply chains and $1.6 million on global ones in 2024, which come to $5.1 million in 2025 prices.

- **In the US**, THL tracks companies' progress, negotiates with those that fall behind and campaigns against those that go silent. In 2025 Subway finished its switch in North America (nearly 340,000 hens) after THL's Eggsposé report, and Walmart came back to talks on its pledge.
- **Abroad**, THL's global team presses multinational companies to report on and keep their pledges.

THL's other lines are its grants (to Open Wing Alliance groups, to THL UK and to US policy groups) and its other programmes (grassroots mobilisation, new commitments, policy, and running the Open Wing Alliance). ACE's UK rows are THL UK's work, which THL's grant pays for, so they are in the grants line. ACE counts the alliance's running as paths of its own, so it is in the other programmes.

### How the model works

One model on the site's corporate-campaign template, with the site's shared figures per hen-year and the site's years a won reform counts.

- **Hens**: THL's 7.5 million hens out of cages in 2025, less two groups credited in the grants line. One is the switches at THL UK's companies (about 0.4 million). The other is those at companies Alliance members pressed (1.6 million). That leaves 5.5 million, nine in ten from battery cages.
- **Brought forward**: we judge 30% wouldn't have switched yet without the pressure. Pressed pledges are kept more often than others (92% against 77%), and these companies had stalled.
- **Credit**: THL's 87% share among the groups (ACE's figures), times this line's share of THL's own resources on the campaigns. Those resources are this line, the grassroots mobilisation in THL's other programmes, and the volunteers' unpaid time. The other-programmes model takes the mobilisation's share of the same pool over the same total, and the volunteers' share goes to no dollar.
- **Side effects**, through the side-effects slider: wider gains from switches (+10% of the hens' gain) and caged eggs sold elsewhere (−10%).

The year's spending is set against the year's switches, so the next dollar counts at THL's own next-dollar figure.

### How we avoid counting the same hens twice

THL has three models of its own on the site, and each hen is credited only once:

- **THL UK's switches**, and its supermarket and broiler work, are in the grants line. They come out of THL's count here.
- **The Alliance members' accountability wins** are one pool. The grants model credits the grants' share of it and the other-programmes model the alliance's running. Those switches come out of THL's count here.
- **The switches at companies THL pressed itself** are shared between this line and the other-programmes model's mobilisation, by each one's share of the same resources. Together they never get more than THL's share.
- **THL's four new commitments of 2025** are in the other-programmes model only.

### What drives the result

- The site-wide sliders: a chicken against a person, the years a won reform counts and how much an unmeasured result counts. THL's next-dollar figure.
- The share of switches that wouldn't have come yet without pressure, and the shared hen figure.
- How many of THL's switches were at companies THL UK or Alliance members pressed.

### Biggest uncertainties

- How many switches the pressure brought forward. Nobody has measured what the pressure itself changed.
- The 2025 split of THL's money between this line and its mobilisation, which sets each one's share. ACE's 2024 costs stand in for 2025's.
- THL's count of hens spared is its own, and it gives no split by country or by who pressed each company.

### How this model was checked

Built with charityevals' corporate-campaign template on 13 November 2026; the mechanical audit and the site's invariants pass.

- **Round 1** of independent review (3 high, 2 medium, 7 low):
  - ACE's US and global costs had been swapped.
  - THL's running of the Open Wing Alliance is not in ACE's accountability costs, so it moved to the other-programmes model with its share of the alliance's wins.
  - The UK switches taken out are now those at THL UK's companies within THL's count, not the UK's whole change.
  - THL's share among the groups is now 0.87 (up to 0.95).
  - The reason for 30% brought forward is now stated in all three THL models.
  - The side effects of dearer eggs now carry the same sign in all three.
  - The UK's two cage-free shares are drawn together in the grants model.
  - The three models take their overhead from the same source.
- **Round 2** (no high, no medium, 8 low):
  - Labels and explainers were corrected in the other-programmes model.
  - The alliance's resources now count THL staff's forgone pay on both sides when credit is shared by resources.
  - The alliance's running is one judgment with one explainer in both models that use it.
  - Left as is: the mobilisation's spending here is a ranged copy of the other-programmes model's figure, since one model can't read another's row.
- **Verification** of those fixes (no high, no medium, 2 low): all settled. The alliance's running stays in the other-programmes model's cost section, since it splits that model's money.
