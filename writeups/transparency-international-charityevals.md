### Summary

Transparency International campaigns against corruption worldwide, through a Secretariat in Berlin and more than 100 national chapters. Our model of the Secretariat's 2025 work comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**. It is a very uncertain figure: how much of corruption's cost one change in a law or rule removes, and how many people an average change governs, are our judgments, with no study that measures them.

### What the charity does

Transparency International e.V., the Secretariat, spent EUR 28.0 million in 2025 with its offices in Brussels and Washington. About half of that, EUR 13.6 million, went to national chapters and other partners as grants. It publishes the Corruption Perceptions Index. It campaigns for global and EU rules: in 2025, a UN anti-corruption convention resolution on political finance built on its standards, the EU's ban on golden passports and two parts of the EU anti-corruption directive. It brought 62 legal submissions with chapters and partners, among them evidence behind a German politician's conviction in the Azerbaijani Laundromat case and a brief in an arbitration that rejected a EUR 148 million claim against Malta. The chapters' Advocacy and Legal Advice Centres supported 14,000 people in 53 countries.

### How the impact happens

Corruption costs people income: contracts go to the wrong firms, roads cost more and last less, and investment and taxes are lost. About {{row:pol_bribery_share}} of the world's income is paid in bribes each year. A new rule, register or practice that is applied removes a small share of that cost for everyone it governs. People who get help from an advice centre may get back a bribe, a service or land. A won case returns public money and deters others.

### How the model works

- **Cost:** the audited consolidated statements. The 2025 wins came from several years of work, so the cost is the average real spending of 2023-2025, about 9% below 2025's. Administration, fundraising, evaluation and the CEO's office are spread over the programmes. Grants to chapters count as the work they fund.
- **Policy branch (about 94% of the result):**
  - the organisation's own count of at least 60 changes in policy, law and practice it contributed to in 2025;
  - each governs on average about 29 million people: three EU-wide rules govern 452 million each, and the national, agency and city changes about 9 million each (3 to 30 million). Global resolutions count no one here: their effect comes through the national laws that implement them. About {{row:pol_eu_people_share}} of the people governed live in the EU;
  - corruption costs about 1.6% of income in the EU (above the European Commission's and the European Parliament's floors) and is taken as about the 2% of income paid in bribes elsewhere. A change removes 0.3% of it where applied, if it works as tested measures do: a judgment anchored on Olken's trial in Indonesia, where audits cut the money missing from road projects by 8 percentage points;
  - 40% of a change is applied as written, and the evidence that such rules work gets a factor of one half;
  - the gain builds up over about four years and is counted for the years a won reform counts on the site, less a yearly chance of repeal;
  - the Secretariat gets about 40% of the movement's credit, and the movement about 15% of all groups', governments' own reformers doing about half the work.
- **Advice-centre branch:** 14,000 people supported. We judge that 10% get their problem put right, gaining about 30% of a year's consumption. 60% of that wouldn't have happened without the centre, and the Secretariat gets 30% of the credit.
- **Legal-submission branch:** 62 submissions, 15% of them successful, recovering or saving about $2 million each. That money is valued as income spread over the people whose budget it is, and deterrence adds as much again.
- **Side effects:** fairer treatment by the state beyond income adds about 30% to the policy branch. Backlash against the work takes away 5%, and reprisals against people who report take away 3% of the advice-centre branch.
- **Next dollar:** about half the past average. Reserves are modest and most of the money is tied to donors' projects.

### What drives the result

The share of corruption's cost a change removes moves the result about fortyfold across its range. Then come the missing-data slider, the years a won reform counts and the movement's share of the credit.

### Biggest uncertainties

- Whether the 60 changes reduce corruption at all, and by how much. Few anti-corruption rules have been tested.
- The count is the organisation's own, and many changes are soft or voluntary rules.
- The Secretariat's share of credit, beside the chapters' own budgets, which aren't published, and other groups' work.

### What isn't modelled

- The Corruption Perceptions Index and research count only through the changes they help bring about.
- Health effects (medicines and services reaching people) are inside the income gain, not counted separately.

### How this model was checked

Built on 14 November 2026 from the rights-advocacy template and checked by independent audit rounds (audits/transparency-international-charityevals/). The first round passed with four medium findings, all fixed: global resolutions now count no people (their effect comes through national laws), the EU's people are valued at the EU's own cost of corruption, the cost is the average of 2023-2025, and the movement's share of credit fell from 30% to 15% to match the work governments do. Together these more than halved the result. The second round (one medium finding) raised the EU's cost of corruption to 1.6%, as the European Parliament's study counts only above-average corruption in the scenarios first used, and built the people governed from the EU rules and the national changes.
