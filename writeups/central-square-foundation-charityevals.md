### Summary

Central Square Foundation (CSF) helps Indian state governments improve what children learn in government schools. Our model counts its spending in the year to March 2026 against a year of the learning gains from the state programmes it supports, credited by CSF's share of everyone's resources behind them, and valued as the children's later earnings. It comes to **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**. The figure is very uncertain: nobody has measured how much the state missions themselves raise learning, a test-score gain partly fades before adulthood, and the earnings start a decade or more later, so the discount rate moves the result a lot.

### What the charity does

CSF is a non-profit trust founded in 2012 in New Delhi. It has over 300 staff working in 16 states and union territories. It spent ₹107 crore (about $12.3 million) in the year to March 2026: ₹87.7 crore on its programmes directly, ₹6.1 crore in grants to partners, and the rest on non-programme staff, premises and depreciation. It works in five areas:

- **Foundational literacy and numeracy (FLN):** state and district project units in 11 states (Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Assam, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Maharashtra, Punjab and Haryana). They help run each state's mission under the national NIPUN Bharat programme: workbooks and teacher guides, teacher training, classroom mentoring, assessments and data. Together these states' schools enrol about 2.6 crore children.
- **Early childhood education:** support for new pre-primary classes in Uttar Pradesh, Odisha and Telangana. About 4 lakh children got pre-primary education for the first time.
- **EdTech and AI:** technical support units that help Andhra Pradesh and Himachal Pradesh run personalised adaptive learning software for about 4.3 lakh students. Also an accelerator for EdTech products, product reviews for states' procurement, and an AI-literacy curriculum.
- **School governance:** school report cards for parents in Jodhpur and Angul districts (about 1 lakh students), and school accreditation frameworks in six states.
- **High-potential students:** a STEM pilot with about 400 students in five residential schools, started in August 2025.

### How the impact happens

Children who can read and do basic maths by Grade 3 learn more in later grades and, as adults, earn more. CSF doesn't teach children itself. It helps governments run programmes that reach millions of them, so its effect is a share of what those programmes achieve.

### How the model works

- **Cost:** all of CSF's spending in the year to March 2026, at the 2025 exchange rate. The accounts don't split spending by programme, so each area's share is our judgment from the annual report: FLN about 63%, EdTech 17%, early childhood 10%, governance 7%, high-potential students 3%.
- **Children reached:** for FLN, one grade's worth of children a year in the 11 states (enrolment ÷ about 4 grades). Each year one cohort finishes the foundational grades with the missions' gain. The other branches use the annual report's counts of children reached in the year; the EdTech accelerator's 30 lakh learners over 2023-2025 count as 10 lakh a year.
- **Learning gain:** in standard deviations of test scores:
  - the state missions: 0.12 by the end of Grade 3, our judgment. Between 2022 and 2024, reading in rural government schools rose 7.1 points, against 2.4 in private schools, which the missions don't cover;
  - a year of adaptive learning: 0.3, from Andhra Pradesh's randomised trial of the same software in the state's schools (0.43 more over 17 months, in maths);
  - a year of pre-primary: 0.15, our judgment;
  - better EdTech products: 0.01, our judgment;
  - report cards for parents: 0.04, our judgment;
  - the STEM pilot: 0.1, our judgment.
- **From learning to earnings:** the site's shared figures for low- and middle-income countries. 40% of a gain lasts into adulthood, and each lasting standard deviation raises earnings {{row:earnings_gain_per_sd}}. The gain is shared in the household and lasts a 40-year working life, which starts at about 20 and is discounted.
- **CSF's credit:** its share of all resources behind each outcome. For FLN that is about 3%: CSF's FLN spending against the mission's ₹2,500 crore central allocation (80% of it spent), grossed up by the states' share and taken for CSF's states, plus other partners' spending. Early childhood about 6% (against the states' spending on the first-time children), adaptive learning about 4%, other EdTech about 25%, governance about 85% (a judged share among those who pressed for the pilots), the STEM pilot about 45%: each of the others CSF's spending against the states' or partners'.
- **Adjustments:** 85% for publication bias in the trials behind the judged gains and the conversion of ASER's shares to standard deviations. 85% of the credit is CSF's own rather than what another technical partner would have done in its place (95% for the report cards and the STEM pilot, which CSF ran itself). The report cards count as won reforms, for the years a won reform counts on the site, with a 50% chance that each becomes lasting practice; each child is credited once, not every year they are covered.
- **Side effects:** a net +5%. Longer schooling, later marriage and better health for the children's own children count for, teachers' time taken by data entry and reporting against.
- **The missing-data discount** applies to every branch except adaptive learning in Andhra Pradesh, where the state counts the students and a trial measured the same software in the same schools.
- **The next dollar** is valued at {{row:marginal_vs_past_average}} of the average. CSF holds about 15 months of spending in cash and deposits, its next programmes go to new districts, grades and workstreams, and the national mission was set to be wound up in 2025-26 unless extended.

### What drives the result

The discount rate, the missing-data slider, the value of a healthy year in units of value, the next dollar's value, how much of a learning gain lasts, household sharing and earnings per standard deviation move the result most. Next come the missions' learning gain, the counterfactual and the evidence adjustment. About half the result is FLN, over a quarter adaptive learning, and the rest early childhood, the report cards and other EdTech.

### Biggest uncertainties

- **The missions' gain:** learning rose in government schools after 2022, but how much of that is the missions, and how much is recovery from the pandemic and other changes, is our judgment. States' own assessments are not independent.
- **Fade-out:** test-score gains from school programmes shrink over the following years. How much reaches adult earnings in India is not measured.
- **CSF's share:** the state budgets behind the missions are only roughly known, and the national mission's future is open: a parliamentary committee noted in March 2026 that it was set to be wound up. CSF's share could be half or twice ours.

### What isn't modelled

Accreditation frameworks and school standards authorities in six states count only once they reach parents, as disclosure; none had yet. National work (the PARAKH survey, NIPUN's extension to Grade 5) is valued only through the states' gains. Wins after March 2026 count only through the next dollar.

### How this model was checked

Audit rounds are listed in audits/central-square-foundation-charityevals/. Round 1 found three high issues: the adaptive-learning trial's effect size was published (CSF's PAL brochure), so that branch is now measured; the evidence adjustment repeated doubts other rows already priced in (0.6 to 0.85); and the EdTech accelerator's three-year count had been taken as one year. It also asked for the mission's spending rather than its allocation, a sourced enrolment share and attribution built from CSF's spending in every branch. With the site's shared education figures, the result went from 0.54 to 0.91. Round 2 passed, with one medium and six low findings, all fixed: a wrong anchor removed, report cards credited once per child, and the governance, STEM and early-childhood credit shares built from spending. That took the result to 0.85.
