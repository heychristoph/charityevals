### Summary

Breast Cancer Now funds breast cancer research in the UK, runs support services for people with breast cancer, and campaigns on screening and treatment. SoGive could not trace the charity's own research to health gains, so it estimated what UK breast cancer research as a whole has cost per year of healthy life gained: £1,000 to £8,800. At the middle of that range, allowing for the share of a donation that goes to research and the years research takes to pay off, that is **<span data-figure="headline" data-model="breast-cancer-now-sogive">0.060</span> years of healthy life per $1,000** on our scale.

SoGive publishes no spreadsheet; the model is built from the figures in SoGive's analysis, each quoted on its row; its two bounds on the unlabelled spending give SoGive's two ends. SoGive rates Breast Cancer Now "Not Recommended (tentative)".

### What the charity does

Breast Cancer Now funds research from biology to drug discovery, runs health-promotion schemes and support services, and campaigns on access to treatment and screening. It spent £40m in 2019–20, without a breakdown by project.

### How the impact happens

SoGive uses Glover et al. (2014), which estimated the returns to UK cancer research:

1. **Spending:** UK cancer research cost £15.1bn from 1970 to 2009. 40% of it is labelled by cancer type, and breast cancer has 19% of the labelled spending. Counting none or all of the unlabelled spending as breast cancer's gives £1.15bn to £10.2bn.
2. **Health gained:** breast cancer screening and treatment gained 1.155 million quality-adjusted life years from 1991 to 2010.
3. Dividing gives £1,000 to £8,800 per healthy year, against SoGive's benchmark of £100 to £200.

### How the model works

SoGive's figures come first, ending at its cost per healthy year. SoGive gives only the two bounds, so the central value is ours: the unlabelled spending split like the labelled (19% breast cancer), £2,484 per healthy year. Our steps follow:

- **Research share:** only the part of a donation spent on research is credited with SoGive's healthy years. In 2019–20 the charity spent £14.6m on research out of £39.7m in all (£17.0m of it on fundraising): 37%.
- **Pounds to dollars** at the 2021 average exchange rate.
- **The lag:** research pays off about 15 years later (Glover et al.), discounted at the shared rate.
- **Staff pay:** with credit by share of resources on, staff's forgone pay counts as part of the cost.

### What drives the result

- **How much of the unlabelled research was about breast cancer**, which spans SoGive's whole range (ninefold).
- **The research share of spending** (37% in 2019–20).
- **The research lag and the discount rate.** At the default 4%, a 15-year lag roughly halves the figure.
- **Whether Breast Cancer Now's research is average.** SoGive assumes so, having no sign it is better.

### Where we differ from SoGive

- **A central figure** within SoGive's range.
- **Research share:** SoGive's figure is per pound of research; we count fundraising, support services and campaigning as part of the cost of the healthy years.
- **Discounting** for the years between paying for research and the health it brings; SoGive's ratio compares past spending with past gains.
- **Staff pay:** with credit by share of resources, staff's forgone pay counts as an in-kind contribution.
- **Past average, not the next dollar (ours):** SoGive's figure divides UK breast cancer research spending over 1970–2009 by the healthy years that research is credited with, an average. The next dollar does less than the average did, since an organisation pursues its best opportunities first: like every model built on an organisation's past spending over its past wins, our view counts the next dollar at the share of the average set under Your values, "How much the next dollar does compared with the average dollar so far" (50% by default, our judgment; 18% to 100%). The evaluator view keeps 100%.

### Biggest uncertainties

The average return on past research says little about the next pound: SoGive notes research can duplicate or go stale, while the gains after 2010 aren't counted, and research outside cancer and outside the UK both feed into UK cancer outcomes. SoGive doesn't say in which year's prices the spending is stated; we convert at the 2021 rate. SoGive credits all of the healthy years to UK research, though research abroad contributed too, which flatters the figure.

### What isn't modelled

Breast Cancer Now's support services, outreach and campaigning.
