### Summary

Malaria Consortium helped South Sudan's health ministry run its bednet campaigns on phones instead of paper, registering and mapping every household. The Life You Can Save credits the digital tools with the 13.55% more people the registration found than the projections and estimates $98 per DALY averted; valuing each child's death averted by age and discounting the nets' later years, that is **{{result}} years of healthy life per 1,000 dollars**.

The model reproduces every calculated cell of the sheet's South Sudan tab. The Life You Can Save calls its figures highly approximate.

### What the charity does

From 2022 Malaria Consortium supported South Sudan's mass net campaigns: field teams registered households on phones with geo-tags and unique IDs, supervisors watched coverage live, and nets were allocated from the registers. The campaigns reached 7.7 million people with 4.3 million nets.

### How the impact happens

1. **More people reached:** the digital registration found 13.55% more people than the paper projections, about 1.05 million, who the sheet takes would otherwise have gone without nets.
2. **Malaria averted:** 44% fewer episodes for those using the nets (85% of them), over three years.
3. **Children's deaths averted:** 5.6 lives saved a year per 1,000 children protected (the Cochrane review), for the 17.4% of them under five.
4. **Cost:** Malaria Consortium's $118,769 in 2024, plus the nets for the extra people at $5.62 each delivered, whoever paid for them.

### How the model works

The Life You Can Save's sheet comes first, ending at its cost per DALY. Our steps follow:

- **Deaths valued by age.** The sheet counts a child's death averted as the remaining life expectancy, discounted at 3%; the site values it by GiveWell's moral weight for a child under five and the value-of-a-life slider.
- **The nets' later years discounted** at the shared rate.
- **What would have happened anyway:** ×1, since the sheet already credits only the people the digital tools found and counts their nets' cost.
- **Staff pay:** with credit by share of resources on, staff's pay below market counts as part of the cost.

### What drives the result

- **Whether the extra people found would otherwise have missed out.** The sheet takes all 13.55% as people who got nets only because of the registers.
- **The value of a child's death averted**, nearly all the healthy years gained.
- **The cost of the extra nets**, most of the cost counted.

### Where we differ from The Life You Can Save

- **Deaths valued by age** with the site's moral weights, rather than discounted life expectancy (28 years in the sheet).
- **Discounting** the nets' second and third years at the shared rate; the sheet adds them up at face value.
- **Staff pay** with credit by share of resources on: a step the sheet leaves out.

### Biggest uncertainties

- The 13.55% is how far the registers exceeded the projections; part of it may be people the paper campaign would also have reached, or projections that were too low.
- Only children's deaths are counted; deaths at five or older aren't.

### What isn't modelled

- Fewer infections among people without nets, and income gained from less illness.
- Better planning of later campaigns from the registers.
